Find your way through Self-as-an-End

The SAE corpus now contains more than 350 papers across eight broad domains and over forty named series and clusters. This page is not another catalogue. It is a map: where to begin, what must be read in order, and where you can safely follow your own question.

Reading paths by interest

These are recommended routes, not claims that the corpus has only one correct order. Arrows indicate a useful dependency; commas indicate papers or branches that can be chosen independently.

Core SAE and subjecthood

Methodology M→Systems & Personhood→Internal Colonization→Complete Framework→Inter-Ontological→Institution

The shortest route to the framework's central normative argument and its move from the subject to relations and institutions.

Philosophy, ethics, and law

Methodology M→The First Critique→Critique of Ethics→Moral Law 0→Rights Prequel, Law I

Use this route for SAE's relation to Kant, one's own law, multi-subject morality, rights, and jurisprudence.

Mathematics and ZFCρ

Methodology M→ZFCρ I→II→III→continue sequentially through LXXIV

ZFCρ is the largest and most internally sequential series. The SAE Mathematics papers and applied prime pre-sieve are adjacent branches, not substitutes for the main sequence.

Cosmology and fundamental physics

Methodology M→Cosmo 0→Four Forces 0→Four Forces I→Mass I

For quantum, information, and black-hole questions, branch into QM I, Information Theory I, or Black Hole I.

Biology and medicine

Methodology M→Metabolic oncology, speech loss, eating disorders, or any Biology Note matched to your question

The ten Biology Notes are applications rather than a single cumulative argument. Read by topic. The Life Cycle Table is the more systematic biological sequence.

AI, LLMs, and human–AI symbiosis

Finding Remainders with AI→Human–AI Symbiosis→Epistemology I→AI I→AI Architecture I

This path separates using AI as a chisel from claims about AI subjectivity, emergence, memory, and alignment.

Cognition, learning, and psychology

Epistemology I–IV→Learning I–IV; then Introspection, Dreams & Anesthesia, or Psychoanalysis I

The epistemology and learning series are sequential. The psychology and self-with-self papers are thematic branches.

Institutions, power, rights, value, and economics

Inter-Ontological→Institution→Power Prequel, Rights Prequel, Value Prequel, Economics

These series share a question: how constructs coordinate subjects without replacing the subjects as ends.

Chinese philosophy and Buddhist interfaces

Methodology M→Daodejing I–IX, Zhuangzi I–IV, Buddhist Interface I–VI

Each cluster has its own internal order. They can be read independently of one another after the methodological overview.

History, anthropology, arts, and myth

Anthropology Prequel→Civilization History I→II→III→IV; or branch to Temporal Arts and Mythology

Civilization History is sequential. The arts and mythology papers are independent applications of chisel, construct, and remainder.

Read well

What should be read in order?

The numbering system does not always imply the same kind of dependency. Use these three reading modes.

Read sequentially

ZFCρ, Epistemology, Moral Law, Daodejing, Zhuangzi, Quantum Mechanics, Information Theory, Thermodynamics, Learning, and Civilization History develop cumulative arguments. Begin at the first paper unless a later paper explicitly says it is standalone.

Begin with the prequel, then branch

Power, Rights, Value, Anthropology, and Four Forces provide a prequel or zero paper before opening into narrower questions. Read that framing paper first, then follow the branch that matches your question.

Choose by question

Biology Notes, applied philosophy, psychology, individual philosopher studies, temporal arts, mythology, and many thought experiments are designed to be entered through the topic itself.

Read bilingually

Language and reference access

Most papers provide English and Chinese versions through tabs at the top of the paper page; a smaller number are English-only. If a translated phrase seems structurally important, compare both versions. For machine reading, use llms.txt for orientation and papers.json for the flat metadata index. The complete human- and machine-readable corpus remains on the homepage.
