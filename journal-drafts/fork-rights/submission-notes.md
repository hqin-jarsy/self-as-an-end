# Fork Rights — Submission Notes

## Current status

This is a first journal-oriented reconstruction, not a revision of the existing SAE foundation paper. It deliberately does not rely on the SAE framework, the Base Layer / Emergence Layer distinction, or the DD sequence.

The manuscript's distinctive claim is not merely that exit must be affordable. That position is already well represented in the literature on substantive and fair exit. The proposed contribution is:

> Forking is continuity-preserving divergence: an agent carries a normatively adequate portable stake from a shared or accumulated past into a path governed independently of the incumbent institution.

## Main editorial decisions

1. **The paper acknowledges prior uses of “the right to fork.”** Open-source scholarship already uses the term. The claim is a philosophical generalization beyond software, not invention of the phrase.
2. **The paper distinguishes fork from exit by continuity, not by cost alone.** This is necessary in light of Rouméas (2023), Taylor (2017), and the wider substantive-exit literature.
3. **The right is pro tanto and graduated.** It protects an adequate portable stake, not unrestricted copying or a cost-free transition.
4. **The examples are limited to employment, platforms, and education.** They test the concept across domains without trying to reproduce the entire SAE systems analysis.
5. **The argument rests on temporally extended agency and an Anti-Hostage Principle.** This supplies a normative basis independent of SAE.
6. **The published foundation paper is cited in the third person.** This is necessary because the concept and some formulations already appear in a public Zenodo record. For masked review, follow the target journal's precise self-citation instructions rather than hiding the prior work from the editor.

## Issues to resolve before submission

- Decide whether “Fork Rights” should remain plural and capitalized. A conventional journal may prefer “the right to fork” in lower case.
- Verify every bibliographic entry against the target journal's required style and add page-specific citations during line editing.
- Decide whether the paper should retain all three applications or develop employment as the single extended case.
- Strengthen the section on jointly produced goods. A demanding reviewer may argue that the “portable stake” criterion is still underdetermined.
- Add a short discussion of economic switching-cost literature if submitting to a technology or interdisciplinary journal.
- Reduce or expand to the target word limit only after selecting a journal.
- Check the target journal's policy on preprints and prior online publication. The draft should not be added to the public website or Zenodo before that check.
- In the cover letter, disclose that the manuscript develops one concept previously sketched inside a much broader, publicly archived work, and explain that the new article supplies a new independent argument and literature engagement.
- Prepare a fully anonymized version. Remove author, affiliation, acknowledgments, self-identifying repository links, and document metadata.

## Suggested first venue strategy

1. **Journal of Applied Philosophy** — strongest fit if the three institutional applications remain central.
2. **Philosophy & Technology** — strongest fit if the digital-platform section becomes the main case.
3. **Journal of Social Philosophy** — plausible if the paper foregrounds institutional freedom and social justice.
4. **Journal of Value Inquiry** — a broader fallback after revision.

## Recommended next revision

The next pass should be argumentative rather than stylistic:

1. formulate one hard case in which exit is affordable and fair but a fork is unavailable;
2. show exactly what moral fact explains the remaining wrong;
3. specify how an “adequate portable stake” is determined without assuming controversial ownership claims; and
4. test the view against a case where refusing portability is justified.
