Interpretive Reconstruction of Dream of the Red Chamber · Paper 3 (专题三)

The Guiyou Version of Dream of the Red Chamber: The Public Record, Theories, and What This Project Adopted
癸酉本：公开记录、诸说与本项目的采择

Han Qin (秦汉) · Independent Researcher · 2026
DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.23087801 · Full PDF on Zenodo · CC BY 4.0
Abstract

The "Guiyou version" (Guiyou ben 癸酉本) is a set of twenty-eight chapters continuing Dream of the Red Chamber after chapter 80. It has circulated online since 2008 and has been printed in several forms since 2014; it is also called the "He Chu version" and Wu's Story of the Stone, Revised, with Trial Commentary. Published scholarship mostly regards it as a modern forgery; according to a 2018 report in Guangming Daily, the holder himself admitted to the reporter that two key commentary notes in it were his own fabrications. In reconstructing chapters 81–110 of Dream of the Red Chamber, this project read it and also borrowed from it. This paper gives a full account of that, without taking part in the debate over its authenticity.

The paper first registers the public record by provenance, and reports the forgery view, the authenticity view and the reading of it as mourning for the Ming. It then sets out the project's own reading: a later re-creation based on one version of Cao Xueqin's draft. This reading is only the basis on which the project wrote, not a finding of textual research. The paper gives the reasons for this reading together with its difficulties, including those that tell against the project.

The core of the paper is an item-by-item list of what was adopted: seventy-two points in the current draft of the reconstruction that relate to the Guiyou version. For each item it checks where the item stands in the Guiyou version, where it stands in the reconstruction, and whether the text of the first eighty chapters or the Zhiyan Zhai commentary gives it independent support. The result is that most of what the project really borrowed from the Guiyou version is writing material: plot outlines, details and scenes, together with a few chapter titles, epithets in the Roster of Feeling, and phrases. Only a minority of the items can stand on the first eighty chapters and the commentary alone.

Finally, the paper answers the question the forgery view raises: if the Guiyou version is a modern work, what in the project has to change? Items with independent support, and items with the project's own sources, stay as they are. Plot elements that come only from the Guiyou version are writing choices, and their source must be relabelled. Matching wording must be acknowledged or rewritten. The main thesis and method of the overview are not affected.

Keywords　Dream of the Red Chamber; Guiyou version; Wu shi Shitou ji; continuations; rules for using sources; interpretive reconstruction

1 Introduction

In reconstructing the last thirty chapters of Dream of the Red Chamber, the project relies on the text of the first eighty chapters and the Zhiyan Zhai commentary. Yet at many junctures in the writing another book lay open at the project's elbow: the "Guiyou version," which claims to come from an old manuscript and runs to 108 chapters. It writes out many of the things the first eighty chapters foreshadow, but mostly in a direction opposite to the project's: the realm falls into chaos, roving bandits break into the capital, characters die wretched deaths in batches, and the tragedy is scored to a lament for the fallen Ming. The project borrowed from it and, at the same time, went the other way. Section 4.6 of the overview set out the discipline for this, and left the representative arguments and a direct engagement with them to the present paper.

The public record weighs heavily on where this book came from. It appeared online; the original has never been made public; its versions differ from one another; and according to a 2018 report in Guangming Daily, the holder admitted to the reporter that two notes mentioning Wu Meicun were his own forgeries, and said that the text circulating online had been restored from memory and revised by several people. The mainstream of scholarly opinion regards it as a modern work. This project takes no part in the dispute over its authenticity. That is not evasion. The project chooses to hold, with paper 2, that Dream of the Red Chamber was built up in layers and that its drafts existed in several versions, and for the work of reconstruction a binary of genuine or forged does not help. This is the project's working position, not a verdict on the forgery view; the evidence the forgery view gives about the book's origins is set out in full in §3.4. More importantly, the way the project uses the Guiyou version never presupposed, in the first place, either that it is genuine or that it is forged. What this paper does is lay that out so that readers can check it.

So the question of this paper is not "Is the Guiyou version genuine or forged?" but three plainer questions: What did the project borrow from the Guiyou version? Does what was borrowed still stand without it? And if the forgery view is right, what in the project has to change?

To answer them, the materials must first be laid out. Section 2 says which texts the paper relies on; §3 registers the public record by provenance; §4 reports the theories; §5 states the project's own reading and its difficulties; §6 sets out the rules the project laid down for using the book; §7, the core of the paper, counts item by item what was borrowed; §8 says what was not taken; §9 answers the question the forgery view raises.

A few positions run through the whole paper and are settled here. The paper reports the forgery view, giving the representative arguments one by one with their sources; it neither cuts them nor rebuts them. The project's reading of the Guiyou version is only a reading: a working hypothesis, not load-bearing. A reader who does not accept it will find that the paper's list and conclusions stand just the same. This has the same structure as paper 2's "depends on one layer only." The project has considered several conjectures about who produced the Guiyou version; the paper records only that it considered them, rules on none of them, and rests nothing on any of them. "Cao Xueqin" is taken as a role in the text, meaning the one named in the chain of titles and in the commentary, who "read it over for ten years and revised it five times"; who this person was historically is not this paper's concern.

2 What the "Guiyou Version" Is

2.1 Names

The book has many names, and each has a history. When it first appeared online in 2008, an internet user, Zhao Zhendong, called it the "He Chu version" (He chu ben 何初本) for convenience. At the end of that year, on the strength of a chapter-end note reported by the holder, "Fair copy of the whole completed in the twelfth month of guiyou. Meicun" (癸酉腊月全部誊清　梅村), Jin Junjun, one of those who released it online, renamed it the "Guiyou version"; in later texts the note reads 癸酉腊月全书誊清, "fair copy of the whole book." Because the notes name Wu Meicun as the author, some have called it the "Wu version" or the "Wu ancestral version." In print it has had two titles, The Last 28 Chapters of the Guiyou Story of the Stone (癸酉本石头记后28回) and Wu's Story of the Stone, Revised, with Trial Commentary (吴氏石头记增删试评本); the latter is said to be the title on the cover, but in 2018 the holder told a reporter that it was "a made-up title." This paper keeps the project's own name for it, "Guiyou version," as a name only, without implying that it was made in a guiyou year.

The book has no standard text. The texts this paper has seen, from the blog posts of 2008 to the printed edition of 2017, take at least six forms; there is also a branch that claims to hold four hundred new notes on the first eighty chapters, and a set of pages written in imitation of a manuscript. 2.3 The working text and the third-draft text

This paper takes the working text as its base for quotation because it is the text the project used in writing. More than ten of the Guiyou sentences the project quoted at the time are found only in the working text and not in the third-draft text: for example the cook's "corpses of the starved fill the roads" (殣尸盈道) in chapter 83; "great bandits surge, seize the moment to topple the court; the sovereign is alarmed and his ministers in disarray" (大盗势飙，乘衅倾朝，主惊臣乱) in chapter 89; and Baochai's "the shields and spears are put away" (载戢干戈) in chapter 102. Whichever text the project quoted at the time, the paper must quote; otherwise readers could not check it.

The chapter titles of the twenty-eight later chapters differ between the two texts in only three places. In chapter 83 the working text has 暮年哀聊发白头吟　金萱悲情洒儿女泪 ("In old age, grieving, she sings the White Hair lament; / the mother, sorrowing, sheds tears for her children"), and the third-draft text 史太君临终念子孙　王夫人膏肓托儿女 ("The Lady Dowager, dying, thinks of her descendants; / Lady Wang, mortally ill, entrusts her children"); in chapter 84, 霭烟缘 ("a match of haze") against 金玉缘 ("the match of gold and jade"); in chapter 105, 藉词 against 借词, two written forms of the same word (jieci, "using words"). These three places, together with the chapter-head note on chapter 81 that speaks of the text "needing collation and revision" (须校雠酌改) and the opening poem of chapter 83, agree with a 108-chapter complete text found online. The working text is very probably that text, or comes from the same source.

The two texts differ considerably in length. Leaving out notes, editorial remarks and punctuation, the twenty-eight later chapters run to about 214,000 characters in the working text and about 189,000 in the third-draft text. Most of the third-draft text can be found in the longer working text; what the working text has in addition is concentrated in political commentary and chapter-end notes, in poems and prose mourning the Ming (most prominent is a set of pentasyllabic poems on late-Ming figures in the second half of the elegy in chapter 98), in passages on the supernatural and on bringing in bandits, and in places where, as its editor says, "the base copy and the polished copy both appear." The paper does not settle the direction of change from this: whether one text cut the other or one added to the other, it does not judge.

The two texts also differ in the narrative at several points; three of them bear on this paper. Lady Wang's death: in the working text she falls into the lake at the end of chapter 83 and dies in chapter 84; in the third-draft text she is already dead in chapter 83. The house to which Wang Ren sells Qiaojie is called the Hongxiang Court (红香院) in the working text and the Yanxiang Court (烟香院) in the third-draft text. The place name Guazhou: the working text uses both 瓜州 and 瓜洲, the third-draft text only 瓜洲. Wherever this paper mentions these points it follows the working text and notes the third-draft text.

The working text also carries remarks by an unnamed editor. After a note in chapter 1 attributed to Wu Meicun he writes, "whether this note is genuine is unknown" (此条批语真假未知). After the Ten Solitary Chants (Shi du yin 十独吟) in chapter 82 he writes, "in the base copy the eight poems were partly or wholly eaten by worms, and were afterwards written in by the copyist himself … genuine and false are hard to tell apart." In chapter 103 he writes, "this is the third place where the content of the base copy and of the polished copy unexpectedly both appear." These statements agree with what a 2015 post by the Sina blogger "Huzhu Tazhu" 护珠塔主 says: "two base copies were transcribed at the same time, and Wu Meicun's text and the polished draft developed in parallel." That post says its source is a "transcribed copy" collated by Wu Xuesong (first-hand, extracted with a tool; whether the blogger is Wu Xuesong is unproven). The working text most likely comes from this line; the paper has not established it. The editor's remarks are treated here only as editorial remarks, not as text belonging to the copy itself.

Two lines of commentary coexist in the working text's notes. Chapter 1 has both "This book was originally an old work of Mr Wu Meicun" (此书本系吴氏梅村旧作), as collected by the Jin Junjun–He Xuanhe branch, and "Xueqin formerly had a book, A Mirror for the Romantic, written by the late elder brother Xitang" (雪芹旧有风月宝鉴一书，乃西堂故兄所作), as claimed by the Wang Xiaofeng branch (§3.6). Two further notes are found only in the working text; §5.3 reports them.

Section 4.6 of the overview calls the Guiyou version "a … manuscript." According to the public record, only the holder claims to have read a manuscript, and the original has never been made public; what the public has seen is blog posts, printed editions, printouts, digitized texts and a set of imitation pages. The next version of the overview will say "text" (Appendix 6).

3 The Public Record

3.1 2008: appearance online

There are three accounts of when the Guiyou version first appeared. The blurb of the Jiuzhou edition and a 2016 blog post by Liu Junjun (that is, Jin Junjun), one of those who released it both say the end of August 2008; one account gives 31 August. A 2017 article by Zhang Xiaojun and Zhang Ruixue says that it "first appeared in September 2008 on Mr Zhao Zhendong's blog (the post has since been deleted)" (reprint). Others say it circulated on the Tianya forum or "on the major Honglou meng forums."

The earliest original post that can still be opened is "The chapter titles of the latter half of the 'He Chu version' of the Story of the Stone," posted on 13 October 2008 on the Sina blog "Guiyou ben Shitou ji," which lists the titles of chapters 81 to 108; the same blog has a post with the text of chapter 81. The publication times shown on the two pages are 18:04:55 and 18:15:48 (first-hand). A post of the next day, "Record of an exchange with Miss He," preserves a QQ group chat in which Zhao Zhendong, He Lili and Junjun all took part (first-hand). On 17 October the blog posted "He Chu version: selected Zhiyan Zhai notes on chapter 81" and "The state and provenance of the He Chu Story of the Stone." On 5 December, on the strength of the chapter-end note reported by He Lili, the blogger renamed it the "Guiyou version," took guiyou to be 1753, and said that this copy was "a year earlier even than the Jiaxu manuscript." On 21 December the post with the text of chapter 108 went up, and the twenty-eight chapters had all been posted online.

These original posts survive today and are the earliest checkable form of the book. They show one thing: from its first appearance the book existed in more than one wording. In the post of chapter titles of 13 October, the second line of the chapter 81 title reads 承辱怀怨以身试墙 ("bearing disgrace and nursing resentment: throwing oneself against a wall"); the post with the text, whose displayed time is about eleven minutes later, has the title 承辱怀怨蓄恶结党 ("bearing disgrace and nursing resentment: harbouring malice and forming a gang"). Whether the page text was later edited, and when this difference arose, the paper has not checked; §3.5 returns to it.

3.2 Publication and naming

In 2014 Jin Junjun and He Xuanhe published The Last 28 Chapters of the Guiyou Story of the Stone with Jiuzhou chubanshe; its blurb says that "a few modern expressions in the text have been replaced." In 2015 He Lili and Wang Xiaofeng published Wu's Story of the Stone, Revised, with Trial Commentary with Xianzhuang shuju; according to Guangming Daily, its cover reads "by Cao Xueqin," while the same article records that the original text of the transcribed copy that Wu Xuesong had printed and bound reads "original author: Wu Meicun." In the same year Wu Xuesong wrote a preface for the "second edition" of the Jin–He branch, saying he had obtained "photocopied material of the transcribed copy." In the 2017 "third draft," the first preface, by Xiao Wenlin, says that "guiyou means 1693, and 'Meicun' means Wu Meicun"; the foreword, by Jin Junjun and He Xuanhe, admits that the book is "an edited text made from image material of the transcribed copy, not the original text of the true copy, and its authenticity awaits expert appraisal" (reprint).

Over ten years the claims about title and author changed several times. In December 2008 the official blog dated guiyou to 1753 by Cao Xueqin's lifetime (posts of 5 and 15 December), while a post of 17 October that year records He Lili's words: "She says she has never claimed that this latter half was certainly written by Cao Xueqin." By 2015 to 2017 the claim that Wu Meicun was the author had become the position of the Jin–He branch that released it, and guiyou was moved to 1693; the cover of the Xianzhuang edition, meanwhile, read "by Cao Xueqin." And according to Guangming Daily, He Lili maintained that the title on the cover of the manuscript was the three characters 红楼梦, Honglou meng. For one and the same book, the title, the author and the guiyou year each have more than one account.

3.3 The holder and the original

The holder goes by the online name "He Lili." Accounts of this person disagree. A post on the official blog of 17 October 2008, "The state and provenance of the He Chu Story of the Stone," uses "she" and says "He Lili is 23 this year" (first-hand); Liu Junjun in 2016 calls the holder "Miss He Lili," from Fuyang in Anhui; Guangming Daily in 2018 says "a pseudonym; male; junior-middle-school education"; the 2017 foreword says "a collector surnamed Zhao in Fuyang, Anhui." The account of how the book was obtained also varies. The 2008 post says only that "her grandmother was the wife of a Nationalist army doctor" and that there were "twelve volumes, nine chapters each"; Liu Junjun in 2016 says that the grandfather was an army doctor on the Shanxi front, and that a wounded soldier gave the book to the grandmother, who was a nurse; Guangming Daily records the holder's side as saying that the grandparents came by it by chance, twelve volumes of nine chapters each; a 2019 report on The Paper's account platform (Pengpai hao) records that the director of the county cultural centre wanted to borrow it, so it was copied into notebooks and lent out. The copyist is said to have been a younger female cousin, or an elder sister, or a sister and brother.

The original has never been made public. There are three accounts of what became of it: it was sold as waste paper by the holder's mother; it may have been stolen by an outsider; it was sent to relatives in Taiwan for safekeeping. The releasing side says it obtained a photograph of the cover and, in 2015, "a photocopy of the whole of the transcribed copy." The 2019 report on Pengpai hao carried a picture captioned "the only image of the base copy's cover the releaser has published to date"; neither the object nor a complete photocopy has been verified.

On 25 February 2009 a post titled "Miss He Lili's apology to fans of the Red Chamber" appeared on the Baidu Tieba forum, containing the words "by some strange compulsion I fabricated the last 28 chapters of the He Chu version and deceived everyone" (as recorded in the article by Zhang Xiaojun and Zhang Ruixue; this paper has checked the sentence against the reprint; the original forum post has not been seen; second-hand). Guangming Daily later recorded that such a text did exist online, and that He Lili denied its content. Whether the apology came from He Lili, this paper cannot determine.

3.4 The 2018 investigation

On 27 February 2018, page 7 of Guangming Daily carried an investigative report by its reporter Liu Jian, "The collapse of Wu's Story of the Stone." This is the most important public record of the Guiyou version's origins. The paper uses the page of the newspaper's digital edition, and each sentence quoted from it was checked against that page at the time of writing (first-hand).

The report turns on two commentary notes: one in chapter 1, beginning "This book was originally an old work of Mr Wu Meicun"; and one at the end of chapter 108, beginning "Here the book ends. Fair copy of the whole book completed in the twelfth month of guiyou. Meicun's long-held wish is fulfilled" (本书至此告终，癸酉腊月全书誊清。梅村夙愿得偿). The claim that Wu Meicun was the author rests mainly on these two notes. The report says: "But when the reporter sought confirmation, He Lili said several times that both notes were his forgeries, and that he deeply regretted it." He said that "'Wu's Story of the Stone, Revised, with Trial Commentary' is a made-up title; at the time Wu Xuesong, Liu Junjun and others said that 'Wu Meicun wrote Dream of the Red Chamber' was fairly plausible, so he himself forged those two notes connected with Wu Meicun." The report also summarizes a 2014 text by He Lili, saying that "the content of the last 28 chapters circulating online is not the original text of the 'transcribed copy' but a plot he restored from memory, which had been revised many times by himself and others." That Wang Xiaofeng and He Lili still used that title for the 2017 reissue was "for reasons to do with the book number" (the ISBN).

The report also records voices on the other side. Wu Xuesong insisted that He Lili's words were not to be trusted. Du Chungeng of the Chinese Society of Honglou meng told the reporter that the twenty-eight chapters circulating online "ought to have a base copy in existence; if it is entirely an invention, then the forger is of some ability"; at the same time he said that in the manuscript He Lili recalled, "part of the plot of the first 80 chapters comes from a text created by Wu Keqi in the late Qing." These two points of Du Chungeng's are so far found only in this report; the paper has found no earlier article that makes them.

3.5 Changes between versions

The text of the Guiyou version was unstable from the start. The clearest example is chapter 81. The post of chapter titles of 13 October 2008 gives 避祸念旧凭带为媒　承辱怀怨以身试墙 ("Avoiding disaster and mindful of old ties, a sash serves as go-between; / bearing disgrace and nursing resentment: throwing oneself against a wall"); the post with the text of the same day has as its second line 承辱怀怨蓄恶结党, and in the text Siqi is beaten and dismissed, and does not die. But the extract of notes four days later contains a sentence, "seeing Siqi dash herself against the wall and die, … was startled too," followed by a note: "Xueqin has made a great mistake! Siqi has already left in an earlier chapter. Where would this passage in the Jia household come from? Most improper; cut it out entirely and redraft." That is to say, the draft with "dash herself against the wall and die" survives only as a fragment in the extract of notes, accompanied by a note demanding its deletion (all first-hand). In the later working text and third-draft text, the title reads 惜昵近公子做良媒　讳笞罚丫鬟结恶党 ("Cherishing intimacy, the young master acts as a good go-between; / shunning punishment, the maid forms an evil gang"): after her dismissal Siqi forms a gang with Pan You'an, and the note "Xueqin has made a great mistake" is gone.

There are many changes of the same kind. The first line of the chapter 82 title was 贾宝玉佯捧万章经 ("Jia Baoyu makes a show of poring over the 'Wan Zhang' scripture") in 2008; both later texts have 王熙凤病求千翼方 ("Wang Xifeng, ill, seeks the Qianyi prescription"), and Baoyu's reading of the "Wan Zhang" chapter of the Mencius survives only in the text of the chapter. For chapter 84, "a match of mist and haze" (雾烟缘, 2008) appears in the working text as "a match of haze" (霭烟缘), with an editorial remark that "two characters in the base copy were eaten by worms" and were supplied by the copyist; the third-draft text has "the match of gold and jade" (金玉缘), with its own remark: "two characters in the base copy were eaten by worms; the two characters 'gold and jade' were supplied later during collation." Both texts say worms ate the characters, and they supply different characters. In chapter 92, according to the 2019 report on Pengpai hao, the 2008 online version had a magistrate who "has already kicked the bucket" (已经死翘翘了, a modern colloquialism), where the third-draft text has "is already dead" (已经死了). The chapter-end note of chapter 108 was reported in 2008 as 癸酉腊月全部誊清　梅村; in the post with the text of 21 December 2008 the name "Meicun" does not appear; the two texts read "Here this book comes for now to a pause … Meicun's long-held wish is fulfilled" (是书至此暂告一段落……梅村夙愿得偿), and the working text adds "this note not collated"; Guangming Daily quotes it as "Here the book ends" (本书至此告终). Which version each of these three wordings belongs to, the paper has not been able to assign. As for the Ten Solitary Chants, the editorial remark in chapter 82 of the third-draft text says that only two, by Zhu Shuzhen and Li Qingzhao, survive complete, while the working text contains twelve, noted by its editor as written in by the copyist; both texts say in chapter 104 that the cycle "takes ten women, Zhu Shuzhen, Liu Rushi, Bian Yujing, Li Xiangjun and others, as its subjects."

The forgery view and the authenticity view read these changes in opposite ways: one side sees the traces of a fabricator revising step by step; the other sees the traces of deletions and changes in transcription and of characters supplied in collation. The paper registers only the changes themselves.

3.6 Two lines of commentary, and "the late elder brother Xitang"

On 28 August 2017 the Sina blog "Honglou meng duan sanbai nian" 红楼梦断三百年 published a commentary note said to come from chapter 1 of a "new transcription": "Xueqin formerly had a book, A Mirror for the Romantic, written by the late elder brother Xitang (西堂故兄); it covered only the affairs of Jia Rui, Qin Keqing and Qin Zhong, and was somewhat thin" (first-hand). In another post the blogger connects this "new transcription" with Wang Xiaofeng; whether the blogger is Wang Xiaofeng, the paper has not established. In the working text this note is longer, going on: "Xueqin vowed to write a great work to hand down to later ages … Xueqin would not put his name to it, and answered: why not borrow the names Meicun, Zuicha, Xicun, Tuo'an and Shilin to string onto it? … Xueqin's polishing stopped at chapter eighty."

This note and the Jin–He branch's "an old work of Mr Wu Meicun" occupy the same position: one says the original was by Xitang, the other by Wu Meicun. Its sentence pattern is the same as that of the Jiaxu upper-margin note "Xueqin formerly had a book, A Mirror for the Romantic, prefaced by his younger brother Tangcun" (雪芹旧有《风月宝鉴》之书，乃其弟棠村序也; 1.61); and its last words, "somewhat thin" (稍嫌枯索), also appear in that "old work of Mr Wu Meicun" note, one of the two the holder admitted forging. The paper simply sets these textual facts side by side and draws no inference. The paper has found no scholarship that discusses this note specifically. With this, the lead left by paper 2 has now been largely accounted for; what remains open is listed in Appendix 5.

4 Theories

4.1 The forgery view

That the Guiyou version is a modern work is the mainstream view in published scholarship. The representative writings this paper has seen are: Liu Jian's 2018 investigation in Guangming Daily, which quotes Du Chungeng, Duan Jiangli, Ren Xiaohui and Zhang Hui; Yin Jing's 2019 report on Pengpai hao, which quotes the president of the Jiangsu Honglou meng Society, a professor in the School of Liberal Arts of Nanjing University, given in the report as "Miao Weiming" (whether this is Miao Huaiming remains to be confirmed); Zhang Xiaojun and Zhang Ruixue, "New means of forging ancient books in the internet environment: three online forgeries concerning Dream of the Red Chamber" (reprint, 2017; original place of publication to be traced); Zhao Jianzhong, "Exposing the Guiyou Story of the Stone as a forgery" (search results show a title record in the VIP database, but the page could not be opened, and the full text has not been seen); Kong Degang's 2023 commentary on The Paper; Yang Yifei 杨依菲's 2024 article in Guandong xuekan; an English article in Sixth Tone, July 2025, under the byline Yang Yifei; and a December 2025 report in Beijing News on the annual conference of the Beijing Cao Xueqin Society, quoting Hu Deping (reporter He Anan).

The arguments in these writings fall into five groups.

The first concerns provenance. According to Guangming Daily, even the holder's side said that "only He Lili has ever read this set of books"; Kong Degang says that "He Lili has never been able to produce the original manuscript for scholars and enthusiasts to examine"; Hu Deping says that "this version has not produced its base copy." The holder's admission that two notes were forged (§3.4) also belongs to this group.

The second concerns the claim of authorship. Guangming Daily records that Du Chungeng, Duan Jiangli, Ren Xiaohui and others call the claim that Wu Meicun wrote Dream of the Red Chamber "groundless talk, not worth refuting." Its opponents cite an argument of Zhao Jianzhong's (second-hand): Daiyu's continuation of a poem in chapter 21 contains the line 作践南华庄子因, alluding to the Zhuangzi yin 庄子因, a commentary on the Zhuangzi completed in the twenty-seventh year of Kangxi (1688), whereas Wu Meicun died in the eleventh year of Kangxi (1672). Du Chungeng further says that part of the first eighty chapters of that manuscript comes from Wu Keqi's late-Qing text.

The third concerns the text itself. The 2008 online version of chapter 92 has the phrase "has already kicked the bucket," and the blurb of the Jiuzhou edition itself says that "a few modern expressions" were replaced. The "Miao Weiming" quoted in the report says: "First, as to plot, it is random fabrication, with nothing of Master Cao's direction. Second, its expression is very clumsy. Third, it has no talent at all." Kong Degang says it has "crude writing, poems that break the rhyme scheme, far below the level of the first eighty chapters." Zhang Hui joked on a social-media account that the episodes in which the Grand View Garden is sacked "were not written by Wu Meicun; they must have been written by Shi Nai'an," the author of The Water Margin (second-hand). Others point to contradictions with the settings of the first eighty chapters: its Roster of Feeling lists men such as the "twelve young masters," which disagrees with chapter 5, where the departments of the Land of Illusion "all hold the registers of the past and future of all the women under heaven" (the pen name 詹姆斯旁, "James Pang," 2022, reprint).

The fourth concerns its agreement with the results of tanyi. The number 108 agrees with the view of Zhou Ruchang and others (zh.wikipedia, second-hand); much of what the commentary on the first eighty chapters foreshadows is "fulfilled" in the twenty-eight later chapters. Supporters cite this as evidence of authenticity (§4.3); opponents cite it as evidence that the text was written to the notes. Of dedicated pieces by the opponents, this paper has seen only the title of a Zhihu post, "Three ironclad proofs that the fake Guiyou version (the fake Wu's Story of the Stone) is plagiarized and forged," and has not been able to read its text.

The fifth concerns the inconsistency of the versions and the many changes made to them (§3.5). He Lili himself said in 2014 that the text circulating online had "been revised many times by himself and others."

The overall judgement of scholars is stated most directly in the Sixth Tone article: "experts unanimously agree that the Guiyou version is a 21st-century forgery." Yang Yifei says that it "has been shown false by scholars, yet the more it is 'debunked,' the hotter it gets." Zhang Xiaojun and Zhang Ruixue say: "the so-called 'He Chu version' is a typical forgery" (reprint).

One point must be stated. Whether Honglou meng xuekan (Journal of Studies of Dream of the Red Chamber) or Cao Xueqin yanjiu (Cao Xueqin Studies) has published any article on the Guiyou version, neither this paper nor the supplementary search it commissioned was able to obtain auditable search pages from CNKI, Wanfang or VIP. "Not found" does not mean "does not exist."

4.2 More lenient views

Besides the negative opinions there are more lenient ones, but they are all cautious. Du Chungeng says that the twenty-eight chapters circulating online "ought to have a base copy in existence," yet in the same report he rejects the Wu Meicun claim and says that part of the first eighty chapters of the manuscript comes from Wu Keqi's text. In the report this "base copy" is not said to be Cao Xueqin's original draft, nor an eighteenth-century draft. Zhao Zhendong, one of those who first posted it, held, according to Liu Junjun's 2016 blog post, that "this text is a fairly good continuation by someone else, of considerable value for tanyi, but it is not the true text" (second-hand). Even the releasing side's own 2017 foreword says that "its authenticity awaits expert appraisal." The paper has not found any professional scholar who has publicly maintained that it preserves elements of Cao Xueqin's drafts.

4.3 The authenticity view, the Wu Meicun view, and the mourning-the-Ming reading

On the evidence seen, those who maintain that the Guiyou version is genuine, or the original work of Wu Meicun, do not publish as members of academic institutions. Their claims are only reported here.

When the official blog dated the guiyou year by Cao Xueqin's lifetime in December 2008, it already proposed another reading: a post of 15 December says that "the last 28 chapters contain much that is anti-Qing and mourns the Ming, anti-Manchu and pro-Han," and a post of 10 March 2009 says that the names Jia Rong and Jia Qiang "are homophones of Rong Qiang" (first-hand). Wu Xuesong's 2015 preface says: "from the notes in the book it can be known that A Mirror for the Romantic is a work of Wu Meicun," and that the twenty-eight later chapters "are, beyond doubt, lost chapters of the Story of the Stone" (reprint). Xiao Wenlin's 2017 preface reads the wars of the twenty-eight later chapters as "a miniature of the struggle between the late-Ming peasant armies and the Qing armies for the realm of the Great Ming" (reprint). A 2016 blog post by He Xuanhe says that, comparing the notes on the first eighty chapters, he "sorted out 59 notes that secretly foreshadow the content of the lost draft … of which 48 are confirmed" (reprint; posted on the blog "Shensuanzi Qin Yigua," signed "author: He Xuanhe; revised and edited by Qin Zhaoyan"). The 2017 foreword by Jin Junjun and He Xuanhe likewise says that the scenes foreshadowed by the notes on the first eighty chapters are "confirmed one by one" in the twenty-eight later chapters, and that "this is strong evidence that this text is the true, complete draft of Dream of the Red Chamber"; the same foreword says that its authenticity awaits appraisal.

The mourning-the-Ming reading has a continuous textual basis in the twenty-eight later chapters: the overseas "Yuhu Island" 玉户岛 raids the border, and the Rong Qiang covet the realm; Yuanchun is put to death by slicing on a charge of "colluding with the Rong Qiang"; roving bandits break into the capital, and the Rong Qiang seize power and ascend the throne; in chapter 100 the sons of disgraced officials cry together, "We swear to wipe out the barbarians and take back our rivers and mountains" (誓灭胡虏，还我河山); in chapter 102 Chen Yejun gives Miaoyu three poems, titled in the working text Song of Jiangdong (江东行), with the line "the leftover subjects, in tears of blood, long for the state's restoration." The project takes none of this (§6, §8).

4.4 Popular Redology

The Guiyou version became really popular on video platforms. Kong Degang regards the explanatory videos of "Muyu Shuixin" and others from 2019 on as the point at which it entered public view, and criticizes the way this phenomenon let "feelings for family and nation" and the "mourning-the-Ming theory" crowd out attention to feeling and human nature. Yang Yifei, taking Bilibili as an example, discusses the Guiyou version in connection with the revival of the suoyin ("hidden-meaning") school and the gap between the academy and the public. This section is written because most of this project's readers are likely to have met the Guiyou version here.

5 The Project's Reading

5.1 One version of the draft

The project reads the Guiyou version as "a later re-creation based on one version of Cao Xueqin's draft."

The words "one version" follow from the position of paper 2. Dream of the Red Chamber is layered, and its drafts existed in several versions; each later event the commentary refers to belongs to some version, and Cao Xueqin very probably had more than one design for the second half of the book. If the Guiyou version does descend from a draft, it descends from only one of those versions, not necessarily the one the commentators saw. So where it disagrees with the commentary, there is no need to force agreement; and its running against the direction of the project does not prevent the project from borrowing from it.

What the project relies on in borrowing from it is one judgement within this reading: whatever in the Guiyou version agrees with the temperaments of the characters as established in the first eighty chapters may, the project holds, preserve something of Cao Xueqin's original intention, and is worth borrowing; whatever does not agree with them is not taken (rule 7 in §6). This is the author's reading, not a finding of textual scholarship. If the Guiyou version is a modern work, the judgement falls back to a literary standard: writing that agrees with the temperaments of the characters in the first eighty chapters is worth borrowing, whoever wrote it (§9).

This reading is the reading and the basis for writing that the project used in handling the Guiyou version. It is a working hypothesis and is not load-bearing (overview §1.4). It does not rebut the forgery view, nor does it need the forgery view to give way. The weight of the public record has been set down as it is (§3), and readers may decline this reading; the list in §7 and the conclusions in §9 do not depend on it. In the course of writing, the project also considered several conjectures about who produced the Guiyou version and when. They are not consistent with one another, and none has public evidence behind it; the paper rules on none of them and rests nothing on any of them.

5.2 A note on chapter 42: the reason, and its difficulties

The author has given one reason for holding this reading. Chapter 42 has a note (42.6) that begins, "'If only these words come true, that would be good': how could the commentator's heart not ache?" and goes on: "Only on the day of the meeting at the Prison Temple does one know that 'meeting disaster she finds good fortune, meeting ill she turns it to good' was a line laid a thousand li ahead. Alas, alas! The writing that follows is too painful to read through. Winter of xinmao." This note links Qiaojie's "meeting disaster she finds good fortune" with a "meeting at the Prison Temple" (狱庙相逢). In the Guiyou version, Qiaojie's misfortune is followed directly by the episode in which Pinger and Qiaojie are taken under guard to the Yue Temple (嶽神庙) and meet Baoyu there; the author holds that this fulfils the note.

This reason is recorded here as given; so are its difficulties, of which there are four.

First, this fulfilment can be explained on either view. On the reading of it as a re-creation from a draft, it is a trace the draft left behind; on the forgery view, it was written to the note: the continuator had read the commentary and simply followed it. The modern editor of the third-draft text even attached this note as a footnote directly to the sentence "quickly take them to the Yue Temple and hold them there" in chapter 94. So this fulfilment cannot be used to decide between the two views.

Second, the provenance of the note itself is disputed, and three accounts have been seen. The project base text labels it 该批 ("this note"); paper 1, item T1-18, records that "a reviewer recalls that it is usually given as a Jing upper-margin note; if so, it is reference-weight; to be checked against facsimiles"; the footnote in chapter 94 of the third-draft text calls it "a Mengfu side note on chapter 42," and the wording it gives is identical, character for character, with the project base text. If the note belongs to the Jing manuscript, the authenticity of the Jing manuscript is itself disputed: Gao Shuwei's Exposing the Jing Manuscript of Dream of the Red Chamber as a Forgery (Zhonghua shuju, 2024) argues that it is forged, as paper 2 has reported.

Third come the limits of the project's own choices. Paper 1's audit records this note as "half fulfilled": the layer that links the "meeting at the Prison Temple" with Qiaojie is not taken by the reconstruction, and is recorded as N5. In the reconstruction, those who meet at the Prison Temple are Baoyu, Qianxue and Xiaohong, and Qiaojie's "meeting disaster she finds good fortune" falls in Rec. chs. 99 and 100. In other words, the reason the project gives for its reading is precisely the layer the project itself did not follow in the reconstruction. To be prompted by a note and then, by an openly stated criterion, not to write it as the note says, is not self-contradiction; if the Guiyou version descends from one version, that version and this one choice of reconstruction can differ. The real limit lies elsewhere: this fulfilment cannot tell an inherited old draft from a modern continuation written to the note (the first difficulty). Readers should know this.

The fourth is a single character. The Guiyou version writes 嶽神庙, the Yue Temple (the temple of the god of the Sacred Peak), throughout, fourteen times in the twenty-eight later chapters of the working text and three more times in the notes on the first eighty chapters; the commentary writes 狱神庙, the Prison Temple (20.20, 26.24 and others). In 1982 Wu Shih-ch'ang criticized Chao Kang for "writing an article about the Prison Temple while picturing in his mind the Yue Temple" (reprint). This shows that something close to the "Yue Temple" had been imagined in tanyi writing long before. The paper only records the fact, and does not use it to decide which came first.

5.3 Two self-accounts in the working text

The working text has two notes that are found only in it. They share one voice, and they bear on the account the book gives of itself, so the paper reports them.

The first is an unsigned passage before chapter 81, set in square brackets rather than in the 【】 used for the other notes; in form it resembles a note, and below it is called "the long note." Its author and the date at which it was added have not been checked; it speaks in the first person and mentions Xueqin and relatives and friends, but the paper does not, on that account, treat it as the testimony of an old commentator. The third-draft text does not have it, nor does the 2008 post with the text of chapter 81 (checked first-hand). It begins: "The last chapter said that 'in the end one never knew the whole story'; does the whole book then end at chapter eighty? Xueqin's original design had twenty-eight more chapters, roughly outlined as follows." There follows a summary of later events, including Tanchun married far away to Yuhu Island, Baoyu losing the jade, Zhen Baoyu becoming a monk and coming to return the jade, and Baochai marrying Yucun. It then says: "Xueqin's original intention was that the Jia house was ruined and its people scattered because Jia Qin and others brought in bandits and wasted the gifts of heaven, not that the state fell and families were thrown into chaos; but on reflection this was as tasteless as chewing wax, the story flat, and we did not adopt it." It further says that the twenty-eight later chapters were, "relatives and friends earnestly urging, filled out into a finished draft," "setting aside Xueqin's original plan, to tell directly of the national calamities and bandit disasters of Han and Tang." It ends: "A Mr Liu has remarkable ideas and a fine design; he wishes, with colleagues, to continue the Red Chamber again, removing every offending word and attending only to the affairs of Baoyu, Daiyu and Baochai; I have consented." The second is the second chapter-end note of chapter 108: "I hope that the forty chapters being continued by relatives and friends will soon be completed and circulate in the capital."

Taken together, the two make up a self-contained account: the twenty-eight later chapters were completed by "relatives and friends," setting aside Xueqin's original plan; and there is a further continuation by a "Mr Liu" that writes only of Baoyu, Daiyu and Baochai, apparently meaning the Cheng–Gao edition. In its own notes, then, the Guiyou version calls its twenty-eight later chapters a continuation, which on its face is close to the project's reading. But these two notes are also the narrowest in provenance in the whole system, found only in the working text. The paper reports them, and does not use them as evidence for the project's reading.

6 Rules of Use

The project read the Guiyou version mainly as a negative mirror, showing the way not to go. The Guiyou version scores its tragedy to the chaos of the realm, writing the second half as bandits breaking into the capital and most of the characters dying wretched deaths; the project lets the tragedy happen quietly in a world going on as usual, because if the times are in chaos, guilt is diluted by the chaos. As the project sees it, whenever the Guiyou version turns to politics, the characters' voices often become mouthpieces for political commentary; the project takes only the characters' own voices. Each step it takes forces the project to say clearly why it does not take that step itself.

Within this general direction the project laid down, over time, ten rules, listed below. These are the rules that can continue to be applied now; the list does not claim that every step in the past kept to them. The last column of each row says whether the rule still holds once any assumption about the author is removed from it. Its anchor in the text is the goddess Jinghuan's distinction in chapter 5 between "lust of the mind" (yiyin 意淫) and "the lust of the skin" (overview §1.2); for Qin Keqing, the Roster of Feeling in the reconstruction writes, "her sin was not beauty but politics" (然其孽非色，乃政; overview §3.5; paper 2). In the current draft, Miaoyu is set to menial labour but is not violated, and Qiaojie, in the Hongxiang Court, "is beaten every day and will not give in"; both are where this criterion lands (§7.5). Between the rules and the practice there is one gap, recorded here as it is: under rule 3, a few sentences in the text of the current draft are near-identical to the working text: not everything was rewritten (§7.4).

7 What Was Borrowed

7.1 How the count was made

This paper lists, item by item, the places in the current draft that relate to the Guiyou version, seventy-two in all, and does three things for each: it checks in the working text which chapter of the Guiyou version the item is in, and which chapter of the current draft; it searches the text of the first eighty chapters (including the verdicts and songs) and the commentary for independent support; and it states how the item should be handled if the Guiyou version is a modern work. The full table is in Appendix 3.

"Independent support" is judged strictly. It must be a foreshadowing of a later event, or a direct cause from which the event follows, and it must agree with the item in at least two of persons, events and things. Evidence that provides only the origin of a character or an object, or only a similar temperament or atmosphere, counts only as "tangential," not as support. An item with several layers is classed by its main line. Where the main line comes only from the Guiyou version, the item is class G, even if one of its layers has support elsewhere: for example #7, the reality of servants' skimming, in which the layer of the rotted ginseng can follow from Lady Wang's search for ginseng in chapter 77. Where the main line has support and one attached layer is borrowed from the Guiyou version, the item is class I, marked with an asterisk, and the borrowed layer is noted (#1, #2, #23). The classes are only summary labels and do not replace the columns of Appendix 3: an item with independent support may in fact have been borrowed, and matching wording does not by itself show the direction of transmission.

In the Chinese version the classes are named 甲, 乙, 丙, 同 and 注. The English uses the letters I, G, W, S and N, which are meant to be remembered by what they stand for, and are not to be confused with the overview's Grades A–D or with paper 1's Tiers 1–3.

7.2 Items with independent support

In this class the Guiyou version acts as a prompt. It had already written out events that the first eighty chapters and the commentary foreshadow; the project used it to locate and shape them, while the support was in the first eighty chapters and the commentary all along.

Xiren marries Jiang Yuhan, leaving Sheyue behind as she goes (Rec. ch. 81): there is the Gengchen note 20.45, "hence Xiren's words after she is married, 'whatever happens, keep Sheyue,'" and 28.1, on the actor Qiguan, "in a later chapter he and Xiren serve Brother Jade and Sister Bao, to stay with them to the end." That the match is made with the crimson sweat-sash as its token also rests on 28.1; but the layer in which "Baoyu himself acts as go-between" has no support, and is borrowed from the 2008 post of chapter titles, "a sash serves as go-between" (凭带为媒). Tanchun takes leave of the ancestral hall at Qingming and is married far away (Rec. ch. 85): there is her verdict, "At Qingming, in tears, she is seen off, gazing from the riverbank; / a thousand li on the east wind, a dream far away," and 22.103, "this is an omen of Tanchun's marrying far away"; the rite of leave-taking at the ancestral hall and the layer of her adoption as a daughter are borrowed from the Guiyou version. Pinger is raised to be the principal wife (Rec. ch. 90): in chapter 44 Bao Er's wife tells Jia Lian, "when she dies, you can just raise Pinger to be your proper wife." Grannie Liu rescues Qiaojie (Rec. ch. 100): there is the verdict, "By chance, having once helped a woman named Liu, / by good fortune she meets one who repays her." Xichun's painting of the Grand View Garden goes with Grannie Liu to the country (Rec. ch. 101): in chapter 40 Grannie Liu says, "If only someone would paint this garden for me, so I could take it home." Baochai dies in the snow (Rec. ch. 108): her verdict has "the golden hairpin buried in the snow"; in the same item, that Yucun does not die is the project's own ruling, made by turning round the Guiyou version's "Yucun dies of illness"; it has no support in the commentary, and it is not borrowed either.

If the Guiyou version is a modern work, the main line of this class is unchanged; the borrowed layers of #1, #2 and #23 have their source relabelled, as for class G.

7.3 Items that come only from the Guiyou version

Class G has thirty-three items, one of which (#26) is still to be checked. They are writing choices the project borrowed from the Guiyou version, and no direct support for them can be found in the first eighty chapters or the commentary. Examples from the household affairs of the Jia mansion: the reality of servants' skimming (Rec. ch. 82); the proposed match for Miaoyu, with Jia Huan in Baoyu's place, and the old friendship between the Lady Dowager and Miaoyu's grandmother (Rec. ch. 85; for the friendship the author confirms the source as chapter 82 of the working text, "the old lady and her grandmother were close friends too"); Aunt Zhao's complaint as the trigger for the audit of the accounts (Rec. ch. 86). From the Qiaojie line: her weeping a second time in the Grand View Garden and choosing for herself to leave (Rec. ch. 101); Xifeng appearing to Qiaojie in a dream and saying "I came too late" (Rec. ch. 101). From the Miaoyu line: Chen Yejun's suit, which she rejects (Rec. ch. 99); her dream in which she debates "what is purity" with Daiyu (Rec. ch. 105). Around Baoyu: Jiang Yuhan answering his wife with the words of a play (Rec. ch. 106); Xiangyun mourning Daiyu (Rec. ch. 106). From the life of the streets: Yinjie's fierce temper and Bu Shiren selling his daughter (Rec. chs. 107 and 108); Second Girl (Er yatou; Rec. ch. 104); the Prince of Beijing returning a gift unopened (Rec. ch. 108). Details and names of things: Dian'er assigned to Xichun's rooms; the name Ningguta.

The commonest technique in this class is "inversion": taking the form of a scene in the Guiyou version and replacing its meaning. Xifeng's ghost returning to take revenge is inverted into her appearing in a dream to bless her daughter's marriage to Banr; Miaoyu's dream in which Baoyu urges marriage is inverted into her dream debating purity with Daiyu; Jiang Yuhan's "for a man to have three wives and four concubines is only right" is inverted into the words of a play about keeping faith with one person; Yucun bribing the Prince of Beijing to gain office is inverted into the Prince returning the gift unopened. The project calls this technique "the form taken, the meaning inverted" (形采义翻). In what is inverted, the meaning is the project's and the form is the Guiyou version's; so these items belong to class G, and their source must be stated.

There is also a case in which one scene in a group is borrowed while the meaning of the group as a whole is the project's own. Wandering on the road, Baoyu is awakened three times: Liu Xianglian saves him from robbers on the road (Rec. ch. 102), which is the Daoist teaching; the old monk at the Temple of Wisdom Understood makes him gather firewood and light the fire (Rec. ch. 102), which is the Buddhist; Second Girl gives him a bowl of taro and treats him as a person (Rec. ch. 104), which is the Confucian "benevolence is loving others" made real. This awakening through the three teachings is the project's own conception (author's statement). Only the scene in which Second Girl is met again and gives him taro comes from chapter 106 of the working text (#64); the stroke of Liu Xianglian's rescue follows chapter 66, where Liu Xianglian drives off robbers on the border of Ping'an Prefecture and saves Xue Pan; the Temple of Wisdom Understood comes from chapter 2.

Two items need separate mention. The personal name Chen Yejun does not appear in the list of mourners in chapter 14 of the project base text, which reads "Feng Ziying, Wei Ruolan and other young gentlemen of noble houses"; the working text has it, and so does the standard edition; this is to be checked against facsimiles. As between the two texts this project used, even the name in this item is found only in the working text. For the Miaoyu item (#56), the layers of Guazhou and of sinking into the mire have support in the commentary and in her verdict; that she is not violated comes from the project's criterion, while the reading "did not submit" is borrowed from the Guiyou line; see §7.5.

If the Guiyou version is a modern work, class G can be used just the same, since it consists of writing choices in the first place; only the source must be relabelled as "borrowed from a modern continuation."

7.5 One punctuation of the note on chapter 41

The Jing upper-margin note on chapter 41 recorded in the project base text (41.14) is garbled as transmitted: "……他日瓜州渡口劝惩不哀哉屈从红颜固能不枯骨各示□". There are three ways of punctuating it. Zhou Ruchang reads it as 他日瓜州渡口，红颜固［？］屈从枯骨，不能各示劝惩，［岂］不哀哉！ ("Another day at the Guazhou ferry, the rosy-cheeked one indeed [?] submits to withered bones, and they cannot each show admonition and warning; [is it] not lamentable!"); Dai Bufan reads it as 他日瓜州渡口屈从，各示劝惩，［岂］不哀哉 ("Another day at the Guazhou ferry she submits; each shows admonition and warning; [is it] not lamentable"). Both read "submits" (屈从; this paper relies on transcriptions found online; the original books and page numbers are still to be checked). The third reading comes from the Guiyou line: in the text of chapter 41 of the working text there is a note reading 妙玉偏辟处，此所谓过洁世同嫌也，他日瓜州渡口，客示劝惩，红颜固能不屈从枯骨，不哀哉？ ("Where Miaoyu is eccentric: this is what is meant by 'too pure, she is resented by all the world.' Another day, at the Guazhou ferry, a guest shows admonition and warning: the rosy-cheeked one was indeed able not to submit to withered bones. Is it not lamentable?").

The overview chose the third by its criterion, reading "did not submit," classed it as Grade C in the sense of overview §4.2 (selection by the criterion), and stated that this borrowing "touches only emendation, not plot." Two things must be separated here: that Miaoyu is not violated is the project's own judgement; the reading "did not submit" is borrowed. Both are laid out in full below.

The provenance of this reading is that it came into public view as the Guiyou version's own note on chapter 41; the earliest public citation this paper has seen is a blog post of 12 January 2016. It is not any scholar's collated reading, and it belongs to the same system of commentary as the two notes the holder admitted forging. The original note, moreover, comes from the Jing manuscript, whose authenticity is disputed. So two things are unsettled here: whether the original note is reliable, and where the punctuation comes from.

More striking still, the Guiyou version's own plot has her "submit." In chapter 102 of both the working text and the third-draft text, Miaoyu, after being abducted, is "receiving clients every day"; and the 2017 foreword lists "Miaoyu submits at the Guazhou ferry" among the scenes foreshadowed by the commentary and confirmed. However, the 2016 blog post listed in this paper explains the phrase differently: it takes "able not to submit" (能不屈从) to describe a possibility that could have been avoided but was not: the visitor admonished her, and she did not listen, which then leads on to what befalls Miaoyu later. So, in the source's own explanation, the literal "not submit" and the submission in the plot do not conflict. The project took the word order of this reading; it did not take its explanation, nor its plot.

The reason the project writes Miaoyu as not violated lies not in how this note is read but in the criterion: Cao Xueqin does not steer the Twelve Beauties of Jinling, Qin Keqing included, toward lust; sexual humiliation is not his way of writing (§6). The reading "did not submit" only makes the damaged note compatible with this judgement.

Since the punctuation is borrowed, it is recorded as borrowed. If this text is stripped of its standing as a witness to an early variant, the project will still state that the punctuation is taken from the Guiyou line, only no longer using it to prove the ending of Cao's draft. The plot in which Miaoyu is not violated is unaffected, because it came from the criterion in the first place; what is affected is only whether the "did not submit" layer of 41.14 counts as fulfilled. On the readings of Zhou Ruchang and Dai Bufan, the reconstruction does not satisfy this layer, as paper 1 states at item T1-24: "on other readings, the reconstruction does not satisfy this note." To say on a borrowed punctuation that this layer is fulfilled counts, in argument, only as selection by the criterion, not as direct evidence from the commentary; what the overview calls "lowered by one step" is the lowering of this layer.

8 What Was Not Taken

Far more was removed than adopted. Chapter 93 is not taken at all; from chapter 92 only one core is taken, Daiyu seeing fireflies; nothing is taken from chapters 97, 100 and 107 (chapter numbers of the Guiyou version). By reason, what was removed falls roughly into six classes (Appendix 4).

The largest is chaos in the realm: bandits sacking the Grand View Garden, the county yamen seized, characters dying in batches, about sixty items. Next is mourning the Ming and politics: the Rong Qiang, Yuhu Island, Yuanchun put to death by slicing and the charge of "colluding with the Rong Qiang," Chen Yejun's three poems, the set of poems on late-Ming figures in the second half of the elegy, "we swear to wipe out the barbarians," the new dynasty, about forty items. Then come voices or fates of characters that do not fit what the first eighty chapters established: Daiyu beating Xiaohong to death, Baochai murdering Zhui'er, Yuanyang cursing the Jia family, Baoyu writing a letter to win Miaoyu's heart, about thirty items. The supernatural: the jade of spiritual understanding fleeing by itself, Xifeng's ghost taking revenge, the Land of Illusion crossing names off and returning them to their places, about twenty items. Things that do not connect, with the first eighty chapters or within the Guiyou version itself, about ten items: for example, the actresses coming back to the garden to settle scores with Xiren does not connect with where chapter 77 of the first eighty chapters sends them, "Ruiguan and Ouguan both went with Yuanxin of the Ksitigarbha Convent" (蕊官藕官二人跟了地藏庵的圆心; the working text has 圆信), and a reason for their return would have to be supplied. Architecture is the smallest class: 108 chapters, a 108-person Roster, men on the Roster, "the Passionate Monk," about six items. Across these classes runs one more criterion: sexual humiliation is not Cao Xueqin's way of writing (§6). Miaoyu sinking into prostitution until old age, the maids raped and killed, Baochai's secret meetings with Yucun, Jiang Yuhan consorting with boy favourites and betraying Xiren: these fall in different classes, and all run against this criterion. The numbers are only approximate; about eighty further items are choices of plot and are not classed.

This list is much longer than the list of what was adopted, and that is its meaning: the project borrowed from the Guiyou version more as a negative example. It wrote the second half as the realm in chaos and most of the characters dying wretched deaths; the question the project sets out to answer is how people, in a world going on as usual, come to their own ends.

9 If the Guiyou Version Is a Modern Work

The overview originally assigned this paper "a direct engagement with the forgery view." Under the author's later ruling, the paper does not decide authenticity, so this engagement answers only the bearing of the forgery view on the way the project uses its materials and on the standing of its arguments: if the forgery view is right, what in the project has to change?

This section works on the assumption least favourable to the project: the Guiyou version is not treated as an independent witness to any of Cao's drafts, and everything the paper borrowed is treated as text written or edited by a modern person. A modern writer might also have had access to old material, and modern composition does not in itself mean there can be no old source; this section does not rely on that. On this assumption, four questions are answered.

First: can the literary choices stay? Yes. The fourteen items whose main line has independent support, and the seven with the project's own sources, never depended on the Guiyou version; the thirty-three items that come only from it were writing choices from the start. The judgement in §5.1, that "content that agrees with the temperaments of the characters in the first eighty chapters is worth borrowing," then falls back to a literary standard: writing that agrees with the characters' temperaments may be borrowed, whoever wrote it. The episode in which Miaoyu is not violated also stays; it comes from the criterion (§7.5).

Second: is the standing of corroboration withdrawn? Yes. Wherever, in the writing, a plot element found in both texts was taken as corroboration of a base draft, that standing is withdrawn. The punctuation of the note on chapter 41 no longer serves as a witness to an early variant.

Third: must the disclosure of sources and wording change? The source of class G items is relabelled "borrowed from a modern continuation"; the same applies to the borrowed layer of the class I items marked with an asterisk. Of the seventeen items of matching and near-matching wording, chapter titles and Roster epithets, being few characters, need only an acknowledgement of their source; for near-matching sentences of the text, rewriting is preferable. These disclosures are made now, without waiting for the forgery view to be proved (§7.4). #70 is a note outside the text written by the project itself; it names the Guiyou version outright, and must be rewritten or annotated.

Fourth: where did the overview actually use this corroboration? In one place only: the punctuation of the note on chapter 41 in §4.6, which the overview had already classed as Grade C (selection by the criterion). Withdrawing it affects only whether the "did not submit" layer of 41.14 counts as fulfilled; the plot of the reconstruction does not move. Everywhere else the overview mentions the Guiyou version, it treats it only as a source of candidate leads, to be checked against the first eighty chapters and the commentary. The ten rules of use stand; rules 4, 5, 6 and 7 remain usable once their assumptions about the author are restated (§6). The list of what was removed is unchanged. The overview's main thesis and method are unchanged too, because overview §1.4 had already listed the claim about the Guiyou version as not load-bearing.

So the conclusion is a single sentence: the way the project uses the Guiyou version does not depend on whether it is genuine or forged. This is the paper's answer to the forgery view. The paper does not conclude from this that the forgery view is wrong, nor that the project's reading is right.

One thing must be said in the other direction as well. If the Guiyou version really did descend from one version of Cao Xueqin's draft, the project would gain nothing extra from it, because only the first eighty chapters and the commentary were ever accepted as grounds of argument. As re-examined in this paper, the place of the Guiyou version in the project is confined to three roles: a prompter, a negative mirror, and a source of writing material.

10 Conclusion

The paper argues for one thing only: the project used the Guiyou version with discipline and with a register, and without presupposing either that it is genuine or that it is forged. Everything else is reported as found.

Several of the things reported tell against the project, and the paper has written all of them down. Most of what was really borrowed from the Guiyou version is writing material; only a minority can be held up independently by the first eighty chapters and the commentary. Chapter titles, Roster epithets and a few sentences match or nearly match the Guiyou version. The punctuation of the note on chapter 41 is borrowed. The reason the project gives for reading the Guiyou version as a re-creation from a draft is precisely the layer the project did not follow in its own reconstruction. None of this changes the main thesis of the overview, because the overview never put the Guiyou version in a load-bearing position from the start; but readers ought to know it.

Dream of the Red Chamber is layered, and its drafts existed in several versions. In the project's reading, the Guiyou version is a re-creation built on one version of a draft; if that reading is not accepted, it is already, in any case, one of the texts in the history of how Dream of the Red Chamber has been transmitted and received. In what year and by whose hand it was made, the paper does not decide. The project read it, borrowed from it, and more often went the opposite way. That is what this paper has set down.

Overview §4.6 calls it "the punctuation transmitted in the Guiyou line," and records that Zhou Ruchang and Dai Bufan both read "submitted." On the strict principle it is classed G; if only "Miaoyu sinks at Guazhou" were at issue, it could be classed I.
  • 67: in Rec. ch. 110 only the names of five registers are found, principal, secondary, tertiary, third secondary and fourth secondary (正、副、又副、三副、四副), agreeing with 18.19 (T1-06), "the principal, secondary, tertiary (再副), and third and fourth secondary (三四副) registers." Of the eighteen people given epithets and comments, all but Qing'er, seventeen, appear in the Roster in chapter 108 of the working text, and for sixteen of them the "qing X" epithet is identical with the working text: eleven in the Principal Register (情情、无情、情尊、情敏、情憨、情隐、情懦、情雄、情缘、情槁、孽情) and five in the registers below (Xiangling 情呆, Qingwen 情勇, Xiren 情箴, Baoqin 抱琴, Yuanchun's maid, 情忠, Cuimo 情豁); only Xichun's 情冷 was changed to 情慧. Miaoyu's comment 壁立万仞 and Xichun's 冷心冷面 are also identical with the working text; 冷心冷面 was already said by You Sanjie in chapter 66, belongs to the vocabulary of the first eighty chapters, and is not taken as evidence of matching. The commentary has only the two epithets qingqing and qing bu qing; so 67 is classed W.
  • 69: the pattern 治世之能，乱世之雄 ("capable in an age of order, a hero in an age of disorder") in the current draft has two possible sources: chapter 108 of the working text, 治世之能臣，贪酷之奸雄也 ("a capable minister in an age of order, a greedy and cruel treacherous hero"), and 16.14, which calls Xifeng "a treacherous hero of an age of disorder." 治世之能臣 comes from Xu Shao's judgement of Cao Cao (Pei Songzhi's commentary on the "Annals of Emperor Wu" in the Records of the Three Kingdoms, quoting Sun Sheng's Yitong zayu), so the matching pattern carries very little weight; the two characters 情雄 are identical with the working text, so it is classed W.
  • 55: the name Chen Yejun is not in the project base text. The list of mourners in chapter 14 of the base text reads "Feng Ziying, Wei Ruolan and other young gentlemen of noble houses," while chapter 14 of the working text reads "Chen Yejun, Wei Ruolan (卫若蘭) and other young gentlemen of noble houses." The standard edition also has the name Chen Yejun here; to be checked against facsimiles. As between the two texts this project used, the name is found only in the working text.
  • 21: the old friendship between the Lady Dowager and Miaoyu's grandmother written in Rec. ch. 85 corresponds to chapter 82 of the working text, "the old lady and her grandmother were close friends too"; the layer of Lu'an tea and Old Gentleman's Eyebrows comes from chapter 41. The author confirms that the friendship layer was taken from chapter 82 of the working text; classed G.
  • 12: the sequence in which the Zhen family is raided first and the Jia family later has separate support in chapters 74 and 75; this table classes the item W for the stroke of a cold funeral and the near-matching wording.
  • 26: the Song of Leaving the Net (辞网歌) is in Rec. ch. 85; whether it carries on the core of Tanchun's indignant words in the working text, against treating the realm as a family estate, cannot be checked; recorded as "to be checked."

摘要

“癸酉本”是 2008 年起在网上流传、2014 年起陆续排印的一种八十回后二十八回，又称“何初本”、《吴氏石头记增删试评本》。公开研究多视之为今人伪托；据《光明日报》2018 年的报道，持有者本人也对记者承认，其中两条关键批语是他伪造的。本项目在复原《红楼梦》第八十一至一一〇回时，读过它，也借过它。本文交代这件事的全部来龙去脉，而不参与真伪之争。

本文先照来路登记公开记录，照述伪托说、真本说与悼明之读，再说明本项目自己的读法：后人基于曹雪芹某一版底稿的再创作。这一读法只是本项目的写作依据，不是考证结论。本文把这一读法的缘由与它的难处一并写出，包括对本项目不利的一面。

本文的主体是一份逐项的采择清单：现行稿中与癸酉本有关的七十二项，逐项核它在癸酉本何处、在复原本何处、有无前八十回正文与脂批的独立依据。结果是，本项目从癸酉本真正借来的，大半是写作上的东西：情节的骨架、细节、场面，以及少数回目、情榜名号与字句。能单凭前八十回与脂批站住的，只是少数。

最后本文回答伪托说提出的问题：若癸酉本出于今人，本项目有哪些东西要变。有独立依据的与项目自有来路的不变；只来自癸酉本的情节，是写作选择，须改注来路；同文的字句须注明或改拟；总篇的主论点与方法不受影响。

1 引言

本项目复原《红楼梦》后三十回，所凭的是前八十回正文与脂批。可是在写作的许多关口，项目手边还摊着另一部书：一种自称出自旧抄本、写到一百零八回的“癸酉本”。它把前八十回伏下的许多事都写了出来，写法却与本项目的走向大多相反：天下大乱，流寇破京，人物成批惨死，悲剧配着悼明的调子。本项目一面借它，一面与它相反而行。总篇 4.6 节交代过这件事的纪律，把代表论述与正面对话留给了本文。

这部书的来历，公开记录里分量很重。它从网上出现，原件从未公开；各版文字不一；据《光明日报》2018 年的报道，持有者对记者承认，其中两条提到吴梅村的批语是他伪造的，又说网传正文是凭记忆还原、经多人修改的。学界的主流意见视之为今人所作。本项目不参与这场真伪之争。这不是回避。本项目选择相信书有层累、稿有多版，对复原工作来说，非真即伪的二分帮不上忙。这是本项目的工作立场，不是对伪托说的评判；伪托说的来历证据，第 3.4 节照录。更要紧的是，本项目用它的方式，本来就不以它的真伪为前提。本文要做的，是把这一点摆出来，让读者可以核验。

所以本文的问题不是“癸酉本是真是假”，而是三个更朴素的问题：本项目从癸酉本借了什么；借来的东西，离开癸酉本还站不站得住；如果伪托说成立，本项目有哪些东西要变。

回答这三个问题，要先把材料摆清楚。第 2 节说本文所据的是哪些本子；第 3 节照来路登记公开记录；第 4 节照述诸说；第 5 节说本项目自己的读法及其难处；第 6 节是项目用它时立下的纪律；第 7 节是全文主体，逐项清点借来的东西；第 8 节说不取什么；第 9 节回答伪托说的问题。

有几条立场贯穿全文，先在这里说定。本文照述伪托说，代表论述逐条列出处，不删，也不驳。本项目对癸酉本的读法，只是读法，是工作假说，不承重；读者不接受它，本文的清单与结论照样成立。这与专题二“只依赖一层”构造相同。本项目对癸酉本出于谁手，思考过几种设想，本文只写明思考过，不判，也不作依据。“曹雪芹”取文本角色，指题名串与脂批所称、“披阅十载，增删五次”的那一位；此人历史上是谁，本文不涉。

2 何为“癸酉本”

2.1 名称

这部书名目很多，每个名字背后都有一段来历。2008 年它初在网上露面时，网友赵振东为称呼方便，拟名“何初本”。同年年底，据持有者转述的一条回末批“癸酉腊月全部誊清　梅村”，发布者金俊俊改称“癸酉本”；后来的本子里，这条批语作“癸酉腊月全书誊清”。又因批语自述作者为吴梅村，有人称“吴本”“吴祖本”。排印出版时用过《癸酉本石头记后28回》与《吴氏石头记增删试评本》两种书名；后一种据说是封面所题，但持有者 2018 年对记者说，这是“虚构的书名”。本文沿用项目的旧称“癸酉本”，只作称呼，不含“成于癸酉年”之意。

2.3 工作本与第三稿本

本文以工作本为引文底本，是因为它就是本项目写作时用的本子：项目当时所引的癸酉原句，有十几条只见于工作本，第三稿本里没有。例如第八十三回厨役口中的“殣尸盈道”，第八十九回的“大盗势飙，乘衅倾朝，主惊臣乱”，第一〇二回宝钗的“载戢干戈”。项目当时引的是哪一本，论文就得引哪一本，不然读者核不上。

两本后二十八回的回目只有三处不同：第八十三回，工作本作“暮年哀聊发白头吟　金萱悲情洒儿女泪”，第三稿本作“史太君临终念子孙　王夫人膏肓托儿女”；第八十四回，“霭烟缘”与“金玉缘”；第一〇五回，“藉词”与“借词”。这三处，连同第八十一回回前批的“须校雠酌改”、第八十三回的开篇诗，都与网上流传的一种一百零八回在线全本相同。工作本很可能就是那一本，或出自同一来源。

两本的篇幅差得不少。去掉批语、按语与标点，工作本后二十八回约 21.4 万字，第三稿本约 18.9 万字。第三稿本的内容，大部分可在较长的工作本中找到；工作本多出的，集中在政论与回末批、悼明诗文（第九十八回诔文后半一组写明末人物的五言诗最显）、神怪与招匪的段落，以及整理者所说“底本与润色本重出”之处。增删的方向，本文不据此定：是一本删了另一本，还是一本补了另一本，本文不判。

正文也有几处两本不同，与本文相关的有三处。王夫人之死，工作本在第八十三回末落湖、第八十四回命绝，第三稿本在第八十三回已死。王仁卖巧姐的院子，工作本作“红香院”，第三稿本作“烟香院”。地名“瓜州”“瓜洲”，工作本混用，第三稿本只作“瓜洲”。本文凡说到这几处，都照工作本，另注第三稿本。

工作本还有一位未署名整理者的按语。第一回吴梅村一条批语后，他注“此条批语真假未知”；第八十二回《十独吟》后，他注“底本八首诗部分或全部蛀毁，后经过录人自己补写……真假难辨”；第一〇三回，他注“此第三处惊现底本与润色本内容”。这些说法，与新浪博客“护珠塔主”2015 年一文所说“两个底本同时被抄录，吴梅村文字与润色稿文字平行发展”相合；该文称所据是吴雪松勘校的“过录本”（一手，工具摘录；博主是否即吴雪松，未证）。工作本多半出自这一系，本文未坐实。整理者的按语，本文只当按语，不当作本子自带的文字。

工作本的批语里，两支并存：第一回既有金俊俊、何玄鹤一支所收的“此书本系吴氏梅村旧作”，也有王晓丰一支所称的“雪芹旧有风月宝鉴一书，乃西堂故兄所作”（第 3.6 节）。另有两条批语只见于工作本，第 5.3 节照述。

总篇 4.6 节称癸酉本为“一部……抄本”。据公开记录，只有持有者自称读过抄本，原件从未公开；公众所见，只有网帖、排印本、打印本、数字化本与一组仿抄页。总篇下一版改作“文本”（附录六）。

3 公开记录

3.1 2008 年：网上的出现

关于癸酉本最早何时出现，有三种说法。九州本简介与发布者刘俊俊（即金俊俊）2016 年的博文，都说是 2008 年 8 月底，一说 8 月 31 日。张霄军、张瑞雪 2017 年一文说，它“最早于2008年9月出现在赵振东先生的博客上(现在博文已删除)”（转载）。还有人说流传于“天涯论坛”或“各大红楼论坛”。

能直接打开的最早原帖，是 2008 年 10 月 13 日新浪博客“癸酉本石头记”上的《石头记“何初本”后半部回目》，列出第八十一至一〇八回的回目；同一博客另有第八十一回正文一帖。两帖页面标示的发布时间，分别是 18:04:55 与 18:15:48（一手）。次日一帖《与何小姐交流记录》保存了一段 QQ 群聊天，赵振东、何莉莉、俊俊三方都在场（一手）。10 月 17 日，博客又发《何初本—81回脂批摘选》与《何初本石头记情况及来历》。12 月 5 日，博主据何莉莉转述的回末批，把它改称“癸酉本”，推癸酉为 1753 年，说这个本子“比甲戌本还早一年”。12 月 21 日，第一〇八回正文帖发出，网上的二十八回至此发完。

这批原帖今天还在，是这部书最早的可核之形。它们显出一件事：这部书从露面起，文字就不止一种。10 月 13 日的回目帖里，第八十一回下句作“承辱怀怨以身试墙”；标示时间晚约十一分钟的正文帖，题目作“承辱怀怨蓄恶结党”。页面文字后来有无编辑、这一差异何时形成，本文未核；第 3.5 节再说。

3.2 出版与定名

2014 年，金俊俊、何玄鹤在九州出版社出版《〈癸酉本石头记〉后28回》，简介自称“对正文中的个别现代语进行了替换”。2015 年，何莉莉、王晓丰在线装书局出版《吴氏石头记增删试评本》，据《光明日报》，封面题“曹雪芹著”；同篇又记，吴雪松打印装订的过录本原文，则写着“原著：吴梅村”。同年，吴雪松为金、何一系的“第二版”作序，自称得到“过录本影印资料”。2017 年的“第三稿”，序一出自肖文林，称“癸酉是指1693年，‘梅村’是指吴梅村”；前言出自金俊俊、何玄鹤，自承本书是“过录本影像资料的整理稿，而非真本原文，真实性也有待专家鉴定”（转载）。

书名与作者之说，在十年里变了几次。2008 年 12 月，官方博客按曹雪芹的年代推癸酉为 1753 年（12 月 5 日、15 日两帖）；而同年 10 月 17 日一帖记何莉莉的话：“她说她从没有声称这后半部一定是曹雪芹写的。”到 2015 至 2017 年，吴梅村作者说成了金、何一系发布方的主张，癸酉也改推为 1693 年；线装本的封面则题“曹雪芹著”。而据《光明日报》，何莉莉认定抄本封面所题是“红楼梦”三字。同一部书，书名、作者与癸酉之年，都不止一说。

3.3 持有者与原件

持有者网名“何莉莉”。关于此人，各方说法互相出入：2008 年 10 月 17 日官方博客《何初本石头记情况及来历》一帖以“她”相称，说“何莉莉今年23岁”（一手）；刘俊俊 2016 年称“何莉莉小姐”，安徽阜阳人；《光明日报》2018 年称“化名，男，初中学历”；2017 年版前言称“安徽阜阳某赵姓藏家”。得书的经过也不止一说：2008 年那一帖只说“她的祖母是国民党军医的老婆”，书“有12本，每本九回”；刘俊俊 2016 年说，祖父在山西战场做军医时，一个伤员交给做护士的祖母；《光明日报》记持有一方之说，祖父母偶然得到，共十二册，每册九回；澎湃号 2019 年记，县文化馆主任想借阅，于是抄入笔记本借出。抄录者或说表妹，或说姐姐，或说姐弟。

原件从未公开。它的下落有三说：被母亲当废品卖掉，可能被外人偷走，送往台湾亲戚家收藏。发布方自述得到过一张封面照片，2015 年又得到“过录本全文影印资料”。2019 年澎湃号的报道配过一幅图，图注称“发布者至今唯一公布的底本封面图”；实物与完整影印本，都未获核验。

2009 年 2 月 25 日，百度贴吧出现一篇《何莉莉小姐对红迷的道歉书》，内有“本人鬼使神差编造了何初本后28回，欺骗了大家”等语（据张霄军、张瑞雪文所录，本文已对转载页核过这句；贴吧原帖未见，二手）。《光明日报》后来记下，网上确有此文，而何莉莉本人否认其内容。这份道歉书是否出自何莉莉本人，本文无从判定。

3.4 2018 年的调查

2018 年 2 月 27 日，《光明日报》第 07 版刊出记者刘剑的调查《“吴氏石头记”的倒塌》。这是关于癸酉本来历最重要的一份公开记录，本文据数字报原页，文中所引各句写作时已对原页核过（一手）。

报道的核心是两条批语：一条在第一回，以“此书本系吴氏梅村旧作”起；一条在第一〇八回回末，以“本书至此告终，癸酉腊月全书誊清。梅村夙愿得偿”起。吴梅村作者说主要就立在这两条上。报道写道：“而面对记者的求证，何莉莉多次表示，这两条批语都是他伪造的，而他本人对此也十分后悔。”他说，“‘吴氏石头记增删试评本’是虚构的书名，当时吴雪松、刘俊俊等说‘吴梅村作《红楼梦》’比较靠谱，所以自己伪造了那两条与吴梅村相关的批语”。报道又概述何莉莉 2014 年的一篇文字，说“网络传播的后28回内容并非‘过录本’原文，而是由他凭着记忆还原的情节，并经过了他本人及其他人的多次修改”。王晓丰与何莉莉 2017 年再版仍用那个书名，“是出于书号考虑”。

报道也记下了反面的声音。吴雪松坚称何莉莉的话不可信。中国红楼梦学会的杜春耕则对记者说，网传后二十八回“应当有一个底本存在，如果是完全创作，那么造假者还是有一定水平的”；同时他又说，何莉莉回忆的抄本，“前80回情节中有一部分来自清末吴克岐创作的本子”。杜春耕这两层意思，目前只见于这篇报道，此前有无文章提出，本文未查到。

3.5 各版的变动

癸酉本的文字，从头就不稳定。最清楚的例子在第八十一回。2008 年 10 月 13 日的回目帖作“避祸念旧凭带为媒　承辱怀怨以身试墙”；同日的正文帖，下句作“承辱怀怨蓄恶结党”，正文里司棋挨打被撵，并没有死。四天后的批语摘选，却有一句“见司棋撞墙而亡，也唬了一跳”，下接一条批：“雪芹大错矣！司棋前回已出。贾门何来此文，极不妥，删净重拟。”也就是说，“撞墙而亡”的那一稿，只以残句的形式留在批语摘选里，旁边还带着一条要它删掉的批语（均一手）。到了后来的工作本与第三稿本，回目作“惜昵近公子做良媒　讳笞罚丫鬟结恶党”，司棋被撵后与潘又安结党，“雪芹大错矣”那条批不见了。

同类的变动还有不少。第八十二回回目上句，2008 年作“贾宝玉佯捧万章经”，两本都改作“王熙凤病求千翼方”，宝玉读《孟子·万章》一节只存于正文。第八十四回的“雾烟缘”（2008），工作本作“霭烟缘”，按语说“底本两字蛀毁”，由过录人补；第三稿本作“金玉缘”，也有一条按语：“其中底本两字蛀毁，‘金玉’二字为校勘时后补。”两本都说蛀毁，补的字不同。第九十二回，据 2019 年澎湃号的报道，2008 年网络版有县令“已经死翘翘了”一句，第三稿本作“已经死了”。第一〇八回的回末批，2008 年转述为“癸酉腊月全部誊清　梅村”，2008 年 12 月 21 日的正文帖里不见“梅村”；两本作“是书至此暂告一段落……梅村夙愿得偿”，工作本注“此批未校”；《光明日报》所引，又作“本书至此告终”。这三种句式各属哪一版，本文未能分配。《十独吟》一题，第三稿本第八十二回的按语说只剩朱淑真、李清照两首完整，工作本则收了十二首，整理者注明是过录人补写的；两本第一〇四回都说它“以朱淑真、柳如是、卞玉京、李香君等十人为目”。

这些变动，伪托说与真本说读法相反：一方以为是编造者逐次修改的痕迹，一方以为是过录删改与校勘补字的痕迹。本文只登记变动本身。

3.6 两支批语与“西堂故兄”

2017 年 8 月 28 日，新浪博客“红楼梦断三百年”公布了一条批语，称出自一个“新钞本”的第一回：“雪芹旧有《风月宝鉴》一书，乃西堂故兄所作，仅贾瑞、秦可卿、秦钟事也，稍嫌枯索。”（一手）博主在另一文中，把这个“新钞本”与王晓丰联系在一起；博主是否即王晓丰，本文未证。工作本里这条批语更长，后接“雪芹立誓着一巨作传世……雪芹不肯署名，答曰：何妨假以梅村、醉茶、西村、箨庵、石林缀之乎……雪芹润色止于八十回”等语。

这条批语与金俊俊、何玄鹤一支的“吴氏梅村旧作”一条，占的是同一个位置，一说原作出于西堂，一说出于吴梅村。它的句式，与甲戌本眉批“雪芹旧有《风月宝鉴》之书，乃其弟棠村序也”（1.61）相同；末尾“稍嫌枯索”四字，也见于持有者自认伪造的那一条。本文只并列这几件文本事实，不作推论。学界有无专门讨论这一条，本文未查到。专题二留下的这条线索，到此大体交代，未了之处列入附录五。

4 诸说

4.1 伪托说

视癸酉本为今人所作，是公开研究里的主流意见。本文见到的代表文献，有 2018 年《光明日报》刘剑的调查，其中引杜春耕、段江丽、任晓辉、张惠诸家；2019 年澎湃号殷静的报道，引江苏省红楼梦学会会长、南京大学文学院教授的意见，报道署作“苗伟明”，是否即苗怀明，待证；张霄军、张瑞雪《网络环境下古籍作伪新手段——以〈红楼梦〉三大网络作伪案为例》（转载，2017，原刊待查）；赵建忠《“癸酉本”〈石头记〉辨伪》（搜索结果显示维普收有题录，页面未能打开，全文未见）；孔德罡 2023 年在澎湃新闻的评论；杨依菲 2024 年在《关东学刊》的论文；2025 年 7 月 Sixth Tone 上 Yang Yifei 的英文文章；以及同年 12 月《新京报》对北京曹雪芹学会学术年会的报道，其中引胡德平的意见（记者何安安）。

这些文字的论据，可以归成五组。

第一组是来历。据《光明日报》，连持有一方自己也说，“只有何莉莉曾经读过这套书”；孔德罡说，“何莉莉始终无法拿出原本书稿供学界和爱好者检阅”；胡德平说，“该版本并未拿出底本”。持有者自认伪造两条批语一事（第 3.4 节），也归在这一组。

第二组是作者说。《光明日报》记杜春耕、段江丽、任晓辉等认为吴梅村作《红楼梦》之说是“无稽之谈，不值一驳”。反驳者转引赵建忠之说（二手）：第二十一回黛玉续诗有“作践南华庄子因”一句，《庄子因》成书于康熙二十七年（1688），而吴梅村卒于康熙十一年（1672）。杜春耕又指前八十回有一部分来自清末吴克岐的本子。

第三组是文本本身。2008 年网络版第九十二回有“已经死翘翘了”一句，九州本简介也自称替换过“个别现代语”。报道所引“苗伟明”说：“第一从情节上讲是胡编乱造，全无曹公的情节走向。第二语言表达十分拙劣。第三毫无才气。”孔德罡说它“文字粗糙、诗歌不合韵脚，与前八十回文字水平相距甚远”。张惠在公众号里戏言，那些洗劫大观园的情节“不是吴梅村写的，一定是施耐庵写的”（二手）。还有人指出它与前八十回的设定抵牾：情榜列入“十二公子”等男子，与第五回所说太虚幻境各司“皆贮的是普天之下所有的女子过去未来的簿册”不合（詹姆斯旁，2022，转载）。

第四组是它与探佚成果的相合。一百零八回之数合于周汝昌等人之说（zh.wikipedia，二手）；前八十回脂批所伏之事，后二十八回多有“兑现”。支持者举此为真本之证（第 4.3 节），反对者举此为依批而写之证。反方的专文，本文只见知乎题名《伪癸酉本（伪吴氏石头记）抄袭造假的三大铁证》，未能读到正文。

第五组是各版文本不一，多次改动（第 3.5 节）。何莉莉 2014 年自己也说，网传正文“经过了他本人及其他人的多次修改”。

学界的总体判断，Sixth Tone 一文说得最直：“experts unanimously agree that the Guiyou version is a 21st-century forgery”。杨依菲说它“已被学界证伪，却越‘打假’越火”。张霄军、张瑞雪说：“那所谓的‘何初本’就是典型的造假。”（转载）

有一点须写明。《红楼梦学刊》《曹雪芹研究》上有无讨论癸酉本的专文，本文与所托的补查都没能取得知网、万方、维普可审计的检索页。“未查到”不等于没有。

4.2 较宽之说

在否定的意见之外，也有较宽的说法，但都很谨慎。杜春耕说网传后二十八回“应当有一个底本存在”，同一篇报道里却又否定吴梅村说，并说抄本前八十回有一部分来自吴克岐的本子。报道中这个“底本”，没有被指为曹雪芹原稿，也没有被指为十八世纪的旧稿。首发者之一赵振东，据刘俊俊 2016 年的博文转述，认为“这个本子是个比较好的他人续本，有较大的探佚学价值，但不是真本”（二手）。连发布方自己的 2017 年前言，也说“真实性也有待专家鉴定”。本文没有查到有专业学者公开主张，它保存了曹雪芹底稿的成分。

4.3 真本说、吴梅村说与悼明之读

主张真本或吴梅村原作的一方，据所见资料，都不以学术机构的身份发表。他们的主张，本文只照述。

官方博客 2008 年 12 月按曹雪芹的年代推定癸酉之年，同时已经提出另一种读法：12 月 15 日一帖说“后28回有很多反清悼明，反满拥汉的内容”，2009 年 3 月 10 日一帖说贾蓉、贾蔷“谐音‘戎羌’”（一手）。吴雪松 2015 年的序说：“据书中诸批语可知，《风月宝鉴》是吴梅村作品”，后二十八回“是不容置疑的《石头记》佚文”（转载）。肖文林 2017 年的序把后二十八回的战乱，读作“明末农民军、清军争夺大明江山的缩影”（转载）。何玄鹤 2016 年一篇博文说，他对比前八十回的批语，“整理出59条暗伏遗稿内容的批语……其中48条批语得到了印证”（转载；博文发在“神算子秦一卦”，署“作者：何玄鹤　增删整理：秦昭言”）。金俊俊、何玄鹤 2017 年的前言，也说前八十回批语所伏的场景在后二十八回“一一印证”，“这是该本作为《红楼梦》全本真稿的有力证据”，同一篇又说真实性有待鉴定。

悼明之读，在后二十八回里有贯穿的文本依据：海外“玉户岛”犯边，“戎羌”觊觎；元春以“与戎羌勾结”的罪名凌迟；流寇破京，戎羌夺权登基；第一〇〇回犯官子弟齐喊“誓灭胡虏，还我河山”；第一〇二回陈也俊赠妙玉三诗，工作本题作《江东行》，有“遗民血泪盼国复”之句。这些内容，本项目一律不取（第 6、8 节）。

4.4 大众红学

癸酉本真正流行，是在视频平台上。孔德罡把 2019 年起“木鱼水心”等人的讲解视频，看作它进入大众视野的节点，并批评这一现象以“家国情怀”“悼明说”压过了对情感与人性的关注。杨依菲以 B 站为例，讨论癸酉本与索隐派的复兴、学院与大众的隔阂。本文写这一节，是因为本项目的读者多半从这里接触癸酉本。

5 本项目的读法

5.1 某一版底稿

本项目把癸酉本读作“后人基于曹雪芹某一版底稿的再创作”。

“某一版”三个字，是接着专题二的立场说的。书有层累，稿有多版；脂批所指的后文，各指某一版，曹雪芹对后半部很可能有过不止一种设计。癸酉本若承自底稿，承的也只是其中一版，未必是脂批所见的那一版。所以它与脂批不合的地方，不必强求一致；它与本项目的走向相反，也不妨碍本项目从中借取。

本项目借它，凭的是这一读法里的一条判断：癸酉本中凡与前八十回所立人物性格相合的内容，本项目以为可能留有曹雪芹的原意，值得借鉴；与前八十回人物性格不合的，不取（第 6 节规则 7）。这是作者的读法，不是考证。若癸酉本出于今人，这条判断就退为一条文学上的标准：合于前八十回人物性格的写法，不论出自谁手，都值得借鉴（第 9 节）。

这一读法是本项目用它时的读法与写作依据。它是工作假说，不承重（总篇 1.4 节）。它不反驳伪托说，也不需要伪托说为它让路。公开记录的分量本文已经照实写了（第 3 节），读者可以不接受这一读法；本文第 7 节的清单与第 9 节的结论，都不靠它成立。本项目在写作中，对癸酉本出于谁手、成于何时，还思考过几种设想，彼此不一，都没有公开证据；本文不判，也不以任何一种作依据。

5.2 第四十二回一批：缘由与它的难处

作者持这一读法，举过一条缘由。第四十二回有一批（42.6），起首是“‘应了这话就好’，批书人焉能不心伤？”，下接：“狱庙相逢之日始知‘遇难成祥，逢凶化吉’实伏线于千里，哀哉伤哉！此后文字不忍卒读。辛卯冬日。”这条批把巧姐的“遇难成祥”，与一处“狱庙相逢”连在一起。癸酉本写巧姐遭难，紧接着就是平儿、巧姐被押进嶽神庙、与宝玉相见的一节；作者以为，这正兑现了这一批。

这一缘由，本文照录；它的难处，本文也照录，共有四层。

第一层，这一兑现两说都能解释。底稿之读以为，这是底稿留下的痕迹；伪托说以为，这是依批而写，续作者读过脂批，照着写就是。第三稿本的今人编者，还把这条批语作为脚注，直接挂在第九十四回“快带往嶽神庙看押”一句之下。所以这一兑现，不能用来判断两说谁是。

第二层，这条批语本身的来路也有争议，所见有三说。项目底本标作“该批”；专题一甲18 记，“审读者记得通行作靖本眉批；若是，即属参级，待影印本核”；第三稿本第九十四回的脚注，则称之为“第四十二回蒙本侧批”，所录文字与项目底本一字不差。若属靖本，靖本的真伪又有争，高树伟《红楼梦靖藏本辨伪》（中华书局，2024）主伪，专题二已述。

第三层，是项目自身取舍的限度。专题一审计把这条批登记为“半兑现”：其中“狱庙相逢”与巧姐相连的一层，复原本不取，记“不采五”。复原本在狱神庙相逢的是宝玉与茜雪、小红，巧姐的遇难成祥落在第九十九、一〇〇回。也就是说，本项目持这一读法的缘由，恰是本项目自己在复原中没有照走的那一层。受一条批的启发，又按公开的判准不照写，这不算自相矛盾；癸酉本若承某一版，那一版与本项目的这一种复原取舍，可以不同。真正的限度在别处：这一兑现分不出是旧稿承传，还是今人依批作续（第一层）。读者应当知道这一点。

第四层是一个字。癸酉本全书作“嶽神庙”，工作本后二十八回十四处，前八十回批语里另有三处；脂批作“狱神庙”（20.20、26.24 等）。吴世昌 1982 年批评过赵冈，说他“笔下写的是有关狱神庙的文章，脑中想的则是嶽神庙的形象”（转载）。可见探佚文字里，早就有过近于“嶽神庙”的想象。本文只记这一事实，不据以判先后。

5.3 工作本里的两条自述

工作本有两条批语，只见于这一本，口吻相同，关系到这部书对自己的交代，本文照述。

第一条是第八十一回回前的一段无署名文字，用方括号，不用其余批语的【】，体例像批语，下文仍称“长批”。它的作者与编入时间都未核；文中自称说到雪芹、亲朋，本文不因此把它当作旧批者的证词。第三稿本没有它，2008 年第八十一回的正文帖也没有（一手核过）。它先说：“上回表过‘终不知端的’，岂非全书终于八十回乎？雪芹本旨尚有廿八回，粗略概述如下”，下接一段后事梗概，有探春远适玉户岛、宝玉丢玉、甄宝玉做了和尚前来送玉、宝钗适雨村等。接着说：“雪芹本意贾府因贾芹等招接匪类暴殄天物而致家亡人散，非国破家乱，然思之味同嚼蜡，故事平淡，吾等不予采纳。”又说，后二十八回是“有亲朋苦劝补成完稿”，“舍雪芹原拟，直述汉唐国殇贼祸”。末了说：“刘氏有奇思佳构，欲与同僚再续红楼，别尽碍语，仅着意于宝黛钗事，余允之。”第二条是第一〇八回回末的第二条批语：“冀盼亲友所续四十回早日完稿，流传京都”。

两条合起来，自成一套交代：后二十八回是“亲朋”补成的，舍了雪芹原拟；另有“刘氏”再续一种，只写宝黛钗事，似指程高本。这在癸酉本自己的批语里，就把后二十八回说成了续作，与本项目的读法字面相近。但它们也是这一系统里来路最窄的两条，只见于工作本。本文照述，不借它们为本项目的读法作证。

6 采信纪律

本项目读癸酉本，主要是把它当作一面反面的镜子。癸酉本用天下大乱给悲剧配乐，把后半部写成流寇破京、多数人惨死；本项目让悲剧在如常的世道里无声发生，因为世道若乱，罪责就被乱世稀释了。在本项目看来，癸酉本一写到政治，人物的声口就常常变成政论的传声筒；本项目只取人物自己的声口。它走的每一步，都逼本项目说清楚，自己为什么不这样走。

在这个大方向下，项目陆续立过十条规则，下表列出。这是当前可以继续采用的规则，不是说历来每一步都照此遵守。每条的最后一栏说，把规则里关于作者的前提拿掉之后，它还成不成立。这一栏要紧：有几条规则立下时，带着关于作者的设想；本文不判作者，规则就必须在不判作者的情况下也讲得通。

7.1 清点的办法

本文把现行稿中与癸酉本有关的地方逐项列出，共七十二项，对每一项做三件事：到工作本里核它在癸酉本第几回，在现行稿第几回；到前八十回正文（含判词、曲文）与脂批里，查它有没有独立的依据；写明若癸酉本出于今人，这一项该怎样处置。全表见附录三。

“独立依据”的认定，本文从严。它须是对后事的预示，或是此事直接承接的前因，并与这一项的人、事、物至少两样相合。只提供了人物或物件的出处，只是性格、气氛相近的，都只算“旁及”，不算依据。一项分几层的，按主干定类。主干只来自癸酉本的，记乙，哪怕其中某一层另有依据，例如 #7 揩油实相，其中糟参一层可承第七十七回王夫人寻参。主干有依据、附带一层借自癸酉本的，记甲，类后加“＊”，借来的那一层另注（#1、#2、#23）。类只是摘要标签，不替代附录三的各栏：有独立依据的，也可能实际借过；字句近同，也不单独证明传播的方向。

7.2 有独立依据的

这一类里，癸酉本的作用是提示。它把前八十回与脂批已经伏下的事，先写了一遍；本项目据以定位、定形，而依据本来就在前八十回与脂批里。

袭人嫁蒋玉菡、临走留下麝月（第八十一回），有 20.45 庚辰批“故袭人出嫁后云‘好歹留着麝月’一语”，又有 28.1 批琪官“后回与袭人供奉玉兄宝卿得同终始”。以茜香罗为凭作合，依据同为 28.1，但“宝玉亲为作合”一层没有依据，是借自 2008 年回目帖“避祸念旧凭带为媒”的。探春在清明辞祠远嫁（第八十五回），有判词“清明涕送江边望，千里东风一梦遥”与 22.103“此探春远适之谶也”；辞祠的仪式与认义女一层，则借自癸酉本。平儿扶正（第九十回），第四十四回鲍二家的对贾琏说过：“他死了，你倒是把平儿扶了正”。刘姥姥救巧姐（第一〇〇回），有判词“偶因济刘氏，巧得遇恩人”。惜春的大观园图随刘姥姥去了乡下（第一〇一回），第四十回刘姥姥说过：“怎么得有人也照着这个园子画一张，我带了家去”。宝钗死在雪里（第一〇八回），判词写着“金簪雪里埋”；同项的雨村不死，是本项目翻癸酉本“雨村病亡”而定，没有脂批依据，也不是借来的。

若癸酉本出于今人，这一类的主干不变；#1、#2、#23 借来的那一层，照乙类改注来路。

7.3 只来自癸酉本的

乙类三十三项，其中一项（#26）待核。它们是本项目从癸酉本借来的写作选择，前八十回与脂批里找不到直接的依据。例如贾府内务一线：下人揩油的实相（第八十二回），把妙玉配人而换作贾环、贾母与妙玉祖母的旧谊（第八十五回；旧谊一层，作者确认取自工作本第八十二回“老太太同他祖母亦是知交”），以赵姨娘告状作查账的扳机（第八十六回）。巧姐一线：二哭大观园而主动择走（第一〇一回），凤姐托梦巧姐说“我来迟了”（第一〇一回）。妙玉一线：陈也俊求她被拒（第九十九回），她梦中与黛玉论“何为洁”（第一〇五回）。宝玉身边：蒋玉菡以戏词答妻（第一〇六回），湘云悼黛玉（第一〇六回）。市井一线：银姐性烈、卜世仁鬻女（第一〇七、一〇八回），二丫头（第一〇四回），北静王退礼（第一〇八回）。细节与名物：靛儿归惜春房，宁古塔之名。

这一类里最常见的手法，是“翻”：取癸酉本一个场面的形，换掉它的义。凤姐的鬼魂回阳复仇，翻为托梦祝女儿嫁板儿；妙玉梦宝玉劝婚，翻为梦黛玉论洁；蒋玉菡“男人三妻四妾也是正理”，翻为戏词里的守一人；雨村贿赂北静王得官，翻为北静王原封退礼。本项目把这种手法叫作“形采义翻”。翻过来的东西，义是本项目的，形是癸酉本的；所以它们归乙，来路要写明。

还有一种情形，场面有一处借来，整组的意思却是本项目自己立的。宝玉流落途中三次得点醒：柳湘莲在路劫中相救（第一〇二回），是道；智通寺老僧叫他拾柴生火（第一〇二回），是佛；二丫头一碗芋头，当他是一个人（第一〇四回），是儒家“仁者爱人”之实。这一组三教之悟，是本项目自己的构思（作者说明）。其中只有二丫头重逢、赠芋的场面出自工作本第一〇六回（#64）；柳湘莲救人一笔，承第六十六回柳湘莲在平安州界赶散强盗、救下薛蟠；智通寺出第二回。

有两项要单说。陈也俊这个人名，项目底本第十四回的送殡名单里没有，那里作“冯紫英，卫若兰等诸王孙公子”；工作本有，通行本也有，待对影印本。就本项目所用的两本而言，这一项连人名都只见于工作本。妙玉一项（#56），瓜州、陷泥两层有脂批与判词为据；她不受辱，出自本项目的判准，“不屈从”一读则借自癸酉本一系，见第 7.5 节。

若癸酉本出于今人，乙类照样可以用，它们本来就是写作选择；只是来路要改注为“借自一部今人续书”。

再说情榜。现行稿第一一〇回，榜上写出名号与考语的有十八人，另有二十一人只列名，不写名号。这十八人里，除青儿外，十七人见于工作本第一〇八回的情榜；其中十六人的“情某”二字与工作本同文，正册的情情、无情、情尊、情敏、情憨、情隐、情懦、情雄、情缘、情槁、孽情，副册以下的香菱情呆、晴雯情勇、袭人情箴、抱琴情忠、翠墨情豁，都是。只有惜春，由工作本的“情冷”改作“情慧”。脂批里所见，只有“情情”“情不情”两词（19.80）。考语里也有同文之处：妙玉条的“壁立万仞”，惜春条的“冷心冷面”（现行稿以“或谓”引入，随即翻转；这四字第六十六回尤三姐已说过，是前八十回语汇），凤姐条“治世之能”的句式（此句式出许劭评曹操“治世之能臣，乱世之奸雄”，见《三国志·武帝纪》裴注引孙盛《异同杂语》，同文的分量很轻）。所以本项目的情榜，册次与“情情”“情不情”有脂批为据，其余名号大多出自癸酉本，经本项目逐一复核后保留；每一条考语的写法，则是本项目自己的。这一层留给专题五细论。

最后是正文字句。总篇说正文层“须经本项目笔法重写”，大体是这样做的，但有少数句子与工作本近同。本文先用一道筛查门槛：连续五字以上相同，或整句只作删改、换序，并除去套语与前八十回已有的语汇；过了门槛的，再以工作记录中的取用记录确认。字面相近本身，不定传播的方向。这样的句子共九项，集中在几处。下面所引都是现行稿的字句（第八十一至一〇三回据 r54，以后据 r53），工作本原句与逐项比对，见附录三附二的对照表。第八十三回贾母病笃一段，王太医的“心气已衰……止有定数”，贾母的“只怕熬不过今年了”“还不知下一回，过得过不得了”，贾政命贾琏开单子，“该当的当，该卖的卖”，什物拉到“菜市口摆开卖了”。第八十四回，薛蟠对宝钗说宝玉要娶的是姓林的，“早没有你的份了”。第八十八回马道婆案发，挖出“象牙刻的男女魔王”，八字与工作本第八十九回全同；案发一事，程高本第八十一回也有，作“像牙刻的一男一女……两个魔王”，字面不同。第九十回，凤姐说“外头都传咱们府里金山银山”。第九十九回，王仁卖巧姐一段与工作本第九十四回近同，如“且说王仁带着巧姐”“潮落月起”，鸨母“摸了摸下巴，笑道：‘是个上品’”。第一〇七回，宝玉捧着黛玉当年给的灯，说“就当又看见他了”。这些句子都经过改写，或删，或换语序，或改用词，但近同是看得出来的。第八十三回贾母“你不用虚宽我的心”一句也与工作本同，但前八十回第七十七回宝玉已说过“你不必虚宽我的心”，是前八十回的语汇，不计。

7.5 第四十一回的一条句读

项目底本所录第四十一回靖眉批（41.14），传文残乱：“……他日瓜州渡口劝惩不哀哉屈从红颜固能不枯骨各示□”。怎样句读，有三种读法。周汝昌读作“他日瓜州渡口，红颜固［？］屈从枯骨，不能各示劝惩，［岂］不哀哉！”，戴不凡读作“他日瓜州渡口屈从，各示劝惩，［岂］不哀哉”，两家都作“屈从”（本文据网上转录，原书与页码待核）。第三种出自癸酉本一系：工作本第四十一回正文里有一条批语，作“妙玉偏辟处，此所谓过洁世同嫌也，他日瓜州渡口，客示劝惩，红颜固能不屈从枯骨，不哀哉？”

总篇依判准选了第三种，读作“不屈从”，归总篇 4.2 节的丙级（判准择读），并说明这一借用“只及校字，不及情节”。这里有两件事要分开：妙玉不受辱，是本项目自己的判断；“不屈从”这一读，是借来的。下面把两件事的底细都摆出来。

这条读法的来路，是它作为癸酉本自己的第四十一回批语面世；本文所见最早的公开引用，是 2016 年 1 月 12 日的一篇博客。它不是哪位学者的校读，而且与持有者自认伪造的两条批语，同属一个批语系统。原批又出自靖本，靖本的真伪有争。所以这里是两重未定：原批是否可靠，句读又从何而来。

更有意思的是，癸酉本自己的情节反而是“屈从”。工作本与第三稿本的第一〇二回，妙玉被劫后“日日接客”；2017 年版前言也把“妙玉瓜洲渡口屈从”列为脂批所伏、已得印证的场景。不过，本文所列 2016 年博文对这句另有解释：它把“能不屈从”读作本可避免、却没有避免的一种可能，访客劝过，她没有听，并以此接上后文妙玉的遭遇。所以在来源自己的解释里，字面的“不屈从”与情节里的屈从并不相悖。本项目取了这一读的字序，没有取它的解释，也没有取它的情节。

本项目写妙玉不受辱，理由不在这条批怎么读，而在判准：曹雪芹写金陵十二钗，连秦可卿在内，都不往淫上引，性羞辱不是他的写法（第 6 节）。“不屈从”这一读，只是让残批与这一判断相容。

句读既是借来的，就照借来的写。若撤去这条文字作为早期异文见证的资格，本项目仍注明这一句读取自癸酉本一系，只是不再用它证明曹稿的结局。妙玉不受辱的情节不受影响，因为它本来出自判准；受影响的，只是 41.14 “不屈从”一层算不算兑现。依周汝昌、戴不凡的读法，复原本不满足这一层，专题一甲24 已经写明：“依别的读法，复原本不满足此批。”凭借来的句读说这一层兑现，论证上只算判准择读，不作脂批的直证；总篇所谓“降一格”，降的就是这一层。

8 不取什么

剔的远多于采。第九十三回整回不取，第九十二回只取了黛玉见流萤一核；第九十七、一〇〇、一〇七回没有取用（回次照癸酉本）。按理由，剔除的内容大致归为六类（附录四）。

最多的是天下大乱的一类，流寇洗劫大观园、县衙被占、人物成批死去，约六十项。其次是悼明与涉政，戎羌、玉户岛、元春凌迟与“勾结戎羌”的罪名、陈也俊三诗、诔文后半的明末人物组诗、“誓灭胡虏”、新朝，约四十项。再次是人物之声或遭遇与前八十回所立不合的，黛玉打死小红、宝钗谋杀坠儿、鸳鸯诅咒贾家、宝玉写信讨妙玉芳心，约三十项。神怪一类，通灵玉自逃、凤姐鬼魂复仇、太虚销号归位，约二十项。与前八十回或癸酉本自身前后不接的，约十项，例如戏子回园向袭人算账，与前八十回第七十七回“蕊官藕官二人跟了地藏庵的圆心”（工作本作“圆信”）所写的去向不接，须补出回园的缘由。架构一类最少，一〇八回、一〇八人情榜、男子入榜、“情僧”，约六项。这几类之外，还有一条判准横贯其中：性羞辱不是曹雪芹的写法（第 6 节）。妙玉沦落风尘至老、众丫鬟被奸杀、宝钗与雨村私会、蒋玉菡与娈童厮混而负袭人，分在不同的类里，也都与这条判准相违。项数只是大约，另有约八十项属情节取舍，未归类。

这张清单比采择清单长得多，意思也在这里：本项目借癸酉本，更多是借它当反面。它把后半部写成了天下大乱、多数人惨死；本项目要回答的，正是人在如常的世道里，怎样走到各自的结局。

9 若癸酉本出于今人

总篇原把本文定为“与伪托说的正面对话”。照作者后来的裁定，本文不辨真伪，所以这场对话只回答伪托说对本项目用材方式与论证资格的影响：若伪托说成立，本项目有哪些东西要变。

本节按最不利于本项目的条件处理：不把癸酉本当作任何曹稿的独立见证，本文所借的内容，一律当作今人续写或整理的文字。今人写作也可能接触旧材料，现代成书本身不等于必无旧源；本节不靠这一层。在这一条件下，分四问作答。

第一问，文学上的选择能不能留。能。主干有独立依据的十四项，与项目自有来路的七项，本来不靠癸酉本；只来自癸酉本的三十三项，本来就是写作选择。第 5.1 节“合于前八十回人物性格的内容值得借鉴”那条判断，此时退为一条文学标准：合于人物性格的写法，不论出自谁手，都可以借。妙玉不受辱一节也不变，它出自判准（第 7.5 节）。

第二问，原先作旁证的资格撤不撤。撤。写作中凡把两本同有的情节当作底稿旁证的，一律撤去。第四十一回的句读，不再作早期异文的见证。

第三问，来路与字句的披露要不要改。乙类的来路，改注为“借自今人续书”；甲类标“＊”的各项，借来的那一层同此。同文与近同的十七项，回目与情榜名号字少，注明来源即可；正文近同的句子，以改拟为宜。这些披露现在就做，不等伪托成立（第 7.4 节）。#70 是本项目自撰的书外批语，直举“癸酉”之名，须改拟或加注。

第四问，总篇哪里实际用过这一旁证。只有 4.6 节第四十一回的句读一处，总篇已把它归入丙级（判准择读）；撤去之后，受影响的只是 41.14 “不屈从”一层算不算兑现，复原本的情节不动。总篇其余说到癸酉本的地方，都只当作候选线索，须回前八十回与脂批复核。采信纪律的十条不变，其中第 4、5、6、7 条把关于作者的前提改述之后，照样可用（第 6 节）。剔除的清单不变。总篇的主论点与方法也不变，因为总篇 1.4 节早已把癸酉本之说列为非承重。

所以结论只有一句：本项目用癸酉本的方式，不以它的真伪为前提。这是本文对伪托说的回应。本文不据此说伪托说错，也不据此说本项目的读法对。

反过来也要说一句。若癸酉本真承自曹雪芹的某一版底稿，本项目也不会因此多得什么，因为凡要作论据的，本来就只认前八十回与脂批。经本篇复核，癸酉本在本项目里的位置，限定为一个提示者，一面反面的镜子，和一个写作素材的来源。

10 结语

本文只主张一件事：本项目用癸酉本，有纪律，有登记，不以它的真伪为前提。其余都是照述。

照述的结果，有几处对本项目不利，本文都写了。从癸酉本真正借来的，大半是写作上的东西，能用前八十回与脂批独立撑住的是少数。回目、情榜名号与少数字句，与癸酉本同文或近同。第四十一回的句读是借来的。本项目持“底稿再创作”之读的缘由，恰是本项目在复原中没有照走的那一层。这些都不改变总篇的主论点，因为总篇从一开始就没有把癸酉本放在承重的位置上；但它们应当让读者知道。

书有层累，稿有多版。在本项目的读法里，癸酉本是某一版底稿之上的再创作；不取这一读法，它也已是这部书流传与接受中的一种文本。它出于哪一年、哪一人之手，本文不判。本项目读过它，借过它，更多时候是与它相反而行。这一层，本文照实记下。
