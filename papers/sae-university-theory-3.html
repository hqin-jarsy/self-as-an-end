SAE Theory of the University · Paper 3

University Research
大学的科研

Han Qin (秦汉) · Independent Researcher · 2026
DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.24293742 · Full PDF on Zenodo · CC BY 4.0
Abstract

The university does not only teach; it also does research, and students too practice judgment in research. This paper writes about research. It begins with a change in a Norwegian rule: in September 2024 the Norwegian Directorate for Higher Education and Skills announced that from 2025 publication points would no longer be part of the funding of higher education institutions. A paper is first converted into points, and points into money: this is a written conversion; it can be written into rules, and it can be taken out of them. This paper asks: when the university itself adopts research standards as gates for appointment, promotion and resources, who answers for this conversion, and what has to be answered? The paper states five propositions. The university answers for its adoption and conversion: why it adopts, how it converts, what consequences it attaches, whether there is review, whether it can withdraw. Authorship records doing and being responsible: the author list does not say what each person contributed, and contributions are recorded separately; those who cannot be responsible for the work are not listed as authors; what students and staff did is recorded under the names of those who did it. The credit standard of mathematics: when the university adopts it as a gate, it asks whether understanding has advanced, without judging for mathematics whether its standard is right or wrong. Judgment in research is not stood in for by standards. In research, a student can raise questions, disagree with an interpretation of the data, and refuse to put his name to conclusions he cannot be responsible for, without suffering retaliation for it.

Writing Declaration: This paper was co-drafted with Claude (Anthropic). All intellectual decisions, framework design, and final editorial judgments were made by the author.

Introduction

On 18 September 2024 the Norwegian Directorate for Higher Education and Skills issued an announcement: "Publication points will no longer be part of the funding of the higher education institutions in Norway," effective from 2025. Until then, the part of state funding to institutions that was allocated by results worked this way: "The funding is calculated based on the number of publication points."

A paper is first converted into points, and points into money. This is a written conversion. It can be written into rules, and it can be taken out of them.

The opening asks one question only: when the university itself adopts research standards as gates for appointment, promotion and resources, who answers for this conversion, and what has to be answered?

The announcement proves only one point: the national funding model once had such a conversion, and withdrew it. Whether individual universities still allocate internally by points, the announcement does not say.

The paper has seven sections. §1 says that research standards are kept in two ledgers; §2, adoption and conversion; §3, authorship; §4, the credit standard of mathematics; §5, judgment in research; §6, students' judgment in research; §7 is a dialogue. Sections 2 to 6 each state one proposition at the end, and all are listed together at the end of the paper.

§1 Two Ledgers

The Theory of Disciplines reads a standard in two ledgers: "The descriptive ledger records how a standard says the world is: whether it measures accurately, whether it classifies stably, whether the evidence is sufficient. The normative ledger records what it presupposes: what counts as harm, what counts as health, what counts as fair, what counts as success, whether gains and losses may be offset against one another." (Theory of Disciplines, Prequel, §2)

Research standards are the same. Whether a standard for evaluating research measures accurately and classifies stably is recorded in the descriptive ledger: whether journal quartiles, say, really sort papers by quality. What counts as a research output, how credit is counted, how many conference papers one journal article is worth, how large a share goes to the first author: these are recorded in the normative ledger. An entry in the normative ledger does not become a description because it is written as a number.

Some of these entries are written: a quartile table of academic journals and a points scheme in promotion regulations each have a party that wrote them. Others are unwritten, customs of a trade: in one field, the corresponding author gets the largest credit; in another, one monograph outweighs ten articles. A custom has no party that wrote it. When the university writes a custom into its own rules for appointment and promotion, the university becomes the party that wrote it.

Research lives in the university, as disciplines do. The Theory of Disciplines asked how a discipline treats its standards; brought down to the university, the question is how the university applies these standards to people.

Here the series parts from Newman once more. Newman said the university is a place of teaching: "If its object were scientific and philosophical discovery, I do not see why a University should have students" (The Idea of a University, Preface). This series writes about research because disciplines live here, and because students also practice judgment in research (Paper 2).

This section states no proposition of its own; it prepares §2.

§2 Adoption and Conversion

The Theory of Disciplines says that a construct that adopts a standard as a gate answers for its adoption (Theory of Disciplines, S34). That proposition spoke of insurance, regulation, courts, platforms and admissions. Brought down to the university: when the university adopts research standards as gates for appointment, promotion and resources, it answers for its adoption.

Answers what? At least five questions. Why this standard is adopted. How it is converted: how journal quartiles become qualifications for appointment, how the order of authors becomes shares of contribution, how a certain kind of output becomes a budget. What consequences it is given: whether one quartile lower means promotion delayed by a year, or a contract not renewed. Whether there is review. Whether it can withdraw: whether the university can stop adopting this standard, and how. When the research of those evaluated does not produce that kind of output, whether there is another path by which they can be evaluated: that is a question this paper adds.

The Norwegian rule was a conversion written in at the national level and then taken out. Conversions inside a university can be written just the same, and questioned just the same.

At times the university is at once the party that writes a standard and the party that adopts it: it draws up its own list of journals and then uses it to evaluate its own people. The two accounts of writing and adopting (not the two ledgers of §1) are not merged into one because they belong to one institution. The party that writes explains what it wrote in, what it guaranteed and what it did not; the party that adopts answers for the trade-offs it adds. The layer added in adoption is not booked back to the party that wrote.

Some will say that the order of authors and the way of counting credit are a discipline's own conventions, and that the university's stepping in exceeds its authority. This proposition does not set conventions for disciplines. It asks only that when the university converts conventions into consequences for appointment, promotion and resources, it answer for this conversion.

DX14 The university answers for its adoption and conversion. What counts as an output and how credit is counted are entries in the normative ledger of standards; written ones have a party that wrote them, unwritten ones are customs of a trade with no party that wrote them, and when the university writes a custom into its rules for appointment and promotion, the university is the party that wrote it. When the university adopts these standards as gates for appointment, promotion and resources, it answers for its adoption: why it adopts, how it converts (how journal quartiles become qualifications for appointment, how the order of authors becomes shares of contribution, how a certain kind of output becomes a budget), what consequences it gives, whether there is review, whether it can withdraw. When the university both draws up and adopts a standard, the two accounts are not merged into one because the organization is the same.

§3 Authorship

Authorship records two things: who did what; who is responsible for it. This section is addressed to the university: in its own rules, evaluations and handling of disputes, the university reads authorship this way; how journals and disciplines define authorship remains their affair.

The author list by itself does not say what each person contributed. The authorship recommendations of the International Committee of Medical Journal Editors put it plainly: "authorship does not communicate what contributions qualified an individual to be an author." So contributions are recorded separately. The CRediT Contributor Roles Taxonomy lists fourteen roles, from conceptualization through data curation to writing and revision; it also states: "CRediT roles do not determine authorship eligibility." Contributions can be recorded separately, and authorship has conditions of its own.

Those who cannot be responsible for the work are not listed as authors. Several major publishers and preprint platforms provide that AI may not be listed as an author, on the grounds that it cannot take responsibility for the work; the Theory of Disciplines says this provision is one place where "judgment is not cancelled" lands, in authorship (Theory of Disciplines, Paper 9). The reason lies precisely here: authorship records being responsible.

Those who have already made substantial contributions ought to have the chance to take part in revision and final approval. Among the conditions of authorship is something like "taking part in revising and approving the final version"; one cannot first keep a person out of revision and then exclude him from authorship on the grounds that he does not meet that condition.

What students and staff did is recorded under the names of those who did it, either by listing them as authors or by writing it into the record of contributions; it is not taken away, nor diluted, because of high or low position. The record of contributions does not avow the conclusions of the whole work in his place: what he did, and whether he approved the published version and is responsible for its conclusions, are registered separately. Being publicly named as an author or in the acknowledgments is checked separately against the corresponding conditions and his own consent; being unwilling to put his name to the conclusions does not erase the contributions he has already made. The Theory of the Company has a sentence: the price falls on what is done, not on the person. Extended to credit: credit falls on what is done, not on position.

DX15 Authorship records doing and being responsible. Authorship records who did what and who is responsible for it. The author list by itself does not say what each person contributed, and contributions are recorded separately. Those who cannot be responsible for the work are not listed as authors: the reason AI is not listed as an author lies precisely here. Those who have already made substantial contributions ought to have the chance to take part in revision and final approval; the chance is not first blocked and the person then excluded for not meeting the condition. What students and staff did is recorded under the names of those who did it (as authors or in the record of contributions), and is not taken away or diluted because of high or low position.

§4 The Credit Standard of Mathematics

In mathematics, having proved something has been made into the measure. Whoever first proves a theorem gains standing and reputation. Since AI began to take part in proofs, this question has been pushed to the forefront: if a theorem is proved with a machine's help, whose is the credit, and has understanding advanced?

Paper 1 of the Theory of Disciplines raised this point. Thurston set out "progress made by mathematicians consists of proving theorems" as an assumption to be examined; he said that what mathematicians really aim to do is "advance human understanding of mathematics," and that "our strong communal emphasis on theorem-credits has a negative effect on mathematical progress." Merton analyzed disputes over priority in science; Voevodsky wrote about how proofs are verified, and about his own concerns. There the Theory of Disciplines only interfaced with this, saying that how research is evaluated is a matter for the Theory of the University (Theory of Disciplines, Paper 1).

This section takes it up. The descriptive half: the credit standard in mathematics is an entry in the normative ledger, in which "how many theorems were proved" has been taken for "how much was advanced." The normative half: when the university adopts this credit standard as a gate for appointment and promotion, it answers for this adoption and asks one thing: whether understanding has advanced. It does not judge for mathematics whether its credit standard is right or wrong. That is mathematics' own affair. Whether understanding has advanced is answered by peers with the corresponding competence (Paper 7, DX35); the university requires only that the question be part of the review.

Some will say that priority is exactly the incentive that drives discovery. This section does not deny the role of priority. What it asks is what the university looks at when it adopts priority as a gate.

DX16 The credit standard of mathematics. Descriptive: having proved something has been made into the measure, and whoever proves first gains standing and reputation; AI taking part in proofs has pushed this question to the forefront. Normative: when the university adopts the credit standard as a gate for appointment and promotion, it answers for this adoption and asks whether understanding has advanced; it does not judge for mathematics whether its credit standard is right or wrong.

§5 Judgment in Research

In a piece of research there is judgment everywhere: what question to ask, what data to use, how large a sample to take, when to stop, how far the conclusion goes. These choices are judged by those who do the research, and borne by them too.

Besides those doing the research, other positions judge: the ethics review judges whether the research may be done, the party allocating resources judges whether it is worth funding, the journal's reviewers judge whether it can be published. Each answers for its own judgment. "Passed review" does not stand in for any of them: passing ethics review does not mean that the researcher's choices about the data have been checked by anyone; receiving funding does not mean the conclusions have been endorsed.

The Theory of Disciplines says: "Standards do not judge in place of those present, and neither do seats." (Theory of Disciplines, Paper 9, S53) Extended to those who do research, it holds just the same.

When researchers and reviewers use AI, Paper 1, DX4, and Paper 5, DX24, apply: tools can enter a judgment; they do not complete it, and the bearing is not handed over with them.

DX17 Judgment in research is not stood in for by standards. The choices in research (what to ask, what data to use, when to stop) are judged and borne by those who do the research; the party that grants permission and the party that allocates resources each answer for their own judgment, and "passed review" does not stand in for any of them. When researchers and reviewers use AI, DX4 and DX24 apply.

§6 Students' Judgment in Research

Students in research are judging too. A student can propose his own research question; he can disagree with his advisor's interpretation of a set of data; he can refuse to put his name to conclusions he cannot be responsible for.

He does not suffer retaliation for these acts as such. Nor are these acts as such taken as reasons for imposing disadvantages in other positions: wages, course grades, recommendations and degree progress unrelated to this research are not used to put pressure on him to withdraw his judgment. Cultivation Theory's criterion is: "If he does not accept, one does not add disadvantages in other matters" (Cultivation Theory, Paper 5). What is looked at is "other matters."

The position within this research itself is different. If a student is a research assistant on this project and the project changes, or he leaves it, his employed position in this research changes accordingly; the same holds when a stretch of mentoring ends. These are read by Paper 2, DX10 and DX11: the consequences refusal brings and the end of a stretch of mentoring do not, each on its own, amount to crossing the line; the way out has to be usable and not controlled by the original advisor alone; money for the transition that does not depend on the original advisor is that proposition's suggestion. The requirements and consequences in each position are stated separately by Paper 2, DX9, and can be questioned.

Paper 2 left a question: are the advisor and the research supervisor the same person? Where they are the same person, the unbundling of Paper 1, DX3, and the way out of Paper 2, DX11, matter all the more, since the topic, the money and the evaluation may all pass through him. Where they are not the same person, requirements on the research side are recorded separately by DX9.

Montana State University has a page on determining authorship and resolving disputes. First go to the corresponding author and the project lead; failing that, notify the department head; the department head may, as appropriate, ask the dean of the college; where graduate students are involved, the dean is advised to consult the dean of the graduate school; if the matter is still unresolved, it can be reported to the research integrity officer. Both faces have to be read. There is a road that can be walked, written into the rules; the first stop on the road can be a party to the dispute. That page says nothing about freedom from retaliation, and has no section on students; what this page does not say does not mean that none of the university's rules say it.

Some will say that a student's refusal to put his name to a paper will be used as leverage. This proposition says that these acts "as such" bring no retaliation; the end of mentoring or change of project that follows is read by Paper 2, and does not automatically amount to crossing the line.

In Paper 2's constructed case, the stretch on authorship is read here: the student who refused to put his name to the paper suffers no retaliation for it; his judgment is recorded under his name, and conclusions he is unwilling to bear are not avowed in his place.

DX18 Students' judgment in research. In research a student can propose research questions, disagree with an interpretation of the data, and refuse to put his name to conclusions he cannot be responsible for. He does not suffer retaliation for these acts as such; nor are these acts as such taken as reasons for imposing disadvantages in other positions (wages, grades, recommendations and degree progress unrelated to this research), and these positions are not used beyond their authority to put pressure on him to withdraw his judgment. Changes in his employed position in this research that come with a change of project, and the end of mentoring or change of project that follows, are read by DX10 and DX11; money for the transition follows DX11; the requirements and consequences in each position are stated separately by DX9, and remain open to questioning.

§7 Dialogue: Thurston

Thurston's essay is titled "On Proof and Progress in Mathematics," and the three sentences quoted in §4 all come from it. This paper does not read it as opposing proof. Read by those three sentences, and this is this paper's reading, what he asked was whether, once what mathematicians do has been recorded by a way of keeping score, what is recorded is what they are really advancing.

Where they point the same way is §4: the credit standard is an entry in the normative ledger, and it can be questioned. Where they part is that this paper does not draw conclusions for mathematics. Thurston spoke from inside mathematics about what mathematics ought to value; this paper stands on the university's side and asks only what the university looks at when it adopts this standard as a gate. Nor does this paper read "advancing understanding" as lowering the demands of proof. For work that offers a proof, asking what understanding it advanced does not exempt the proof itself from verification; for other mathematical work, it is not concluded that understanding was not advanced merely because no new theorem was added. What standards the university adopts, and what consequences it gives them, it still answers for by DX14.

Where This Paper Stops

What it has not done: it has not judged whether any discipline's credit standard is right or wrong; it has not written the design of research ethics review, which the Theory of Disciplines has written; it has not written the amounts of research funding, as Paper 6 writes only grounds and conditions; it has not judged who is right in any authorship dispute.

This series: DX14 to DX18.

Handed to Paper 4: when a piece of research crosses disciplines, whose standards evaluate it.

摘要

大学不只教书，也做研究；学生也在研究里练判断。这一篇写科研。它从挪威的一条规则变动起：2024 年 9 月，挪威高等教育与技能局公告，从 2025 年起，出版点数不再是高等教育机构拨款的一部分。一篇论文先折成点数，点数再折成钱，这是一条成文的转换；它可以写进规则，也可以从规则里拿掉。这一篇问：大学自己把科研的标准采作聘任，晋升与资源的门时，谁就这一转换作答，答什么。全篇立五条。大学就它的采用与转换作答：为什么采用，怎样转换，给什么后果，有无复核，能不能退出。署名记做事与负责：作者名单不说明每人贡献了什么，贡献另记；不能为工作负责的不列为作者；学生与职员做的事记在做的人名下。数学的功劳标准：大学把它采作门时，问理解有没有推进，不替数学判它的标准错没错。科研里的判断不被标准顶替。学生在科研里可以提出问题，不同意数据的解释，拒绝为自己负不了责的结论署名，不因此受报复。

写作声明：本文与 Claude（Anthropic）共同起草，所有思想决策、框架设计和最终编辑判断由作者做出。

引言

2024 年 9 月 18 日，挪威高等教育与技能局发了一则公告：「Publication points will no longer be part of the funding of the higher education institutions in Norway」，从 2025 年起生效。此前，国家拨给高校的经费里，按成果拨的那一部分，「The funding is calculated based on the number of publication points」：按出版点数计算。

一篇论文先折成点数，点数再折成钱。这是一条成文的转换。它可以写进规则，也可以从规则里拿掉。

开头只问一句：大学自己把科研的标准采作聘任，晋升与资源的门时，谁就这一转换作答，答什么？

这则公告只证到一处：国家一级的拨款模式有过这样一条转换，又撤了。各所大学内部是不是仍按点数分配，这则公告没有说。

全篇七节。第一节说科研的标准有两本账；第二节说采用与转换；第三节说署名；第四节说数学的功劳标准；第五节说科研里的判断；第六节说学生在科研里的判断；第七节是一段对话。第二至第六节节末各立一条，篇末合列。

第一节　两本账

学科论读一条标准，记两本账：「描述账记一条标准说世界是怎样的：量得准不准，分得稳不稳，证据够不够。规范账记它预设了什么：什么算损害，什么算健康，什么算公平，什么算做成了，得失能不能互相抵算。」（学科论前置篇第二节）

科研的标准也是这样。一条评价科研的标准量得准不准，分得稳不稳，记在描述账上：比如期刊的分区，是不是真分出了论文的质量。什么算一项成果，功劳怎样算，一篇论文抵几篇会议报告，第一作者算几成，记在规范账上。规范账上的行，不因为写成了数字就变成描述。

这些行，有的是成文的：一份学术期刊的分区表，一份晋升条例里的计分办法，都有写它的一方。有的不成文，是行规：某个领域里，通讯作者算最大的功劳；某个领域里，一本专著胜过十篇论文。行规没有写它的一方。大学把一条行规写进自己的聘任与晋升规则时，大学就成了写它的一方。

科研住在大学里，学科住在大学里。学科论问的是学科怎样对待它的标准；落到大学，问的是大学怎样把这些标准用到人身上。

这里与纽曼又分开一处。纽曼说，大学是教的地方：「If its object were scientific and philosophical discovery, I do not see why a University should have students」（《大学的理念》序言）。这个系列写科研，是因为学科住在这里，学生也在科研里练判断（第二篇）。

这一节不另立一条，它是第二节的铺垫。

第二节　采用与转换

学科论说，把一条标准采作门的构，就它的采用作答（学科论 S34）。那一条原说保险，监管，法院，平台与录取。落到大学：大学把科研的标准采作聘任，晋升与资源的门时，就它的采用作答。

答什么？至少五问。为什么采用这一条。怎样转换：期刊的分区怎样换成聘任的资格，署名的次序怎样换成贡献的份额，某一种成果怎样换成一笔预算。给它什么后果：差一个分区，是晋升推迟一年，还是合同不续。有没有复核。能不能退出：大学能不能不再采用这一条，怎样退。被评的人做的研究不出那一类成果时，有没有另一条路被评，是这一篇添的一问。

挪威那条规则，是国家一级把一条转换写进去，又拿掉。大学内部的转换，一样可以写，一样可以问。

有时大学同时是写标准的一方与采用标准的一方：它自己定了一份期刊目录，又拿它评自己的人。写与采用的两笔账（不是第一节说的两本账），不因为是同一个机构就合成一笔。写的一方说明它写进了什么，保证了什么，没有保证什么；采用的一方就它新加的取舍作答。采用时加上的那一层，不倒记给写的一方。

有人会说，署名次序与功劳的算法是学科自己的惯例，大学插手是越权。这一条不替学科定惯例。它只要大学在把惯例转换成聘任，晋升与资源的后果时，就这一转换作答。

DX14　大学就它的采用与转换作答。 什么算成果，功劳怎样算，是标准的规范账上的行；成文的有写它的一方，不成文的是行规，没有写它的一方，大学把行规写进聘任与晋升规则时，大学就是写它的一方。大学把这些标准采作聘任，晋升与资源的门时，就它的采用作答：为什么采用，怎样转换（期刊分区怎样换成聘任资格，署名次序怎样换成贡献份额，某种成果怎样换成预算），给它什么后果，有无复核，能不能退出。大学同时制订并采用一项标准时，两笔账不因组织相同合成一笔。

第三节　署名

署名记的是两件事：谁做了什么；谁对它负责。这一节说给大学：大学在自己的规则，评价与争议处理里，照这样读署名；期刊与学科怎样定作者资格，仍是它们的事。

作者名单本身，不说明每个人贡献了什么。国际医学期刊编辑委员会的作者资格建议写得直白：「authorship does not communicate what contributions qualified an individual to be an author」。所以贡献另记。CRediT 贡献者角色分类，列了十四种角色，从概念的提出，到数据的整理，到写作与修改；它也写明：「CRediT roles do not determine authorship eligibility」，角色不决定谁有作者资格。贡献可以另记，作者资格另有条件。

不能为工作负责的，不列为作者。几家主要的出版者与预印本平台都规定 AI 不得列为作者，理由是它不能为作品承担责任；学科论说，这一规定是"判断不被取消"在署名上的一个落点（学科论第九篇）。理由正在这里：署名记的是负责。

已有实质贡献的人，应当有机会参与修改与最终的批准。作者资格的条件里有"参与修改，批准最后的版本"一类；不能先把一个人挡在修改之外，再以他不满足这一条为由，把他从作者里排除。

学生与职员做的事，记在做的人名下，或者列为作者，或者写进贡献的记录；不因为位置的高低被收走，也不被摊薄。贡献的记录，不替他认领整篇工作的结论：记下他做过什么，与他是否批准发表的版本，为结论负责，分开登记。以作者或致谢的方式公开列名，另核相应的条件与本人的同意；不愿为结论署名，也不抹去他已经做过的贡献。公司论有一句：价格落在事上，不落在人上。推到功劳：功劳落在做的事上，不落在位置上。

DX15　署名记做事与负责。 署名记的是谁做了什么，谁对它负责。作者名单本身不说明每人贡献了什么，贡献另记。不能为工作负责的不列为作者：AI 不列为作者，理由正在这里。已有实质贡献的人，应当有机会参与修改与最终批准，不先挡住机会，再以不满足条件为由排除。学生与职员做的事，记在做的人名下（作者或贡献记录），不因位置高低被收走或摊薄。

第四节　数学的功劳标准

数学里，证明出来这件事被当成了尺子。谁先证出一个定理，谁就有地位和声誉。AI 参与证明以后，这个问题被推到了风口上：一个定理若由机器协助证出，功劳算谁的，理解推进了没有？

学科论第一篇说过这一点。Thurston 把"数学家的进步就是证明定理"列为一个待检验的假定：「progress made by mathematicians consists of proving theorems」；他说数学家真正要推进的，是「advance human understanding of mathematics」，并说「our strong communal emphasis on theorem-credits has a negative effect on mathematical progress」。默顿分析过科学里的优先权之争；Voevodsky 写过证明怎样被核验，以及他自己的担心。学科论在那里只作对接，说"科研评价怎样定，是大学论的事"（学科论第一篇）。

这一节接过来。描述的一半：功劳标准在数学里是一种规范账上的行，"证出了几个定理"被当作了"推进了多少"。规范的一半：大学把这一功劳标准采作聘任与晋升的门时，就这一采用作答，并问一件事：理解有没有推进。它不替数学判它的功劳标准错没错。那是数学自己的事。理解有没有推进，这一问由具备相应能力的同行来答（第七篇 DX35）；大学只要求评审里有这一问。

有人会说，优先权正是推动发现的激励。这一节不否认优先权的作用。它问的是大学把它采作门时，看的是什么。

DX16　数学的功劳标准。 描述：证明出来这件事被当成了尺子，谁先证出，谁就有地位和声誉；AI 参与证明，把这个问题推到风口上。规范：大学把功劳标准采作聘任晋升的门时，就这一采用作答，并问理解有没有推进；它不替数学判它的功劳标准错没错。

第五节　科研里的判断

一项研究里，处处是判断：问什么问题，用什么数据，样本取到多少，何时停下，结论说到哪里为止。这些取舍由做研究的人判断，也由他承担。

做研究的人之外，还有别的位置在判：伦理审查的一方判这项研究能不能做，分配资源的一方判它值不值得给钱，期刊的评审判它能不能发表。各方就自己的那一判作答。"评审通过"不替代其中任何一方：通过了伦理审查，不等于研究者对数据的取舍已经被谁核过；拿到了经费，不等于结论已经被认可。

学科论说：「标准不替在场的人判断，席位也不替」（学科论第九篇 S53）。推到做研究的人身上，一样成立。

研究者与评审者借助 AI 时，照第一篇 DX4 与第五篇 DX24：工具可以进入判断，不替他完成，承担不随之交出。

DX17　科研里的判断不被标准顶替。 研究中的取舍（问什么，用什么数据，何时停）由做研究的人判断并承担；许可的一方，分配资源的一方各就自己的那一判作答，不由"评审通过"替代其中任何一方。研究者，评审者借助 AI 时，照 DX4，DX24。

第六节　学生在科研里的判断

学生在科研里，也在判断。他可以提出自己的研究问题；可以不同意导师对一组数据的解释；可以拒绝为自己负不了责的结论署名。

他不因这些动作本身受报复。这些动作本身，也不作为在别的位置上施加不利的理由：与这项研究无关的工资，课程成绩，推荐，学位进度，都不拿来施压，迫使他撤回判断。涵育论的判据是：「他不接受，不在别的事上追加不利」（涵育论第五篇）。看的是"别的事"。

这项研究本身的位置不同。一个学生是这个课题的助研，课题换了，或者他离开了这个课题，他在这项研究里受雇的位置随之变动；一段带教结束了，也一样。这些照第二篇 DX10，DX11 读：拒绝招来的后果，一段带教的结束，各自不等于越线；离开的路要能用，不由原导师独自控制；过渡的钱不依原导师，是那一条的建议。各个位置上的要求与后果，照第二篇 DX9 分别说明，并且可以被问。

第二篇留下一问：导师与科研上的上级，是不是同一个人？是同一个人时，第一篇 DX3 的不捆绑与第二篇 DX11 的离开之路更要紧，因为题目，钱与评价都可能经过他。不是同一个人时，研究一侧的要求照 DX9 另记。

蒙大拿州立大学有一页署名与争议的处理办法。先找通讯作者与课题负责人；不成，通知系主任；系主任酌情请学院院长；涉及研究生时，院长宜与研究生院院长商量；仍未解决，可以报给研究诚信官。两面都要读。有一条可走的路，写在规则里；路的头一站，可以就是争议的一方。那一页没有写不受报复，也没有写学生一节；这一页没写的，不等于学校所有的规则都没写。

有人会说，学生拒绝署名会被拿来要挟。这一条说的是这些动作"本身"不受报复；随之而来的带教结束或课题更换，照第二篇读，不自动等于越线。

第二篇的设例里，署名一段在这里读：那个学生拒绝署名，不因此受报复；他的判断记在他名下，他不愿承担的结论，不替他认领。

DX18　学生在科研里的判断。 学生在科研里可以提出研究问题，不同意数据的解释，拒绝为自己负不了责的结论署名。他不因这些动作本身受报复；这些动作本身，也不作为在别的位置（与这项研究无关的工资，成绩，推荐，学位进度）上施加不利的理由，不借这些位置越权施压，迫使他撤回判断。这项研究的受雇位置随课题更换而变的，以及随之而来的带教结束或课题更换，照 DX10，DX11 读；过渡的钱照 DX11；各位置的要求与后果照 DX9 分别说明，并保留受问。

第七节　对话：Thurston

Thurston 那篇文章题为《论数学中的证明与进展》，第四节引的三句都出自它。这一篇不把它读成反对证明。照那三句读，这是这一篇的读法：他问的是：数学家做的事，被一种计分的办法记下来以后，记下的是不是他们真正在推进的东西。

同向的一处，在第四节：功劳标准是规范账上的一行，可以被问。分开的一处，在这一篇不替数学下结论。Thurston 是在数学的内部说数学该看重什么；这一篇站在大学一侧，只问大学把这一标准采作门时，看的是什么。这一篇也不把"推进理解"读成降低证明的要求。对提出了证明的工作，问推进了什么理解，不免除证明本身接受核验；对其他数学工作，也不只因它没有新增一条定理，就断定没有推进理解。大学采用什么标准，赋予什么后果，仍照 DX14 作答。

这一篇停在哪里

它没有做的：没有判某一学科的功劳标准错没错；没有写研究伦理审查的设计，学科论已写；没有写科研经费的数额，第六篇只写根据与条件；没有判哪一桩署名争议谁对。
