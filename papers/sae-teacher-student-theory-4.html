Abstract

This paper reads what grows out of the back-and-forth. Expectation between teacher and student grows out of the dealings: what the teacher has praised and what he has frowned at, which time the student got it right and which time he did not, all become material. The teacher's expectation is a construct left at his own end; it does not turn into the student's duty, and does not certify that the student has avowed anything. Saying an expectation out loud is a form of questioning; hanging an expectation on what kind of person the student is fixes for him what something means to him, and slides in the direction of colonization. What counts as hanging a condition on it is drawn by function, not by topic; whether it takes hold depends on his later understanding and action, and also on whether his denial has anywhere to land.

Compliance does not stand as proof of avowal: the student's saying "all right," doing as told, and doing it well are all only clues. Where the teacher's approval has become the measure of the student's own judgment, this is read by borrowing "superego internalization" from the Learning series; from outside only clues are seen, and confirmation lies at his end. The student's depending on the teacher to teach is the kind of dependency that is there in any case, and carries no direction of colonization; the kind that was made, and that has locked the path to saying no, carries the direction of colonization. The direction is to make him no longer held by this one teacher; this is only a direction, and is not used as a measure. Where one student is used as the measure of another, what is read first is how the sentence lands with the one who hears it; if it also actually alters the situation of the one used as the measure, that relation is entered separately.

This paper interfaces with the "Record of Learning" in the Book of Rites and with Locke's Some Thoughts Concerning Education; research on teacher expectations is taken only as material. It does not prove that teachers ought not to have expectations, nor that praise is harmful, nor that a student who depends on a teacher has not grown, nor that any one way of praising is bound to head one way.

Introduction

In the first year, when Lu Miao finished a problem she turned to the next page.

Later there was a time when she tested something and found a mistake, and Fang Xu said: "You're so clever." Another time she got the result to match by a different method, and Fang Xu said: "You found this step by testing it yourself." He praised her like this several times.

Later still, when Lu Miao finished a problem she looked up first at Fang Xu's face. Once Fang Xu, looking at her column of working, frowned for a moment and said nothing. Lu Miao rubbed out the line she had just written. The line was right.

One Wednesday Lu Miao had not done the previous week's problems. Fang Xu said: "You have talent. You shouldn't be like this." Lu Miao said: "I don't have talent. I couldn't understand those ones." Fang Xu said: "Which ones?" She turned to them and showed him. The next Wednesday Fang Xu did not bring up what he had said the last time. Lu Miao again had one problem not done, and what he asked was: "Which step did you get stuck on?" After that he never said "you have talent" again.

A child upstairs was learning an instrument. The teacher said to her: "Without me, nobody would be willing to teach you like this."

In Master Zheng's workshop, A-Shu's tenons were the tightest. Master Zheng said to Xiaoman and Dongsheng: "Look at A-Shu's." From then on, whenever A-Shu made a mistake, Master Zheng would say: "How can even you do this?" Later he had A-Shu look over Xiaoman's and Dongsheng's work every day before they finished.

Among these there are several things to be read apart. In the first year she did not look at his face, and later she looked first: what grew in between? The two sentences of praise: where do they differ? "You have talent, you shouldn't be like this" and "make up this page next week": are these the same kind of sentence? Lu Miao's "I don't have talent": where did it land? Rubbing out the line that was right: what can be read from it, and what cannot? Lu Miao's not being able to do without Fang Xu's teaching, and "without me, nobody would be willing to teach you like this": are these the same kind of dependency? And "look at A-Shu's": whom did this sentence reach?

What this paper reads. The previous two papers read teaching and the student's questions. This paper reads what the back-and-forth sediments into over time: expectation.

This paper reads five things. First, how expectation grows out of the dealings. Second, hanging an expectation on "what kind of person you are." Third, that compliance does not stand as proof of avowal, and the teacher's approval becoming the measure of the student's judgment. Fourth, the two senses of dependency. Fifth, using one student as the measure of another.

Shared words. "Expectation" follows the usage at the layer of relation: what one party, on the strength of dealings, arranges on his own side and points at the other party's later conduct.1 "Hanging on" is Relation Theory's particular expression: attaching a qualifying condition to an expectation (§2); it is drawn by function, not by topic. "Dependency" has two senses (§4). "Measure" occurs in two places in this paper: the teacher's approval becoming the measure of the student's own judgment (§3), and one student being set up as the measure of another (§5); the two are not one thing.

Method. As in the earlier papers. This paper contains several pairs of contrasting sentences. Which way a sentence heads is not decided by the form of the words alone: which way that occasion headed is read by what it actually opened or sealed at the time; what it sediments into later is read separately, and is not used to judge the occasion backward.

§1 Expectation Grows Out of the Dealings

TS19　Expectation between teacher and student grows out of the dealings: what the teacher has praised and what he has frowned at, which time the student got it right and which time he did not, all become material. The teacher's expectation is a construct left at his own end; it does not turn into the student's duty, and does not certify that the student has avowed anything

Material

Expectation grows out of repeated dealings: response and non-response, on time and not on time, all become material.2 It does not pass through anyone's consent. Fang Xu never said "when you've finished, wait for me to nod," and Lu Miao never agreed to; in the first year she finished and turned the page, later she looked at his face first, and what lies between is several rounds of praise and one frown. From length of time and habit it cannot be inferred who has avowed what.3

Material accumulates at both ends. At Fang Xu's end, he later said: "I thought you would test it yourself." This is his expectation, said out loud by himself. At Lu Miao's end, what can be read is words and deeds: she still asks, and when she finishes a problem she has begun to look at his face first. What expectation she has made of these dealings is not written for her.

The other party's response is material, not a settling of the account.4 Lu Miao gets one right and Fang Xu nods: this enters the conditions of her next problem, and settles nothing. Fang Xu's having answered her questions ten times does not make him owe an eleventh; Lu Miao's having got ten in a row right does not make her owe an eleventh.

A construct at the teacher's end

The teacher's expectation is a construct left at his own end. It does not certify that the student has avowed anything: Fang Xu's expecting her to like mathematics is not her having avowed mathematics. Nor does it turn into the student's duty: Fang Xu's expecting Lu Miao to finish the problems does not make her owe this page of problems.1

For expectation to leave the slot of "only an expectation," upstream writes two exits: the person of whom it is expected takes it on in his own law; or it becomes a role requirement explicitly arranged by both parties and maintained by relational power. Expectation alone does not establish, in reverse, that either of these holds.5 Lu Miao's taking on, herself, "I am going to finish this page" is the first exit. Neither exit is produced by Fang Xu's expectation of itself.

Homework set by a teacher in a school is not read under this proposition: the authority to set it is given by a construct, and its source and force are not judged; how the teacher uses it is read as in Paper 1.

Expectation changes conditions

Expectation stays at the teacher's end, but does not stop there. It can enter the other party's conditions of occurrence by way of the words and deeds of the one who expects; the person of whom it is expected has not thereby avowed anything.6 Fang Xu's praise and frown are words and deeds. Lu Miao finishes a problem and looks first at his face: the conditions of her doing problems from then on have one thing more in them. The reading goes this far and no further: the conditions were altered. What she takes that face to be cannot be read.

Sediment, read in three things

What dealings sediment into, upstream reads in three things: how one effect changes the conditions for what can next be said, heard and revised; how it is in turn changed by the other side's later action; how the sediments on the two sides meet.7

Lu Miao and Fang Xu are read by these three. The first: Fang Xu praised several times and frowned once, and from then on Lu Miao looks at his face first when she finishes a problem. The second: suppose Fang Xu has seen this looking up, and from then on, while she is doing a problem, he makes no sound and turns his face to the window, and speaks only when she has finished testing it herself. Her looking up has altered how he is present from then on. The third: she waits for his face, and he does not give this face; what has sedimented on the two sides meets. What follows after that depends on the dealings between the two that come next: she may go and test it herself, or she may wait longer; neither is vouched for by Fang Xu's turning his face away.

§2 Hanging It on "What Kind of Person You Are"

TS20　Saying an expectation out loud is a form of questioning. To hang an expectation on what kind of person the student is, for instance "a good student wouldn't do this" or "you have talent, you shouldn't be like this," is to fix for him what something means to him, and slides in the direction of colonization; whether that takes hold depends on his later understanding and action

Saying an expectation out loud is questioning

Saying an expectation out loud is a form of questioning.8 "Make up this page next week" is Fang Xu's putting his expectation in front of Lu Miao; she can say she cannot finish it, and she can ask why this page. "If this page isn't done, what comes after won't connect" speaks of the conditions of the matter; "you didn't do it, and I'm a little anxious" speaks of Fang Xu's own situation. These are all still saying an expectation out loud.

Hanging on: by function, not by topic

To attach a qualifying condition to an expectation, hanging it as a criterion on who the other is or what he cares about, is to fix for the other what something means to him, and slides in the direction of colonization.8

What counts as a qualifying condition upstream draws by function, not by topic: whether it fixes for the other in advance what something means to him, what his conduct counts as, who he is.9 Speaking of what kind of person she is is not, in itself, hanging something on; the topic is only a pointer.

"You have talent. You shouldn't be like this." There are two things in this sentence. One is Fang Xu's expectation: the problems are to be done. The other is what is hung on: not having done the problems has been fixed in advance as "something a person with talent ought not to have." What not having done them means to Lu Miao, whether she was too tired this week, whether she could not understand those problems, or whether she no longer wants to do them, is a matter at her end; this sentence fixed it for her. "A good student wouldn't do this" has the same shape.

A neighboring sentence is not of this kind: "It looks to me as if you didn't put much into it this week." This is Fang Xu saying how he sees her conduct; it is a fallible reading, she can say it is not so, and what she says still counts. Handing over a fallible reading and asking the other to respond is not fixing it for him in advance.9

Where her denial lands

Sliding is a direction, not an accomplished fact. Whether it takes hold depends on her later understanding and action; whether this sentence went one step further depends on whether her denial has anywhere to land.

Lu Miao says: "I don't have talent. I couldn't understand those ones." Fang Xu says: "Which ones?" On this occasion her denial was not rewritten back into the original judgment; what Fang Xu went on to ask was which ones she could not understand. What that sentence left with her afterward is seen separately.

Suppose a different response: "See, it takes someone with talent to make excuses like that." She says "no," and the "no" too is rewritten into the same meaning. Where the qualifying condition has become a position, such that no response of the other party can be registered as a refutation, upstream has a further reading at the layer of value.10 That is the state of a position, not a matter of one sentence; this series reads only that this sentence heads there.

When a third person reads this stretch, upstream says he speaks only of direction, position and signature. Signature is a reading at the layer of value, and this series does not read it; what can be said here is the first two: that sentence hung on a condition; and whether, afterward, her saying "no" has anywhere to land. What cannot be said is: whether she really has talent, whether she really cares.

A change that was not said out loud

The next Wednesday Fang Xu did not bring up what he had said the last time. Lu Miao again had one problem not done, and what he asked was: "Which step did you get stuck on?" After that he never said "you have talent" again.

Turning back is owning up to that occasion and making some change in the teaching and dealings that follow (Paper 1). What can be seen from outside is the second half: the problems are again not done, and this time what he asks about is the problem; he does not again hang it on what kind of person she is. The first half, whether he owned up to that occasion, he did not say; confirmation lies at his own end.

That half-sentence was not taken back. The direction is read by how the words were used at the time; the other's later rebuttal, or the speaker's changing what he says afterward, does not wipe out the direction that sentence already had,9 and the same holds where the change is made only in what he does. "You have talent, you shouldn't be like this" was said; afterward it was not said again, and it was not taken back: both are entered as they are.

Two sentences of praise

"You're so clever" and "you found this step by testing it yourself" are both praise. The topic of the first is what kind of person she is; the topic of the second is a thing she did. On the reading above, the topic does not settle the direction.

"You're so clever," said after she had tested something and found a mistake, can be simply a sound of gladness; it did not use "clever" to fix what any one thing counts as. Suppose that afterward, whenever she makes a mistake, Fang Xu says "you're so clever, how could you get this wrong": "clever" has been made into a criterion, and making a mistake has been fixed in advance as something a clever person ought not to have. Only then is something hung on.

"You found this step by testing it yourself" is not vouched for by the form of the words either. Which way that occasion heads is read by what it actually opened or sealed at the time;11 said at a time when she looks up after every step and waits for this sentence, it is another matter. How it sediments afterward is read separately, and is not used to judge the occasion backward. It too can grow into something else: the teacher is pleased only when everything has been found by testing it herself.

Between saying an expectation out loud and hanging a condition on it there is only half a sentence. Upstream has said that within a relation, managing not to colonize is hard; and it makes clear that it writes "hard," not "cannot be done."12

§3 Compliance Does Not Stand as Proof of Avowal

TS21　Compliance does not stand as proof of avowal: the student's saying "all right," doing as told, and doing it well are all only clues. Where the teacher's approval has become the measure of the student's own judgment, this is read by borrowing "superego internalization" from Learning III; from outside only clues are seen, and confirmation lies at his end

Clues

The student's saying "all right," doing as told, and doing it well are all only clues. Outward compliance cannot vouch for avowal: under the giving side's position, the other's "yes" is only a clue.13 Lu Miao comes every Wednesday, does all the problems, and gets them right: what can be read is these words and deeds. Whether she has avowed mathematics cannot be read.

"All right" can be a promise. Fang Xu says "make up this page next week," and Lu Miao says "all right": she has promised to do this page. A promise is read as commitment, which is another book;14 it does not stand as proof that she has avowed this matter as her own.

Approval become the measure

Lu Miao finishes a problem and looks first at Fang Xu's face. The line was right; he frowned, and she rubbed it out.

Two things can be seen: no test was added on the paper; and between his frown and her rubbing out there was no other movement. Whether she tested it in her head cannot be seen from outside.

The Learning series reads, at the layer of purpose, a form called superego internalization: an outside authority is internalized as one's own voice; in form it is "I should," and its source runs from outside in.15 The original speaks of a person's direction; it is borrowed here to read the measure of judgment: the teacher's approval has become the ground on which the student herself judges right and wrong.

From outside only clues are seen. Lu Miao's rubbing out that line may be that she took his expression as her ground; it may also be that she herself saw some other fault, or that her hand was simply quick. Confirmation lies at her end. What the teacher's end can read is that his own frown actually entered the conditions of her doing the problem; this entry is made in Fang Xu's relation.

Once this entry is made, the teacher's end has a step of its own. Suppose Fang Xu says: "I frowned just now because I hadn't looked properly. Did you test that line?" Lu Miao says: "No." Only at this point can "not tested" be read: she said it herself. Fang Xu says: "Then test it once, and whatever the test gives, that's what it is." On this occasion the ground for judging right and wrong has been put back on the test. Whether it takes hold still depends on what she does afterward: next time, whether she tests first, or looks at his face first.

§4 The Two Senses of Dependency

TS22　The student's depending on the teacher to teach is the kind of dependency that is there in any case, and carries no direction of colonization. The kind that was made, and that has locked the path to saying no, for instance "without me you'll never learn it," or not letting him go to anyone else, carries the direction of colonization. The direction is to make him no longer held by this one teacher; this is only a direction, and is not used as a measure

The kind that is there in any case

Lu Miao depends on Fang Xu to teach her. This is the kind of dependency that is there in any case: people depend on each other in any case, a child on care, an apprentice on a master.16 This kind carries no direction of colonization. That she cannot do without Fang Xu's explaining does not show that Fang Xu is occupying anything.

The kind that was made

The kind that was made, and that has locked the path to saying no, carries the direction of colonization.17

"Without me, nobody would be willing to teach you like this." If this is only a sentence, there is no judgment on the sentence alone. What is read is what it is joined to: whether the teacher has kept that child from going to hear others play, whether she stopped the lessons when the child spoke of wanting to change teachers, whether "only I am willing to teach you" has been taught in over and over. Upstream has read a master who taught hand over hand and, while teaching, taught in along with it the sentence "without me, you're nothing."18 Whether the dependency was made, and whether the path to saying no has been locked, both have to be accounted for.

A direction, not a measure

The direction is to make the student no longer held by this one teacher. This is only a direction, and is not used as a measure:19 "she still cannot do without me" is not used to judge that a teacher taught badly, nor "she no longer needs me" to judge that he taught well. Autonomy need not end in no longer needing support.20

§5 Using One Student as the Measure of Another

TS23　Where one student is used as the measure of another, for instance "look at so-and-so," what is read first is how the sentence lands with the student who hears it. If it also actually alters the situation of the one used as the measure, that relation is entered separately; the line is not drawn by whether he knows, nor is the effect entered as having reached him merely because his name was mentioned. Using a piece of homework to show one way of doing something, and setting up one student as the measure of another student's whole person, are read apart

First, the one who hears the sentence

"Look at A-Shu's." This sentence is said to Xiaoman and Dongsheng. What is read first is how it lands with those who hear it: one entry in Xiaoman's relation, one in Dongsheng's. What it is that lands is read on that occasion. Master Zheng picking up A-Shu's tenon and showing them how the shoulders are to sit flush is using a piece of work to show one way of doing something. Master Zheng saying "why can't you two be like A-Shu" is setting one person up as the measure of two others. The two are read apart.

The one used as the measure

A-Shu's relation is entered separately, and on a condition: if this sentence also actually alters A-Shu's situation. Collision means that one party's act actually enters the other party's conditions of occurrence, whether or not the parties know.21 So the line for this entry is not drawn by whether A-Shu knows; nor is the effect entered as having reached him merely because his name was mentioned.

In this example the entry holds. From then on, whenever A-Shu makes a mistake he is told "how can even you do this": the cost of his making a mistake has changed because he was set up as the model. He is called on to look over the other two's work every day: his time, and his position among the three, have changed too. These are entered in the relation between Master Zheng and A-Shu.

Dealings among three or more are broken down into effects between pairs and entered on separate accounts.22 What grows from this among Xiaoman, Dongsheng and A-Shu is theirs; the act that reached them by way of Master Zheng is entered with Master Zheng.

§6 Interfacing with the Tradition

The "Record of Learning": leading without dragging. The "Record of Learning" says of how the noble man teaches: he leads and does not drag, urges on and does not press down, opens the way and does not carry it through to the end.23 Upstream has already cited this sentence;24 it is referred back to here. Where the two reflect each other: the teacher may have expectations, and may urge; what the second of these phrases divides is urging from pressing. Where they part: these phrases write a way of teaching from the teaching side; this series reads another face, what the urging, once said, sediments into at the student's end.

Locke: reasoning with children. In Some Thoughts Concerning Education Locke argues for reasoning with children: the reasons must be suited to the child's understanding, must be plain, and must be such as he can, as it were, feel and touch.25 Where the two reflect each other: that the student cannot for the present check a content does not mean that he cannot take a reason; a reason can be given to where he can reach. At the end of the same section Locke goes on to say that if no other, more particular reason is at hand, two are always available: that it will be a discredit to him, and that it will displease the grown-ups. Here the adult's displeasure is listed as a motive that can move the child's action. Saying one's own displeasure is not, in itself, crossing anything: in §2 of this paper, "you didn't do it, and I'm a little anxious" is read as saying one's own situation. Only if this displeasure goes further and becomes the final ground on which the student judges right and wrong does it connect with what §3 reads. The nearness and the parting are read in the same paragraph, and not excerpted into a single sentence that heads one way.

Material: research on teacher expectations. The study by Rosenthal and Jacobson reported that when teachers were told certain pupils "would show gains," those pupils' later test scores rose more.26 After the study was published its method was criticized; later reviews hold that effects of this kind are on the whole small, and may be larger for particular groups and under particular conditions. It is taken here only as material, not as an interface, and not as a ground: it says that expectation can enter the student's conditions by way of the teacher's words and deeds, which heads the same way as §1; this series does not take its causal conclusion, nor say on that basis that high expectation is good or bad.

Historical material: Carnegie on his teacher. Andrew Carnegie writes in his autobiography of his only schoolmaster, Mr. Martin. Martin showed him particular favor; the other boys gave him a nickname for it, "Martin's pet," and sometimes called it after him in the street. He writes that he did not then fully know what the word meant, only that it seemed to him a term of the utmost opprobrium; and he knows that it was this nickname that kept him from responding as freely as he otherwise would have to this teacher. He writes, too, that he owes this teacher a debt of gratitude, and regrets that before the teacher died he never had the opportunity to do more than acknowledge it.27 Only how the adult Carnegie writes is read here. A teacher's regarding one student differently came back, by way of the other students, upon this student: what was altered was his situation among his schoolfellows, and also how he responded to the teacher from then on. This connects with §5: the one who is specially treated has had his situation actually altered, and there is a separate entry in that relation. It also connects with §1: what grows out of the back-and-forth turns back and changes the conditions of the next exchange. "A debt of gratitude" is his own phrase, from the student's mouth, and is an acknowledgment at his end. This series does not on that basis certify which direction Martin was headed in.

§7 Drawing Together, and Downstream

This paper sets up five propositions. Taken together they say: expectation between teacher and student grows out of the dealings; the teacher's expectation is a construct at his own end, does not turn into the student's duty, and does not certify avowal, yet it can enter the student's conditions by way of praise and frowns. Saying an expectation out loud is questioning; hanging it on what kind of person the student is fixes for him what something means to him, and slides in the direction of colonization; whether something is hung on is drawn by function, not by topic; whether the student's denial was rewritten back into the original judgment, and what that sentence left with him afterward, are read apart; the teacher's no longer speaking that way afterward is a change that can be read from what he does, and does not cancel what went before. Compliance is only a clue, an "all right" can be a promise, and neither stands as proof of avowal; where the teacher's approval has become the measure of the student's judgment, it is read by borrowing superego internalization, and confirmation lies at his end. Depending on the teacher to teach carries no direction; the kind that was made and that has locked the path to saying no carries the direction of colonization; making him no longer held by this one teacher is a direction, not a measure. Where one student is used as the measure of another, the one who hears is read first; the one used as the measure is entered separately only where his situation was actually altered.

This paper does not prove that teachers ought not to have expectations, nor that praise is harmful, nor that a student who depends on a teacher has not grown, nor that any one way of praising is bound to head one way.

What the next paper reads. One Wednesday Lu Miao says: "Uncle Fang, what I'd really rather do is draw." How does Fang Xu respond to this? The thing she herself cares about has come up: can this be taught? Master Zheng has three apprentices, and one sentence of his reaches three people: what then?

摘要

这一篇读一来一回里长出来的东西。师生之间的期待从来往里长出来：老师夸过什么、对什么皱眉，学生哪一次做对、哪一次没做到，都成为材料。老师的期待是他这一端留下的构，不落成学生的义务，也不认证学生认领了什么。说出期待是追问；把期待挂到学生是什么样的人上，是替他定下了一件事对他意味着什么，滑向殖民的方向。什么算挂上了，看功能，不看话题；成没成，看他此后的理解与行动，也看他的否认有没有地方落地。

顺从不代证认领：学生说「好」、照做、做得好，都只是线索。老师的赞许成了他自己判断的尺子的，借学习系列的「超我内化」读；从外面只见线索，确认在他那一端。学生依赖老师教，是本来就有的那一种依赖，不带殖民这个方向；被造出来、又锁住了说不之路的那一种，带殖民这个方向。方向是让他不再受制于这一位老师；这只是方向，不作尺子。拿一个学生作另一个学生的尺子，先读这句话怎样落到听的那一个那里；若也实际改动了被拿来作尺子的那一个的处境，那一段另记。

这一篇与《礼记·学记》、洛克《教育漫话》对接，教师期待的研究只作材料。它不证明老师不该有期待，不证明夸奖有害，不证明依赖老师的学生没有长成，也不证明哪一种夸法一定朝哪一向。

第一节 期待从来往里长出来

材料

期待从反复的来往里长出来：回应与不回应、按时与不按时，都成为材料。2 它不经过谁的答应。方叙没有说过「你做完了要等我点头」，陆苗也没有答应过；头一年她做完就翻页，后来先看他的脸，中间隔着的是几回夸、一回皱眉。从时长与习惯里，推不出谁认下了什么。3

两头都有材料在积。方叙这一端，他后来说过一句：「我以为你会自己验的。」这是他的期待，由他自己说了出来。陆苗那一端，读得出的是言行：她后来仍会问，做完题也开始先看他的脸。她把这些来往接成了什么样的期待，不替她写定。

对方的回应是材料，不是结算。4 陆苗做对了一道，方叙点了头：这一下进了她下一回做题的条件，不结清什么。方叙答过她十回问，不因此欠下第十一回；陆苗连着做对了十回，也不因此欠下第十一回。

老师这一端的构

老师的期待，是他这一端留下的构。它不认证学生认领了什么：方叙期待她喜欢数学，不等于她认下了数学。它也不落成学生的义务：方叙期待陆苗把题做完，陆苗不因此欠下这一页题。1

期待离开「只是期待」这一格，上游写了两条出口：被期待的人在自己的法里认下；或成为双方明确安排、并由关系权力维持的一项角色要求。仅有期待，不倒推这两种里的任何一种成立。5 陆苗自己认下了「这一页我要做完」，是前一条。两条都不由方叙的期待自己生出来。

学校里老师布置的作业，不在这一条里读：布置的权限是构给的，来源与效力不判；老师怎样用它，照第一篇读。

期待改变条件

期待留在老师这一端，却不停在这一端。它可以经由期待者的言行，进入对方的发生条件；被期待的人不因此认领了什么。6 方叙的夸与皱眉，是言行。陆苗做完题先看他的脸：她此后做题的条件里，多了一样。这一处读到这里为止：条件改动了。她把这张脸当作什么，读不出。

沉积，读三件

来往沉积成什么，上游读三件事：一次作用怎样改变下一次能说、能听、能修订的条件；它又怎样被另一端此后的行动反过来改变；两边的沉积怎样碰在一起。7

照这三件读陆苗与方叙。头一件：方叙夸过几回，皱过一回眉，陆苗此后做完题先看他的脸。第二件：设方叙看见了她这一抬头，此后她做题的时候，他先不出声，脸转向窗外，等她自己验完了再开口。她那一抬头，改动了他此后怎样在场。第三件：她等他的脸，他不给这张脸；两边沉积下来的东西碰在了一起。再往后怎样，看两个人接下来的来往：她可以自己去验，也可以等得更久；哪一样，都不由方叙转过脸去这一下担保。

第二节 挂到「你是什么样的人」上

TS20　说出期待是追问。把期待挂到学生是什么样的人上，例如「好学生不会这样」「你是有天分的，不该这样」，是替他定下了一件事对他意味着什么，滑向殖民的方向；成没成，看他此后的理解与行动

说出期待是追问

说出期待，是一种追问。8 「下星期把这一页补上」，是方叙把他的期待摆到了陆苗面前；她可以说做不完，可以问为什么是这一页。「这一页不做，后面的接不上」，说的是事情的条件；「你没做，我有些着急」，说的是方叙自己的处境。这几句都还是说出期待。

挂上去：看功能，不看话题

给期待挂上限定的条件，把它挂到对方是谁、在乎什么上作判准，是替对方定下了一件事对他意味着什么，滑向殖民的方向。8

什么算挂上了限定的条件，上游看功能，不看话题：有没有替对方预先定下一件事对他意味着什么，他的作为算什么，他是谁。9 说到了她是什么样的人，不就是挂上了；话题只作提示。

「你是有天分的，不该这样。」这一句里有两样。一样是方叙的期待：题要做。另一样是挂上去的：没有做题这件事，被预先定成了「有天分的人不该有的事」。没做题对陆苗意味着什么，是这一周太累，是那几道题她看不懂，还是她不想做了，是她那一端的事；这一句替她定了。「好学生不会这样」同形。

相邻的一句不是这一种：「我看你这一周没怎么用心。」这是方叙说出他怎么看她的作为，是一个可错的读法，她可以说不是，她的说法仍算数。交出一个可错的读法请对方回应，不是预先替他定下。9

她的否认落在哪里

滑向是方向，不是落成。成没成，看她此后的理解与行动；这一句有没有再往下走一步，看她的否认有没有地方落地。

陆苗说：「我不是有天分。那几道我没看懂。」方叙说：「哪几道？」这一回，她的否认没有被改写回原来的判断；方叙接着问的，是哪几道没看懂。那一句话此后在她那里留下什么，另看。

设另一种回法：「你看，有天分的人才这样找借口。」她说「不是」，「不是」也被改写成了同一个意思。限定的条件成了位置，使对方的任何回应都不能作为反驳被登记，上游在价值一层另有一读。10 那是位置的状态，不是一句话的事；这个系列只读到这一句朝那里去。

旁人读这一段，上游说只说方向、位置与签名。签名是价值一层的读法，这个系列不读；这里能说的是前两样：那一句挂上了条件；此后她说「不是」有没有地方落地。不能说的是：她其实有没有天分，她其实在不在乎。

没有说出口的改

下一个星期三，方叙没有提上回那一句。陆苗又有一道没做，他问的是：「哪一步卡住了？」此后他没有再说过「有天分」。

回头是认下那一次，并在此后的教与来往里有所改（第一篇）。从外面看得见的，是后一半：同样是题没做，这一回他问的是题，没有再把它挂到她是什么样的人上。前一半，他认没认下那一次，他没有说；确认在他自己那一端。

那半句没有被收回。方向按当时的用法读；对方后来反驳，或说的人随后改口，都不抹去当初那一句已有的方向，9 只在做法上改的也一样。「有天分的，不该这样」说出去了，此后没有再说，也没有收回：两件都照记。

两句夸

「你真聪明」与「这一步是你自己验出来的」，都是夸。前一句的话题是她是什么样的人，后一句的话题是她做了的一件事。照上面的读法，话题不定方向。

「你真聪明」说在她验出一处错以后，可以只是一声高兴；它没有拿「聪明」去定哪一件事算什么。设此后她一出错，方叙就说「你这么聪明，怎么会错」：「聪明」被拿来作了判准，出错被预先定成了聪明人不该有的事。这时候才是挂上了。

「这一步是你自己验出来的」，也不由话的样式担保。那一次朝哪一向，照当时实际打开或封住了什么读；11 说在她每做一步都抬头等这一句的时候，是另一回事。后来怎样沉积，另读，不拿来倒判当时。它也可以长成另一样东西：处处自己验出来，老师才喜欢。

从说出期待到挂上一个条件，只差半句话。上游说过，在一段关系里做到不殖民是难的；也说明只写难，不写做不到。12

线索

学生说「好」、照做、做得好，都只是线索。表面的顺从不能代证认领：在给的一方的位置之下，对方说「好」只是一条线索。13 陆苗每个星期三都来，题都做，做得对：读得出的是这些言行。她认没认下数学，读不出。

「好」可以是一个答应。方叙说「下星期把这一页补上」，陆苗说「好」：她答应了做这一页。答应照承诺读，那是另一本账；14 它不代证她认下了这件事是她自己的。

赞许成了尺子

陆苗做完题，先看方叙的脸。那一行是对的，他皱了眉，她就擦掉了。

看得见的有两样：纸上没有添验算；他皱眉与她擦掉之间，没有别的动作。她心里验没验过，从外面看不见。

学习系列在目的那一层读过一种形态，叫超我内化：外面的权威内化成了自己的声音，形式上是「我应该」，来源是从外到内。15 原处说的是一个人的方向；这里借它读判断的尺子：老师的赞许，成了学生自己判断对错的根据。

从外面只见线索。陆苗擦掉那一行，可以是她把他的脸色当了根据；也可以是她自己看出了别的毛病，或者只是手快。确认在她那一端。老师这一端读得出的，是自己的皱眉实际进入了她做题的条件；这一笔记在方叙这一段。

这一笔记下以后，老师这一端有他自己的一步。设方叙说：「我刚才皱眉，是我没看清。那一行你验过没有？」陆苗说：「没有。」到这里，「没验」才读得出：是她自己说的。方叙说：「那你验一遍，验出来是什么，就是什么。」这一回，判对错的根据被放回到验上。它成没成，仍看她此后：下一回她是先验，还是先看脸。

第四节 依赖的两义

TS22　学生依赖老师教，是本来就有的那一种依赖，不带殖民这个方向。被造出来、又锁住了说不之路的那一种，例如「离了我你学不成」、不让他去找别人，带殖民这个方向。方向是让他不再受制于这一位老师；这只是方向，不作尺子

本来就有的那一种

陆苗依赖方叙教。这是本来就有的那一种依赖：人本来就互相依赖，孩子依赖照顾，学徒依赖师傅。16 这一种不带殖民这个方向。她离不开方叙讲题，不说明方叙在占着什么。

被造出来的那一种

被造出来、又锁住了说不之路的那一种，带殖民这个方向。17

「离了我，没人肯这样教你。」这一句若只是一句话，只凭它不判。读的是它连着什么：教琴的老师有没有不让那个孩子去听别人弹，有没有在她提起想换一位老师的时候把课停掉，有没有把「只有我肯教你」一遍一遍教进去。上游读过一位师傅：他手把手地教，一边教，一边把「离了我你什么也不是」这一句一起教了进去。18 依赖是不是被造出来的，说不的路有没有被锁住，两样都要交代。

方向，不作尺子

方向是让学生不再受制于这一位老师。这只是方向，不作尺子：19 不拿「她还离不开我」判一位老师教坏了，也不拿「她已经不需要我」判他教成了。自主不必以不再需要支持收场。20

第五节 拿一个学生作另一个的尺子

TS23　拿一个学生作另一个学生的尺子，例如「你看人家」，先读这句话怎样落到听这句话的那一个学生那里。若也实际改动了被拿来作尺子的那一个的处境，那一段另记；不以他知不知道为界，也不只凭名字被提到，就记成作用已经到了他那里。用一份作业示范一种做法，与把一个学生立成另一个学生整个人的尺度，分开读

先读听这句话的那一个

「你们看阿树的。」这句话是对小满和冬生说的。先读它怎样落到听的人那里：小满那一段记一笔，冬生那一段记一笔。落下去的是什么，照那一次读。郑师傅拿起阿树的榫头，指给他们看肩要怎样齐，是用一件活示范一种做法。郑师傅说「你们怎么就不能像阿树」，是把一个人立成了另外两个人的尺度。两样分开读。

被拿来作尺子的那一个

阿树那一段，另记，而且有条件：这句话若也实际改动了阿树的处境。碰撞说的是一方的行动实际进入另一方的发生条件，不论双方知不知情。21 所以这一笔不以阿树知不知道为界；也不只凭他的名字被提到，就记成作用已经到了他那里。

在这个例子里，这一笔成立。此后阿树一出错，就被说「怎么连你也这样」：他出错的代价，因为被立成了榜样而变了。他被叫去每天看另外两个人的活：他的时间与他在三个人里的位置，也变了。这些记在郑师傅与阿树那一段。

三人以上的来往，拆成两两之间的作用分别记账。22 小满、冬生与阿树之间由此长出来的东西，是他们之间的；经过郑师傅到了他们那里的那一下，记在郑师傅这里。

第六节 与传统对接

《礼记·学记》：道而弗牵。 《学记》说君子教人：「道而弗牵，强而弗抑，开而弗达。」23 引着走，不牵着走；督促，不压着；把路打开，不替他说尽。上游已经引过这一句，24 这里回指。相照的一处：老师可以有期待，可以督促；「强而弗抑」分的，是督促与压着。分开的一处：这几句从教的一方写教法；这个系列另读一面，督促说出去以后，在学生那里沉积成了什么。

洛克：向孩子说理。 洛克在《教育漫话》里主张向孩子说理：理由要贴着孩子的理解，要浅近，要让他像摸得到一样。25 相照的一处：学生当下核对不了一项内容，不等于接不住理由；理由可以给到他够得着的地方。同一节的末尾，洛克又说：若没有别的更具体的理由，还有两样随时用得上，一是这样做会使他丢脸，一是会使大人不高兴。这里是把大人的不悦列作可以影响孩子行动的动机。说出自己的不悦，不就是越了什么：这一篇第二节里，「你没做，我有些着急」读作说出自己的处境。若这一份不悦进一步成了学生判断对错的最后根据，才接到第三节读的那件事。相近与分开，放在同一段里读，不摘成一句同向的话。

材料：教师期待的研究。 罗森塔尔与雅各布森的研究报告过：告诉老师某些学生「将有进步」，这些学生后来的测验分数升得更多。26 这项研究发表以后，方法受到批评；后来的综述认为这类效应总体较小，在特定的群体与条件下可能较大。这里只作材料，不作对接，也不作根据：它说的是期待可以经由老师的言行进入学生的条件，与第一节同向；这个系列不取它的因果结论，也不据此说期待高是好是坏。

史料：卡内基写他的老师。 安德鲁·卡内基在自传里写他唯一的一位学校老师，马丁先生。马丁待他格外照顾；别的男孩因此给他起了一个外号，叫他「马丁的宠儿」，有时在街上冲着他喊。他写，那时候他不全懂这个词，只觉得是极难听的话；他知道，是这个外号使他没能像本来会的那样，放开去回应这位老师。他又写，他欠这位老师一份感激，遗憾的是老师去世以前，他除了表达过这份感激，没有机会再做更多。27 这里只读成年的卡内基怎样写。老师对一个学生另眼相看，经过别的学生，回到了这个学生身上：改动的是他在同学里的处境，也是他此后怎样回应老师。这与第五节相接：被特别对待的那一个，处境实际改动了，那一段另有一笔。它也与第一节相接：一来一回里长出来的东西，回过头改了下一次来回的条件。「欠一份感激」是他自己的话，出自学生之口，是他那一端的认。这个系列不据此认证马丁在哪个方向上。

第七节 收束与下游

这一篇立了五条。合起来说的是：师生之间的期待从来往里长出来；老师的期待是他这一端的构，不落成学生的义务，不认证认领，却可以经由夸与皱眉进入学生的条件。说出期待是追问；把期待挂到学生是什么样的人上，是替他定下一件事对他意味着什么，滑向殖民的方向；挂没挂上看功能，不看话题；学生的否认有没有被改写回原来的判断，与那句话此后在他那里留下什么，分开读；老师此后不再那样说，是从做法上读得出的改，不倒销当初。顺从只是线索，一声「好」可以是答应，不代证认领；老师的赞许成了他判断的尺子的，借超我内化读，确认在他那一端。依赖老师教不带方向；被造出来又锁住说不之路的那一种，带殖民这个方向；让他不再受制于这一位老师，是方向，不作尺子。拿一个学生作另一个的尺子，先读听的那一个；被拿来作尺子的那一个，处境实际改动了才另记。

这一篇不证明老师不该有期待，不证明夸奖有害，不证明依赖老师的学生没有长成，也不证明哪一种夸法一定朝哪一向。

下一篇读的。 有一个星期三，陆苗说：「方伯伯，我其实更想画画。」这一句，方叙怎样回？她自己在意的东西冒出来了，这一件教不教得了？郑师傅有三个徒弟，他一句话说出去，到的是三个人：这时候又怎样？

下游。 学生自己的方向、老师这一端给得了的空间、一位老师与许多学生，第五篇读。学生的不得不与老师的法相遇，第六篇读。教过的不记作偿还，第七篇读。
