What a University Is Not
大学不是什么

Han Qin (秦汉) · Independent Researcher · 2026
DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.24293733 · Full PDF on Zenodo · CC BY 4.0
Abstract

More than a hundred and seventy years ago, Newman said that knowledge is capable of being its own end. The main line of this series looks like the opposite: the university fosters judgment; knowledge is the material of judgment, not the end of the university. This paper first makes clear what that sentence says and what it does not. It clears away seven "nots": the university is not a place where knowledge is delivered, not a place where diplomas are issued, not the walls of disciplines, not only students and professors, neither a utopia nor a gate for sorting people; it does not teach people how to judge well; and it is not its own end. It marks out which university is under discussion: a university whose consensus includes "a human being is an end"; reality remains mixed, and everyone has moments of treating people as means; consensus is recognized by where it lands, not by reading minds, and it is not a condition of admission. It takes Action Theory's definition of judgment, and takes as its root Kant's point that judgment cannot be taught by rules, only practiced, without taking over the layer in which he calls judgment a natural gift. It says what the series criticizes: treating a means as an end point, which lands in three places, knowledge, measures and divisions; it distinguishes two strengths and gives criteria that can be checked. It writes teaching, nurturing and leading after the threefold of Education as Subject-Condition, and revises the line that paper left for the university: the university teaches knowledge and craft as the material of judgment; for cultivation it gives the conditions for practicing judgment; leading can happen only by personal demonstration. Cultivation has no end point. Finally it settles the accounts received from upstream, names the two items handed on, and lists the seven papers. This paper states no numbered propositions.

Writing Declaration: This paper was co-drafted with Claude (Anthropic). All intellectual decisions, framework design, and final editorial judgments were made by the author.

Introduction

In 1852 Newman lectured in Dublin on the idea of a university, and his fifth discourse was titled "Knowledge Its Own End." He said: "Knowledge is capable of being its own end." (Discourse V, §2) The main line of this series looks like the very opposite.

Read closely, the side on which the two are near comes out first. The knowledge Newman speaks of is not a heap of things remembered. It "is an acquired illumination, it is a habit, a personal possession, and an inward endowment" (§6). Information piled up does not yet deserve the name of science or philosophy; only when reason has permeated it is it knowledge in his sense. What he opposed was precisely taking memorizing for learning. On this point the series walks the same road as he does.

Where they truly part is one place, and that place has two faces. One face is where knowledge leads. Newman's liberal knowledge is "independent of sequel"; borrowing Aristotle's remark on possessions, he says that of such knowledge "nothing accrues of consequence beyond the using" (§4): it is completed in contemplation. The judgment this series speaks of lands in doing and not doing: a matter is set before a person, and he marks off what he does not do, marks off what he cannot not do, and lets the rest stay. The other face is what the university forms. Newman says the university forms a habit of mind: "A habit of mind is formed which lasts through life" (§1). This series' judgment does not presuppose a formed character: everyone, faced with a matter, is judging. §4 returns to both faces.

Two further things need saying first. Neither is a disagreement with Newman.

First, when this series says that knowledge is not the end of the university, it does not say that knowledge cannot be anyone's end. A person may seek knowledge only because he wants to know, without first accounting for some other use. A university is a kind of construct, and a construct is a means; the standing of an end in itself belongs to persons, not to constructs. The main line says two things: the delivery of knowledge is not where the university's accountability ends; and the university itself is not an end either.

Second, what this series criticizes is registering the coverage, delivery or reproduction of knowledge as completion. That is not Newman's position. He himself kept intellectual formation apart from any guarantee of virtue: "Knowledge is one thing, virtue is another" (§9).

Now to this framework itself. Education as Subject-Condition drew up a timeline of education; in the column for ages eighteen to twenty-two it wrote "university," with the form "Teaching + leading" and the content "History of philosophy + specialized knowledge." The same paper says that actual universities stretch to twenty-two, even to twenty-six, and that the extra years are almost all spent repeating the transmission of knowledge. This series begins by revising that line.

Knowledge is now at hand everywhere; what is scarce is taking it up to judge matters for which rules give no answer. Nor did nurturing stop at fourteen. Education places the window of nurturing at ages three to fourteen; this series reads it thus: three to fourteen is the window in which nurturing opens, not its end point; cultivation has no end point. (Nurturing and cultivation render one Chinese word, 涵育; §5 explains its two uses.) In the university, cultivation lies between an advisor or a teacher and one student, and the university gives conditions for it, namely the conditions for practicing judgment. So the university's line is rewritten: teaching, with knowledge and craft as material; cultivation, giving the conditions for practicing judgment; leading, possible only by personal demonstration. This is a revision the framework makes to itself. It is registered in this series, and the text of Education is not revised back.

The main line of this series is:

The university fosters judgment: knowledge is the material of judgment, not the end of the university.

How to read. This paper states no numbered propositions; it only clears the ground and gives an account. §1 says what a university is not; §2, which university is under discussion; §3, judgment; §4, what the series criticizes; §5, teaching, nurturing and leading; §6, knowledge and not-knowing; §7, the three layers and the standing of material; §8 settles the accounts received from upstream and names the two items handed on; §9 lists the seven papers. Oughts are established in the papers that follow; this paper proves none.

§1 A Few "Nots"

The university is not a place where knowledge is delivered. Knowledge enters the university so that judgment has material. This does not mean teaching less knowledge: judgment without knowledge is empty. Paper 1 writes how teaching lets students go through application, choice, explanation and revision for themselves.

The university is not a place where diplomas are issued. A degree states what work a person has done, and on what materials and by what standards the university made what evaluation; it also states how far its proof reaches. When others take a degree as a condition of hiring or admission, and it becomes a door those concerned cannot get around, it is a chokepoint: it calls for a separate account, and it can be questioned. One view holds that the value of a degree lies in screening, not in what is learned.1 Even if a degree is used as a screening signal, the university is not thereby relieved of giving an account: what it verified, what it evaluated, what it recorded. Paper 5 writes this.

The university is not the walls of disciplines. Disciplines live in the university; the discipline remains, but is no longer a wall. It is the university itself that divides a university into departments. Paper 4 writes this.

The university is not only students and professors. It also has research, staff, governance and money. Staff sit in positions with knowledge and responsibilities of their own; they are not a backdrop between students and professors. Papers 2, 3, 6 and 7 write this.

The university is neither a utopia nor a gate for sorting people. The university this series discusses has a consensus that includes "a human being is an end," and reality remains mixed. It does not admit people by their direction; the scope of discussion is not a classification of qualifications. §2 writes this.

The university does not teach people how to judge well. Judgment cannot be taught by rules; it can only be practiced. What the university gives is a place to practice and people who question students' judgments, not a set of standards for good judgment. §3 and Paper 1 write this.

The university is not its own end. "Any institution, including the most elegant constitution, is a means, not an end." (Institution Theory, Prequel, quoting How Is Institution Possible) A university may have ends written into its charter, and goals that people give it; this series says two things: the delivery of knowledge is not its end point, and the university itself is not an end. Paper 7 writes this.

§2 Which University Is Under Discussion

The university of this series is an institution that grants degrees, takes teaching as its proper work, and in most cases also does research; colleges that grant only bachelor's degrees are included. Training inside companies is not included, nor are primary and secondary schools (§8).

What this series writes about is a university whose consensus includes "a human being is an end"; it asks what the institutions of such a university look like. This is a demarcation of the object of discussion, not a thought experiment: it does not assume that everyone has already got there. Nor is it a condition of admission: the university does not check who is headed in which direction.

Consensus is recognized by where it lands, not by reading minds. Where a university's consensus lies is seen in where it lands: written into its charter and academic regulations; available to be invoked when decisions are made, and to be used in questioning a decision; carried by each position as its accountability. The university does not require inner assent, and does not admit, appoint, retain or dismiss people by whether they assent. The Prequel of the Theory of the Company quotes the sentence: "A construct may require a position to do something; it may not require a person to think something."

Marking out the scope of discussion is not sorting people into qualified and unqualified. The lines this series draws for the university hold, whole, for every person in the university.

This series begins from a student like this: he was cultivated in high school, understands at least that a human being is an end, and is himself willing to grow and willing to be led. This is the starting point of the writing, not a finished judgment on every student. Students who have not walked that stretch of road are also within the conditions the university gives for cultivation. Practices such as resorting to any means for the sake of grades are not taken as the starting point of discussion; Paper 2 handles them by event, and they do not become an identity of the person.

"An institution does not presuppose that its internal subjects are all acting by their own law; its presupposition is mixed reality." (Institution Theory Z3) Everyone has moments of treating people as means, professors included. "An institution's identification of a person goes no further than the event and does not become an identity" (ibid.). Nor is direction a state to be inspected and accepted: "The Kingdom of Ends is a direction; it is not an ought, nor a state to be signed off as achieved" (Institution Theory Z39).

At times a university headed in the direction of means is used for contrast. Such a contrast judges a practice; it does not characterize a university.

Leaving the direction does not mean being free of constraint. A university that has changed direction leaves the scope of this series; the lines drawn for every construct still hold: it does not cross the line, it can be questioned, and people can leave it. The Prequel of the Theory of the Company writes the same of companies.

§3 Judgment

Judgment in this series follows Action Theory's definition: "Judgment is not a special kind of cleverness; it is simply a person's capacity, when a matter is set before him, to mark off what he does not do, to mark off what he cannot not do, and to let the rest stay." (Action Theory, Paper 1, §5) The same section says: "Rules can come in, facts can come in, reasons and feelings can all come in; they are all material for his judgment. But which rule to apply, which one to take, and, once the judgment is made, taking it up and carrying it: only he can do that." So "what judgment gives is not being right." What it gives is this: this time it is he himself who judges; if he judged wrong, he can go back to check, and go back to revise.

The word "material" in the main line comes from here. Knowledge, skills and craft all come in as material.

Action Theory separates judgment from what Aristotle calls practical wisdom: the latter depends on ethical virtue, looks to the good life as a whole, and is not yet possessed by the young; "this series' judgment does not presuppose a character that has been formed" (Action Theory, Paper 0, §3). This series follows that.

In the introduction to the transcendental power of judgment in the Critique of Pure Reason, Kant says three things, and this series takes them as its root.2 Judgment cannot be taught by rules; it can only be practiced. The capacity to use rules correctly has to belong to the student himself: rules can be taught to him, but the step of using them only he can take. Examples sharpen judgment, and they also often lead people to use rules as formulas. In the same place Kant also calls judgment a natural gift whose lack no school can make good; this series does not take over that layer. By Action Theory's definition, everyone faced with a matter is judging; this series does not prejudge who is innately lacking in judgment, or who cannot be fostered.

How a person practices judgment by himself has already been written in Exercises and Games of Judgment, along the one path of games. This series writes the other side: the university as the party that arranges practice. It writes only the conditions of practice, not what practice is to produce, nor what good judgment is. "Fostering" in this series is read as giving conditions, not as manufacturing: by Institution Theory, a construct gives conditions and is "concerned with conditions, not with outcomes" (Institution Theory Z23).

To say that the university gives conditions is not an empty phrase. At least these can be checked: the chance to encounter materials and methods; room to form, explain and revise judgments; getting feedback; still being able to go on learning after raising an objection; evaluation that states its grounds; every student having an advisor of his own (Paper 1).

§4 Direction and Misalignment

Knowledge is the material of judgment. Material is not only the knowledge in books; it includes skills and craft. Schools grounded in a craft, such as music, clinical practice and engineering design, are all included. Judgment is already inside the practice of a craft; one need not first leave practice and add a class in reflection before it counts as practicing judgment. Training whose condition of completion is operational ability, such as a clinical procedure, a performance or a piece of design, is checked separately by its own accountability, and is not thereby judged a misalignment.

What this series criticizes is treating a means as an end point. This one target lands in three places. Knowledge is the first: treating the coverage, delivery or reproduction of knowledge as the end point of teaching. Measures are the second: credentials, metrics and scores are means for looking at a piece of work, and are treated as the work itself (Papers 3 and 5). Divisions are the third: disciplines and departments are means of dividing labor, and are treated as boundaries (Paper 4).

Within this framework, three places point the same way. Education as Subject-Condition says: "Under the institutional logic of 'everything measurable,' they do not exist," and speaks of "compressing teaching, nurturing, and leading into 'teaching' alone, so that nurturing and leading vanish from the institutional landscape." The Theory of Disciplines says that the establishment of a discipline "is a means, not an end" (Theory of Disciplines, Paper 1, S1). Institution Theory says that institutions are means (§1). Read this way, "the university no longer teaches only knowledge" is not a stance against knowledge.

What is treated as an end point is the coverage, delivery or reproduction of knowledge, not knowledge itself. A person may seek knowledge only because he wants to know; calling knowledge material does not require him to recast his seeking as the pursuit of an output called judgment. The university may set requirements of knowledge, and may evaluate; it cannot, because these requirements have been met, conclude that the conditions it gives are in place. A student reads a poem again and again only because he has not yet understood it. The university can evaluate whether his interpretation has grounds in the text; once the evaluation is done, it does not on that basis certify that his judgment or his direction has been formed.

Treating a means as an end point comes in two strengths. In the strong one, the means becomes the ultimate criterion, and people are traded off against it. The Theory of the Company has two cells: "profit can be one of a company's goals," while it is "a condition and a return, not the ultimate criterion"; and "The direction of the Kingdom of Means is the one in which profit has become the ultimate criterion." To go that far is a matter of direction. In the weak one, a means is registered as an end point: for instance, the coverage, delivery or reproduction of knowledge is registered as the completion of the teaching task, and no one goes on to ask how the student understands, applies, chooses and revises. This series calls the weak one misalignment. Once it reaches the strong one, it is no longer called misalignment: the direction has changed.

Misalignment is read in what is registered. In the rule texts of three places, degree requirements, course evaluation, and appointment and promotion, if a means is written as a sufficient condition of completion, and nowhere else are understanding, application, choice and revision asked about, that is misalignment in the text; how the rules are carried out is checked separately. In appointment and promotion, the question is whether a means has stood in for judgment of the work itself (Paper 5). What is looked at is not whether words such as "understanding" or "revision" appear in the text. Direction is read by the boundary between limit and violation (Institution Theory Z13). Forms such as heavy lecturing or counting grade points are only signals: "Looking at forms, one cannot judge whether the line was crossed." (Institution Theory, Paper 2, §6) Heavy lecturing or counting grade points does not, on its own, make a misalignment either. Both criteria are used to read particular arrangements, not to classify a whole university. A person's own ends, a project's working goals and a university's positioning are not merged into one layer either.

Education also used the word "misalignment," for the mismatch between the stages institutions cut by age and the positions people have actually reached. The two senses are related; this series uses the sense above.

The introduction said that where this series truly parts from Newman is one place with two faces. Now it can be said plainly. Newman's knowledge is completed in contemplation; this series' judgment lands in doing and not doing, and once it is made, the one who judged bears it. Newman wants the university to form a habit of mind that lasts through life; this series does not take a formed character as the precondition of judgment: what the university gives is the conditions of practice, and how the practice turns out is not in its hands. Neither face says that Newman was wrong: he wrote an ideal of liberal education; this series writes how a kind of construct gives conditions.

§5 Teaching, Nurturing, Leading

Education as Subject-Condition divides education into three things. Teaching is the transmission of knowledge and ability. Nurturing is giving space and not suppressing, so that a person chisels out his own self-consciousness and his own ends. Leading is adults themselves living out recognition of others, so that children see a direction. "Teaching can be scaled; nurturing can happen only one-on-one; leading can happen only by personal demonstration. The three cannot substitute for one another." Education also says that doing leading by way of teaching, assessing "respect others" as a rule, yields obedience, not recognition.

Teaching, nurturing and leading are all things done by the giving side. Practice is what students do themselves; it is not among the three.

Leading can happen only by personal demonstration. It is not registered as the accountability of any position, and it is not assessed. Leaving space for leading, which cannot be institutionalized, is an open question Education left; Paper 1 lays its premises, and Paper 7 answers it.

The university counts cultivation in, and cultivation has no end point. The word 涵育 has two uses in this framework, and this series writes both. The published English renders them differently: "nurturing" in Education as Subject-Condition, "cultivation" in Cultivation Theory. One use is in Education's threefold: "Nurturing cannot be designed or planned; it can only happen one-on-one"; all it needs is not to be blocked. In the university, this use simply means not blocking. The other use is in Cultivation Theory: cultivation is one person giving conditions to another; "What is given is conditions, not results" (Cultivation Theory, Prequel). It is persons who cultivate; the university gives conditions for cultivation. Cultivation Theory's definition contains no age. Action Theory too says: "When a person is truly taken as an end in a matter, cultivating him is a direction." (Action Theory, Paper 6) So cultivation does not stop with youth. The same paper also says: "The one who cultivates can also be cultivated; the two sides are not fixed as giver and receiver." Those who teach are also within the conditions the university gives for cultivation.

"Only one-on-one" says that cultivation happens within a relationship with one person at each end; it does not say that a person can have only one cultivator in a lifetime. From this the series requires that every student have an advisor of his own (Paper 1); whether that relationship becomes cultivation is not in the university's hands.

The introduction said that the line Education left for the university was "Teaching + leading." This series rewrites it: teaching, with knowledge and craft as material; cultivation, giving the conditions for practicing judgment; leading, possible only by personal demonstration. The middle item takes the Cultivation Theory sense: the university does not add a fourth thing beside the three; it gives conditions for cultivation between persons. This is a revision the framework makes to itself, and it does not overturn Education's threefold.

Education also left an open question: machines can teach, but can they nurture? This series registers the question and does not answer it. Paper 1 answers only one thing: when tools are used, the judgment still has to be completed by a person.

§6 Knowledge and Not-Knowing

Epistemology said one thing: "If there is nothing on the not-knowing side, the bridge has nowhere to go. Cognition idles. You can pile up knowing to infinity, but if the position of not-knowing is empty, all your knowing is mere information, not cognition." (Epistemology, Paper 1) The position of not-knowing is the starting condition of cognition.

Delivering knowledge as an end point can fill that position. If a course pairs every question with its answer, and an exam asks only whether things were remembered, then the student faces no matter for which rules give no answer; knowledge piles ever higher, and judgment has nowhere to begin. This series does not say that knowledge crushes judgment. What it says is this: arrangements for delivering knowledge can fill the position of not-knowing, and the university is to leave it open.

§7 Three Layers, Positions, and the Standing of Material

This series speaks in Institution Theory's three layers: the construct takes the ought, the position takes accountability, the person takes his own law. What is addressed to the university is ought. Professors, advisors, staff and students sit in positions and carry their respective accountability. How a person judges in a matter is his own affair.

This series' claims stand at the normative layer. Designs at the rule layer, such as the form of tutorials or the way of grading, are only suggestions. Rule texts and public records are evidence: what a rule says, and how far it proves, is how far the series speaks; that a rule is written does not mean it was carried out as written.

This series does not teach people how to judge. It does not say what good judgment is, nor does it set out a universal standard of good judgment. This does not mean that a particular piece of work cannot be evaluated as passing or failing: the evaluator makes a limited judgment about a piece of work, and states the grounds. Paper 5 writes this.

This series does not say that such a university is more cost-effective or more competitive; nor does it say that, because it is expensive, it ought not to exist. What it says is how a university whose consensus includes "a human being is an end" ought to give conditions.

§8 Accounts Received from Upstream, Accounts Handed On

This section writes only what was received and in which paper it is handled; it does not write ahead what will be delivered.

The Theory of Disciplines handed over several items. Standards of research evaluation, including the credit standard of mathematics: the university answers for its adoption and conversion of these standards, in Paper 3. Authorship after AI takes part in proofs: authorship records the doing and the being responsible, in Paper 3. The gates of resources: among the gates where the university divides resources, at least one entrance that does not demand conversion is left, in Paper 6. Degrees, certification and admission: this series writes only the side of a degree as a record, in Paper 5; how admission and certification are designed is handed to later dedicated papers. The Theory of Disciplines also says that the construct that writes a standard and the construct that adopts it as a gate ought to give the standard a channel through which it can be questioned, with those the door reaches and who cannot get around it inside as well (Theory of Disciplines, Paper 9, S46); how this lands on the university, as one kind of construct, is in Paper 7. On the question of how disciplines ought to be divided, this series gives no scheme of division, only conditions, in Paper 4.

Education left two open questions. Machines can teach, but can they nurture: this series registers the question and does not answer it; Paper 1 answers only that when tools are used, the judgment still has to be completed by a person. Leaving space for leading, which cannot be institutionalized: Paper 1 lays the premises, and Paper 7 answers.

Cultivation Theory handed three things to the institutional side: separating assessment from those who give conditions, a place to appeal, and a way to exit (Cultivation Theory, Paper 5). Leaving and appeal are written in Papers 1 and 2. How assessment and the giving of conditions are separated is written in Paper 5: evaluation is a power and is subject to checks; conclusions such as degrees and qualifications are reached by positions separate from the advisor. Who sets these rules, and how they are questioned, is written in Paper 7.

The Second Enlightenment asked whether a gate of resources can leave an entrance that does not first demand proof of worth (The Second Enlightenment, Papers 5 and 6). This series answers only for the university's kind of gate; the step from "can" to "ought" is this series' own undertaking, in Paper 6.

Political Economy says that when a degree becomes a door others cannot get around, it is a chokepoint (Political Economy, Paper 5, E31). How admission and certification are designed, this series does not take up, and hands to later dedicated papers; Paper 5 writes only the side of a degree as a record.

The Overview of Exercises and Games of Judgment leaves one item at its end: "Institutions organizing games, that is, doing things with play at the level of institutions, also awaits separate treatment (3.4)." This series writes the general conditions under which a construct arranges practice, not games alone; the item of institutions organizing games is still left open (Paper 7 hands it downstream).

Two items are handed on. Student-to-teacher ratios in primary and secondary schools, compulsory enrollment and public provision: this series does not write them, and hands them to a later Theory of Schools. The design of admission qualifications and of admission is handed to later dedicated papers.

§9 The Papers

Prequel What a University Is Not Clearing the ground; the scope of discussion; judgment; direction and misalignment; teaching, nurturing, leading; accounts and the list of papers
Paper 1 University Teaching How judgment is practiced; knowledge as material; every student has an advisor of his own; no substitution; the teacher's own judgment; tools; teaching assistants
Paper 2 University Students One student passing through the positions of studying, research, teaching assistance and employment; declining and changing direction; the way out of an advising relationship; the position after an event; students' voices

Paper 4 University Disciplines The discipline remains, but is no longer a wall; departmental rules; practicing judgment at the borders
Paper 5 University Evaluation Evaluation is power and is subject to checks; evaluation does not turn judgment back into rules; evaluation handed to machines; evaluation does not reward the step of treating people as means; a degree is a limited proof; the same for evaluating professors; trust and checking
Paper 6 University Funding The price falls on what is done; money carries requirements, not direction; sports; an entrance that does not demand conversion; when it can no longer be sustained
Paper 7 University Governance Bringing together the "who decides" of the first six papers; the positions that set rules can be questioned; the accountability of academic judgment; leaving space for leading; the university itself is also a construct

The paper on students is organized by person and written by position: when one person passes through several positions, which requirements and consequences may not be borrowed from one another. The paper on evaluation writes students and professors together: the mechanisms by which evaluation is adopted as a gate can be read side by side, while the accountability and consequences of the two remain separate. Governance comes last: only after the first six papers have each stated who makes a decision and who bears its consequences does Paper 7 have something to bring together.

Where This Paper Stops

What it has not done: it has stated no numbered propositions and proved no ought, since oughts are established in the papers that follow; it has judged no university; it has not written the design of admission qualifications, admission or certification; it has not written about primary and secondary schools; it has not revised the text of Education, and its revision of that paper's line for the university and of its sentence on the window of nurturing is registered only in this series.

摘要

一百七十多年前，纽曼说知识可以是它自己的目的。这个系列的主线句看上去与他正相反：大学培养判断力；知识是判断的材料，不是大学的目的。这一篇先说清这句话说的是什么，不是什么。它清出七个"不是"：大学不是知识的交付处，不是文凭的发放处，不是学科的墙，不只是学生和教授，不是乌托邦也不是筛人的门，不教人怎样判断得好，不以自身为目的。它划定讨论的是哪一种大学：共识里含"人是目的"的大学；现实仍是混合的，人人都会有把人当手段的时刻；共识按落处认，不读心，也不是入学条件。它取行动论对判断力的定义，以康德"判断力不能由规则教，只能练"作根，不承他把判断力说成天赋的那一层。它说明这个系列批评的是什么：把手段当作终点，在知识，尺子与划分三处落下；它分出两种强度，给出可以核查的判据。它照教育篇的三分写教，涵育与引领，并修订那一篇给大学留的一行：大学教知识与技艺，作判断的材料；为涵育给练判断的条件；引领只能以身示范。涵育没有止境。最后它结算上游交来的账，写明交出去的两项，列出七篇的篇目。这一篇不立编号命题。

写作声明：本文与 Claude（Anthropic）共同起草，所有思想决策、框架设计和最终编辑判断由作者做出。

引言

1852 年，纽曼在都柏林讲大学的理念，第五讲的题目是"知识以自身为目的"。他说：「Knowledge is capable of being its own end.」（第五讲 §2）这个系列的主线句看上去与他正相反。

细读，相近的一面先出来。纽曼说的知识，不是记住的一堆东西。它「is an acquired illumination, it is a habit, a personal possession, and an inward endowment」（§6）。信息累积起来，还不配叫科学或哲学；要经理性浸透，才是他说的知识。他反对的，正是把记住当作学会。在这一点上，这个系列与他同路。

真正分开的是一处，这一处有两面。一面在知识通向哪里。纽曼说的 liberal knowledge「independent of sequel」；他借亚里士多德论所有物的一句，说这样的知识「nothing accrues of consequence beyond the using」（§4）：它在观照里完成。这个系列说的判断，落到做与不做：一件事摆到面前，一个人划出不做，划出不得不做，把余下的留着。另一面在大学养成什么。纽曼说，大学让人形成一种心智的习惯，「A habit of mind is formed which lasts through life」（§1）。这个系列的判断力不以一种养成的品格为前提：人人面对一件事，都在判断。这两面，第四节再说。

还有两件事要先说明，它们不是与纽曼的分歧。

第一，这个系列说"知识不是大学的目的"，不是说知识不能是谁的目的。一个人可以只因为想知道而求知，不必先交代别的用途。大学是一种构，构是手段；目的本身的地位在人，不在构。主线句说的是两件：知识的交付不是大学职责的终点；大学自己也不是目的。

第二，这个系列批评的，是把知识的覆盖，交付或复现登记为完成。这不是纽曼的主张。他自己就分开了智识的养成与德性的保证：「Knowledge is one thing, virtue is another」（§9）。

再转到这个框架自己。教育篇排过一张教育的时间线，十八到二十二岁一栏写着"大学"，形态是「教+引领」，内容是「哲学史 + 专业知识」。同一篇又说，现实里的大学延长到二十二岁，甚至二十六岁，多出来的年头几乎都在重复知识的传递。这个系列从改这一行开始。

知识现在随手可得，稀缺的是拿它去判断规则给不出答案的事。涵育也没有在十四岁停下。教育篇说涵育的窗口是三到十四岁；这个系列读作：三到十四岁是涵育打开的窗口，不是它的终点；涵育没有止境。在大学里，涵育落在导师，教的人与一个学生之间，大学为它给条件，给的是练判断的条件。所以大学那一行改写为：教，以知识与技艺作材料；涵育，给练判断的条件；引领，只能以身示范。这是这个框架对自己的一处修订，登记在这个系列里，教育篇的正文不回改。

这个系列的主线句是：

大学培养判断力：知识是判断的材料，不是大学的目的。

读法。 这一篇不立编号命题，只清场与交代。第一节说大学不是什么；第二节说讨论的是哪一种大学；第三节说判断力；第四节说这个系列批评的是什么；第五节说教，涵育与引领；第六节说知识与不知；第七节说三层与材料的地位；第八节结算上游交来的账，写明交出去的两项；第九节列出七篇。应当在各篇立，这一篇一条也不证。

第一节　几个"不是"

大学不是知识的交付处。 知识进入大学，是为了让判断有材料。这不是说少教知识：没有知识的判断是空的。第一篇写教学怎样让学生自己经历适用，取舍，说明与修订。

大学不是文凭的发放处。 学位说明一个人做过什么工作，大学凭什么材料与标准作了什么评价，也说明它证明到哪里。一张学位被别人拿去作录用或录取的条件，成了有关的人绕不过去的门时，它是一处关口，要另作说明，也可以被问。有一种看法认为，学位的价值在筛选，不在学到什么1。即使学位被人当作筛选的信号，大学也不因此免去说明的责任：它核实了什么，评价了什么，记录了什么。第五篇写。

大学不是学科的墙。 学科住在大学里；学科还在，但不再是墙。把一所大学分成系的，是大学自己。第四篇写。

大学不只是学生和教授。 大学还有科研，职员，治理与钱。职员坐在有自己知识与责任的位置上，不是学生与教授之间的背景。第二，三，六，七篇写。

大学不是乌托邦，也不是筛人的门。 这个系列讨论的大学，共识里含"人是目的"，现实仍是混合的。它不按方向录人；讨论范围不是资格分类。第二节写。

大学不教人怎样判断得好。 判断力不能由规则教，只能练。大学给的是练的地方和问的人，不给一套好判断的标准。第三节与第一篇写。

大学不以自身为目的。 「任何制度，包括最优雅的宪法，都是手段而非目的。」（制度论前置篇，引《制度如何可能》）大学可以有章程上写的目的，也可以有人赋予它的办学目标；这个系列说的是两件：知识的交付不是它的终点，大学自己也不是目的。第七篇写。

第二节　讨论的是哪一种大学

这个系列说的大学，是授予学位，以教学为本职，多数也做科研的机构；只授本科学位的学院在内。公司内部的培训不在内，中小学不在内（第八节）。

这个系列写的，是共识里含"人是目的"的大学；它问的是，这样的大学，制度是什么样子。这是讨论对象的划定，不是思想实验：它不假设人人都已做到。它也不是入学条件，大学不去核谁朝着什么方向。

共识按落处认，不读心。一所大学的共识在哪里，看它落在哪里：写进章程与学术规则；做决定时可以被援引，也可以拿来问一项决定；各个位置照它持职责。大学不要求人内心认同，也不按认不认同录取，聘任，定去留。公司论前置篇引过一句：「构可以要求一个位置做什么，不能要求一个人想什么」。

划出讨论的范围，不是把人分成合格与不合格。这个系列说给大学的线，对大学里的每一个人都整条成立。

这个系列从这样的学生写起：他在高中已被涵育过，至少懂得人是目的，自己愿意成长，愿意被引领。这是写作的起点，不是对每个学生下的完成式判断。那一段路没有走过的学生，也在大学为涵育给条件的范围里。只为分数不择手段，这一类做法不作讨论的起点；它在第二篇按事件处理，不变成对人的身份。

「制度不预设内部主体都在行自己的法；它的预设是混合现实。」（制度论 Z3）人人都会有把人当手段的时刻，教授也在内。「制度对人的识别只到事件为止，不变成身份」（同上）。方向也不是一种要验收的状态：「目的王国是方向，不是应当，也不是要验收的状态」（制度论 Z39）。

有时要拿手段方向的大学作对照。对照判的是一项做法，不给一所大学定性。

出了方向，不等于没有约束。一所大学改了方向，出了这个系列的范围；说给一切构的线照样在：它不越线，它可以被问，人离得开。公司论前置篇对公司也是这样写的。

第三节　判断力

这个系列的判断力，照行动论的定义：「判断力不是一种特殊的聪明，它就是一个人在一件事摆到面前的时候，划出不做、划出不得不做、把余下的留着的那个能力。」（行动论第一篇第五节）同一节说：「规则可以进来，事实可以进来，理由和感受都可以进来，它们都是他判断的材料；可是用哪一条、取哪一样、判下来之后担起来，只能由他自己完成。」所以「判断力给的不是对」。它给的是：这一次是他自己在判；判错了，他可以回头去查，回头去改。

主线句里"材料"一词，就从这里来。知识，技能与技艺进来，都是材料。

行动论把判断力与亚里士多德说的实践智慧分开：后者与伦理德性相依，着眼整个好生活，年轻人还没有它；「本系列的判断力不以一种养成的品格为前提」（行动论 Paper 0 第三节）。这个系列照此。

康德在《纯粹理性批判》先验判断力的导论里说了三件事，这个系列以它们为根2。判断力不能由规则教，只能练。正确使用规则的能力，必须属于学生本人：规则可以教给他，用规则的那一步只能他自己走。例子磨判断，也常让人把规则当公式用。康德在同一处还把判断力说成一种天赋，缺了，学校补不上；这一层，这个系列不承。照行动论的定义，人人面对一件事都在判断；这个系列不预判谁先天缺乏判断力，谁不可培养。

一个人自己怎样练判断力，《判断力练习与游戏》已经写了，只写游戏这一条路。这个系列写另一侧：大学是安排练习的一方。它只写练的条件，不写练成什么样，也不写好的判断是什么。"培养"一词在这个系列里读作给条件，不读作制造：照制度论，构给条件，「只管条件，不管结果」（制度论 Z23）。

说大学给条件，不是一句空话。可以核的，至少有这几样：接触材料与方法的机会；形成，说明与修订判断的空间；得到反馈；提出异议以后还能继续学习；评价说明根据；每个学生都有自己的导师（第一篇）。

第四节　方向与错位

知识是判断的材料。材料不只是书本上的知识，也包括技能与技艺。以技艺为本的学院，比如音乐，临床，工程设计，都在内。技艺实践里本来就有判断，不必先离开实践，再外加一堂反思课，才算练判断。以操作能力为完成条件的训练，比如一项临床操作，一场演奏，一件设计作品，照它的职责另核，不因此判为错位。

这个系列批评的，是把手段当作终点。这一个靶子在三处落下。知识是第一处：把知识的覆盖，交付或复现当作教学的终点。尺子是第二处：证明，指标，分数本来是看一项工作的手段，被当作了工作本身（第三篇，第五篇）。划分是第三处：学科与系本来是分工的手段，被当作了边界（第四篇）。

这个框架里，有三处与此同向。教育篇说：「现代教育体系将"可测量"等同于"存在"，把三件事全部压缩为"教"，涵育与引领在制度中消失」。学科论说学科的建制「是手段，不是目的」（学科论第一篇 S1）。制度论说制度是手段（第一节）。这样读，"大学不再只教知识"不是反知识。

被当作终点的，是知识的覆盖，交付或复现，不是知识本身。一个人可以只因想知道而求知；称知识为材料，不要求他把求知改作追求一种判断力的产出。大学可以设知识的要求，也可以评价；它不能凭这些要求已经满足，就认定它给的条件已经到位。一个学生反复读一首诗，只因为还没读懂。大学可以评价他的解释有没有文本的根据；评价完成以后，不据此认证他的判断力或方向已经养成。

把手段当作终点，有两种强度。强的一种，手段成了最终标准，人被拿去抵换。公司论有两格：利润「可以是目标之一，不作最终标准」；「另一种方向，是利润成了最终标准」。走到这一步，是方向的事。弱的一种，一样手段被登记为终点：比如把知识的覆盖，交付或复现登记为教学任务已经完成，不再问学生怎样理解，运用，取舍与修订。这个系列把弱的一种叫错位。走到强的一种，就不再叫错位，是方向变了。

错位看登记。学位要求，课程评估，聘任晋升，这三处的规则文本里，一样手段被写成完成的充分条件，又没有别处再问理解，运用，取舍与修订，就是文本上的错位；规则怎样执行，另核。聘任晋升一处，问的是对工作本身的判断有没有被一样手段顶替（第五篇）。看的不是文本里有没有"理解""修订"几个字。方向看限与侵之界（制度论 Z13）。讲授多，计绩点，这些形态只是信号：「看形态，判不出越没越线」（制度论第二篇第六节）。单凭讲授多，计绩点，也不判错位。两条判据都用来读具体的安排，不给整所大学归类。个人的目的，一个项目的工作目标，一所大学的定位，也不合为一层。

教育篇也用过"错位"一词，说的是制度按年龄切的阶段，与人实际走到的位置不对应。两义相关；这个系列用的是上面这一义。

引言说，这个系列与纽曼真正分开的是一处，有两面。现在可以说清。纽曼的知识在观照里完成；这个系列的判断要落到做与不做，判下来之后，由判的人担起来。纽曼要大学养成一种持续终身的心智习惯；这个系列不以养成的品格作判断力的前提，大学给的是练的条件，练成什么样，不在它手里。两面都不是说纽曼错了：他写的是一种博雅教育的理想，这个系列写的是一种构怎样给条件。

第五节　教，涵育，引领

教育篇把教育分成三件事。教，是知识与能力的传递。涵育，是给空间，不压制，让一个人自己凿出他的自意识与目的。引领，是大人自己活出对他者的承认，孩子看到方向。「教可以批量化，涵育只能一对一，引领只能以身示范。三者不能互相替代。」教育篇还说，用教的方式做引领，把"尊重他人"当成一条规则来考核，得到的是顺从，不是承认。

教，涵育，引领，都是给的一方做的事。练是学生自己做的事，不在三件之列。

引领只能以身示范。它不登记成任何位置的职责，也不考核。为不可制度化的引领留出空间，是教育篇留下的一个开放问题；第一篇给它前提，第七篇作答。

大学算涵育，涵育没有止境。"涵育"一词，这个框架里有两种用法，这个系列两义并写。一义在教育篇的三分里：「涵育不能设计，不能规划，只能一对一」，它要的只是不被挡。在大学里，这一义就是不挡。另一义在涵育论：涵育是人对人给条件，「给出去的是条件，不是结果」（涵育论前置篇）。涵育的是人；大学为涵育给条件。涵育论的定义里没有年龄。行动论也说：「在一件事上真把一个人当目的的时候，涵育他是一个方向」（行动论第六篇）。所以涵育不止于少年时。同一篇还说：「涵育者也可以被涵育；双方不固定为施予者与接受者」。教的人，也在大学为涵育给的条件之内。

"只能一对一"，说的是涵育落在一段关系的一头，不是说一个人一生只能有一位涵育他的人。这个系列由此要求每个学生都有自己的导师（第一篇）；那一段关系成不成涵育，不在大学手里。

引言说过，教育篇给大学留的一行是"教+引领"。这个系列改写为：教，以知识与技艺作材料；涵育，给练判断的条件；引领，只能以身示范。中间一项取涵育论一义：大学不是在三件之外添了一件，它是为人与人之间的涵育给条件。这是这个框架对自己的修订，不推翻教育篇的三分。

教育篇还留了一个开放问题：机器可以教，能不能涵育。这个系列登记这一问，不答。第一篇只答一件：用了工具，判断仍要有人完成。

第六节　知识与不知

认知论说过一件事：「如果没有不知这一端，桥往哪里架？认就空转了。你可以无限堆积知，但如果不知的位置是空的，所有的知都只是信息，不是认知。」（认知论第一篇）不知的位置，是认的启动条件。

把知识当作终点来交付，可能把这个位置填上。一门课把所有的问题都配好答案，一场考试只问记没记住，学生面前就没有一件规则给不出答案的事；知识越堆越多，判断却没有地方开始。这个系列不说知识压住了判断。它说的是：交付知识的安排，可以把不知的位置填满；大学要把它留出来。

第七节　三层，位置与材料的地位

这个系列照制度论分三层说话：构用应当，位置持职责，人用自己的法。说给大学的是应当。教授，导师，职员，学生坐在位置上，持各自的职责。一个人在一件事上怎样判，是他自己的事。

这个系列的主张在规范层。规则层的设计，比如导师课的形态，评分的方式，只作建议。规则文本与公开记录作证据：一份规则写了什么，证到哪里，就说到哪里；一份规则写了，不等于它照写执行了。

这个系列不教人怎样判断。它不写好的判断是什么，也不写一套普遍的好判断的标准。这不等于不能评价一项具体的工作合不合格：评价者对一项工作作有限的判断，并说明根据。第五篇写。

这个系列不说这样的大学更划算，更有竞争力；也不因为它贵，就说它不该有。它说的是：一所共识里含"人是目的"的大学，应当怎样给条件。

第八节　上游交来的账，交出去的账

这一节只写接到什么，在哪一篇处理，不预写兑现。

学科论交来几项。科研评价的标准，含数学的功劳标准：大学就它对这些标准的采用与转换作答，第三篇写。AI 参与证明以后的署名：署名记的是做事与负责，第三篇写。资源的闸门：大学分资源的那几处闸门里，至少留一个不要求换算的入口，第六篇写。学位，认证与录取：这个系列只写学位作为一份记录的一面，第五篇写；录取与认证怎样设计，交给日后的专论。学科论还说，写标准的构与把标准采作门的构，应当让标准有一条可以被问的通道，门所及又绕不开它的人也在内（学科论第九篇 S46）；这一条落到大学这一种构上，第七篇写。"学科应当怎样分"一问，这个系列不给分法，只给条件，第四篇写。

教育篇留下两个开放问题。机器可以教，能不能涵育：这个系列登记，不答；第一篇只答用了工具，判断仍要有人完成。为不可制度化的引领留出空间：第一篇给前提，第七篇作答。

涵育论把三样交给制度一侧：考核与给条件的人分开，有处申诉，有路退出（涵育论第五篇）。离开与申诉，第一篇与第二篇写。考核与给条件怎样分开，第五篇写：评价是一种权力，受制衡；学位与资格一类的结论，由与导师分开的位置作出。这些规则由谁定，又怎样被问，第七篇写。

二次启蒙论问过：资源的闸门，能不能留一个不要求先证明它值的入口（二次启蒙论第五篇，第六篇）。这个系列只答大学这一种闸门；从"可以"到"应当"，由这个系列自己承担，第六篇写。

政治经济学说，一张学位成了别人绕不过去的门时，是一处关口（政治经济学第五篇 E31）。录取与认证怎样设计，这个系列不做，交给日后的专论；第五篇只写学位作为记录的一面。

《判断力练习与游戏》总论末尾留下一项：「机构办游戏，即用游戏做事在机构一层的样子，也待另论（3.4）。」这个系列写构安排练习的一般条件，不只写游戏；机构办游戏这一项，仍然留出（第七篇交下游）。

交出去的有两项。中小学的师生比，义务入学与公共供给，这个系列不写，交给日后的学校论。入学资格与录取的设计，交给日后的专论。

第九节　篇目

篇 篇名 问什么
前置篇 大学不是什么（What a University Is Not） 清场；讨论范围；判断力；方向与错位；教，涵育，引领；结算与篇目
第一篇 大学的教学（University Teaching） 判断力怎样练；知识作材料；每个学生都有自己的导师；不顶替；教的人自己的判断；工具；助教
第二篇 大学的学生（University Students） 一个学生经过学习，科研，助教，受雇几个位置；回绝与改向；离开一个指导关系的路；事件之后的位置；学生的声音
第三篇 大学的科研（University Research） 大学对科研标准的采用与转换；署名；数学的功劳标准；科研里的判断；学生在科研里的判断
第四篇 大学的学科（University Disciplines） 学科还在，但不再是墙；系的规则；在交界处练判断
第五篇 大学的评价（University Evaluation） 评价是权力，受制衡；评价不把判断变回规则；评价交给机器；评价不奖励把人当手段的那一步；学位是有限的证明；教授的评价同此；信任与核查
第六篇 大学的经费（University Funding） 价格落在事上；钱带要求，不带方向；体育；不要求换算的入口；维持不下去时
第七篇 大学的治理（University Governance） 收回前六篇的"谁定"；定规则的位置可被追问；学术判断的职责；为引领留出空间；大学自己也是构

"学生"一篇按人立，篇内按位置写：同一个人经过几个位置时，哪些要求与后果不能相互借用。"评价"一篇学生与教授并写：评价被采作门的机制可以并读，两者的职责与后果仍分开。治理放在最后：前六篇各自写明谁作决定，谁承担后果，第七篇才有东西可以收回。

这一篇停在哪里

它没有做的：没有立编号命题，也没有证任何一条应当，应当在各篇立；没有判任何一所大学；没有写入学资格，录取与认证的设计；没有写中小学；没有回改教育篇的正文，对它大学一行与涵育窗口一句的修订，只登记在这个系列里。
