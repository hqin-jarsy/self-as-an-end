Abstract

This paper reads the student's own cannot-not, once it has grown, meeting the teacher's law and the teacher's plans for him. At the turn at the layer of purpose, the student's own cannot-not crystallizes; rebellion is not the mechanism by which it comes about, and is often its behavioral by-product. In this stretch, the teacher's end heading toward 15DD in one operation is read, in this matter, as: acknowledging that this cannot-not is the student's own, even where, as the teacher sees it, that direction will fail. The teacher may state his disagreement in full; he does not hand it over as a piece of self-knowledge the student has already completed. After falling back, this end owns up to that occasion; what has already happened is entered as it is.

Mutual chiseling is judged by process, not by identity: where teacher and student, in some matter, enter one and the same reciprocal process and actually chisel open each other's judgments, there is mutual chiseling in that matter; that there is mutual chiseling does not certify that the student is already headed in the 15DD direction. Where the requirement at the student's end and the teacher's law require incompatible actions in the same situation: whether that matter enters the Conflict Treatise is checked item by item against its two gates, and is not settled by the teacher-student identity or by incompatibility itself; what does not enter is read as dealings and collision.

The student's not agreeing with the teacher in some matter, or surpassing him: this is not read as betrayal, nor is it used to certify that this relation heads toward cultivation or that the teacher's end is headed in the 15DD direction. That this skill was taught by this teacher is entered as fact. "The blue that comes from the indigo plant" is not used as the measure of this relation. This paper interfaces with Han Yu's "On Teachers," with Xunzi's "Exhortation to Learning," and with Aristotle. It does not prove that students ought to surpass their teachers, nor that the teacher ought to yield, nor that not agreeing with one's teacher is a mark of maturity.

Introduction

A few years on. Lu Miao was selected for the training camp for the mathematics competition. She came to Fang Xu and said: "I'm not going to the camp. I'm going to sit the entrance exam for a school where I can study drawing."

Fang Xu said: "You don't really want to draw. You're afraid of the camp." Lu Miao said: "No." She picked up her bag and left. The next Wednesday she did not come.

The Wednesday after that, she came. Fang Xu said: "What I said last time was wrong. Whether you are afraid, whether you want it, is not something I can say for you. What I think is a different matter, and I'll tell you." He said three things: he did not think well of this road; how far she had come in mathematics he could see, and in drawing he could not; the camp comes only this once in the year, and studying drawing could wait a year. Lu Miao said: "I know. I've thought about it." Fang Xu said: "That is all I think. The road is yours."

For that first encounter, one can also suppose a different reply: "I've taught you all these years, and this is how you repay me?"

There are two more things, from before this.

One is a proof. Lu Miao brought a proof shorter than the one Fang Xu had taught. Fang Xu looked at it for a long time and said: "At this step you've used something that hasn't been proved yet." Lu Miao took it away, and the next Wednesday she had filled it in. Fang Xu looked at it again: "That's right. It's better than mine." From then on, whenever he came to this place, he used her proof.

The other is writing out the grounds. Fang Xu required her to write out the ground of every step in a proof. Once Lu Miao said: "What I can see, I don't want to write." Fang Xu said: "A proof with no grounds written, I don't read." The sheet lay on the table all afternoon. The next Wednesday Lu Miao asked: "Why does what can be seen have to be written too?" Fang Xu said: "Being able to see it and being able to prove it are not the same thing. What your proof left out was precisely a 'can be seen.'" Lu Miao said: "Then I'll write only the steps that can't be seen at a glance. Which steps count as seen at a glance, I'll mark." Fang Xu thought about it and said: "All right. What you mark, we'll go through one by one." From then on her proofs carried one more kind of mark.

Among these there are several things to be read apart. Lu Miao is not going to the camp: what is it that has come up? She picked up her bag and left: is that the root of the matter? What was Fang Xu's sentence the first time, and what was it the second time? He did not think well of the road: how far did he go in saying so, and where did he stop? "A proof with no grounds written, I don't read": is that a conflict, and how was that arrangement later changed? The back-and-forth over the proof: what is it? She surpassed him at this point: what does that show, and what does it not?

What this paper reads. The previous paper read the thing the student herself cares about coming up, and the teacher's end giving space. This paper reads it once it has grown: the student's own cannot-not meeting the teacher's law and the teacher's plans for her.

This paper reads five things. First, the meeting. Second, what the teacher's end being headed in the 15DD direction looks like in this stretch. Third, mutual chiseling. Fourth, the threshold of the Conflict Treatise. Fifth, the student's not agreeing with the teacher, and surpassing him.

Shared words. "Cannot-not" speaks of the student's own: a direction that does not depend on permission from outside and that, pressed down, comes back elsewhere.1 "The teacher's law" here means the rules by which the teacher himself teaches; Fang Xu's law has this one in it: a proof is to have its grounds written out. "The teacher's plans for him" are the teacher's expectations (Paper 4). "Collision" follows the usage at the layer of relation: one party's act actually entering the other party's conditions of occurrence; it has two subclasses, conflict and mutual chiseling.2 "Conflict" is used only once the gates of the Conflict Treatise have been passed (§4); what has not passed is read as dealings and collision.

Method. As in the earlier papers. Where Lu Miao later went, and whether it came to anything, this paper does not write.

§1 The Meeting

TS29　At the 14DD turn, the student's own cannot-not crystallizes and begins to meet the teacher's law and the teacher's plans for him. Rebellion is not the mechanism by which 14DD comes about; it is often its behavioral by-product

The cannot-not has grown

Lu Miao says "I'm going to sit the entrance exam for a school where I can study drawing." Between "what I'd really rather do is draw" in Paper 5 and this sentence lie several years: first it came up, and later it could not be put down. The Learning series has given its structure: it still cannot be put down after scrutiny, and "this road leads nowhere" said from outside does not put it out.3

From Fang Xu's end only clues are seen. She was told "you're afraid of the camp," and said "no"; she came back after a Wednesday's gap and still said "I've thought about it." Not vanishing when stopped is a clue, not a certification.

The meeting

Having grown, it meets two things. One is the teacher's law: the rules by which Fang Xu teaches. The other is the teacher's plans for her: Fang Xu expects her to go to the camp, and expects her to go on in mathematics.

The meeting is not something gone wrong with this relation. Upstream says it in two halves: in dealings in which a requirement appears on the other side that he bears in the first person and by which he goes on organizing his action in the same situation, conflict cannot be guaranteed not to occur so long as the relation is not exited; in a relation in which no such requirement appears, at least collision cannot be guaranteed not to occur.4 In both halves, collision cannot be guaranteed not to occur. Whether what Lu Miao holds is such a requirement is read as in §4; in whichever half it falls, Fang Xu's law and his expectations are there, and the two are still at one table. That they have run into each other is not read as something gone wrong with this relation.

Rebellion is not the root

The student's talking back to the teacher in this stretch is not the mechanism by which this comes about. Upstream writes it clearly: rebellion is not the mechanism by which this layer comes about, but it is often its behavioral by-product, and it can serve as one of its confirmation conditions in boundary encounters.5 Talking back can be one of the clues; it does not by itself read out whether there is a cannot-not.

So neither way is it read on this alone. It is not read, from Lu Miao's picking up her bag and leaving alone, that her direction has come to stand: a student who walks off may simply have been pushed too hard that day. Nor is it read, from not talking back alone, that there is no direction: a student who never answers back may be quite unable to put her own matter down. When Lu Miao says "I don't want to write it," and says "I'm not going to the camp," what is read is what she has raised; it is not read as "she has reached the age of answering back."

§2 The Teacher's End

TS30　In this stretch, the teacher's end heading toward 15DD in one operation is read, in this matter, as: acknowledging that this cannot-not is the student's own, even where, as the teacher sees it, that direction will fail. The teacher may state his disagreement in full; he does not hand it over as a piece of self-knowledge the student has already completed

Acknowledging that this cannot-not is her own

In this stretch, the teacher's end heading toward 15DD in one operation is read, in this matter, as: acknowledging that this cannot-not is her own, even where, as the teacher sees it, that direction will fail. Upstream writes this as the condition of cultivation in this stretch;6 here, following the scope, it is read as the direction of one operation, and falling back is read as it occurs.

Acknowledging is not changing one's view.7 The second time, Fang Xu still says he does not think well of this road. "The road is yours" does not say that this road will lead somewhere; it says to whom this cannot-not belongs. Not thinking well of it, and acknowledging that it is hers, are set down together.

Stating the disagreement in full

The teacher may state his disagreement in full. The giving side's own share can be given in full: what he has seen, what he has thought, his disagreement.8 Fang Xu said three things: that he does not think well of it; what he can see and what he cannot; that the camp comes only this once. He did not, for the sake of acknowledging her direction, swallow his own view. Acknowledging the other does not cancel the teacher's own position.

Law can say you may not crush the other; it cannot say you should yield;9 nor does this series say that the teacher should yield, or that the teacher should keep quiet.

Not handed over as self-knowledge she has already completed

Stating in full has a boundary: one may give one's own judgment, and does not hand it over as the other's completed knowledge of himself.10

"I don't think well of this road" is Fang Xu's judgment, given in Fang Xu's name. "You don't really want to draw; you're afraid of the camp" takes Fang Xu's judgment as Lu Miao's knowledge of herself and says it for her: whether she wants to draw, whether she is afraid, are matters at her end. The difference between the two sentences does not lie in how heavy the tone is, but in the form in which they are handed over.

Falling back, and turning back

What Fang Xu said the first time was precisely the latter sentence. This is a falling back: in this matter, that occasion did not head toward acknowledging her direction; it headed toward saying for her what was going on with her.

Lu Miao said "no." At the time this denial had nowhere to land: Fang Xu did not take it up, she left, and the next Wednesday she did not come.

Falling back is read as it occurs, and does not overturn the direction; what is read is whether, after falling back, this end can turn back (Paper 1). The second time, Fang Xu says: "What I said last time was wrong. Whether you are afraid, whether you want it, is not something I can say for you." What bears the "owning up" is this sentence he says out loud: he owns up that on that occasion he spoke for her, and he gives his own view over again as a separate share, in his own name. Only at this point did Fang Xu respond to her denial of that day, and own up to what he had said on her behalf.

Turning back does not rewrite what has already happened. The Wednesday she did not come is entered as it is; so are the two weeks after that sentence was said. How she takes it after the turning back is a matter at her end: she said "I've thought about it," and these are words and deeds; whether she has forgiven, this paper does not read, and she does not come to owe forgiveness because the teacher has turned back.

Had the first time been the other sentence, "I've taught you all these years, and this is how you repay me," something else would have been added: entering what was taught as something she owes, and entering her direction as repayment. This is read in Paper 7.

The face that is seen

How Fang Xu spoke these two times, how he took it back, and how he stopped, Lu Miao sees. The adult embodies recognition, the child sees the direction: the education paper calls this leading.11 This face is there all along. This series only registers that it is there, and does not unfold it.

§3 Mutual Chiseling

TS31　Mutual chiseling is judged by process, not by identity: where teacher and student, in some matter, enter one and the same reciprocal process and actually chisel open each other's judgments, there is mutual chiseling in that matter. The matter in which the teacher is chiseled open by the student is read by the process on that occasion. That there is mutual chiseling does not certify that the student is already headed in the 15DD direction

Judged by process

Mutual chiseling is judged by process, not by identity. When upstream wrote this sentence, teacher and student were among the examples it gave.12 Carried to a party who has not yet come of age, it has already been set up between parent and child: where, in some matter, the two enter one and the same reciprocal process and actually chisel open each other's judgments, there is mutual chiseling in that matter; it is not established by identity or by the name of a stage.13

Having entered the same process is the premise, not the whole; the judgments on both sides must also actually be chiseled open by the other within that process.

That proof

Lu Miao brings a proof; Fang Xu points out a place where something has not been proved; Lu Miao fills it in; Fang Xu says "it's better than mine," and from then on uses hers. That the two entered one and the same process can be seen from their words and deeds.

Whether a judgment was chiseled open is read end by end. At Fang Xu's end there are his own words, said out loud: "it's better than mine," and from then on he changed what he used; his judgment of how this problem should be proved changed at this point. At Lu Miao's end, what is seen from outside is that she filled in that step; whether she owned that the step had been missing, or merely added a line as he said, is seen only by clues. Upstream, in the same kind of place, first enters that the arrangement was changed, and reads the judgment conditionally.14 The same is done here: if the judgments on both sides were each actually chiseled open at one place by the other in this matter, then in this matter there is mutual chiseling.

The matter in which the teacher is chiseled open by the student is read by the process on that occasion. It does not hold because "the teacher learning from the student" sounds agreeable, nor does it fail to hold because of the teacher-student identity. The one who cultivates can also be cultivated; the two sides are not fixed as giver and receiver.15

What it does not certify

That there is mutual chiseling does not certify that the student is already headed in the 15DD direction. Lu Miao chiseled open one of Fang Xu's judgments: this speaks of the process in this one matter; how she regards Fang Xu, and where her end is heading, this series does not read. On this axis of stages there is no 15DD of the student's; in this series 15DD appears only at the teacher's end, as the direction of one operation (Paper 1).

§4 The Threshold of the Conflict Treatise

TS32　Where the requirement at the student's end and the teacher's law require incompatible actions in the same situation: whether that matter enters the Conflict Treatise is checked item by item against its two gates, and is not settled by the teacher-student identity or by incompatibility itself. What does not enter is read as dealings and collision

Two gates

"A proof with no grounds written, I don't read." "What I can see, I don't want to write." In the same situation, the two sides require incompatible actions.

Whether this is conflict in the sense of the Conflict Treatise is checked item by item against its two gates.16 The first gate asks after structure: two already-formed laws of one's own requiring incompatible action in the same situation, where, besides, neither can certify the other as having arrived, neither can legislate for the other, and the account cannot be settled by one side's unilateral exit.17 The second gate asks after the handling of the matter: whether it would put at risk the handling party's position of going on revising and going on being open to questioning;18 the student's side enters this gate by one question only: whether handling it this way would put the teacher in the position of constituting on her behalf. Both gates must be passed.

It is not settled by the teacher-student identity, nor by incompatibility itself. Nor does the size of the matter decide: a matter is not judged to be outside the gate because it is small, nor to have entered because it bears on the future.19

Neither certifying nor cancelling

There are two things the teacher's end does not do. One is certifying: whether Lu Miao's "I don't want to write it," or her "I'm going to sit the entrance exam for a school where I can study drawing," is already a law of her own, Fang Xu does not decide for her. The other is cancelling on her behalf for want of certification:20 he cannot say "that's only a passing fancy" and treat it as nothing. Fang Xu's sentence the first time, "you don't really want to draw," did exactly the latter; he later took it back.

"Cannot legislate for the other" speaks of qualification, not of whether it can be stopped.21 Fang Xu's law can say how he himself acts: "a proof with no grounds written, I don't read" says what he does not do. It cannot be written as what Lu Miao should become.

The matter of writing out the grounds

On the material this example now gives, the items of the first gate have not all been checked. That the actions the two sides require are incompatible can be seen. Whether Lu Miao's "I don't want to write it" is already a formed law of her own cannot be read from the material, and Fang Xu does not decide it for her; whether Fang Xu's "write out the ground of every step" is a law of his own is confirmed at his end. For the two items, that neither can certify the other as having arrived and that the account cannot be settled by one side's unilateral exit, the material is likewise insufficient. The material at hand has not completed the check of the first gate, and for the time being the matter is read as dealings and collision.

Read through, it is an exchange of this kind. Lu Miao asks: why does what can be seen have to be written too? This rule can be questioned, and what Fang Xu answers with is a reason, not "because I say so" (Paper 2): being able to see it and being able to prove it are not the same thing, and he points back to the place she herself had left out. Lu Miao puts forward a change: write only what cannot be seen at a glance, and she will mark which steps count as seen at a glance. Fang Xu accepts, and adds one thing: what is marked is gone through one by one.

This matter is entered as: the arrangement was changed. It is not entered as Lu Miao's winning and Fang Xu's giving way; nor as Fang Xu's holding firm and Lu Miao's giving way (Paper 3).22 What was changed is the arrangement between the two: "write every step" became "what is marked and has been gone through need not be written." Whether the rule by which Fang Xu teaches was changed is not decided for him. Collision runs two ways; that the teacher's law is changed on account of the student is a possibility, not a necessity, and is read by the process on that occasion.

The matter of the training camp

For the matter of the camp there is rather more material. The requirement at Lu Miao's end appears as one she herself bears and by which she goes on organizing her action: she was told something, and left; she came back after one gap, and it was still this. Fang Xu's plan requires a different action: going to the camp.

Whether it has entered the Conflict Treatise is likewise checked against the two gates, and is not decided for it here. Only this is pointed out: which sentence the second gate asks about here, whether Fang Xu's handling it this way would put him in the position of constituting on her behalf. The sentence of the first time headed exactly there; the second time he took that sentence back, gave his view in his own name, and stopped at "the road is yours."

Should Lu Miao later say that she does not really want to study drawing: on the strength of this sentence alone, it cannot be told whether the first reading was mistaken or she later changed direction. Where it is later confirmed that the object never obtained, one exits this matter, and does not turn and judge her;23 the dealings that have already happened are entered as they are.

§5 Not Agreeing, and Surpassing

TS33　The student's not agreeing with the teacher in some matter, or surpassing him: this is not read as betrayal, nor is it used to certify that this relation heads toward cultivation or that the teacher's end is headed in the 15DD direction. That this skill was taught by this teacher is entered as fact. "The blue that comes from the indigo plant" is not used as the measure of this relation

Not read as betrayal

The student's not agreeing with the teacher in some matter is not read as betrayal. "This is how you repay me" reads not agreeing as betrayal: the premise of that reading is that the teacher holds a share in her direction. That share the teacher does not have (Paper 7).

Not used to certify

The student's surpassing the teacher is not used to certify anything either. That Lu Miao's proof is better than Fang Xu's does not certify that this relation heads toward cultivation, nor that Fang Xu's end is headed in the 15DD direction. Which way a single act goes is decided by what it actually opened or sealed at the time, and is not rewritten by the other's later success or failure;24 whether the giving side took on the direction, what his act actually opened or sealed, and whether the receiving side completed that step himself, are three questions, asked separately, and no side's own account settles the other two.25 That the student later goes far cannot answer the first two questions. The students of a person who treats students only as a signboard can go far too.

The reverse holds as well: that the student has not surpassed the teacher does not show that this relation did not head toward cultivation.

Entered as fact

That this skill was taught by this teacher is entered as fact. Fang Xu taught Lu Miao how to write a proof; of the things used in her proof, several were learned at this table. This is a fact; it is not wiped out because she later took another road, or surpassed him at this point; nor does it on that account become a share of Fang Xu's in her direction.

"The blue that comes from the indigo plant" is not used as the measure of this relation: "whether the student has surpassed the teacher" is not used to measure a teacher, nor to measure a student.

§6 Interfacing with the Tradition

Han Yu: the disciple need not be inferior to the teacher. "On Teachers": therefore the disciple need not be inferior to the teacher, nor the teacher more worthy than the disciple; one hears the Way earlier and another later, each has his own special field of skill, and that is all.26 Where the two reflect each other: the division between teacher and disciple is not a division of higher and lower; the student's surpassing the teacher in some matter is not an anomaly in this relation. Where they part: Han Yu uses this sentence to defend "following a teacher," and what it says is that the teacher need not be the more worthy; this series reads what comes after the surpassing, and does not use it to certify anything.

Xunzi: blue is taken from the indigo plant. The opening of "Exhortation to Learning": blue is taken from the indigo plant, and is bluer than the plant; ice is made of water, and is colder than water.27 This sentence later became a saying about the student outdoing the teacher. Returned to the original, it follows the line that learning must never cease, and speaks of learning: through learning, a person can come to differ from what he was. It is not measuring teacher against student. Only this difference is taken here: "the blue that comes from the indigo plant" speaks of the change in the one who learns; this series does not turn it into a measure between teacher and student.

Aristotle: truth and friends. Before criticizing the theory of Forms in the Nicomachean Ethics, Aristotle says: those who introduced this doctrine are dear to him, and this makes the inquiry an uphill one; yet for the sake of preserving the truth, even what is close to one must be given up; both are dear, and piety requires honoring the truth first.28 Later generations compressed this into "I love my teacher, but I love truth more." Where the two reflect each other: not agreeing with what the teacher holds is not turning one's back on the teacher as a person; the two can be kept apart. The parties are adults, and only structure is read here.

The "Record of Learning": teaching and learning grow together. The passage from which these words come runs: only after learning does one know one's insufficiency, and only after teaching does one know one's perplexity; knowing one's insufficiency, one can then turn back upon oneself; knowing one's perplexity, one can then strengthen oneself; hence it is said that teaching and learning grow together.29 Literally, it says that the one who teaches comes to know his own perplexity through teaching: teaching lets the one who teaches see where he himself has not got through. It does not assign learning and teaching to two persons, nor does it say that the teacher is altered by the student. So it is not used here as evidence for §3; the ground for the teacher's being chiseled open by the student lies in the places cited in §3.

Historical material: Lewis on Kirkpatrick. C. S. Lewis writes in his autobiography that in his teens he was sent to be taught in the house of a retired headmaster, Kirkpatrick. This teacher would question even a remark thrown out in passing. Lewis writes that only after being floored often enough did he learn to parry and to hit back a little, and that his thinking grew some sinew; and that in the end, by his own account, he became a sparring partner not to be despised.30 Only how the adult Lewis writes is read here. The word he uses is "sparring": two people in one and the same process, giving and taking. This connects with §3: mutual chiseling is judged by process, not by identity; a student who has not yet come of age can, in some matter, enter such a process. What he writes is his own end; whether the teacher's judgment was chiseled open by him this passage does not say, and it is not filled in for him here. This series does not on that basis certify which direction Kirkpatrick was headed in.

§7 Drawing Together, and Downstream

This paper sets up five propositions. Taken together they say: the student's own cannot-not crystallizes, and meets the teacher's law and the teacher's plans for him; rebellion is not the mechanism by which it comes about. In this stretch, the teacher's end heading toward 15DD in one operation is read as acknowledging that this cannot-not is the student's own, even where the teacher does not think well of it; acknowledging is not changing one's view; the teacher may state his disagreement in full, and does not hand it over as self-knowledge the student has already completed; after falling back, it is by owning up to that occasion that the student's denial of that day is answered, and what has already happened is entered as it is. Mutual chiseling is judged by process: where teacher and student actually chisel open each other's judgments in some matter, there is mutual chiseling in that matter; from outside it is first entered that the arrangement was changed, and the judgment is read conditionally; it does not certify which direction the student is headed in. Where the two sides require incompatible actions, whether the matter enters the Conflict Treatise is checked item by item against the two gates, and is not settled by identity, by incompatibility, or by the size of the matter; the teacher does not certify the student's end, nor cancel it on his behalf for want of certification; what does not enter is read as dealings and collision, and where an arrangement was changed it is entered that the arrangement was changed, with no winning or losing. The student's not agreeing with the teacher, or surpassing him, is not read as betrayal, nor used to certify this relation or this teacher; what was taught is entered as fact; "the blue that comes from the indigo plant" is not used as a measure.

This paper does not prove that students ought to surpass their teachers, nor that the teacher ought to yield, nor that not agreeing with one's teacher is a mark of maturity.

What the next paper reads. Lu Miao can check a proof; where she is going has come to stand; one day Fang Xu is to move away. Are these three one thing? Which of them is the boundary up to which this series reads? And what are these years of teaching, on the road she takes afterward?

Downstream. The boundary of coming of age, the test that lies on the student's side, that having taught is not entered as repayment, ending early, and by whom what follows is read: Paper 7. The face of the teacher that the student sees is only registered in this series.

再下一个星期三，她来了。方叙说：「上回那一句，我说错了。你怕不怕、想不想，不是我能替你说的。我的看法是另一回事，我说给你听。」他说了三样：这条路他看不好；她在数学上走到了哪里，他看得见，画上，他看不见；集训一年只有这一回，学画可以晚一年。陆苗说：「我知道。我想过了。」方叙说：「我的看法就是这些。路是你的。」

头一回，还可以设另一种说法：「我教了你这些年，你就拿这个回报我？」

不得不长出来

陆苗说「我要去考学画的学校」。从第五篇的「我其实更想画画」到这一句，中间隔了几年：先是冒出来，后来是放不下。学习系列给过它的结构：经过审视仍然放不下，外面说「这条路走不通」也熄不掉。3

从方叙这一端，只见线索。她被说了「你是怕集训」，说「不是」；隔了一个星期三再来，仍说「我想过了」。被拦了一下不消失，是线索，不是认证。

相遇

它长出来，就与两样东西相遇。一样是老师的法：方叙据以教人的那些条。一样是老师对她的打算：方叙期待她去集训，期待她在数学上走下去。

相遇不是这段关系出了毛病。上游分了两半说：另一端有一项以他第一人称承担、在同一情境里持续组织其行动的要求出现的来往里，关系不退出，冲突就不能保证不发生；没有这样一项要求出现的关系里，至少碰撞不能保证不发生。4 两半里，碰撞都不能保证不发生。陆苗这一条是不是那样一项要求，照第四节核；落在哪一半，方叙的法与期待都在，两个人还在一张桌子边上。碰上了，不读作这段关系出了毛病。

叛逆，不是根

这一段里学生顶撞老师，不是这件事的发生机制。上游写得清楚：叛逆不是这一层的发生机制，却常是它的行为副产品，并可在边界碰撞中充当确认条件之一。5 顶撞可以是线索之一；它不独自读出有没有不得不。

所以两头都不单凭这一样读。不单从陆苗拿起书包走了，读出她的方向已经站住：一个学生甩手走开，可以只是那一天被说急了。也不单从不顶撞读出没有方向：一个从不回嘴的学生，可以在自己的事上放不下得很。陆苗说「不想写」，说「集训我不去了」，读的是她提出了什么，不读成「到了顶嘴的年纪」。

认，不等于改了看法。7 方叙第二回仍说这条路他看不好。「路是你的」这一句，没有说这条路走得通；它说的是这条不得不归谁。看不好，与认它是她的，两样同时放着。

把不同意说足

老师可以把自己的不同意说足。给的一方自己那一份可以给足：他看到的，想过的，他的不同意。8 方叙说了三样：他看不好；他看得见什么、看不见什么；集训只有这一回。他没有因为要认她的方向，就把自己的看法咽下去。认对方，不取消老师自己的位置。

法可以说不可以压垮对方，不能说该让步；9 这个系列也不说老师该让步，不说老师该闭嘴。

不当作她已经完成的自我认识交过去

说足，有一条界：可以给自己的判断，不把它当作对方已经完成的自我认识交过去。10

「这条路我看不好」，是方叙的判断，以方叙的名义给出。「你其实不是真想画画，你是怕集训」，是把方叙的判断当作陆苗对自己的认识，替她说了出来：她想不想画画，她怕不怕，是她那一端的事。两句的分别不在语气轻重，在交过去的样子。

回落，与回头

方叙头一回说的，正是后一句。这是一次回落：在这件事上，那一次没有朝认她的方向去，朝的是替她说她是怎么回事。

陆苗说「不是」。这一声否认当场没有地方落：方叙没有接，她走了，下一个星期三没有来。

第三节 互凿

TS31　互凿按过程判，不按身份判：师生在某一件事上进入同一个互向的过程，并实际互相凿开对方的判断，那一件事上就有互凿。老师被学生凿开的那一件，照那一次过程读。有互凿，不认证学生已在 15DD 方向上

按过程判

互凿按过程判，不按身份判。上游写这一句的时候，举的例子里就有师生。12 推到还没有成年的一方，亲子之间已经立过：在某一件事上进入同一个互向的过程，并实际互相凿开对方的判断，那一件事上就有互凿；不凭身份或阶段的名称认定。13

进了同一个过程，是前提，不是全部；还要两边的判断在那个过程里实际被对方凿开。

那个证法

陆苗拿来证法；方叙指出一处没有证的东西；陆苗补上；方叙说「比我的好」，此后改用她的。两个人进了同一个过程，这一点从言行看得出。

判断有没有被凿开，分两端读。方叙这一端，有他自己说出来的话：「比我的好」，并且此后改了用法；他对这道题该怎样证的判断，在这一处改了。陆苗那一端，从外面见到的是她补上了那一步；她是认下那一步原先漏了，还是只照他说的添了一行，只见线索。上游在同样的地方先记安排被改了，判断照条件式读。14 这里照样：两边的判断若在这件事上都实际被对方凿开了一处，这一件事上就有互凿。

老师被学生凿开的那一件，照那一次过程读。它不因为「老师向学生学」听起来顺耳就成立，也不因为师生身份就不成立。涵育的人也可以被涵育；双方不固定为给的一方与受的一方。15

不认证什么

有互凿，不认证学生已在 15DD 方向上。陆苗凿开了方叙的一个判断，说的是这一件事上的过程；她怎样看方叙，她那一端朝哪里去，这个系列不读。这条阶段轴上没有学生的 15DD；15DD 在这个系列里只出现在老师那一端，作一次运作的方向（第一篇）。

第四节 冲突论的门槛

TS32　学生那一端的要求与老师的法在同一情境里要求不相容的行动：那一件事是否进入冲突论，照它的两道门逐项核，不由师生身份或不相容本身完成认定。没有进入的，照来往与碰撞读

两道门

「不写根据的证明，我不看。」「我看得出来的，不想写。」同一个情境里，两边要的是不相容的行动。

这是不是冲突论所说的冲突，照它的两道门逐项核。16 第一道门问结构：两条已经成形的自己的法，在同一情境里要求不相容的行动，而且不能互相认证到达，不能替对方立法，也不能由一方单方退出而完成结算。17 第二道门问这件事的处理：会不会把处理的一方继续修订、继续受问的位置置于风险里；18 学生那一侧只以一问进这道门：这样处理，会不会使老师占据替她构成的位置。两道门都要过。

不由师生身份完成认定，也不由不相容本身完成认定。事情的大小也不定：不由事情小就判它在门外，不由事情关乎将来就判它已经入场。19

不认证，也不撤销

老师这一端有两样不做。一是不认证：陆苗那一句「不想写」，或那一句「我要去考学画的学校」，是不是一条已经成形的她自己的法，方叙不替她判定。二是不以未能认证为由替她撤销：20 不能说一句「你不过是一时兴起」，就把它当作没有。方叙头一回那一句「你其实不是真想画画」，做的正是后一样；他后来收回了。

「不能替对方立法」，说的是资格，不是拦不拦得住。21 方叙的法可以说他自己怎样做：「不写根据的证明，我不看」，说的是他不做什么。它不能写成陆苗该成为什么。

写根据这一件

照这个例子现有的材料，第一道门的几项没有核完。两边要的行动不相容，这一项看得见。陆苗那一句「不想写」，是不是一条已经成形的她自己的法，材料里读不出，方叙也不替她判定；方叙那一条「每一步都写根据」是不是他自己的法，确认在他那一端。不能互相认证到达、不能由一方单方退出而完成结算这两项，材料同样不够。现有材料没有完成第一道门的核对，暂照来往与碰撞读。

读下来是这样一个来回。陆苗问：为什么看得出来的也要写。这一条可以被追问，方叙答的是理由，不是「因为我说的」（第二篇）：看得出来与证得出来不是一回事，并指回她自己漏过的那一处。陆苗提出一种改法：只写不是一眼看得出的，哪几步算一眼看得出，她标出来。方叙认了，添了一条：标出来的，一条一条对。

这一件记作：安排被改了。不记作陆苗赢了，方叙让了；也不记作方叙守住了，陆苗让了（第三篇）。22 改了的是两个人之间的安排：「每一步都写」成了「标出来、对过的可以不写」。方叙据以教人的那一条改没改，不替他判。碰撞是两向的；老师的法因学生而改，是可能，不是必然，照那一次过程读。

集训这一件

集训这一件，材料多一些。陆苗那一端的要求，是以她自己承担、持续组织着她行动的样子出现的：被说了一句，走了；隔一回再来，仍是这一条。方叙的打算要的是另一样行动：去集训。

它进没进冲突论，同样照两道门核，这里不替它判。只指出第二道门在这里问的是哪一句：方叙这样处理，会不会使他占据替她构成的位置。头一回那一句，朝的正是那里；第二回，他把那一句收回，把看法以自己的名义给出，停在「路是你的」。

若后来陆苗说，她其实并不想学画：仅凭这一句，分不清是当初读错了，还是她后来改了方向。对象后来确认没有成立的，退出这一件，不倒判她；23 已经发生的来往，照记。

第五节 不认同，与超过

TS33　学生在一件事上不认同老师、超过老师：不读作背叛，也不拿来认证这段关系朝涵育去，或老师那一端在 15DD 方向上。那一样本事是这位老师教会的，照事实记。「青出于蓝」不作这段关系的尺子

不读作背叛

学生在一件事上不认同老师，不读作背叛。「你就拿这个回报我」，是把不认同读成了背叛：这一读法的前提，是她的方向里有老师的一份。那一份，老师没有（第七篇）。

不拿来认证

学生超过老师，也不拿来认证什么。陆苗的证法比方叙的好，不认证这段关系朝涵育去，也不认证方叙那一端在 15DD 方向上。一次作为朝哪一边，由它当时实际打开或封住了什么来定，不由对方后来的成败改写；24 给的一方是否承担了方向，他的作为实际打开或封住了什么，受的一方自己是否完成了那一步，是三问，分开问，哪一方的自述都不替另外两问结账。25 学生后来走得远，回答不了前两问。一位把学生只当作招牌的人，他的学生也可以走得远。

反过来也一样：学生没有超过老师，不说明这段关系没有朝涵育去。

照事实记

那一样本事是这位老师教会的，照事实记。陆苗会写证明，是方叙教的；她的证法里用到的东西，有几样是在这张桌子上学的。这是事实，不因为她后来走了别的路、或在这一处超过了他，就抹去；也不因此成为方叙在她的方向里的一份。

「青出于蓝」不作这段关系的尺子：不拿「学生有没有超过老师」量一位老师，也不拿它量一个学生。

第六节 与传统对接

韩愈：弟子不必不如师。 《师说》：「是故弟子不必不如师，师不必贤于弟子，闻道有先后，术业有专攻，如是而已。」26 相照的一处：师与弟子之分，不是高下之分；学生在一件事上超过老师，不是这段关系里的反常。分开的一处：韩愈拿这一句为「从师」辩护，说的是师可以不必贤；这个系列读的是超过以后，不拿它认证什么。

《荀子》：青，取之于蓝。 《劝学》开篇：「青，取之于蓝，而青于蓝；冰，水为之，而寒于水。」27 这一句后来成了说学生胜过老师的话。回到原文，它接在「学不可以已」后面，说的是学：一个人经过学，可以与原先不同。它不是在量师生高下。这里只取这一处分别：「青出于蓝」说的是学的人自己的变化；这个系列不把它转成师生之间的一把尺。

亚里士多德：真理与朋友。 亚里士多德在《尼各马可伦理学》里批评理念论以前，先说了一段话：提出这一学说的是他亲近的人，这使探究为难；然而为了保全真理，亲近的也得舍去；两者都可亲，以真理为先才合乎虔敬。28 后人把它概括成「吾爱吾师，吾更爱真理」。相照的一处：不认同老师的说法，不是背弃老师这个人；两件事可以分开。当事人是成年人，这里只读结构。

《礼记·学记》：教学相长。 这四个字的原段是：「学然后知不足，教然后知困。知不足，然后能自反也；知困，然后能自强也。故曰：教学相长也。」29 字面上说的是教的人自己因教而知困：教，使教的人看见自己不通的地方。它没有把学与教分属两个人，也没有说老师被学生改动。所以这里不拿它替第三节作证；老师被学生凿开，根据在第三节所引的几处。

史料：刘易斯写柯克帕特里克。 C. S. 刘易斯在自传里写他十几岁的时候，被送到一位退了休的校长柯克帕特里克家里受教。这位老师对随口说出来的话都要追问。刘易斯写，他被打倒的回数够多了，才学会几手招架与还击，长出些思想上的筋骨；到后来，照他自己的说法，他成了一个还拿得出手的对练的人。30 这里只读成年的刘易斯怎样写。他用的是「对练」这个词：两个人在同一个过程里，一来一往。这与第三节相接：互凿按过程判，不按身份判；一个还没有成年的学生，在一件事上可以进到这样一个过程里。他写的是自己这一端；老师的判断有没有被他凿开，这段文字没有说，这里也不替他补。这个系列不据此认证柯克帕特里克在哪个方向上。

第七节 收束与下游

这一篇立了五条。合起来说的是：学生自己的不得不结晶出来，与老师的法、老师对他的打算相遇；叛逆不是它的发生机制。这一段，老师那一端在一次运作里朝 15DD 去，读作认这条不得不是他自己的，即使老师看不好；认不等于改了看法；老师可以把不同意说足，不把它当作学生已经完成的自我认识交过去；回落了，认下那一次，才回应了学生当时的否认，已经发生的照记。互凿按过程判，师生在一件事上实际互相凿开对方的判断，那一件事上就有互凿；从外面先记安排被改了，判断照条件式读；它不认证学生在哪个方向上。两边要求不相容的行动，是否进入冲突论，照两道门逐项核，不由身份、不相容或事情的大小认定；老师不认证学生那一端，也不以未能认证为由替他撤销；没有进入的，照来往与碰撞读，安排被改了就记安排被改了，不记输赢。学生不认同老师、超过老师，不读作背叛，也不拿来认证这段关系或这位老师；那一样本事是这位老师教会的，照事实记；「青出于蓝」不作尺子。

这一篇不证明学生应当超过老师，不证明老师应当让步，也不证明不认同老师是成熟的标志。

下一篇读的。 陆苗会核对证明了；她要往哪里去，站住了；方叙有一天要搬走。这三件，是一件事吗？哪一件是这个系列读到的界？教过的这些年，在她后来的路上算什么？

下游。 成年这条界、检验在学生那一侧、教过不记作偿还、提前结束、此后归谁读，第七篇读。老师被学生看在眼里的那一面，这个系列只登记。
