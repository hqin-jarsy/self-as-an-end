Interpretive Reconstruction of Dream of the Red Chamber · Paper 1 (专题一)

An Audit of Forward References in the Zhiyan Zhai Commentary on the First Eighty Chapters of Dream of the Red Chamber: What Is Adopted and What Is Not
前八十回脂批前指审计：采与不采

Han Qin (秦汉) · Independent Researcher · 2026
DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.23007235 · Full PDF on Zenodo · CC BY 4.0
Abstract

This is the first topical paper in a series on an interpretive reconstruction of the last thirty chapters of Dream of the Red Chamber. It sets out how the project handles every note of the Zhiyan Zhai commentary on the first eighty chapters. The notes are treated as testimony whose provenance must be kept. Some record what a commentator saw in the drafts before him; others are his inferences and judgements. Different notes need not rest on the same draft, and Cao Xueqin may well have had more than one design for the second half of the book. The paper therefore does not rule on the historical truth of any note. It records only how the project treats each one: adopted, adopted with adaptation, half adopted, read differently, or not adopted. For every note not adopted, it states whether the reason is a divergence from the theme the project has chosen, a different plot arrangement, or something else. On this basis all 4,099 notes in the project's base text were audited in tiers. A keyword list yielded 244 candidate forward references, among them 25 items of hard forward reference (28 notes), each ruled on individually. The remaining 3,855 notes were first sampled and then screened in full in four further rounds, which entered 34 missed notes. The project's fulfilment entries for 23 Tier 1 items and for 13 Tier 2 notes that state concrete later events were then put through one round of fulfilment check. An analyst who had not seen the reconstruction broke each note, using only the note and the passage it comments on, into propositions about later events, and these propositions were checked one by one against the reconstruction. Of thirty-six units checked, the check changed the entries for sixteen, in both directions. Because the materials prepared for the blind breakdown had an extraction defect, fifteen of the notes were broken down a second time by a different model. Finally, the notes not adopted are gathered together, and the other second halves that can be read out of them are set down as they stand, to show why the project did not adopt them, and that what the project wrote is only one reading. The full audit table and the check records are published with the paper.

1 Stance: The Commentary as Testimony

A certain class of notes in the Zhiyan Zhai commentary speaks of what happens after chapter 80: "the last thirty chapters," "the Prison Temple," "Wei Ruolan's archery ground," "letting go at the cliff's edge," "Jinghuan's Roster of Feeling," and remarks such as "this foreshadows her early death in the later text" or "when the family's affairs collapse." Continuations, the search for the lost ending (tanyi), and reconstructions have always treated them as clues to the second half of the book. This project has used them in the same way. What this paper sets out is how the project handles each such note, and why.

A word of background for readers new to the field. Dream of the Red Chamber circulated in the 1750s and 1760s as a manuscript of about eighty chapters, copied together with a running commentary written by readers close to the author, chief among them the figures known as Zhiyan Zhai ("Red Inkstone Studio") and Jihu Sou ("the Old Man of the Odd Tablet"). The surviving manuscripts, conventionally named by a cyclical year recorded on them, by a former owner or by a preface (Jiaxu, Jimao, Gengchen, Mengfu, Qixu, and the lost Jing manuscript, known only from transcribed notes), differ in the commentary they carry. Many notes point ahead to what happens later in the story, some to events within the eighty chapters and some to events the eighty chapters do not reach. This paper calls such notes forward references; its concern is chiefly with those that reach beyond chapter 80.

Three principles come first.

The commentary is testimony whose provenance must be kept. Some notes record what a commentator saw in the drafts before him; others are his inferences, judgements, wishes and hypotheses; and different notes need not rest on the same draft. The book was made, as its first chapter says, by "ten years of reading and revising, five rounds of additions and deletions," and the second half may well have had more than one design; there was also more than one commentator. When two notes disagree, it may be because two drafts differed, or because one commentator's inference differs from another's report; it does not follow that one of them is wrong. So this paper does not rule on the historical truth of any note, and does not call any note a "mistaken note" or "the commentary's own error."

What the audit records is the project's handling. For each note, the paper records only how the project treats it: adopted (fulfilled), adopted with adaptation (transposed fulfilment), half adopted (half fulfilled), read differently, not adopted, or reserved (kept without a ruling). What is recorded is the project's choice, not the truth of the note.

Where a note is not adopted, the paper states only where the divergence lies. The reason given for not adopting a note says only which of the project's choices it cannot go with. It may diverge from the theme the project has adopted: from the overview's "people have qing," and from the readings chosen by the criterion "persons are ends in themselves." It may simply be that a plot arrangement already fixed by the project cannot coexist with it, which is a creative choice. It may conflict with the text of the first eighty chapters, or two notes may be at odds so that only one can be taken. In no case does the paper say that the note is wrong.

Given these three principles, the value of the audit lies not in rulings but in leaving a trace: every forward reference the project has identified has a record of how it was handled, which the reader can check item by item, or, taking a different theme, re-rule item by item.

2 Why an Audit

When the project was writing the last thirty chapters, it first worked "from back to front": it went looking for commentary to support what had been written. That can show that what was written has support; it cannot show that everything with support was written. Nor did the notes that were not adopted leave any trace, so that one could not tell "examined and not adopted" from "never seen." The audit supplies that missing link: all the notes on the first eighty chapters are gone through, and every note that points to later events is given a record of how it was handled.

The audit only records; it does not revise the reconstruction. Whether the reconstruction is to be rewritten because of a note is for the author to rule on separately; where such a ruling leads to supplementary writing, the entry says that the fulfilment came after that writing (§6.4).

4 Screening and Tiers

4.1 Keyword screening and three tiers

Candidates for forward reference were first separated from the 4,099 passages with a keyword list. The list developed in batches. The first Tier 1 list was built around "lost" (迷失), "no manuscript" (无稿), "Roster of Feeling" (情榜), "Prison Temple" (狱神庙), "archery ground" (射圃), "letting go" (撒手) and "after several tens of chapters" (数十回后). In the course of the audit it turned out that "great turning" (大过节), "great crux" (大关键), "later events" (后事), "together to the end" (同终始) and "the last thirty chapters" (后三十回) had been missed, and these were added, followed by "great episode" (大节目), "another day" (他日), "last age" (末世), "final outcome" (归结), "second half" (后半) and others; two homographs, 伏侍 and 伏待 ("to wait upon," which contain the character 伏, "foreshadow"), were excluded. The final list has 45 terms. Matching is by substring, on the whole text inside the brackets including the manuscript label (Appendix 2). Rerunning the final list over all the notes yields 244 forward-reference candidates.

The candidates fall into three tiers:

  1. Tier 1 (hard forward references): notes that point directly to events after chapter 80, such as the lost drafts, the Roster of Feeling, the Prison Temple, the archery ground, letting go, "the last thirty chapters," "great crux." There are 25 items, numbered T1-01 to T1-25 (甲01 to 甲25 in the data), comprising 28 notes (some items combine two notes with the same wording or at the same place), all ruled on individually.
  2. Tier 2 (forward-reference candidates): the other candidates, routed by where their reference lands.

- Tier 2a: notes whose reference lands within the first eighty chapters are recorded as answered within the first eighty chapters; only the note number is recorded, without individual argument. - Tier 2b: notes whose reference reaches beyond chapter 80 are ruled on individually. - Among the candidates there is also a group that are only appraisal, praise, remarks on technique or instructions for reading, and point to no later event; these are recorded as not forward references.

  1. Tier 3 (the rest): 3,855 notes.

This is the initial division. Later checks and reviews changed the handling of some items (several Tier 2b notes, for example, were re-recorded as answered within the first eighty chapters). The data keep the initial division in the column "initial tier" and record the current ruling in the column "handling." The one exception is four notes, 1.73, 3.171, 7.41 and 52.4, initially placed in Tier 2b, whose reference was found during the fulfilment check to lie within the first eighty chapters; their initial tier was changed to Tier 2a (§7.2).

4.2 Sampling and supplementary screening of Tier 3

Tier 3 is not ruled on note by note; it is only tested for notes that the keyword list missed. A missed note counts only if it is a forward reference that reaches beyond chapter 80: the note must point to, or allow one to infer, an event after chapter 80. Notes that only lament without pointing to anything (such as "I sigh for Xiren," "it makes my nose sting") and notes whose reference lands within the first eighty chapters are not counted.

Sampling. With the fixed seed 5432, 382 notes, about one tenth, were drawn from Tier 3. The sampling program, the note numbers of the sample and the checksum of the original sample file are given in Appendix 2, so that anyone can reproduce the draw. To reduce the blind spots of the first-order keyword list, a second-order screen was set up along three axes, fate, time and lament, using terms different from those of the first-order list. The difference in terms only means that the two sets of rules differ; it does not make them independent in any statistical or semantic sense. The second-order screen hit 27 notes in the sample. Each was read, and two were confirmed as forward references reaching beyond chapter 80: 13.3 (Qin Keqing's dream visit, a note with missing characters) and 28.106 ("here the later writing on the two Baos is made plain"). The figure 2/382 is only the rate at which this screening procedure found such notes in the sample. The 355 notes that the screen did not hit were not ruled on individually at this step, so the figure cannot be used to estimate the rate of remaining missed notes in Tier 3 as a whole.

4.3 Regression reconciliation against the final list

The keyword list grew in batches, and each batch used a different list, so gaps could open between batches. For example, 1.94, a Jiaxu side note, "another Jinghuan appears, all at points of great crux": the first-batch list had no "great crux," and the later rescan list, which did have it, reported only new additions, so the note was entered in neither. At the close of the audit, therefore, the final keyword list was rerun over the whole body and reconciled number by number against the notes entered in the table; wherever the count entered and the count found by machine disagreed, the discrepancy was traced note by note. This reconciliation turned up two notes, 1.94 and 20.2, which have since been ruled on; the entered count and the machine count now both stand at 244 and match one to one. For notes with missing characters the keyword may lie in the lost text, which no machine screen can reach; these must be gone through by hand.

5 Handling and Routing

5.1 Categories of handling

Category Meaning
fulfilledthe plot of the reconstruction satisfies what the note says
transposed fulfilmentthe letter of the note is fulfilled but its referent is rewritten; or its timing or placing is adapted
half fulfilledof the several layers of what the note says, some are fulfilled and the rest are not adopted

There are two further marks. Referent inferred: the note says only "a foreshadowing line," "foreshadows what follows," "a line laid a thousand li ahead," "an omen of dispersal," without saying what is foreshadowed, so that the referent is the project's inference. Such notes are recorded as "fulfilled (referent inferred)," with the ground of the inference stated; what is fulfilled is the project's inference, not anything the note says outright. Tier 1 items T1-02 and T1-04 are of this kind; the check showed only that the reconstruction satisfies the project's inference, not that the note admits only this reading. Negative: where a note states a negative proposition, of the kind "seen only this once" or "never again," and the reconstruction still does not write the event by the point where the matter is finally settled, the note is satisfied, and is recorded as "fulfilled (negative)," as with 18.128, 44.2 and 25.104.

There are also two destinations. Moved to paper 5: notes that decode prophetic lines are handled in paper 5 together with the lines they decode (for the boundary see §5.3). Filed as a record: notes recording the state of the base text or of the book's making (such as "Mid-Autumn poem missing; awaiting Xueqin"), which fall outside the scope of this audit; they are filed and left unruled.

"Fulfilled" is not evidence. "Fulfilled" records a fact about the entry. Most of the reconstruction was written according to the commentary, so fulfilment is a result of construction; the number of fulfilments is not evidence that the reconstruction is close to the original. The title of Rec. ch. 90 takes over directly the fourteen characters quoted in the chapter-head note on chapter 21 (T1-21); this is direct adoption, and still less is it evidence.

5.2 Three rules

  1. The count is fixed; entries are layered. Each bracketed passage 【】 is one row, and the counts do not change. Where one note says several things, each layer is entered: the main handling records the layer that reaches beyond chapter 80, and the other layers are written in the column "routing and non-adoption" and in the explanation.
  2. A negative proposition is judged where it is settled. Notes such as "never again," "seen only this once" or "though the matters were not carried out" count as fulfilled only at the point where the matter is finally settled. Where the first eighty chapters show only a first sign, or a temporary obstacle, this is recorded as a beginning within the first eighty chapters, and the note as a whole is not counted as answered.
  3. Duplicated notes are entered in one place. Notes with the same wording in different manuscripts are entered at one place and marked "duplicate."

5.3 The boundary for notes that decode prophecy

Prophetic texts themselves (the verdicts, the songs, lantern riddles, poems read as prophecy, drinking-game lines, the "Won-Done Song" gloss) are handled in paper 5. Where a note, in its own words, states an event that can be checked (going to marry far away, becoming a nun, "never again," ruin, death, an elegy and the like), it is a forward reference and is entered in this paper, marked "copied to paper 5." Where a note only uses the word "prophecy" (谶), only says "foreshadows what follows" or "the future state," or only matches a prophetic line to a person or place ("such-and-such and her like," "the Xiaoxiang Lodge, the Purple Rue Studio and such places"), it moves to paper 5. The test can be put in one sentence: a note stays in this paper only if it clearly points to an identifiable person or family in the book and states an event that can be checked. The referent may be fixed by an existing passage of the text that the note quotes or clearly refers back to, and that passage need not come before the note. "When the tree falls, the monkeys scatter" is said by Qin Keqing in chapter 13 and refers to the Jia family, so 5.125 (on chapter 5) and 22.97, which quote the saying, both stay in this paper. The "who" can be fixed by a quoted phrase, but the "what" must be in the words of the note: 79.23, "it is precisely this kind of thing that comes to pass," has only a pronoun for its event, so it moves. A generic term does not count as a "who": 1.177, "speaking of the day after the parents' death," only explains when "the later day" is, and "parents" names no particular family, so it moves to paper 5 along with the prophetic lines. This is a working rule for dividing labour between two papers of the project, not a general definition of foreshadowing. Paper 5 takes over, as listed, the decoding notes that this paper leaves to it, and does not enter them again. Twelve notes are currently copied to paper 5: 1.168, 1.170, 5.125, 18.114 to 18.117, 18.128, 22.97, 22.103, 22.105 and 79.5.

R1 to R3 are not refusals: a note that is not a forward reference has nothing to adopt; a note answered within the first eighty chapters needs no adoption; and "read differently" means two readings standing side by side. N5 allows "the author chose to write it another way" to stop at a creative choice, without being raised every time into a divergence of theme. N7 has not been used anywhere in the table so far; its definition is kept on record. 18.17, recorded in the original audit table as "the commentary's own error," is now N8, and the reason given is only that it conflicts with the text of chapter 5 and the text is followed. 7 The Fulfilment Check

7.1 What is checked

The fulfilment check checks the project's own entries: where the project says a note is "fulfilled," does the plot of the reconstruction really satisfy what that note says? It does not touch the question of whether the notes are true or false. There are only two possible outcomes: the entry is upheld, or the project's entry must change.

The overview called this step a "reverse audit," saying that it "breaks a note into falsifiable propositions and then tries to falsify them against the text of the reconstruction." That sentence can easily be read as setting out to falsify the commentary, which does not accord with the stance of §1. From this paper on the step is called a fulfilment check, and the next version of the overview will be revised accordingly.

7.2 Scope

Not every note entered as fulfilment can be checked. At the time of the check, 62 Tier 2b notes were entered as "fulfilled," but only 13 actually stated a concrete later event from which propositions could be drawn. 34 said only "foreshadowing," the referent being inferred by the project; these cannot be checked against the note, and could only be re-recorded as "referent inferred." 11 were prophecy notes, moved to paper 5. The other 4 had been wrongly entered and were in fact answered within the first eighty chapters. (Reviews and the fourth round of screening later changed a few more: in Class 2, 1.77 and 3.79 were re-recorded (§6.2), and 1.177 moved to paper 5 with the group of notes on the "Won-Done Song" gloss, so that Class 2 now has 31 notes; in Class 3, 19.109 was re-recorded as answered within the first eighty chapters and 79.5 was brought back to this paper, and with 1.177 added Class 3 now has 10.)

The scope of this round was therefore the 23 Tier 1 items with a fulfilment entry (fulfilled, transposed or half fulfilled), plus the 13 Class 1 notes of Tier 2b: thirty-six units in all (Tier 1 counted by audit item, where an item may contain two notes with the same wording). Class 2 is not checked; the notes entered in the fourth round were found after the check, and have not been checked either.

Before this, six groups of targeted tests had been run (overview §9.3), and several items had been checked while the overview was being written.

7.3 Procedure

Blind breakdown. First an analyst was given only the notes and the passages they comment on (about 260 characters before the note and 120 after), without the reconstruction and without the audit table, and was asked to break down what each note says about later events into propositions, written as "who | what event or situation | when | whether negative or not yet realized," and to check against the full text of the first eighty chapters whether the later text the note speaks of already appears within them. The point was to keep the project's answers from being read into the questions.

Check. Next, the check was made in two groups: one checker for the 23 Tier 1 items and one for the 13 Class 1 notes of Tier 2b, so that each note was examined by only one checker. The checkers took the propositions from the blind breakdown, compared them one by one with the reconstruction, quoted the relevant text word for word, and judged by these guidelines:

  1. whether identity and situation are fulfilled; the rhetoric of a note is not checked item by item as a specification (for example "black robes and begging for food"; see below);
  2. where the same name has different referents, check by meaning;
  3. timing: whether the order the note states holds in the reconstruction;
  4. agent and patient: who does what to whom;
  5. negation and the not-yet-realized (such as "though the matters were not carried out") must also be checked;
  6. read the whole passage before judging;
  7. where the blind breakdown says a note may already be answered within the first eighty chapters, judge whether that is so.

The checkers' recommendations were ruled on by the author one by one.

Who did it. The blind analyst and the two checkers were all newly opened AI sessions, using the model Claude Opus 5.5, the same model as the consolidating editor who wrote the entries; the date was 27 September 2026 (UTC). The blind analyst's tool-call log shows that it read only the blind materials and the base text of the first eighty chapters (it also listed the directory they were in once; see Attachment 12). "It was not given the reconstruction" can be verified from the record. "It had never before encountered the project's answers" can be asserted only to the extent that a new session carries none of the project's context; the model was the same, so the independence is limited. The prompts and run records are in Attachment 12.

One thing that must be stated plainly. The example given in the blind prompt under "rhetoric and specification" was "black robes and begging for food is only a way of saying 'a person who has left the household life,'" which is exactly the author's ruling on 22.105; in effect, the answer was carried into the question. This was changed for the second breakdown (§7.5). The two checkers' prompts gave the same example in the first check guideline. The checkers could see the current entries and the author's existing rulings, and were asked to apply the rulings and to note separately any doubts (this was by design: the check asks whether the entries are accurate, not to re-rule in the author's place), so on 22.105 the check was not independent. This ruling on the rhetoric of 22.105 is recorded as the author's reading. Since the Sui and Tang dynasties, Chinese Buddhism has combined farming with meditation (Baizhang Huaihai's saying "a day without work, a day without food," in the chapter on Baizhang Huaihai in Wudeng huiyuan, juan 3), and going out with the begging bowl is not the ordinary practice of a convent; the reconstruction writes that Xichun lives a hard, frugal life at the Zhengjue Convent, and only identity and situation are checked. This is a general institutional norm; it explains only why the author does not take "begging for food" literally, and is not used to conclude that the words of the note cannot refer to actual begging. Nor does it serve as a general exemption.

What was checked against. Four compilations of chapters 81–110 of the reconstruction, each identified by checksum (Appendix 3).

8 Root Causes of Misjudgement, and Procedures

The misjudgements found so far come down to a few root causes:

  1. taking fragmentary text as settled text (the first ruling on T1-24);
  2. taking word frequency as meaning (the ancestral graves in T1-20; 13.89 and 28.110 in the check after the second breakdown, where in the case of 13.89 the entry's failure to state its grounds also played a part);
  3. taking the rhetoric of a note as a practical specification (the first ruling on "black robes and begging for food" in 22.105);
  4. quoting a song after reading only half of it (recorded in overview §9.1);
  5. assigning a tier by keyword without first rereading the passage commented on (1.185 attached to the wrong place);
  6. not first searching the first eighty chapters before ruling that a forward reference reaches beyond chapter 80 (1.73, 3.171, 7.41, 52.4, 44.12, 3.79, the desolation layer of 45.15 and others);
  7. recording the wrong person in an entry (37.54);
  8. not revising the entry after an addendum had been carried out (19.36);
  9. a first-order keyword list lacking the single character 伏 and lacking 后来, 后日 and 兆, and a second-order screen run only over the sample (24.3, 24.61, 6.1, 45.12 and others);
  10. notes placed under the lines of a prophetic text to decode them contain no keyword, and so cannot be caught by a word screen (the notes on the "Won-Done Song" gloss);
  11. giving a whole bracketed passage a single judgement when it contains several layers (19.80, 18.128, 45.15, 38.1);
  12. in extracting context, opening the window first and removing notes afterwards, so that other notes get in at the edge of the window;
  13. carrying the project's own ruling into the blind prompt;
  14. an entry giving only the fulfilment chapter, not the grounds (13.89);
  15. in comparing old and new propositions, treating a change in the time reading as a minor difference and not sending it to be checked (26.153);
  16. notes that only lament, without pointing to anything, being counted among the missed notes because of a keyword (13.14);
  17. in assigning a type, taking the project's inferential reading of a note for the note's own words (the N9 of T1-12);
  18. a doubt noted by a checker not being answered point by point in the ruling (the register of T1-03);
  19. a rule being set up without all existing entries being swept again (44.2, whose explanation already treated it as a negative proposition while its main handling was not so marked).

The procedures set up in response:

  1. collate fragmentary text before ruling on it;
  2. check cases of the same name with different referents by meaning (ancestral graves, convents, unicorns, registers, dreams);
  3. do not read rhetoric as a specification; check only identity and situation, but say why the words are rhetoric;
  4. read a whole song or passage before quoting it;
  5. reread the passage commented on before assigning a tier;
  6. before ruling that a forward reference reaches beyond chapter 80, first search the first eighty chapters;
  7. once an addendum has been carried out, revise the entry;
  8. run a regression reconciliation against the final keyword list;
  9. go through notes with missing characters by hand;
  10. in a fulfilment check, first break down the propositions blind, then compare with the reconstruction;
  11. run the second-order screen over the whole body, not only the sample;
  12. screen separately for single-character forward-reference words (伏, 兆) and for 后来 and 后日;
  13. go through, by position, the notes placed under the lines of prophetic texts;
  14. where one note contains several layers, enter them in layers (§5.2);
  15. for context, first remove the notes from the whole chapter, then cut the excerpt, and assert that it contains no 【】;
  16. examples in a blind prompt must not be taken from the project's rulings;
  17. an entry gives the fulfilment chapter together with its grounds;
  18. in comparing old and new propositions, go through the three kinds of discrepancy fixed in advance column by column, and do not decide on one's own that a difference is minor;
  19. before counting a note as missed, first separate out, by the stated definition, the notes that only lament without pointing;
  20. before recording two notes as at odds, first confirm that their words conflict;
  21. answer the doubts in a checker's output point by point in the ruling, stating whether each is adopted or not;
  22. whenever a new rule is set up, sweep all the entries again.

9 Other Second Halves in the Commentary

If one gathers the notes that were not adopted, the unadopted halves of the half-fulfilled notes, the side set aside where two notes are at odds, the points adapted away in the transposed fulfilments, and the notes read differently, and reads the book by them, one can make out the outlines of several other second halves. This section sets them out one by one, to say why the project did not adopt them. It does not reconstruct these second halves, nor judge whether they are right. Each is set out under three heads: what the commentary says outright; what can be read out along this line; and how the project handles it.

1. Baoyu marries Baochai and they become husband and wife, with Sheyue beside them; later Baoyu "casts them aside and becomes a monk"; Qiguan and Xiren look after "Brother Jade and Baochai," together to the end.

  • The commentary says: 20.45, "after Xiren marries, there is still one person beside Baoyu and Baochai"; 20.72, "later, when they have become husband and wife," with "the feeling of talking over old times"; 21.65, "had another man had Baochai for wife and Sheyue for maid, how could he cast them aside and become a monk?"; 28.1, "[Qiguan] in the later chapters, with Xiren, looks after Brother Jade and Baochai, together to the end."
  • Read along this line: Baoyu and Baochai really become husband and wife and live together after the wedding, with Sheyue beside them, looked after by Qiguan and Xiren; later Baoyu casts them aside and becomes a monk. 21.65 speaks of casting aside, 28.1 of being together to the end; the two notes can be read as one following the other (the looking after first, the casting aside later), or as coming from two drafts.
  • The divergence: what the project adopts is Baoyu choosing for himself to leave, not having them and casting them aside; Baochai's greatest difficulty is her status, not a life of being looked after; and after his awakening Baoyu returns to the human world and lives as a lay disciple, without taking the tonsure (overview §§6.4, 7.2). Recorded as N4; the words of 67.1, "why then shave the head?", are about Xianglian, and its implication serves only as supporting evidence (§6.1, T1-12 and T1-25).
  • The project: Baoyu walks away when the engagement is fixed and the wedding is at hand, and Baochai's status is settled after the event; Sheyue beside them, and Qiguan and Xiren looking after Baoyu, are each half fulfilled.

2. Baochai and Daiyu as one person.

  • The commentary says: 42.1, "Chai and Yu are two in name but one person; this is a stroke of illusion … see the writing on Baochai after Daiyu's passing, and you will know my words are not mistaken."
  • Read along this line: this is first of all the commentator's reading of chapter 42. That Baochai and Daiyu "merge into one" can be an interpretation of the structure of the characters and of the writing; it need not be an event. What it expects is that the writing on Baochai after Daiyu's death will bear this reading out.
  • The project: reads Daiyu as the core, each of the two complete in herself (overview §6.3). The Rhapsody on Qingqing says: "You are yourself, not the ground of my being; I too am myself, and do not wait on you to be made clear." Recorded as read differently. After Daiyu's death there is indeed writing on Baochai: in Rec. ch. 108 Daiyu enters Baochai's dream and says, "That is something you wrote yourself. This stroke, no one can make for you." The scene can be a candidate for a response, but what it writes is each of the two on her own, and this paper does not use it to fulfil the note.

3. Baoyu enters the Roster of Feeling as qing bu qing; the Roster lists everyone, and fixes the principal, secondary, further secondary, and third and fourth secondary registers.

  • The commentary says: 8.140, "according to Jinghuan's Roster of Feeling, Baoyu is 'feeling for the unfeeling'"; 19.80, "later, in the Roster, one sees the epithets 'Baoyu: feeling for the unfeeling,' 'Daiyu: feeling for those who feel'"; 18.19, "only at the chapter's end, with Jinghuan's Roster of Feeling, will one know the names in the principal, secondary, further secondary, and third and fourth secondary registers."
  • Read along this line: men and women are listed together on the Roster, and the registers are fixed. In the commentary the two epithets are praise.
  • The divergence: the Roster of Feeling is alive and not fixed once for all (overview §6.2), N4; the meaning of qing bu qing and qingqing the project reads differently, recorded as read differently.
  • The project: Rec. ch. 110 writes the Roster of Feeling, listing only women and not the whole roster; qing bu qing falls in the chapter title and in the sharp shout in the Office of the Ill-Fated.

4. Only on the day of the meeting at the Prison Temple does one learn that Qiaojie's "meeting disaster, she finds good fortune; meeting ill, she turns it to good" was a line laid long before.

  • The commentary says: 42.6, "only on the day of the meeting at the Prison Temple will one know that 'meeting disaster she finds good fortune, meeting ill she turns it to good' was a line laid a thousand li ahead; alas, alas! The writing that follows is too painful to read through."
  • Read along this line: the meeting at the Prison Temple is bound up with Qiaojie's rescue.
  • The project: the meeting at the Prison Temple is between Baoyu and Qianxue and Xiaohong; Qiaojie's rescue comes after it, carried out by Grannie Liu and her party, and is written as a chance encounter, not as a reward for hidden merit (overview Appendix 5, the row on "Blessings Left Behind"). This is a different plot arrangement, not a matter of theme; N5, and the note is only half fulfilled.

5. Brother Yun's chivalrous visit to the convent.

  • The commentary says: 24.3, Jing, "the chapter on the Drunken Diamond foreshadows Brother Yun's chivalrous visit to the convent."
  • Read along this line: Jia Yun acts chivalrously, in a way that answers to the chapter on Ni Er, the Drunken Diamond, and what he visits is a convent; whom he visits the note does not say.
  • The project: Jia Yun's chivalry is written at the Prison Temple: he visits the prisoners in Rec. ch. 92, and in Rec. ch. 94 he and Ni Er and others get into the Prison Temple by a ruse. The "visit to the convent" is not written; to read the convent as the Prison Temple would be forced, and it is not taken. This too is a different plot arrangement, N5; half fulfilled.

6. Letting go in the last chapter.

  • The commentary says: 67.1, "the 'letting go' in the last chapter is that of one already awakened."
  • Read along this line: Baoyu's awakening and letting go are placed in the last chapter of the book; the note does not say whether it is the last event of that chapter.
  • The project: letting go is in Rec. ch. 106; the last chapter writes the Roster of Feeling and the return of the jade to Greensickness Peak; transposed fulfilment. That Baoyu has several chapters of life in the human world after letting go is required by the third level of awakening that the project writes (overview §6.4).

7. Had Tanchun not gone far away, the sons and grandsons might have escaped being scattered when the family fell.

  • The commentary says: 22.103, "this is the prophecy of Tanchun marrying far away. Had she not gone far away, then when the family's affairs collapsed, its sons and grandsons would not have been scattered. How sad, how grievous!"
  • Read along this line: Tanchun's going is one reason why the sons and grandsons were scattered when the family fell; had she been there, the family's affairs might have been put in order. This is a counterfactual of the commentator's, and also a kind of hope.
  • The project: the layer of marrying far away is fulfilled. Because she did in fact marry far away, the antecedent of the counterfactual is never realized; so the reconstruction's writing Tanchun's distant marriage does not refute it, and cannot prove it true either. The layer "had she not gone far away" does not assert what the reconstruction ought to write; it is an understanding of why the family fell. The project's narration gives no explanation in terms of cause and retribution, and writes the family scattered but not extinguished; recorded as read differently.

8. Preaching the Law through feeling, to awaken the world.

  • The commentary says: 35.26, "this chapter preaches the Law through feeling, to awaken the world … the river of love is bottomless; how can one let it overflow? Once drowned in it, one does not stop short of death."
  • Read along this line: this note is not a forward reference, but it reads the whole book as a teaching to warn the world: feeling is a river in which one is bound to drown, and once drowned, one does not stop short of death.
  • The project: the narrator gives no explanation in terms of retribution (overview §4.5); recorded as read differently.

9. The three verdicts of the Tertiary Register are Qingwen, Xiren and Xiangling; Ping'er is in the Tertiary Register.

  • The commentary says: 18.17, "又有又副册三断词乃晴雯、袭人、香菱三人，余未多及，想为金钏、玉钏、鸳鸯、苗云（下有按语一句，略）、平儿等人无疑矣" ("then there are the three verdicts of the Tertiary Register, which are Qingwen, Xiren and Xiangling; the rest are not much touched on; presumably they are Jinchuan, Yuchuan, Yuanyang, Miaoyun [one sentence of comment follows here; omitted], Ping'er and others, without doubt"); Jihu's upper-margin note in the same chapter (18.19, that is, T1-06) admits that "the twelve beauties cited here are none of them certain; all are guesses"; and 6.48 says of Ping'er, "presumably she too is in the Secondary Register."
  • Read along this line: the three verdicts of the Tertiary Register belong to Qingwen, Xiren and Xiangling, and Xiangling is not in the Secondary Register. This conflicts with the text of chapter 5, where Xiangling is in the Secondary Register; it may be another draft of chapter 5, or a guess of the commentator's. As to the others in the Tertiary Register, the commentator presumes them to be Jinchuan, Yuchuan, Yuanyang, Miaoyun, Ping'er and others, which puts Ping'er in the Tertiary Register; 6.48 instead says of Ping'er, "presumably she too is in the Secondary Register." The two notes place her in different registers.
  • The project: for the three verdicts, the text is followed, N8; for Ping'er, 6.48 is taken, and Rec. ch. 110 has been supplemented so that Ping'er's page is in the Secondary Register (Addendum 003); this layer of 18.17 is N9.

10. Writing lost from the original, or deleted by the author.

  • The commentary says: the original drafts of the Prison Temple and archery ground chapters were lost (20.20, 26.24, 26.120, 26.153); and by the commentator's own account in 13.8, he once ordered Qinxi to delete the passages on the "hairpin left behind" and "changing clothes" (this account is found only in the Jing manuscript).
  • Read along this line: the second half originally had a long chapter on the Prison Temple and the writing on Wei Ruolan at the archery ground, and the earlier chapters originally had Qin Keqing's "hairpin left behind" and "changing clothes." These are not another design, but text that was lost or deleted.
  • The project: either writes them afresh (the Prison Temple, the archery ground) or does not write them (the hairpin, the changing of clothes); in neither case is the original form reconstructed; N6.

The project adopts none of these second halves, or adopts only part of them. They are recorded here as they stand, to show two things. First, going by the commentary itself, Cao Xueqin's second half may well have had more than one design, and what the commentators saw and thought differed; this is a possibility, and the paper does not claim on this basis that several such complete drafts actually existed. Second, what the project wrote is one reading, chosen by the criterion "persons are ends in themselves" from among the readings the evidence still allows; with a different criterion, many of the "not adopted" here could become "adopted." The commentary here is not an opponent to be refuted, but the witness to several other possibilities. The overview says that "the Roster of Feeling is alive"; this holds for the reconstruction itself as well.

10 Conclusion

The conclusion of the audit can be put briefly. For every note on the first eighty chapters that the project has identified as pointing to later events, a record has been left, note by note: which were adopted and how, which were not adopted and why, the reader can check. The fulfilment check then went through the project's own entries again and changed a good many of them; the supplementary screening found a batch of overlooked notes, some of them well known; and after the paper was sent for review, the reviewers and the second breakdown forced out several layerings and several root causes. No note has been judged wrong here; what has been tested is only the project.

1 立场：批语是证词

脂批里有一类话，说的是八十回以后的事：“后三十回”“狱神庙”“卫若兰射圃”“悬崖撒手”“警幻情榜”，还有“伏下后文短命”“将来事败”之类。续书、探佚、复原，历来把它们当作后半部的线索。本项目也是这样用的。本文要交代的，是本项目对每一条这样的批语怎样处理，以及为什么。

先立三条。

批语是需保留来路的证词材料。批语里有对所见稿本的记述，也有批者的推断、评议、愿望与假设；不同批语所据未必同稿。书是“披阅十载，增删五次”改出来的，下半部很可能有过不止一种设计，批者也不止一人。两条批语说法不同，可能是两稿不同，也可能是一人的推断与另一人的见闻不同，不一定有一条说错了。所以本文不裁批语的历史真伪，也不说哪一条是“误批”“自误”。

审计登记的是本项目的处理。对每一条批语，本文只记本项目怎样处理它：采（兑现），变通地采（改兑现），采一半（半兑现），理解不同，不采，或存而不判。登记的对象是本项目的取舍，不是批语的真伪。

不采，只说分歧所在。不采的一条，理由只写它与本项目哪一项取舍不合：与本项目所取的主旨有分歧，也就是与总篇所立的“人是有情的”，与以“人是目的”为判准择出的那些读法，走不到一起；或者只是本项目已定的情节布置与之不能并存，属创作取舍；或者与前八十回正文不合，或两条批语互异而只能取其一。都不说批语错了。

由这三条，审计的价值不在裁决，而在留痕：本项目已识别的每一条前指，都有一个处理记录，读者可以逐条查，也可以换一个主旨，逐条改判。

2 为什么要审计

本项目写后三十回的时候，原先是“从后往前”找依据：为已写的情节去找批语。这样做只能证明所写有据，不能证明有据者都写了；不采的批语也不留痕，分不清“审过而不采”与“根本没看见”。审计补的就是这一环：把前八十回的全部批语过一遍，每一条指向后事的批语，都留下处理记录。

审计只记录，不改稿。复原本要不要因某条批语改写，由作者另裁；另裁而补写的，登记写明是补写后的兑现（6.4 节）。

4.3 终版表回归对账

关键词表是分批加词的，各批用表不同，批次之间会留下夹缝。例如 1.94 甲戌侧批“又出一警幻，皆大关键处”：第一批词表无“大关键”，后来的回扫表有此词，却只报新收，两头都没有登记。所以审计收尾时，以终版词表在全量上重跑一次，与表内登记的批号逐号对账；登记数与机筛数不符，就逐条追平。本轮对账追出 1.94 与 20.2 两条，已补裁；现在登记与机筛都是 244 条，一一相符。缺字批的关键词可能就在阙文里，机筛无解，须人工过。

5.1 处理档

另有两个标注。所指推定：批语只说“伏线”“伏下后文”“千里伏线”“将散之兆”，没说伏的是什么事，所指是本项目推出来的。这类登记为“已兑现（所指推定）”，写明推定的依据；它兑现的是本项目的推定，不是批语的明言。甲层的甲02、甲04 也属此类，经过复核，也只证明复原本满足了本项目的推定，不证明批语只容许这一解。否定：批语说的是“只此一见”“不再”一类否定命题，复原本到其落定之处仍不写其事，即为满足，登记为“已兑现（否定）”，如 18.128、44.2、25.104。

另有两种去向。移专题五：解谶之批，与所谶之文一并在专题五处理（界线见 5.3 节）。报存：底本或成书状况之记（如“缺中秋诗俟雪芹”），不属本审计口径，存而不裁。

“已兑现”不作证据。已兑现记的是登记之实。复原本多是依批而写的，兑现由构造所致；兑现的多少，不作复原本近于原稿的证据。第九十回回目直接用了第二十一回回前批所举的十四字（甲21），是直采，更不作证据。

5.2 三条规矩

  1. 计数不变，登记分层。一处【】仍算一行，统计不变。一条批里有几层话，就分层登记：主处理记其中跨到八十回以后的那一层，其余各层写入“分流与不采”一栏与说明。
  2. 否定命题要看落定处。“不再”“只此一见”“其事虽未行”这类批语，要到事情最终落定的地方才算兑现；前八十回里只露出苗头、或者一时受阻的，记为前八十回内之端，不算整条已应。
  3. 重出之批同处登记。不同本子里同文的批，登记在同一处，注明“重出”。

5.3 解谶之批的界线

谶文本身（判词、曲文、灯谜、诗谶、酒令、好了歌注），在专题五处理。批语自己的字里说出了一件可以核的事（远适、为尼、不再、败、死、诔之类），就是一条前指，在本文登记，注“抄送专题五”；只点一个“谶”字，只说“伏后文”“将来形景”，或只把谶文对到某人某处（“某某一干人”“潇湘馆、紫芸轩等处”），就移专题五。测法是一句：批语明确指向书中可辨认的人或家，并说出可核的事，才留本文。所指可由批语明引或明确回指的已存正文确定，不限在该批以前：“树倒猢狲散”出第十三回秦氏之口，说的是贾家，所以引这句俗语的 5.125（批在第五回）、22.97 都留本文。“谁”可由所引之语确定，“何事”却须在批语字面：79.23“偏是此等事体等到”，事是代词，所以移。泛称不算“谁”：1.177“言父母死后之日”只解释“日后”的时点，“父母”未指明哪一家，随谶文移专题五。这是本项目在两篇专题之间分工的工作规则，不是关于一切伏笔的普遍定义。专题五照单接过本文留下的解谶批，不重复登记。现在抄送专题五的有十二条：1.168、1.170、5.125、18.114 至 18.117、18.128、22.97、22.103、22.105、79.5。

7 兑现复核

7.1 查的是什么

兑现复核查的是本项目自己的登记：本项目说某条批语“已兑现”，复原本的情节层是不是真的满足了那条批语所说的事。它不碰批语的真伪。可能的结果只有两种：维持，或本项目的登记要改。

总篇称这一步为“反向审计”，说它“把批语拆成可证伪的命题，再用复原本文本去证伪”。那句话容易读成要证伪批语，不合第 1 节的立场。本文起改称“兑现复核”，总篇下一版随改。

7.2 范围

7.3 做法

盲拆。先请一位分析者，只看批语与所批正文（批语前约 260 字、后约 120 字），不看复原本，也不看审计表，把每条批语对后事说了什么拆成命题，写成“谁｜何事或何种处境｜何时｜是否含否定或未然”；并用前八十回全文查，这条批语所说的后文是否在前八十回之内已经出现。这样做，是为了不把本项目的答案读进题目里。

复核。再分两组核对：甲层 23 项一位核对者，乙-2 一类 13 条一位核对者，每条只经一位核对者。核对者拿盲拆出的命题逐条对复原本，逐字摘出引文，按以下准则判定：

  1. 身份与处境是否兑现；批语的修辞不作逐项规格核（如“缁衣乞食”，见下）；
  2. 同名异指，按语义复核；
  3. 时序：批语说的先后，复原本里是否成立；
  4. 施受：谁对谁做；
  5. 否定与未然（如“其事虽未行”）也要核；
  6. 整段读完再判；
  7. 盲拆指出前八十回内可能已应的，判断是否属实。

核对者的建议，由作者逐条裁定。

执行者。盲拆者与两位核对者都是新开的 AI 会话，模型为 Claude Opus 5.5，与撰写登记的归并方是同一个模型，日期 2026 年 9 月 27 日（UTC）。盲拆者的工具调用记录显示，它只读了盲拆材料与前八十回底本（另列过一次所在目录，见附件12）。“没有给它复原本”有记录可查；“它此前从未接触本项目的答案”只能说到新会话里没有本项目的上下文为止，模型相同，独立性有限。提示词与运行记录见附件12。

一处须照实交代的。盲拆提示词在“修辞与规格”一栏举的例子，是“缁衣乞食只是出家人的代称”，这正是作者对 22.105 的裁定，等于把答案带进了题目。重拆时已改（7.5 节）。两位核对者的提示词，在复核准则第一条里举了同一个例子；核对者看得到现登记与作者既有的裁定，并被要求照裁定执行、有疑另写（设计如此：复核查的是登记是否属实，不是替作者重裁），所以在 22.105 这一点上，核对并不独立。22.105 的这条修辞裁定，照登为作者的读法：汉传佛教自隋唐农禅并重（百丈怀海“一日不作，一日不食”之说，见《五灯会元》卷三百丈怀海章），托钵乞食不是庵中常制，复原本写惜春在正觉庵清苦度日，只核身份与处境。这是一般的制度常态，只说明作者为何不取“乞食”的字面，不据以断定批语字面必非实指；它也不作通用的豁免。

所据。复原本第八十一至一一〇回四个合集，各记校验值（附录三）。

7.5 重拆

v0.1 送审时，审读者发现数据里“所批正文”两栏有十三行提取有误：十二行混进了相邻批语的残段，一行（16.74）前文截短。病因是先按字数开窗、再去批语，窗口边上切断的半条批语去不掉。盲拆材料的上下文用的是同一种办法，只是窗口较长：40 条里有 15 条（16 处），窗口边缘混进了相邻批语的残段。紧贴批语的正文多数是对的；只有 21.4、13.89、26.153 三条回前、回末批，前后本来没有正文，窗口里取到的全是别的批语。

  1. 修正提取。先把整回的批语剥净，再截取；截到回首标“回首”，含回目，截到回末标“回末”。全表“所批正文”两栏也照此重提，十三行与审读者的复算逐条相符。

8 误判的病根与工序

已查出的误判，归起来有几种病根：

  1. 拿残文当定本（甲24 初判）；
  2. 拿词频当语义（甲20 的祖茔；重拆核对时的 13.89、28.110，其中 13.89 登记当时没写依据，也有份）；
  3. 拿批语的修辞当实务规格（22.105 的“缁衣乞食”初判）；
  4. 引曲文只读半支（总篇 9.1 节所记）；
  5. 凭关键词定层，不先回看所批正文（1.185 挂错位）；
  6. 定为跨八十回前指之前，没有先查前八十回（1.73、3.171、7.41、52.4、44.12、3.79、45.15 冷落一层等）；
  7. 记错人（37.54）；
  8. 补记条落实以后，没有回改登记（19.36）；
  9. 一阶词表缺单字“伏”，缺“后来”“后日”“兆”；二阶筛只扫了样本（24.3、24.61、6.1、45.12 等）；
  10. 解谶的句下批不含关键词，按词筛不出（好了歌注一组）；
  11. 一处【】里有几层话，只给整条一个判断（19.80、18.128、45.15、38.1）；
  12. 提取上下文时先开窗后去批，窗口边上混进别的批语；
  13. 盲拆提示词里带了本项目的裁定；
  14. 登记只写兑现位，不写依据（13.89）；
  15. 比对新旧命题时，把时间解释的变化当成小差别，没有送核（26.153）；
  16. 只叹不指的批语，因关键词进了漏网（13.14）；
  17. 定型时把本项目对批语的推读当作批语字面（甲12 的不采九）；
  18. 核对者记下的疑点，裁定时没有逐条回应（甲03 的册次）；
  19. 立了规矩，没有回扫已登记的各条（44.2 说明里已认作否定命题，主处理却未标）。

由此立的工序：

  1. 残文先校后裁；
  2. 同名异指以语义复核（祖茔、庵、麒麟、册、梦）；
  3. 修辞不作规格解，只核身份与处境，但须说出为何是修辞；
  4. 整支、整段读完再引；
  5. 定层之前先回看所批正文；
  6. 定为跨八十回前指之前，先在前八十回里查一遍；
  7. 补记条落实，回改登记；
  8. 终版表回归对账；
  9. 缺字批人工过；
  10. 兑现复核先盲拆命题，再对复原本；
  11. 二阶筛扫全量，不只扫样本；
  12. 单字前指词（伏、兆）与“后来”“后日”另筛；
  13. 解谶的句下批按位置过；
  14. 一条批里有几层话，分层登记（5.2 节）；
  15. 上下文先整回去批，再截取，并断言不含【】；
  16. 盲拆提示词里的例子，不得取自本项目的裁定；
  17. 登记写兑现位，同时写依据；
  18. 比对新旧命题，照事先所立的三种出入逐栏过，不自行判为小差别；
  19. 定为漏网之前，先按口径分出只叹不指者；
  20. 记两批互异之前，先确认两条批语字面相抵；
  21. 核对输出里的疑点，裁定时逐条回应，采与不采都写明；
  22. 新立一条规矩，回扫全部登记。

9 批语里的另几种下半部

把不采的批语、半兑现里没采的那一半、两批互异时舍去的那一边、改兑现时变通掉的那一处，以及理解不同的几条合起来看，按这些批语读，可以得到另几种下半部的轮廓。这一节把它们逐一写明，说明本项目为什么不采。本节不重建这些下半部，也不判它们对错；每一种都分开写：批语明说了什么，按这一路可以读出什么，本项目怎样处理。

一、宝玉娶宝钗，成其夫妇，麝月在侧；后来宝玉“弃而为僧”；琪官与袭人供奉“玉兄宝卿”，得同终始。

  • 批语明说：20.45“袭人出嫁之后，宝玉、宝钗身边还有一人”；20.72“后文成其夫妇时”，还有“谈旧之情”；21.65“若他人得宝钗之妻、麝月之婢，岂能弃而为僧哉”；28.1“后回与袭人供奉玉兄宝卿得同终始”。
  • 按这一路读：宝玉与宝钗真成了夫妇，婚后同处，麝月在侧，由琪官、袭人供奉；宝玉后来弃而为僧。21.65 说弃，28.1 说同终始，两批可读作先后相承（供奉在先，弃在后），也可读作出于两稿。
  • 分歧：本项目所取的，是宝玉自择出走，不是得而后弃；宝钗最大的难题是名分，不是被供奉的生活；宝玉悟后回到人间，为居士而不剃度（总篇 6.4、7.2 节）。记不采四；67.1“是何必削发”字面说的是湘莲，其含意只作旁证（6.1 节甲12、甲25）。
  • 本项目：宝玉在婚约已定、婚礼临头时走开，宝钗的名分事后追定；麝月在侧、琪官袭人供奉宝玉，各兑现一半。

二、钗黛一身。

  • 批语明说：42.1“钗玉名虽两个，人却一身，此幻笔也……请看黛玉逝后宝钗之文字便知余言不谬矣”。
  • 按这一路读：这一条首先是批者对第四十二回的读法，钗黛“合而为一”，可以是人物结构与文体上的解释，不一定是一件事。它所期待的，是黛玉逝后宝钗的文字能印证这种读法。
  • 本项目：读黛玉为核心，二人各自具足（总篇 6.3 节）；《情情赋》说：“尔自为尔，非为吾之宗极；吾亦自为吾，不待尔而后明。”记理解不同。黛玉逝后，宝钗确有文字，第一〇八回黛玉入宝钗梦，说的是“那是你自己写的。这一笔，没有人替你落”。这一场可作回应的候选，但它写的是二人各自，本文不拿它来兑现此批。

三、宝玉以“情不情”入情榜；情榜列出全谱，定出正、副、再副及三四副。

  • 批语明说：8.140“按警幻情榜，宝玉系‘情不情’”；19.80“后观《情榜》评曰‘宝玉情不情’，‘黛玉情情’”；18.19“至回末警幻情榜方知正、副、再副及三四副芳讳”。
  • 按这一路读：情榜男女同列，册次定下。两评在批语里是褒语。
  • 分歧：情榜是活的，不定死（总篇 6.2 节），记不采四；“情不情”“情情”之义，本项目另有读法，记理解不同。
  • 本项目：第一一〇回写情榜，只列女子，不列全谱；“情不情”落在回目与薄命司的断喝里。

四、狱庙相逢之日，才知巧姐“遇难成祥，逢凶化吉”的伏线。

  • 批语明说：42.6“狱庙相逢之日始知‘遇难成祥，逢凶化吉’实伏线于千里，哀哉伤哉！此后文字不忍卒读。”
  • 按这一路读：狱神庙的相逢与巧姐的得救连在一起。
  • 本项目：狱神庙相逢的是宝玉与茜雪、小红；巧姐之救在其后，由刘姥姥一行完成，写作偶遇，不作阴功之报（总篇附录五《留余庆》一行）。这一处是情节布置不同，不涉主旨，记不采五，此批只兑现一半。

五、芸哥仗义探庵。

  • 批语明说：24.3 靖本“‘醉金刚’一回文字，伏芸哥仗义探庵”。
  • 按这一路读：贾芸仗义，与醉金刚倪二一回相伏，去探的是一座庵；探的是谁，批未明言。
  • 本项目：贾芸的仗义写在狱神庙，第九十二回探狱，第九十四回同倪二等计赚狱神庙；“探庵”一层没有写，把庵读作狱神庙，牵强，不取。这也是情节布置不同，记不采五，半兑现。

六、撒手在末回。

  • 批语明说：67.1“末回‘撒手’，乃是已悟”。
  • 按这一路读：宝玉悟而撒手，被安排在全书末回；批语未限定它是否为末回最后一事。
  • 本项目：撒手在第一〇六回，末回写情榜与玉返青埂峰，记改兑现。宝玉撒手之后还有几回人间的日子，是本项目所写悟后的第三层（总篇 6.4 节）要的。

七、探春若不远去，家败后子孙可不流散。

  • 批语明说：22.103“此探春远适之谶也。使此人不远去，将来事败，诸子孙不致流散也，悲哉伤哉！”
  • 按这一路读：探春之去，是家败时子孙流散的一个缘故；她若在，家事或可收拾。这是批者的一个反事实，也是一种寄望。
  • 本项目：远适一层兑现。实际远嫁并没有实现这一反事实的前件，所以复原本写探春远嫁，不构成对它的反驳，也不能证明它为真。“使此人不远去”一层，不断言复原本该写什么，而是对家败之因的一种理解；本项目的叙述不作因果、报应的解释，照写散而不灭，记理解不同。

八、以情说法、警醒世人。

  • 批语明说：35.26“此回是以情说法，警醒世人……爱河之深无底，何可泛滥？一溺其中，非死不止”。
  • 按这一路读：这一条不是前指，但它读全书为警世之教，情是必溺之河，一溺其中，非死不止。
  • 本项目：叙述者不作报应的解释（总篇 4.5 节），记理解不同。

九、又副册三断词作晴雯、袭人、香菱；平儿在又副册。

  • 批语明说：18.17“又有又副册三断词乃晴雯、袭人、香菱三人，余未多及，想为金钏、玉钏、鸳鸯、苗云（下有按语一句，略）、平儿等人无疑矣”；同回畸笏眉批（18.19，即甲06）自承“是处引十二钗总未的确，皆系漫拟也”；6.48 说平儿“想亦在副册内者也”。
  • 按这一路读：又副册三断词乃晴雯、袭人、香菱，香菱不在副册。这与第五回正文香菱在副册相抵，或许是第五回的另一稿，或许是批者的漫拟。又副册其余诸人，批者想为金钏、玉钏、鸳鸯、苗云、平儿等，平儿在又副册；6.48 却说平儿“想亦在副册内”，两批所说的册次不同。
  • 本项目：三断词从正文，记不采八；平儿取 6.48，第一一〇回补写平儿一页在副册（补记条〇〇三），18.17 这一层记不采九。

十、原稿已失或作者自删之文。

  • 批语明说：狱神庙、射圃诸回的原稿迷失（20.20、26.24、26.120、26.153）；“遗簪”“更衣”诸文，据 13.8 批者自述，曾命芹溪删去（只见于靖本）。
  • 按这一路读：下半部原有狱神庙一大回、卫若兰射圃之文，前文原有秦可卿“遗簪”“更衣”诸事。这些不是另一种设计，是失去或删去的文字。
  • 本项目：或另立（狱神庙、射圃），或不写（遗簪、更衣），都不复原其原形，记不采六。

这几种下半部，本项目都不采，或只采一部分。它们照录在这里，说明两件事。一是按批语本身，曹雪芹的下半部很可能不止一种设计，批者所见所想各有不同；这是一种可能性，本文不据此断言历史上确有这样几部完整的稿子。二是本项目所写，是依“人是目的”这一判准，在证据仍然允许的读法之间择出来的一种；换一个判准，这里的许多“不采”就可以改成“采”。批语在这里不是被驳倒的对手，而是另几种可能的证人。总篇说“情榜是活的”，对复原本自己也是如此。

10 结语

审计的结论可以说得很短：本项目已识别的、前八十回里指向后事的批语，逐条留了痕；采了哪些，怎样采，不采哪些，为什么不采，读者都可以查。兑现复核又把本项目自己的登记查了一遍，改了不少；补筛又查出一批漏看的，其中有熟知的几条；送审之后，审读者与重拆又逼出了几处分层与几处病根。没有一条批语在这里被判为错；被检验的，只是本项目。
