Abstract

This paper is Paper 0 of Marriage Theory: written last, placed first. It lays down no propositions; it only does a few things for those who read this series: it states this series' place in SAE, sets out how far it reads and what its main line is, gives the method and the shared words the papers have in common, interfaces as a whole with several traditions, says once, all together, the directions of colonization read in the papers, lists together the forty-five propositions of the whole series, and registers the remainders that stay unsettled.

Marriage Theory is the second branch downstream of SAE Relation Theory, following Love Theory. Relation Theory establishes only the part all relations have; Love Theory reads the part that lies in love, and handed over the stretch in which there are both commitment and institutional protection. Marriage Theory reads only the marriage of two people, and does not claim that other forms are not marriage; what it reads is marriage that carries the 15DD direction, that is, marriage in which both people are headed in the direction of recognizing the other as an end too. This direction is the premise; under this premise, marriage is made up of three things: love, each person's own commitment, institutional endorsement. The main line in one sentence: You are you, I am I, and only then is there an us; we each look beyond ourselves, toward the other, toward someone who has not yet come. The seven papers read, in order: what the marriage this series reads is; "we" and each their own; bringing each other into their own; the long years; preparing for a child; after a third one has come; staying and leaving.

This paper interfaces as a whole with Augustine, with Xian and Heng in the Book of Changes, with Li Zhi and with Brake. The directions of colonization read in the papers borrow things that are, or may come to be, in a marriage: endorsement, vows, "we," the household, division of labor, children; some of these are not in every marriage, and are not on that account components of marriage either. These things carry no direction in themselves; the direction is read on the one effect in which they are taken up to do something. Where many things are bound together, the places that can be taken up to slide in the direction of colonization are many too; relations are all mixed, a single sliding is read on that one occasion, and it is not on that account judged that a marriage has exited scope. This paper proves no proposition at the object layer.

Introduction

The seven papers that follow each read one thing. Paper 1, Joined, reads what this series means by marriage: one premise, three things; commitment; endorsement; where exclusivity comes from; which kinds of marriage are not in this series. Paper 2, Each Their Own, reads the "we" in marriage: You are you, I am I, and only then is there an us. Paper 3, Into One's Own, reads looking toward the one person at one's side, letting the other grow into the other's own self. Paper 4, The Years, reads the long years: people change, and after they change, they choose again together; division of labor and exchange; expression and expectation; conflict and sediment. Paper 5, For One Not Yet Here, reads two people creating conditions together for someone who has not yet come. Paper 6, Three, reads what lies between husband and wife after a third one has come. Paper 7, Staying and Leaving, reads how the marriage this series reads ends: the ending of love, breaking the commitment, exit and divorce, bereavement of a spouse.

Written this way, several things are left that no single paper takes care of: why this series is here, and how far it reads; how the main line strings the seven papers together; the method the papers share, and what the same word means in which paper; several traditions whose questions concern the whole series; what the directions of colonization read in the papers look like when set side by side. This paper takes care of these.

Method as in the papers: the conclusions of upstream papers are said in plain words, and sources and extensions go in the notes. This paper lays down no propositions, and alters no sentence of the papers that follow. How the pieces upstream handed to Marriage Theory are placed is for readers who want to check against upstream, and goes in the appendix at the end.

§1 Place: The Second Branch Downstream of the Relational Side

SAE has three sides: the subject side, the relational side and the institutional side. The relational side is unfolded by Relation Theory; once Relation Theory is written, there are several branches downstream: Love Theory, Marriage Theory, Parent-Child Theory and Cooperation Theory; friendship is not given a branch of its own, and serves as a running example through all the branches.1 Love Theory is the first branch, Marriage Theory the second.

The division of labor between the two layers Relation Theory has written out: Relation Theory in the abstract establishes only what all relations have; what holds only in one kind of relation is handed to the series on that kind of relation to argue.2 So whatever has already been laid down at the layer of relation, this series uses directly and does not rewrite: the minimal conditions of a relation, the two directions of cultivation and colonization, expectation, commitment, the sediment of dealings, ending, are all read by Relation Theory. Love within marriage is read by Love Theory, and is not rewritten either.

The boundary with Love Theory is handed over on one line of The Story of the Western Wing: "May all the lovers under heaven be joined as husband and wife." Love Theory reads the lovers; joined as husband and wife, "with commitment and, besides, institutional protection," they have come into Marriage Theory. Love does not end because two people have been joined as husband and wife; there is love after marriage too.3 Paper 1 picks up from this line, and reads it as three things: being lovers is love; being joined as husband and wife is commitment and endorsement.

What holds only in marriage has to be compared with the relations next to it. Against friendship: How Is Institution Possible (Foundational Paper 6; cited as F6) writes of friendship: "Subtract exclusivity and deep commitment from marriage";4 against love: exclusivity and commitment are both already present in Love Theory. What holds only in marriage falls where commitment and endorsement are together, and on the public side that endorsement brings to commitment and exit (Paper 1, MA7). Shared life and preparing for a child can be in a marriage, but are not only in marriage, and need not be in a marriage; they are content this series has to read, not a threshold of marriage.

Besides Relation Theory and Love Theory, this series' main upstream includes the paper of Meaning Theory that reads the outward side of a relation.5 Relation Theory reads the effects between two people; Meaning Theory reads a person's direction, and where its outlet falls. Bringing each other into their own and preparing for a child both have the outlet of a direction falling outside oneself; this layer is read by Meaning Theory.

§2 Scope and Main Line

Scope. There are six items.

First, the marriage of two people. This series reads only marriage in the traditional sense, that is, the marriage of two people; it does not claim that marriages of other forms are not marriage; other forms it does not read, and does not judge. The parties are adults, of any sex; for illustration the examples use the traditional pairing of a man and a woman.

Second, marriage that carries the 15DD direction. In SAE's levels, the layer at which the other is recognized as an end too is written 15DD.6 What this series reads is marriage in which both parties are headed in this direction. A direction, not a state: headed toward it, not necessarily already there; people are all mixed; a layer records one operation, not a person, nor a marriage, and falling back is read as it occurs. "Both" is scope, not a requirement between husband and wife: each party can confirm only his own end; the other end appears as the other party's first-person avowal and where he stands, which I do not certify, nor cancel on his behalf (Paper 1, MA2). The whole series is written as a conditional: when two people are headed in this direction, how marriage is made up, and how it works.7 From outside, a real marriage is not characterized; only clues are seen.

Third, the premise and the three things. The direction is a premise, not a component. Under this premise, marriage is made up of three things: love, each person's own commitment, institutional endorsement. The three do different work and cannot stand in for one another; they are also read differently: love is seen from the subject's side; commitment is seen from outside, by words and deeds and the structure they leave; endorsement is seen by that institution's rules (Paper 1, MA1). Marriage cannot simply be reduced to commitment.

Fourth, what is not read. A purely transactional marriage; a marriage in which the love between the two is entirely gone, whether the two still need this marriage or keep it up only by their own commitments; a marriage whose exit has been sealed off, however it was sealed. Each of the three is read differently; where either of the latter two arises after marriage, that marriage exits scope from then on; this is not traced back, and this series does not turn and judge the two people (Paper 1, MA9). Where one party's love is gone, that marriage too is no longer in this series from then on (Paper 7, MA40). Here only structure is looked at; minds are not read, and there is no criticism: of the two kinds without love, no separate judgment is made of who has treated whom as a means. Not reading them is not saying they are not marriages. The concrete process by which a child comes into the world is not within this series' range.

Fifth, the upper bound stops at 15DD. The instant at which two one-ways meet, that is, the moment of 16DD, Love Theory Paper 6 has already read; it is pointed back to here, not read again. The kind in which two people are in mutual non dubito at any time and in any place, Relation Theory hands to each branch to argue for itself;8 Marriage Theory's answer is the same as Love Theory's: it does not take it up; it only registers it, and does not prove that it does not exist.

Sixth, the boundary with neighboring series. Love within marriage belongs to Love Theory; what lies between parents and children belongs to Parent-Child Theory; doing things together in general belongs to Cooperation Theory, and Marriage Theory reads only doing things together within marriage; the institutional side of marriage belongs to the institutional side, and is only registered here; what has been laid down at the layer of relation is read by Relation Theory.

Main line. In plain prose it is one sentence: You are you, I am I, and only then is there an us. We each look beyond ourselves: toward the other, toward someone who has not yet come.

The first sentence is the premise; it comes from the SAE Marriage Principles in the appendix to F6,9 and is argued in Paper 2. Whenever "we" is said in marriage, it divides, as in Relation Theory, into two branches, doing things together and mutual recognition; neither branch comes before the two people, nor stands above them; it does not legislate, and does not pass final judgment on either party's own law (Paper 2, MA10).

The second sentence speaks of two kinds of looking toward. Put technically: in the 15DD direction, the meaning of each of the two people in a marriage belongs to each, and the outlet of a direction falls outside oneself: on the other, letting the other grow into the other's own self; on someone who has not yet come, creating conditions for him together. The attribution of meaning is in each person: "the attribution endpoints are closed in one person";10 the outlet can be outside, the two people's directions can cross, and each one's relation is still anchored in each one, not merged into one (Paper 2, MA11). Nor does it follow from the closure of attribution in one person that a person is self-sufficient and needs no outside.11

The two kinds of looking toward differ in structure. Looking toward the other is bringing each other into their own: two one-ways, neither waiting for the other to bring him into his own first, have met; if they do not meet, each one's share is still there as before; bringing someone into their own is cultivation, and creating conditions and questioning are its two main ways (Paper 3). Looking toward someone who has not yet come is preparing for a child: the two people each have their own purposes and do one thing together: you for A, I for B, and we do C together; C is the doing of creating conditions together for that person, not the child (Paper 5).

One more sentence strings the seven papers together: marriage is not one promise; it is the same promise made anew each day; whoever stands there on any given day is the person of today (Paper 1, MA4). How the long years are lived (Paper 4), how things are after a third one has come (Paper 6), how it ends (Paper 7), are all read under this sentence.

§3 Method

The whole series is written by the following rules.

A layer records one operation; people are all mixed. It does not record a person, nor a marriage; between husband and wife, neither judges at which layer the other is.

Structure, not good and bad. Marriages are not ranked by length, by stability, by whether there are children, or by what has been achieved.

Issue no ought; do not argue for specific rules. The SAE Marriage Principles in the appendix to F6 are a working demonstration, not evidentiary.12 This series argues for the principles they contain, writes them by structure, and quotes their sentences as they stand; specific rules serve only as examples. Where a sentence carries evaluative or quantitative wording, the place where it is quoted marks on the spot that this layer is not taken; where they do not fit published Relation Theory, Relation Theory governs.

Only relation. The institution's power and recognition-based rights are only registered, not argued, and no propositions are derived from these two concepts; what a person does by way of a construct is read as between person and person; the rules themselves are acknowledged but not judged.13

The object layer and the operation layer kept apart; said only conditionally. The object layer reads marriage, with love, in which both parties are headed in this direction; at the operation layer each party confirms only his own end. Love is seen from the subject's side, and cannot be read from outside; a purely transactional marriage is an ideal type; a person's sealing himself in with a vow is a first-person matter; if what holds up that lock is an outside saying, it is read as colonized, and this cannot be seen from outside either; these are all said only conditionally.

Do not read minds; look at acts, look at structure. Commitment, breaking it, dropping and exit are read by words and deeds and by structure; whether one acknowledges something in one's heart, whether one has been stirred, is not judged. The two faces of non dubito, too, are read from outside only by words and deeds and by structure.

Cultivation and colonization in the broad sense. They name the direction of one effect, not labels attached to persons or marriages: cultivation goes toward making space, colonization toward pouring in and occupying; the direction is read on this one effect, and whether it took hold is seen in the other party's later understanding and action. Violation is judged separately, only at the layer of basic rights.14 "Slides" and "tendency" are not read as having already taken hold.

A moment of exchange and the sediment after it are read apart. The moment is read as falling back, and a whole marriage is not characterized by one occasion; division of labor sliding into a fixed requirement aimed at the other, being surreptitiously swapped within habit, the path to saying no becoming hard to travel: these are read separately, as sediment.

Coin no new words unless necessary; what belongs elsewhere is pointed back to, not rewritten. Love within marriage belongs to Love Theory; what lies between parents and children belongs to Parent-Child Theory; the structures and interfacing at the layer of relation belong to Relation Theory.

Quantitative wording is checked separately. It is not said that the longer, the truer, or the steadier, the better; "more," "the more ... the more" and "most," used of marriage, are all checked separately.

Examples. Only adult characters are used; for illustration the examples use the traditional pairing of a man and a woman; each paper has its own couple. Made-up examples are used only to see structure; a married couple's words and deeds are only clues, and do not certify who is at which layer. Historical examples are read for structure and direction only by the words and deeds in the historical sources, without judging whether the two loved each other or were headed in this direction; only the deceased, whose records are public, are used; matches in which a party was a minor at marriage are not used as examples; a purely transactional marriage is not used as a positive example, and appears only where traditions are interfaced and press on this series.

Interfacing with tradition. Only thought and philosophy are interfaced with; literature serves as examples; a writer of literature who argues a thesis counts as philosophy. For each, this series writes faithfully what he sees, why he looks, where he presses, where they meet and where they part; not every figure needs all these steps, and where no counter-proposition aimed at this series can be drawn from the source, no pressing is invented. Other traditions appear in their own words. Figures are interfaced with by question, and no philosopher is assigned a layer. What has already been interfaced at the layer of relation and the layer of love is pointed back to; when it is pointed back to, the restrictions of that place come along.

Sources and extensions. The conclusions of upstream papers are said in plain words in the main text; sources, original sentences and the proposition numbers of other papers go in the notes; what is carried beyond its original place is stated to be an extension, this series' own undertaking.

§4 Shared Words

Several words are shared across the papers, and their senses have to be kept apart.

Institution. The broad sense: "marriage and friendship are micro-relational institutions";15 when institutional endorsement is spoken of, institution means instead the construct outside the two people that, by its own rules, recognizes this union, formal or informal. The relation between the two people is itself also a kind of construct; it does not legislate, and bears nothing; a little circle of two does not form a third bearer (Paper 1).

Institutional endorsement and institutional protection. They refer to the same thing. Endorsement is thicker, and stronger: protection says only what that institution has given this union; endorsement also says what the two people have done: made this union public to it, recognized its power, and obtained recognition from it in return (Paper 1).

Exchange, obtaining in return, division of labor, and mutuality. Exchange is between husband and wife: you give me this, I give you that; a moment of exchange is falling back (Paper 4). Obtaining in return is between the two people and the construct that endorses; it is between persons and a construct (Paper 1). Division of labor is a present arrangement the two have acknowledged together, not a mutual requirement (Paper 4). Mutuality is two one-ways meeting, not conditioned on the other's giving first, or on the other's being sure to give (Paper 3).

Pure transaction. It speaks only of what lies between husband and wife: both parties treat the other only as a means. A couple hoping for a child to carry on the family line is not on that account purely transactional; if the child is treated only as a condition, that is read separately (Paper 1, Paper 5). A marriage kept up after the love between the two is entirely gone is not on that account read as purely transactional either: it is not in this series because love is lacking, and no separate judgment is made of who has treated whom as a means (Paper 1, MA3).

Purpose and end. In the Chinese of this series these are one word, with two senses. "The layer of purpose," as in Love Theory, is written 14DD: she has become where I am going; "Others also make sense, but I cannot exchange her";16 this is my own direction. The end in "recognizing the other as an end too" is at 15DD: the other has where the other is going, which is not a point within my direction. When it is said that using love to demand exclusivity "falls back to the layer of purpose (14DD)," the former sense is used: taking my direction as her ruler (Paper 1). In everyday speech the same word also means why one does something; when Paper 5 says that two people each have their own purposes, this sense is taken.

Direction. Three senses, as in Love Theory.17 To speak of the 15DD direction is to speak of the place one is headed toward and has not yet reached; to speak of the two directions of cultivation and colonization is to speak of where one effect is headed; to speak of a person's own direction is to speak of where he is going (Paper 3).

Commitment. Read as in Relation Theory: what was promised, in word and deed; where one stands, and revising; the effects left; an implicit commitment counts too, and is still avowal on my side and where I stand. "Each person's own commitment": even if a vow is said by the two together, where each one stands is still each one's own (Paper 1).

"We" and the household. "We" divides, as in Relation Theory, into two branches, doing things together and mutual recognition. In everyday speech it is sometimes only a way of putting what each of the two has done into one sentence; that sets up no "we," and is no third branch. Neither branch is a "we" set above the two people, making decisions for them. "The household" takes the everyday sense; whether a household can separately become a bearer is not judged (Paper 2; §7).

Meaning. Taken in Meaning Theory's sense: the relation, in cross-temporal carrying-forward, between a bearer and the content he avows; the attribution endpoints are closed in one person (Paper 2).

Non dubito. Two faces: not doubting that oneself is an end, not doubting that others are ends. Non dubito is not having no doubts, nor is it the same as trust (Paper 2).

Bringing someone into their own, support and constitution. Bringing someone into their own, as a verb, means letting the other grow into the other's own self; it does not name a result, is not used as a measure, and speaks of direction, not of what has already taken hold. Support is not substitute constitution; constitution is the step in which a person, by his own law, avows a content and makes it his own. The attribution of constitution, and the ownership of things, authorship, credit and responsibility, are several different matters (Paper 3).

Law and arrangement; change and revision; conflict. Law is the layer a person bears in the first person and by which he goes on organizing his action; arrangement is how time, roles and procedures are laid out, and division of labor lies at the layer of arrangement. Change is the rise and fall over the long years; revision is a person's changing, in the first person, one of his own laws, and keeping the books on it; change does not prove that revision has occurred. Conflict is taken in the Conflict Treatise's sense; many quarrels between husband and wife are only collisions (Paper 4).

Preparing for a child, someone who has not yet come, the child and descendants, doing something together, conditions. Preparing for a child takes only one sense: two people creating conditions together for someone who has not yet come. Someone who has not yet come is someone who has not yet come into these two people's lives, and does not necessarily not yet exist. The main text writes "the child"; "descendants" carries a family line, and is used only where traditions are interfaced. The thing the two do together is not called a shared purpose: the purposes are each one's own, and what is shared is only the one thing done. Conditions are what someone who comes later can enter, not a direction fixed for him (Paper 5).

Three persons, shared child-rearing and the lock. Three persons are three bearers, not one three-person subject; the child is a third one, not a third party formed out of the two. Shared child-rearing is read only on its side between husband and wife. In everyday speech one says "using the child as a lock"; Relation Theory uses the word "lock" narrowly: only sealing off exit itself is locking (Paper 6).

Exit and divorce; the exit right; knowing; able to leave. Exit is withdrawing one's own side; it is an act; divorce is the marriage's dissolution within the institution that endorsed it. The exit right has two components; when this series speaks of exit being sealed off, it means the basic component; the procedure of divorce belongs to the recognition component, and is only registered. Knowing has two senses; this series reads the sense of knowing that the other can leave. Able to leave means that the person himself can actually exit these dealings, not that the procedure has been completed or that he has moved out (Paper 7).

Slides, carries and violation; tendency; colonized. The direction of colonization is spoken of in several ways, divided as in Relation Theory: a characterization said aloud, fixing for the other what something means to him, slides in the direction of colonization; raising the cost of the other's exit outward while the way is still passable, or sealing the other's channels for saying no and changing his mind, or making them hard to travel, carries the direction of colonization; sealing off exit itself is, at the layer of basic rights, a violation, and also carries the direction of colonization; anything else that crosses basic rights is judged separately, at that layer, as a violation, and whether it carries the direction of colonization is read separately by that one effect.18 One sentence is one effect, and is read by that sentence; a person is not judged by one sentence, nor is a marriage. Tendency is a general way of speaking: it says where an arrangement is sliding, for instance division of labor sliding into a fixed requirement aimed at the other; when it lands on one effect, it is divided into sliding and carrying. A person's taking an arrangement, on his own, as unchangeable is his self-relation; structurally it has no outward direction, and is not in the tendency. A person's sealing himself in with a vow is read as locking, not called self-colonization; if what holds up that lock is an outside saying, the outside saying has occupied his own step of revision, and it is read as colonized; the entry between his own yesterday and today is recorded separately, and no separate name is given to it (Paper 1). A person's sealing himself in with a vow means the kind in which the vow, in running, makes exit actually unreachable (Paper 1, MA9); this is different from the "sealing" above, in the tier that carries the direction of colonization.

§5 Interfacing as a Whole

The papers each interfaced with figures by question. Paper 1 interfaced with Kant on mutual acquisition, Hegel on marriage as not a contract, Locke on the voluntary compact, Kierkegaard on the aesthetic validity of marriage, and Gilbert's joint commitment; Paper 2 with Hegel on the family, Montaigne, Nozick, Kant on love and respect, and Baier on trust; Paper 3 with Mill, Aristotle on friendship between husband and wife, Tan Sitong, and Kant on the perfection of others; Paper 4 with Cavell, Beauvoir, Aristotle on rule between husband and wife, and Ban Zhao; Paper 5 with "The Meaning of the Marriage Rite" in the Book of Rites and the Mencius, Kant and Arendt; Paper 6 with Aristotle, Hegel on children, and Locke; Paper 7 with Hume, Milton, Hegel on divorce, Lu Xun and the Zhuangzi. Several figures ask about the whole series, and are placed here.

Three goods (Augustine). At the opening of Of the Good of Marriage, Augustine says that each person is a part of the human race, that human nature is social, and that "the first natural bond of human society is man and wife."19 He goes on at once to say that the good of marriage is not only for the begetting of children, but also for the natural companionship itself between the two sexes; otherwise old couples, especially those who have lost their sons or never had children, could no longer be called married.20 He sums up the goods of marriage as three: offspring, faith, sacrament.21 The sacrament keeps the bond of marriage from coming undone through separation: the compact of marriage "is not done away by divorce intervening," and only when husband or wife dies is the bond loosed.22 He looks in order to explain by what marriage is a good. What he sees: there are several different things in marriage, each with its own place, which cannot be merged into one, nor stand in for one another.

He presses at indissolubility: he counts the indissoluble bond among the goods of marriage, while this series says that commitment cannot lock tomorrow and that a person can leave; the two collide here; yet that the bond is not loosed does not by itself determine that exit has been sealed off in running; on this layer, see the second point of parting below. At offspring he presses lightly: he puts offspring into his account of the goods of marriage, but does not on that account cancel marriages without children. They meet at two points. First, preparing for a child is not a condition of marriage's being marriage, and here he stands on the same side as this series; this series reads preparing for a child as content a marriage may have, not as a threshold (§1). Second, marriage is made up of several things that cannot stand in for one another, which has the same shape as Paper 1's three things; faith stands on the same side as commitment and exclusivity. They part at three points. First, his three are an account of the goods of marriage; this series' three are how the marriage it reads is made up: love, commitment, endorsement; the two tables do not ask the same question, can stand side by side, and are not to refute each other by adding or removing items one by one. Second, indissolubility: this series reads endorsement as making public to that institution and taking on its rules; an institution's not permitting dissolution is its rule, acknowledged but not judged; that a person can leave is a layer not given by that institution, and merely not having obtained dissolution within the institution does not by itself prove that exit has been sealed off (Paper 7, MA43). Third, this series does not prove that people ought to marry, nor does it rank marriage above or below not marrying.

Xian and Heng (the Book of Changes). The lower canon of the Book of Changes begins with Xian, and after Xian comes Heng. The Tuan commentary says: "Xian is gan, being stirred"; "Heng is jiu, lasting."23 The Xugua, the Sequence of the Hexagrams, runs from heaven and earth and the myriad things, through man and woman, down to husband and wife, and adds: "The way of husband and wife must not fail to last; therefore Heng follows."24 It looks in order to explain how the sequence of hexagrams comes about, and the order by which human relations are derived from heaven and earth. What it sees: being stirred and lasting are two things; a moment's mutual stirring and the lasting in time of the way of husband and wife are not one thing. It presses on this series: "must not fail to last" is a sentence of "must": the way of husband and wife must last; this series says that commitment cannot lock tomorrow, does not rank marriages by length, and issues no ought; by its lights, this series has lost half of the way of husband and wife. They meet in: the separation of being stirred and lasting corresponds exactly to the division of labor between the two series: Love Theory reads the cannot-not of that moment: this one, at this moment, irreplaceable; Marriage Theory reads the long years, one day after another. Lasting is not done once, either: marriage is the same promise made anew each day (Paper 1, MA4). They part at two points. First, this series does not say that the way of husband and wife must last; lasting is not a criterion, and Paper 4 does not prove that the longer, the better; lasting, here, is only the shape of being made anew each day by the person of today, not a locked state. Second, the Xugua derives from husband and wife the order of father and son, ruler and minister, superior and inferior; this series sets no order of human relations; what lies between parents and children belongs to Parent-Child Theory, and is not derived from marriage.

Husband and wife as the beginning of human relations (Li Zhi). In the Chutan ji, Li Zhi says: "Husband and wife are the beginning of humankind. Only with husband and wife are there father and son; only with father and son are there elder and younger brothers; only with brothers are there superior and inferior"; in "On Husband and Wife," in A Book to Burn, he says: "I have inquired into the beginning of things, and seen that husband and wife are where it starts," and "Taken to its limit, heaven and earth are one husband and wife."25 What he was opposing was the view that takes principle, or the Supreme Ultimate, as the beginning of the myriad things; he put husband and wife at the head of human relations.26 He looks in order to answer where the myriad things and human relations begin. What he sees: what begins is not one, but two; heaven and earth too are two. They meet in: the two people come first, and no "one" is first set up to govern the two; You are you, I am I, and only then is there an us (Paper 2), which runs the same way as the two in "heaven and earth are one husband and wife." This running-together has its limits: "two" does not by itself prove that neither end's own law is legislated for it by someone else; that layer Paper 2 argues separately. They part at: the before and after he speaks of is the order of occurrence and of ethics, where human relations begin; when this series says that Marriage Theory lies downstream of Relation Theory, it speaks of dependence in argument: Relation Theory establishes only what all relations have, and marriage is one kind among them. The two before-and-afters ask different things, and are not set against each other as source and branch; his order serves here only as a side light. Nor does this series derive father and son, or brothers, from husband and wife; parent and child are another branch; where heaven, earth and the myriad things begin, this series does not answer.

Minimizing marriage (Brake). In Minimizing Marriage, Brake argues that a politically liberal state should recognize caring relationships as valuable, and distribute equally the rights that support these relationships; her minimal marriage "would provide rights directly supporting relationships, but not economic benefits, without restriction as to sex or number of parties," and so extends not only to polyamorous relationships but also to asexual and aromantic people.27 She looks in order to answer what marriage law a liberal state may have. What she sees: that institution's recognition and the relationships it recognizes are two things; the rules of recognition can be legislated another way. She presses on this series at two points. First, her minimal marriage has no restriction on the number of parties; this series reads only two people, and so reads only one form of marriage. Second, her minimal marriage is also given to relationships without romantic love; this series says marriage must have love (Paper 1, MA3), which, by her lights, adds to marriage a condition it need not have. They meet in: endorsement counts by that institution's rules, and rules can differ (Paper 1, MA5); this series does not claim that other forms are not marriage either. They part at: what she answers is how the law ought to be, an ought in political philosophy; this series speaks only of relation, only registers the institutional side, acknowledges the rules without judging them, and does not argue how marriage law should be made. Two people, and love, are this series' conditions of scope; they say which kind of marriage it reads, not which kind that institution should recognize.

§6 The Directions of Colonization Read in the Papers, Said Once Together

Every paper read out the direction of colonization on its own item.

Paper 1: using endorsement to avow on the other's behalf, "We've got the certificate, so you're mine," slides in the direction of colonization; using a vow as a reason the other may not leave characterizes the other's exit, and slides in the direction of colonization; announcing consequences carries the direction of colonization; sealing off is a violation, and also carries the direction of colonization; using love to demand exclusivity falls back to the layer of purpose (14DD), taking my direction as her ruler. A person's sealing himself in with a vow is read as locking, not called colonization; if what holds up that lock is an outside saying, it is read as colonized; in both of these cases, that marriage is not in this series.

Paper 2: entering in the name of intimacy; in the name of "this household" or "for us," taking in and replacing one party's direction, which is a direction of colonization that borrows a construct against a person; using "We love each other, so you have to agree" to pass final judgment on a matter done together, which borrows mutual recognition as adjudication; handing one's own meaning over to the other for safekeeping, and then demanding it of the other.

Paper 3: fixing for the other what he should become; converting the conditions one has given into possession of the results of the other's constitution: "Everything you have today, I gave you." Two others are not here: writing bringing someone into their own as exchange is falling back; accommodation is not having stood, and structurally there is no direction of colonization toward the other; how things are on his own side cannot be seen from outside, and is not judged (MA21).

Paper 4: measuring the other as he is now by the other as he was then, and saying it aloud so as to fix for him who he is, slides in the direction of colonization; division of labor sliding into a fixed requirement aimed at the other has a tendency toward colonization: said aloud, it slides in the direction of colonization; making it unchangeable for the other carries the direction of colonization; a person's taking the division of labor, on his own, as unchangeable is his self-relation, and is not here; writing one's own giving as an account the other must repay is read by that one effect; hanging expectations on who the other is and what he cares about, and judging for the other, slides in the direction of colonization.

Paper 5: one party's acknowledging on the other's behalf, or saying aloud so as to fix the other's changing his mind as wronging oneself, slides in the direction of colonization; announcing consequences that can be carried out, sealing the other's paths for saying no and changing his mind, or making them hard to travel, carries the direction of colonization; what crosses basic rights is judged separately as a violation. The child's falling, within the two people's arrangements, to "only as" a condition, of continuing the line, of carrying on to later generations, of support in old age or of keeping the other, is treating the child only as a condition, and carries the direction of colonization; one sentence is read by that sentence itself, and no one sentence is grounds to judge that the child is only a condition.

Paper 6: "for the child's sake" legislating for the two people; using the child as a lock to keep the other, when, within the two people's arrangements, it falls to "only as," is treating the child only as a condition, and carries the direction of colonization; a sentence like "If you leave, you can forget about ever seeing the child again" is read by that sentence itself: it announces a consequence, and carries the direction of colonization.

Paper 7: raising the cost of the other's exit outward carries the direction of colonization; where a person no longer stands where he promised, and lets the semblance of love hold up the other's arrangements in his place, this is read as the structure of dropping, and this is the leaving person's reading of himself; saying on leaving, for the other, "Nothing has been lost these years," slides in the direction of colonization.

Taken together, what these directions borrow are things that are, or may come to be, in a marriage: endorsement, vows, "we," the household, division of labor, giving, children, the arrangements of the long years; some of these are not in every marriage, and are not on that account components of marriage either. These things carry no direction in themselves. One and the same certificate can be only a making public to that institution; one and the same vow can be only where the one who speaks it stands today; one and the same child is another end. The direction is read on the one effect in which they are taken up to do something; whether it took hold is seen in how the other afterward understands and acts.

Not heading toward colonization in a relation is not easy to begin with; in marriage many things are bound together, endorsement, vows, the household, and the division of labor and children there may be, and each one is a place that can be taken up to do something else, to slide in the direction of colonization. So relations are all mixed: mixing is the normal state, and staying always at 15DD is only an ideal type;28 the ideal type does not prove that the kind of mutual non dubito that holds at any time and in any place does not exist; that kind this series does not take up (§2). What this series reads is direction; a single sliding is read on that one occasion, and it is not on that account judged that a marriage has exited scope; exit from scope is read by Paper 1, MA9. This series does not on this account say that marriage is dangerous, nor does it on this account urge people to marry or not to marry; it only reads.

§7 Remainders

Several things this series registers, and does not settle.

First, whether "the household" can separately be established as a bearer. Meaning Theory leaves this undecided (Meaning Theory P3, Y27); this series establishes only that a little circle of two does not form a third bearer, and that "we" and "the household" neither take in nor replace each one's meaning; whether the household itself can separately be established is not judged (Paper 2, Paper 5, Paper 6).

Second, the kind in which two people are in mutual non dubito at any time and in any place: not taken up; only registered, and it is not proved that it does not exist (§2).

Third, a child one party brings into the marriage. When one party already has a child at remarriage, the child comes into the other's life without the two having prepared for him together; on the side between husband and wife, the items of Paper 6 can largely be read as they stand, but the starting point between the two and the child differs; what each has acknowledged, and from when, this series has not read.

Fourth, how one party's self-relation and the dealings between the two are entangled. A person has, for years, spoken to the other by a sentence that came from outside; is that sentence, at this moment, his own, or is an outside saying running through his mouth? This question lies between his own yesterday and today; Paper 4 reads only the stretch between the two.

Fifth, how sparse the moments of love in a marriage can become before this series no longer reads it. Love is read by moments and seen from the subject's side, and the moments cannot be counted from outside; Paper 7 gives no line, and cannot give one.

Sixth, what it means for the two adults that a child cannot choose its first conditions. Paper 5 reads only which direction the conditions go in, and issues no ought; the child's side belongs to Parent-Child Theory.

Seventh, Gilbert's joint commitment. Paper 1 picks up her account at the layer of marriage, and answers by where each one stands, by the effects already left, and by endorsement's public procedure. After interfacing with her, Relation Theory wrote: "this shows what it keeps, and does not amount to having already explained all of Gilbert's normative features".29 This restriction comes along to the layer of marriage, and this series has not made it good.

§8 The Propositions of the Whole Series

The propositions of the papers are listed together in the very words of each paper's propositions, with only a full stop added at the end; argument and qualification are in each paper.

Paper 1, Joined

MA1　The marriage this series reads has as its premise that both parties are headed in the 15DD direction. Under this premise, marriage is made up of three things: love; each person's own commitment; institutional endorsement. The three each do their own work and cannot stand in for one another: the love between the two is something the marriage this series reads cannot do without; if it is entirely gone, that marriage exits this series' scope; it is also where exclusivity comes from; commitment is what each person promised, in word and deed, and where he stands; it binds by where one stands and by the effects already left, and breaking it is entered on the books; endorsement makes this union public to an institution, obtains recognition-based rights in return, and takes on that institution's rules. The three are also read differently: love is seen from the subject's side; commitment is seen from outside, by words and deeds and the structure they leave; endorsement is seen by that institution's rules. Registration and vows can be in the same ceremony, and are entered apart: registration is making public to an institution, and is not read as the words and deeds of commitment.

MA2　The 15DD direction is a direction, not a state. People are all mixed; a layer records one operation, and falling back is read as it occurs. What the object layer reads is marriage in which both parties are headed in this direction; at the operation layer each party confirms only his own end, and does not judge at which layer the other is; the other end appears as the other party's first-person avowal and where he stands, and I do not certify it; if it is later confirmed that the object did not obtain, this series exits this case, and does not turn and judge the other end. From outside, the love, avowal and layer of either end are not certified, and the whole marriage is not characterized on this basis; of structural conditions that can already be checked, one can still say whether this series' premise holds there, without turning to judge the inner life of either end.

MA3　Marriage must have love, and what it must have is the love between the two: where the love between the two is entirely gone, whether the two still need this marriage or keep it up only by their own commitments, it is no longer the marriage this series reads; this series looks at structure, does not read minds, and does not criticize. Where both love the child and there is no love between them, it is not among the marriages this series reads either; love for the child cannot stand in for the love between the two. How love works within marriage is read by Love Theory.

MA4　Marriage is not one promise; it is the same promise made anew each day: whoever stands there on any given day is the person of today. Commitment cannot lock tomorrow; it binds by where one stands and by the effects already left; it is not read as a commitment lapsing every day and being made over every day.

MA5　Institutional endorsement is the two parties' making this marriage public to an institution, recognizing the institution's power, and obtaining recognition-based rights in return. Where that institution's rules are met, it counts; where they are not, that institution has not endorsed. The rules themselves are acknowledged but not judged; this side is only registered, not argued. Whether the endorsement counts goes by that institution's rules; whether that marriage is in this series is another matter. Nor does the existence of a registered endorsement endorse the legitimacy of its rules.

MA6　Endorsement cannot stand in for a person's own commitment, nor for love. To use endorsement to avow on the other's behalf, for instance "We've got the certificate, so you're mine," slides in the direction of colonization.

MA7　Endorsement gives commitment and exit alike a public side; it can also be one of the conditions created for someone who has not yet come.

MA8　The exclusivity of marriage comes from love: love in the 15DD direction is exclusive to begin with. Commitment acknowledges it and says it out loud; the binding of commitment is seen from outside by acts and structure: what was promised, in word and deed; whether later words and deeds crossed the line of what was promised; and the arrangements already left with the other party. A passing stirring of the heart is a matter in one's heart and is not judged; it does not break the commitment, and carries no direction of colonization; whether he still stands there is a matter at his end. Endorsement makes it public. The exclusivity of both is two one-way exclusivities meeting; it is not obtained by exchange, nor generated by agreement. Exclusivity applies to love, not to recognition; pushing exclusivity up to the layer of recognition is preferential love.

MA9　Three kinds of marriage are not in this series, and each is read differently. A purely transactional marriage, in which both parties treat the other only as a means: this is an ideal type; from outside only clues can be seen; it is said only conditionally. A marriage kept up without love between the two: love is seen from the subject's side and cannot be read from outside; it too is said only conditionally; this series does not criticize. A marriage whose exit has been sealed off is not in this series, whether it was sealed off by a construct or by the other party, or the person sealed himself in with a vow. What is sealed off by a construct or by the other party structurally reduces a person to a means: structure is looked at; the structure settled between the two is read, not that institution's rules; whether the person himself at this point still stands where he promised is entered separately. Where the person seals himself in with a vow, this seals off, in running, his own possibility of changing, while in qualification the position is still his: it is counted only where it really makes exit actually unreachable; it is a first-person matter, and is said only conditionally; it is read as locking, not called self-colonization; if what holds up that lock is an outside saying, it is read as colonized. Where the latter two arise after marriage, that marriage exits scope from then on, and this is not traced back; what exits scope is this case; this series turns and judges neither the two people's own direction and love, nor the time before.

Paper 2, Each Their Own

MA10　You are you, I am I, and only then is there an us. When "we" is said in marriage, it divides into two branches: doing things together, and mutual recognition; which branch occurs is checked case by case. Neither branch comes before the two, nor stands above them; neither legislates, nor passes final judgment on either party's own law; the binding force of rules the two have agreed on passes separately through each one's own law, or comes from a procedure both have already acknowledged. Each person's commitment is still entered separately, by each one's avowal, where each stands and the effects already left; it does not form a third branch of "we": in something done together, it is the share each has avowed, and when it is present together with mutual recognition, it is still not borne by "we" on either party's behalf.

MA11　The attribution of meaning lies with each person; the outlet of a direction can lie outside oneself. The directions of two people can cross in one and the same matter done together, while each relation remains anchored in its own bearer, and they do not merge into one; "we" and "the household" do not take in, nor replace, each person's meaning. Whether "the household" can come to stand separately as a bearer is not judged.

MA12　Recognizing the other as an end too: the other has his own cannot-not, which I only recognize as there, and whose content I cannot read out; I do not make for the other the decisions he can make himself; feelings the other voices I can fail to understand, and I do not deny that he really has them.

MA13　Even the most intimate marriage has places that cannot be entered: the other's internality, solitude, external relationships and past are the other's own.

MA14　Non dubito in marriage has both sides: not doubting that oneself is an end, and not doubting that the other is an end. Both sides are choices each person makes anew, time after time, not a state a marriage arrives at; from outside only words and deeds and structure are read, and minds are not read.

MA15　The directions of colonization read from this item: entering in the name of intimacy; judging one party's law for her in the name of "this household" or "for our sake," that is, using "we" or "the household" to take in and replace one party's direction, which is a direction of colonization that borrows a construct against a person; marriage, like other institutions, cannot produce a "we" that gathers each person into one; passing final judgment on something done together on the ground that "We love each other, so you have to agree," which borrows mutual recognition as adjudication; which branch "For the sake of this household, you have to ..." actually borrows is checked by its use on that occasion, not assigned by its sentence form; handing one's own meaning to the other for safekeeping, and then demanding it of the other.

Paper 3, Into One's Own

MA16　To bring the other into his own is to let the other grow into the other's own self. Bringing someone into their own is cultivation, and is a direction; whether it took hold lies at the other's end. To declare on the other's behalf, on the sole basis of my judgment of success, that his constitution has been completed is no longer bringing him into his own.

MA17　I bring you into your own without waiting for you to bring me into mine first. Your direction is yours; I can only make space for it; it is completed at your end, and is not mine to settle. Only when two such one-ways meet is it mutual, and it is not obtained by exchange; if they do not meet, each one's share is still there as before; mutuality is not a state a marriage is meant to reach.

MA18　Bringing someone into their own takes two main ways: creating conditions, and questioning. Questioning leaves the question to the other, and does not draw the conclusion on the other's behalf; to draw the conclusion on the other's behalf is to take my direction as the other's ruler.

MA19　Mutuality requires neither simultaneity nor equality, and keeps no accounts: bringing each other into their own is not entered as an account of exchange awaiting the other's repayment; commitments, contributions and the effects already left are entered as before. Division of labor and cooperation is not exchange, because it is not a mutual requirement. Complementarity is not a problem; demanding of each other that the other be the same as oneself is taking my direction as the other's ruler.

MA20　What the other has gained from me, once avowed in his own law, is his in the attribution of constitution, and does not become a constitution I completed on his behalf; shared property, contribution, credit and responsibility each have their own accounts, and are not settled by this proposition. Within the boundary of not infringing the positions of other subjects, the other may go in directions I did not foresee or do not approve of, and may even go in a direction in which he no longer needs me; this sentence is in the mode of ability, not a statement of fact. Bringing each other into their own has a mutual shape, and does not certify 16DD.

MA21　The direction of colonization read from this matter: fixing for the other what he ought to become; converting the conditions provided into ownership of the other's first-person constitutive results, as in "Everything you have today, I gave you"; the real contribution is entered as before, and what this sentence occupies is the other's step of becoming himself. To write bringing someone into their own as exchange is falling back. To hand over one's own share is accommodation, not bringing someone into their own: it is not having stood, and structurally carries no direction of colonization toward the other; how things are on his own side cannot be seen from outside, and is not judged. Any further pressure applied outward is entered separately, by that one effect; accommodation is not determined from years of giving way.

Paper 4, The Years

MA22　Growth over the long term: each person's yesterday, today and tomorrow land in one and the same stretch of dealings; people change, and after they change, the two choose again together. Choosing again together takes two shapes: neither law changes and a new arrangement is formed, or one party, or both, separately revise, and the two then confirm together that the new way of doing things fits.

MA23　To measure the other as he is now by the other as he was then, and to say it out loud, fixing for the other who he is, slides in the direction of colonization.

MA24　Division of labor is a present arrangement the two have acknowledged together; it can be avowed anew, and it can be changed; its binding force passes separately through each party's own law. Where division of labor slides into a fixed requirement aimed at the other, by being treated as something the other may not change, by being hung on who the other is, or by being swapped out within habit, there is a tendency toward colonization: saying it out loud and characterizing the division of labor slides in the direction of colonization; making it unchangeable for the other in running carries the direction of colonization. One party's own treating the division of labor as unchangeable is his self-relation; structurally it has no outward direction, and it is not within this tendency.

MA25　A moment of exchange is falling back; it is common, and is sometimes words said in anger. The whole marriage is not characterized on the strength of this one occasion; if on this occasion one has already said out loud something that writes one's own giving as an account the other must repay, the direction is read by the effect of this one occasion; whether it has since become fixed into a continuing requirement is read separately, as sediment.

MA26　Expression is read separately by three grounds: a requirement that holds in one's own law; a role requirement explicitly arranged by both parties; only an expectation of the relation; expectation alone does not establish duty or accountability in reverse. To hang an expectation on who the other is, or on what he cares about, and on that basis to judge for the other, slides in the direction of colonization.

MA27　Conflict can become an avenue of chiseling in a relation, and this possibility is read together with its cost. After conflict has occurred again and again, stopping points can have become routine, and stalling too can have become the normal state; a long absence of conflict is not evidence of cultivation. What admits of diagnosis is this operation on this occasion, not this person; consensus does not ratify the past. Whether, after a violation, one can come back, acknowledge and repair is read with two ledgers in parallel.

Paper 5, For One Not Yet Here

MA28　Preparing for a child is two people creating conditions together for someone who has not yet come; it is the shape cultivation takes in the one kind that is creating conditions, and this paper reads only this kind. Questioning, and the one who comes later's own response, are entered separately where there are already actual dealings; the beginning of living together is not taken as the only dividing line.

MA29　When preparing for a child becomes a C that the two each avow and cannot not do, it is read as doing something together: you for A, I for B, and we do C together. C is the undertaking of creating conditions together for someone who has not yet come; it is not the child, nor is it simply either party's A; A and B are each one's own, and neither examines the other. One party's B can be just this: affirming the other's purpose of wanting a child, and being willing to give and to support for it; this too is his own cannot-not; whether a given person's B is this kind only his own end can confirm.

MA30　C must be avowed by both, each having a share he can do himself, and the shares put together able to bring it about; neither party holds its purpose and adjudication alone. What holding alone looks like in structure is one party's A borrowing the other's hands, how this undertaking is done being for one party alone to decide, and the other party's way to take part in deciding, to adjust and to withdraw not being there; then it is not the C spoken of here. If one party is willing to do things according to the other's arrangement, it does not count as holding alone, so long as his way to take part, adjust and withdraw is there. If one party does not avow it, there is no C. One party avowing on the other's behalf, or saying out loud a characterization of the other's changing his mind, slides in the direction of colonization; sealing the other's way to say no and to change his mind, or making it hard to travel, carries the direction of colonization; what crosses basic rights is judged separately as violation. Whether preparation occurred, what conditions it actually left, and whether that person later came are entered apart: that he did not come does not retroactively judge that the earlier preparation never occurred, nor is either party, on this basis, judged to be lacking in something; not having welcomed a child is not automatically the same as that position standing empty.

MA31　The new subject is an end too. The child is another end that preparing for a child welcomes, not a product of this undertaking; what the two leave for him are conditions, and they do not complete the avowal of his direction for him. The child's standing as an end is not created by this undertaking of preparation; nor does his own constitutive position pass to the two because he cannot choose which conditions he first enters. Whether the conditions still count as support is checked separately by the negative boundaries of support: the two people's conditions, judgments or arrangements are not passed off as his constitution; his channels of refusal, revision, exit and change of direction afterward are not sealed; and the conditions are kept for him to be able to unfold without revolving around either of the two. In the years when he cannot yet refuse or revise, what is protected is his existing standing as an end, that the source of his value is not placed outside him, and the openness of the conditions of first-person running; the meaning predicates on the two people's side are not copied onto him. He does not figure only as the continuation of the two, a link in continuing the line, a security for old age, or a condition for keeping the other party; "only as" is read from the two people's arrangements, and a sentence is read by that sentence itself; to fall to "only as" is to treat the child only as a condition, and carries the direction of colonization; the child's side is handed to Parent-Child Theory.

MA32　Preparing to adopt counts too: someone who has not yet come is someone who has not yet come into these two people's lives, not necessarily someone who does not yet exist.

MA33　The two people's looking-toward crosses on one and the same someone who has not yet come; each belongs to each, and they are not merged into one meaning of the household.

Paper 6, Three

MA34　After a third one has come, Marriage Theory reads only what lies between husband and wife: the side of shared child-rearing between husband and wife. What lies between the child and the parents is handed to Parent-Child Theory; where three or more are involved, dealings are entered on separate accounts, pair by pair.

MA35　The child is able to unfold without revolving around me, and also able to unfold without revolving around her: the test is read person by person. This is a test, not that the child must in fact leave; being able is limited to constitutive independence, not real-world self-sufficiency.

MA36　"For the child's sake" cannot legislate for the two people; taking the child as a lock to keep the other party, where it falls to "only as," is treating the child only as a condition, and carries the direction of colonization. "For the child's sake" and "for the sake of this household," when used to judge one party's law for her, have the same form; whether it appeals to something done together, to existing arrangements, or borrows mutual recognition to pass final judgment, is checked separately, case by case.

MA37　The share each person has done in shared child-rearing cannot be acknowledged by someone else in his place; shared child-rearing is not settled by unilateral exit.

MA38　After adoption has welcomed a third one, the side of shared child-rearing between husband and wife is read by this paper; the preparation the two make together for his coming is read by Paper 5.

Paper 7, Staying and Leaving

MA39　This paper reads only how a marriage that carries the 15DD direction ends: one party's or both parties' love ends; the commitment of marriage is broken; divorce; bereavement of a spouse. The kind of marriage that is kept up after the ending is not read.

MA40　Where one party's love is gone and he still stands on the commitment, he is read by the commitment and not judged to be a means; that marriage already lacks one party's love, and from then on this series exits this case; this is not traced back, and this series does not turn and judge that party. Whether love has ended is the affair of the one who loves. For how the two go on from there, this proposition lays out no road.

MA41　Where a person no longer stands where he promised, and lets the semblance of love hold up the other party's arrangements in his place, this is read as the structure of dropping; this is the leaver's reading of himself: what is looked at is the declaration and the structural effect of that promise on the other party, and he does not excuse himself by whether he owned the change in his heart; dropping is not reported from outside. Breaking the commitment of marriage is entered apart: dropping, falsity, non-fulfillment, obstruction, joint revision of the agreement; the direction of colonization falls only on dropping and falsity, and from outside only non-fulfillment, obstruction and joint revision of the agreement are reported, together with traces. Breaking it does not first establish that love has moved away, nor, merely because a new love or an affair outside marriage has appeared, conclude backward that the old love has ended. Leaving must be entered on the books, and there must also be an exit declaration the other party can clearly understand; entering on the books, the declaration and the effects already left are entered apart, and the declaration is not conditional on the other party's agreeing to the exit.

MA42　The qualification to exit cannot be locked, and only the basic component of the exit right is read; whether exit is within reach in running is checked separately, and what has been sealed off is not in this series. Whether one party's staying has grounds in his own law is read party by party; only his end can confirm it, and it is not divided by who knows what. Where exit is within reach, all that is taken away is the lock; cost, stalling and habit that has been surreptitiously swapped may still be what his staying rests on. Where one knows that the other party can leave, and leaving is also a practically available option, staying is no longer fixed solely by there being no way out; this still does not prove the grounds of this staying. The freedom to exit is read on the side of not doubting that oneself is an end: what is asked is whether the relation has left conditions of running for one's own staying and leaving and for choosing again; it is not inferred from the channels' being sealed that the person no longer recognizes himself as an end, nor certified from the channels' being within reach that he has avowed staying or trust. For a person who cannot exit, his position is not taken away from outside; what sealing off takes away is this side's room in running. The side of the other's also being an end is read for each, and is not cancelled automatically because exit has been obstructed. From outside only checkable words and deeds, conditions and clues are read, and non dubito is not certified on that basis.

MA43　The exit declaration lets the other party clearly understand that the person is exiting, and is at the layer of the relation; the dissolution of the marriage within the endorsing institution, with its public procedure, belongs to the recognition component of the exit right, and is registered by that institution's rules. Whether the two steps are completed at the same time is entered apart. Merely not having obtained dissolution within the institution does not by itself prove that the basic component of the exit right has been sealed off; nor does moving out of the shared home by itself prove that exit is already within reach. "Able to leave" means that the person himself can actually exit these dealings, withdraw his own side, and is checked by Relation Theory. A construct's power raising the cost of exit is acknowledged but not judged; raised until a person actually cannot leave, it is sealing off. One party's using a construct to bear down on the other is read as between person and person: raising the other party's cost of exit outward carries the direction of colonization; sealing off the other party's exit itself is, at the layer of basic rights, a violation besides, and that marriage too exits this series.

MA44　Exit does not settle the accounts: shared child-rearing and shared matters are not settled by unilateral exit; saying for the other party, on leaving, "Nothing was lost in all these years" slides in the direction of colonization.

MA45　Bereavement of a spouse: how things stand on the endorsement side goes by that institution's rules, acknowledged but not judged; love is seen from the subject's side, and death is not the same as the ending of love; whether there is another side after death is not judged.

§9 Drawing Together

This paper lays down no propositions, and proves no proposition at the object layer. What it says is this: Marriage Theory lies downstream on the relational side, following Love Theory, and reads, in the marriage of two people, the part that holds only in marriage; its scope is marriage in which both people are headed in the direction of recognizing the other as an end too, and it does not claim that other forms are not marriage; the direction is the premise, and marriage is made up of love, each person's own commitment and institutional endorsement; the main line is You are you, I am I, and only then is there an us; the two each look beyond themselves, toward the other, toward someone who has not yet come; what strings the seven papers together is the same promise made anew each day. The directions of colonization read in the papers borrow things that are, or may come to be, in a marriage; the direction is read on the one effect in which they are taken up to do something; people are all mixed, and a single sliding is read on that one occasion.

摘要

这一篇是婚姻论的 Paper 0，最后写，最先放。它不立命题，只为读这个系列的人做几件事：说明这个系列在 SAE 里的位置，写明它读到哪里、主线是什么，交代各篇共用的写法与同字的词，与几家传统作总对接，把各篇读到的殖民方向合说一次，合列全系列的四十五条命题，登记留下来不结的余项。

婚姻论是 SAE 关系论下游的第二支，接在爱情论之后。关系论只立所有关系都有的那一部分；爱情论读爱情里的那一部分，把承诺与制度保护两件都有的那一段交了过来。婚姻论只读两个人的婚姻，不主张别的形式不是婚姻；读的是带 15DD 方向的婚姻，即两个人都在认对方也是目的的那个方向上。这个方向是前提；在这个前提下，婚姻由三件合成：有爱，两人各自的承诺，制度背书。主线一句：你是你，我是我，然后才有我们；我们各自看向自己之外，看向对方，看向一个还没有来的人。七篇依次读：这个系列读的婚姻是什么；「我们」与各自；互相成就；长年；孕育；第三个人来了以后；去留。

这一篇与奥古斯丁、《周易》的咸与恒、李贽、布雷克作总对接。各篇读到的殖民方向，借的是婚姻里已经在或可能形成的件：背书，誓言，「我们」，家，分工，孩子；有些并非每段婚姻都有，也不因此成为婚姻的组分。这几件本身不带方向，方向在它们被拿来做什么的那一次作用上读。绑定的件多，可以被拿来滑向殖民的地方也就多；关系都是混合的，一次滑向照那一次读，不据此判一段婚姻出了射程。这一篇不证明任何对象层的命题。

第一节 位置：关系侧下游的第二支

SAE 有三侧：主体侧，关系侧，制度侧。关系侧由关系论展开；关系论写完以后，下游有几支：爱情论、婚姻论、亲子论、合作论；友谊不单列一支，作贯穿各支的例子。1 爱情论是第一支，婚姻论是第二支。

两层的分工，关系论写明了：抽象的关系论只立所有关系都具备的；只在某一种关系里成立的，交那一种关系的系列论证。2 所以凡关系一层已经立下的，这个系列直接用，不重写：关系的起码条件，涵育与殖民两个方向，期待，承诺，来往的沉积，结束，都照关系论读。婚姻里的爱，照爱情论读，也不重写。

与爱情论的界，交在《西厢记》的一句上：「愿普天下有情的都成了眷属」。爱情论读有情的；成了眷属，「有了承诺，又有了制度保护」，就到了婚姻论。爱情不因为成了眷属就结束，结婚以后也有爱情。3 第一篇从这一句接下来，读成三件：有情，是爱；成了眷属，是承诺与背书。

只在婚姻里成立的是什么，要与相邻的关系比过。对友谊，基础论文6 说友谊是「从婚姻中减去排他性和深度承诺」；4 对爱情，排他与承诺，爱情论里都已经有了。只在婚姻里成立的，落在承诺与背书同在的地方，以及背书给承诺与退出带来的那一面公开（第一篇 MA7）。共同生活与孕育，婚姻里可以有，却不只在婚姻里，婚姻里也不必有；它们是这个系列要读的内容，不是婚姻的门槛。

这个系列的主承，除了关系论与爱情论，还有意义论读一段关系向外那一面的那一篇。5 关系论读两个人之间的作用；意义论读一个人的方向，出口落在哪里。互相成就与孕育，两样都是方向的出口落在自己之外，这一层照意义论读。

第二节 射程与主线

射程。 有六条。

一，两个人的婚姻。这个系列只读传统意义下的婚姻，即两个人的婚姻；不主张别的形式的婚姻不是婚姻；别的形式，不读，也不判。当事人是成年人；性别不限，举例用传统的男女组合。

二，带 15DD 方向的婚姻。在 SAE 的层级里，认对方也是目的的那一层，记作 15DD。6 这个系列读的，是两方都在这个方向上的婚姻。方向，不是状态：朝着去，不必已经到了；人都是混合的，层按一次运作记，不记一个人，也不记一段婚姻，回落照读。两方是射程，不是夫妻之间的一项要求：每一方只确认得了自己这一端，另一端以对方第一人称的认领与站出现，我不认证它，也不替他撤销（第一篇 MA2）。全系列写成条件式：两个人在这个方向上时，婚姻怎样合成，又怎样运作。7 从外面不给一段真实的婚姻定性，只见线索。

三，前提与三件。方向是前提，不是组分。在这个前提下，婚姻由三件合成：有爱，两人各自的承诺，制度背书。三件作用不同，不能互相替代；读法也不同：爱从主体这一侧看，承诺从外面看言行与它留下的结构，背书看那个制度的规则（第一篇 MA1）。婚姻不能简单地还原为承诺。

四，不读的。纯交易的婚姻；两人之间完全没有了爱的婚姻，不论两个人还需要这段婚姻，还是只凭各自的承诺维持；退出被封死的婚姻，无论怎样封的。三种的读法各不相同；后两种发生在婚后的，那段婚姻自那时起出射程，不追溯，也不倒判两个人（第一篇 MA9）。一方的爱没有了的，那段婚姻自那时起也不在（第七篇 MA40）。这里只看结构，不读心，也不批判：没有了爱的那两种，不另判谁把谁当作手段。不读，不是说它们不是婚姻。孩子怎样来到世上的具体过程，不在这个系列的范围。

五，上界停在 15DD。两个单向碰上的那一瞬，即 16DD 的瞬间，爱情论第六篇已经读过，这里回指，不另读。两个人何时何地都彼此不疑的那一种，关系论交给各支自己论证；8 婚姻论的答复与爱情论同：不接，只登记，不证明它没有。

六，与相邻系列的界。婚姻里的爱，归爱情论；父母与孩子之间，归亲子论；一般的共同做事，归合作论，婚姻论只读婚姻里的共同做事；婚姻的制度那一面，归制度侧，这里只登记；关系一层已立的，照关系论读。

主线。 散文式的一句是：你是你，我是我，然后才有我们。我们各自看向自己之外：看向对方，看向一个还没有来的人。

前一句是前提，出自基础论文6 附录里的《SAE 婚姻原则》，9 在第二篇论证。婚姻里一说「我们」，照关系论分两支，一起做的事与相认；两支都不先于两个人，也不高于两个人，不立法，不终审各自的法（第二篇 MA10）。

后一句说两种看向。技术式的说法是：在 15DD 方向上，婚姻里两个人的意义各归各人，方向的出口落在自己之外：落在对方身上，让对方成长成对方自己；落在一个还没有来的人身上，一起为他创造条件。意义的归属在各人，「归属端点闭于一人」；10 出口可以在外，两人的方向可以交错，各自的关系仍锚在各自身上，不合成一个（第二篇 MA11）。归属闭于一人，也推不出一个人自给自足、不需要外面。11

两种看向，结构不同。看向对方，是互相成就：两个不等对方先成就自己的单向碰上了，碰不上，各人那一份照旧在；成就就是涵育，创造条件与追问是主要的两种方式（第三篇）。看向一个还没有来的人，是孕育：两个人各有各的目的，一起做一件事，你为 A，我为 B，我们一起做 C；C 是一起为那个人创造条件这件事，不是孩子（第五篇）。

把七篇串起来的，还有一句：婚姻不是一次承诺，是每天重新做出的同一个承诺；每一天站在那里的，都是今天的人（第一篇 MA4）。长年怎样过（第四篇），第三个人来了以后怎样（第六篇），怎样结束（第七篇），都在这一句底下读。

第三节 写法

全系列照下面几条写。

层按一次运作记；人都是混合的。 不记一个人，也不记一段婚姻；夫妻之间谁也不判对方在哪一层。

谈结构，不谈好坏。 不以长短、稳定、有没有孩子、有什么成果，给婚姻分等级。

不发应当；不论证具体规则。 基础论文6 附录里的《SAE 婚姻原则》是一处工作展示，不作论证件。12 这个系列论证它所含的原理，照结构写，原句照引；具体规则只作例子。原句里带评价或量的语汇，引到时就地标明不取那一层；与已发布的关系论不合的地方，以关系论为准。

只讲关系。 制度的权力与承认性的权利，只登记，不立论，不借这两个概念推命题；人借构做的事，照人与人读；规则本身认而不判。13

对象层与操作层分开；只条件式地说。 对象层读两方都在这个方向上、有爱的婚姻；操作层每一方只确认自己这一端。爱从主体这一侧看，从外面读不出；纯交易的婚姻是理想型；本人拿誓言把自己封住，是第一人称的事；撑着那道锁的若是外来的说法，读作被殖民，从外面同样看不见；这几处都只条件式地说。

不读心，看动作，看结构。 承诺、违背、撂下、退出，照言行与结构读；心里认不认、动没动，不判。不疑的两面，从外面也只读言行与结构。

涵育与殖民取宽义。 说的是一次作用的方向，不是贴给人或婚姻的标签：涵育朝腾出空间去，殖民朝灌入并占住去；方向在这一次作用上读，成没成看对方此后的理解与行动。侵另判，只在基础权利一层。14 「滑向」「倾向」不读作已经成了。

交换的片刻与此后的沉积分开读。 片刻照回落读，不凭一次给整段婚姻定性；分工滑成指向对方的固定要求、在习惯里被偷换、说不的路变难走，另按沉积读。

如非必要，不造新词；归别处的，回指不重写。 婚姻里的爱归爱情论；父母与孩子之间归亲子论；关系一层的结构与对接归关系论。

量的语汇另核。 不说越久越真、越稳越好；「更」「越……越……」「最」用在婚姻上，一律另核。

例子。 只用成年人物；举例用传统的男女组合；每篇各用各的一对。自设的例子只用来看结构；一对夫妻的言行只是线索，不认证谁在哪一层。历史例子只照史料里的言行读结构与方向，不判那两个人有没有爱、在不在这个方向上；只用已故、史料公开的人物；当事人成婚时未成年的联姻不作例子；纯交易的婚姻不作正面的例子，只在对接与施压处出现。

传统对接。 只接思想与哲学；文学作例子；文学家有立论的，算哲学。每一家照实写他认出了什么、为了什么看、在哪里施压、在哪里相遇、在哪里分开；这几步不每家凑齐，原典里取不出对着这个系列的反命题，就不造施压。别的传统用它自己的词出场。按问题分接各家，不给哲学家判层。关系一层与爱情一层已经对过的，回指；回指时，那一处的限制一并带来。

出处与推及。 上游的结论在正文用平实话说；出处、原句与别篇的命题号放在脚注；推到原处之外的，写明是推及，由这个系列自担。

第四节 同字的词

几个词在各篇里同字，意思要分开。

制度。 宽义：「婚姻和友谊是微观关系制度」；15 说制度背书时，另指两个人之外、依自己的规则认下这一段的那个构，正式的或非正式的。两个人之间的关系本身也是一种构，不立法，不承担；两个人的小圈子不成第三个承担者（第一篇）。

制度背书与制度保护。 同指。背书更厚，也更强：保护只说那个制度给了这一段什么；背书还说两个人做了什么，把这一段向它公开，承认它的权力，从它那里换取承认（第一篇）。

交换、换取、分工与互相。 交换说的是夫妻两人之间，你给我这个，我给你那个，交换的片刻是回落（第四篇）。换取说的是两个人与背书的那个构之间，是人与构（第一篇）。分工是两人一起认下的现在安排，不是互相的要求（第四篇）。互相，是两个单向碰上了，不以对方先给或必给为条件（第三篇）。

纯交易。 只说夫妻两人之间：两方都只把对方当作手段。一对夫妻盼着有孩子延续家族，不因此就是纯交易；孩子若只被当作条件，另读（第一篇、第五篇）。两人之间完全没有了爱还维持的婚姻，也不因此读作纯交易：它不在这个系列里，是因为缺了爱，不另判谁把谁当作手段（第一篇 MA3）。

目的。 两义。「目的那一层」照爱情论，记作 14DD：她成了我往哪里去，「别的人也说得通，我换不掉」；16 这是我自己的方向。「认对方也是目的」的目的，在 15DD：对方有对方往哪里去，不是我的方向里的一处。说拿爱去要排他「落回目的那一层（14DD）」，用的是前一义：把我的方向当成了她的尺子（第一篇）。日常话里，目的也指为什么做一件事；第五篇说两个人各有各的目的，取这一义。

方向。 照爱情论分三义。17 说 15DD 方向，是朝着去、还没到的那一处；说涵育与殖民两个方向，是一次作用朝哪里去；说一个人自己的方向，是他往哪里去（第三篇）。

承诺。 照关系论读：答应的言行，站与改，留下的作用；隐含的承诺也算，仍是我这一侧的认领与站。「两人各自的承诺」：誓言即使两人一起说，各人的站仍是各人的（第一篇）。

「我们」与「家」。 「我们」照关系论分两支，一起做的事与相认。日常话里，有时只是把两个人各自做下的事并在一句里说；那不立一个「我们」，也不是第三支。两支都不是站在两个人上面、替他们做决定的「我们」。「家」取日常义；家能否另行成为一个承担者，不判（第二篇；第七节）。

意义。 取意义论的义：一个承担者在跨时的承接里，与他所认领的内容之间的那段关系；归属端点闭于一人（第二篇）。

不疑。 两面：不疑自己是目的，不疑他人是目的。不疑不是没有疑惑，也不等于信任（第二篇）。

成就、支持与构成。 成就作动词，说的是让对方成长成对方自己；不指成果，不作指标，说的是方向，不说已经成了。支持不是替代构成；构成是一个人以自己的法认领一项内容、使它成为他自己的那一步。构成的归属，与物的所有、署名、功劳与责任，是几件不同的事（第三篇）。

法与安排；变与修订；冲突。 法是一个人第一人称承担、持续组织他行动的那一层；安排是时间、角色、程序怎样排，分工在安排这一层。变是长年里的起落；修订是一个人以第一人称改了自己的一条法，认了账；变不证明修订已经发生。冲突取冲突论的义；夫妻之间许多吵架只是碰撞（第四篇）。

孕育、还没有来的人、孩子与后代、共同做的事、条件。 孕育只取一义：两个人一起为一个还没有来的人创造条件。还没有来的人，是还没有来到这两个人的生活里，不一定还不存在。正文写孩子；后代带着一条家族的线，只在对接传统处用。两个人一起做的这件事不叫共同目的：目的各是各的，共同的只是做的这一件。条件是后来者可以进入的东西，不是替他定下的方向（第五篇）。

三个人、共同养育与锁。 三个人是三个承担者，不是一个三人的主体；孩子是第三个人，不是两个人合成的第三者。共同养育只读它在夫妻之间的那一面。日常说「拿孩子当锁」；关系论用「锁」字用得窄，封死退出本身才是锁了（第六篇）。

退出与离婚；退出权；知情；走得了。 退出是收回自己这一侧，是一个动作；离婚是婚姻在背书的那个制度里解除。退出权有两个分量；这个系列说退出被封死，说的是基础分量；离婚的手续属承认分量，只登记。知情有两义，这个系列读知道对方可以离开那一义。走得了，指本人实际能够退出这段来往，不指办完了手续或搬了出去（第七篇）。

滑向、带与侵；倾向；被殖民。 殖民的方向有几种说法，照关系论的分法：说出口的定性，替对方定一件事对他意味着什么，是滑向殖民的方向；向外抬高对方退出的成本而路仍走得通，或把对方说不、改主意的通道封住、弄得难走，是带殖民这个方向；封死退出本身，在基础权利一层是侵，同时也带殖民这个方向；别的越过基础权利的，在那一层另判为侵，带不带殖民这个方向，照那一次作用另读。18 一句话是一次作用，照那一句读，不据一句话判一个人，也不据它判一段婚姻。倾向是总说，说的是一处安排朝哪里滑，例如分工滑成指向对方的固定要求；落到一次作用上，照滑向与带分。一个人自己把一项安排当作改不得的，是他的自我关系，从结构上看没有向外的方向，不在倾向里。本人拿誓言把自己封住，读作锁定，不叫自我殖民；撑着那道锁的若是外来的说法，外来的说法占住了他自己修订的那一步，读作被殖民；他自己的昨天与今天之间那一笔另记，不另起名字（第一篇）。说本人拿誓言把自己封住，指的是誓言在运行上使退出实际走不到的那一种（第一篇 MA9），与上面带殖民方向的那一档「封住」不同。

第五节 总对接

各篇按问题接了各家。第一篇接康德论相互取得、黑格尔论婚姻不是契约、洛克论自愿的约定、克尔凯郭尔论婚姻的审美有效性与吉尔伯特的共同承诺；第二篇接黑格尔论家庭、蒙田、诺齐克、康德论爱与敬、拜尔论信任；第三篇接穆勒、亚里士多德论夫妻之间的友爱、谭嗣同与康德论他人的完善；第四篇接卡维尔、波伏瓦、亚里士多德论夫妇之间的统治与班昭；第五篇接《礼记·昏义》与《孟子》、康德与阿伦特；第六篇接亚里士多德、黑格尔论孩子与洛克；第七篇接休谟、弥尔顿、黑格尔论离婚、鲁迅与《庄子》。有几家，问的是全系列，放在这里。

三善（奥古斯丁）。 奥古斯丁《论婚姻之善》开篇说，每个人都是人类的一部分，人的本性是社会的，「人类社会第一个自然的纽带，是夫与妻」。19 他随即说，婚姻之善不只为生子，也为两性之间那份自然的结伴本身；不然，老年夫妇，尤其是失了儿子或从未生育的，就不能再叫婚姻了。20 他把婚姻的善合起来说成三样：子嗣，忠信，圣事。21 圣事一样，使婚姻的纽带不因分开而解开：婚姻之约「不因离婚介入而废除」，只有夫或妻一方死了，纽带才解开。22 他为说明婚姻凭什么是一种善而看。他认出：婚姻里有几样不同的东西，各有各的位置，不能合成一件，也不能互相替代。

他施压在不可解除这一处：他把不可解除的纽带列入婚姻之善，这个系列说承诺锁不住明天、人可以离开，两家在这里相撞；可纽带不解，不独自判定退出在运行上被封死，这一层见下面分开二。子嗣一处压得轻：他把子嗣放进婚姻之善的说明，却没有据此取消没有孩子的婚姻。相遇在两处。一，孕育不是婚姻成其为婚姻的条件，他在这一处与这个系列同侧；这个系列把孕育读作婚姻里可能有的内容，不作门槛（第一节）。二，婚姻由几样合成，几样不能互相替代，这与第一篇的三件同形；忠信与承诺、排他同侧。分开在三处。一，他的三样是婚姻之善的说明，这个系列的三件是它所读的婚姻怎样合成：爱，承诺，背书；两张表问的不是同一件事，可以并列，不由一项一项增删来互相反驳。二，不可解除：这个系列读背书，是向那个制度公开、带上它的规则；一个制度不许解除，是它的规则，认而不判；人可以离开，这一层不由那个制度给出，只没有取得制度里的解除，不独自证明退出被封死（第七篇 MA43）。三，这个系列不证明人应当结婚，也不在婚姻与不婚之间分高下。

咸与恒（《周易》）。 《周易》下经以咸始，咸后是恒。《彖》说：「咸，感也」；「恒，久也」。23 《序卦》从天地、万物、男女一路排到夫妇，又说：「夫妇之道不可以不久也，故受之以恒」。24 它为说明卦序的来由、由天地推出人伦的次第而看。它认出：感与久是两件；一时的感应，与夫妇之道在时间里的久，不是一件事。它向这个系列施压：「不可以不久」是一句「必」，夫妇之道必要长久；这个系列说承诺锁不住明天，不以长短给婚姻分等级，也不发应当，在它那里，就失了夫妇之道的一半。相遇在：感与久分开，正好对着两个系列的分工：爱情论读那一刻的不得不，这一个，在这一刻，不可替换；婚姻论读一天一天的长年。久也不是一次做成的：婚姻是每天重新做出的同一个承诺（第一篇 MA4）。分开在两处。一，这个系列不说夫妇之道必要长久；久不是判准，第四篇不证明越久越好；久，在这里只是每天由今天的人重新做出的那个样子，不是一个锁定的状态。二，《序卦》由夫妇推出父子、君臣、上下的次第；这个系列不排人伦的次第，父母与孩子之间归亲子论，不由婚姻推出。

夫妇为人伦之始（李贽）。 李贽在《初潭集》里说：「夫妇，人之始也。有夫妇然后有父子，有父子然后有兄弟，有兄弟然后有上下」；在《焚书》的〈夫妇论〉里说：「吾究物始，而见夫妇之为造端也」，「极而言之，天地一夫妇也」。25 他针对的，是以理、以太极为万物之始的说法；他把夫妇放在人伦之首。26 他为回答万物与人伦从哪里起头而看。他认出：起头的不是一，是两；天地也是两个。相遇在：两个人在前，不先立一个「一」去统两个人；你是你，我是我，然后才有我们（第二篇），与「天地一夫妇」的两，同向。这一同向有它的限度：「两」不单独证明两端各自的法不被代立，那一层，第二篇另行论证。分开在：他说的先后，是发生与伦理的次第，人伦从哪里起头；这个系列说婚姻论在关系论的下游，说的是论证上的依赖，关系论只立所有关系都具备的，婚姻是其中一种。两处的先后所问不同，不作源头与分支的正面对立；他的次第，这里只作旁照。这个系列也不由夫妇推出父子、兄弟，亲子另是一支；天地万物从哪里起头，这个系列不答。

最小化婚姻（布雷克）。 布雷克在《最小化婚姻》里主张，一个政治自由主义的国家应当把关怀关系认作有价值的，平等地分配支持这些关系的权利；她的最小婚姻，「会提供直接支持关系的权利，而不提供经济上的好处，不限性别，也不限人数」，于是不只延及多边的关系，也延及无性、无浪漫之爱的人。27 她为回答一个自由主义的国家可以有怎样的婚姻法而看。她认出：那个制度的承认，与它所承认的关系，是两样；承认的规则可以换一种立法。她向这个系列施压两处。一，她的最小婚姻不限人数；这个系列只读两个人，就只读了婚姻的一种形式。二，她的最小婚姻也给无浪漫之爱的关系；这个系列说婚姻要有爱（第一篇 MA3），在她那里，是给婚姻加了一道它本不必有的条件。相遇在：背书照那个制度的规则算，规则可以各不相同（第一篇 MA5）；这个系列也不主张别的形式不是婚姻。分开在：她答的是法律应当怎样，是政治哲学里的应当；这个系列只讲关系，制度那一面只登记，规则认而不判，不论证婚姻法该怎样立。两个人与有爱，是这个系列的射程条件，说的是它读哪一种婚姻，不是说那个制度该承认哪一种。

第六节 各篇读到的殖民方向，合说一次

每一篇都读出了这一件上的殖民方向。

第一篇：拿背书替对方认领，「领了证，你就是我的人」，滑向殖民的方向；拿誓言当不许走的理由，给对方的退出定性，滑向殖民的方向，宣告后果的，带殖民这个方向，封死的，是侵，同时也带殖民这个方向；拿爱去要排他，落回目的那一层（14DD），把我的方向当成她的尺子。本人拿誓言把自己封住，读作锁定，不叫殖民；撑着那道锁的若是外来的说法，读作被殖民；这两种，那段婚姻都不在这个系列里。

第二篇：以亲密之名进入；以「这个家」「为了我们」之名，收下并替下一方的方向，这是借构对人的殖民方向；以「我们相爱，所以你得同意」终审一件一起做的事，是把相认借作裁断；把自己的意义交给对方保管，再向对方要。

第三篇：替对方定他该成为什么；把所给的条件换算成对对方构成成果的占有，「你今天的一切都是我给的」。另有两样不在这里：把成就写成交换，是回落；迁就，是没有站住，从结构上看没有向对方去的殖民方向，他自己这一侧怎样，从外面看不出来，不判（MA21）。

第四篇：拿当年的对方量现在的对方，说出口替他定他是谁，滑向殖民的方向；分工滑成指向对方的固定要求，有殖民的倾向，说出口的，滑向殖民的方向，使对方改不了的，带殖民这个方向；一个人自己把分工当作改不得的，是他的自我关系，不在这里；把自己的付出写成对方须偿还的账，照那一次作用读；把期待挂到对方是谁、在乎什么上，替对方作判，滑向殖民的方向。

第五篇：一方替另一方认下，或说出口把对方改主意定成对不起自己，滑向殖民的方向；宣告可兑现的后果，把对方说不、改主意的路封住或弄得难走，带殖民这个方向；越过基础权利的，另判为侵。孩子在两人的安排里落到「只作为」延续、继后世、养老或留住对方的条件，是把孩子只当作条件，带殖民这个方向；一句话照那一句本身读，不据一句话判孩子只作为条件。

第六篇：「为了孩子」替两个人立法；拿孩子当留住对方的锁，在两人的安排里落到「只作为」时，是把孩子只当作条件，带殖民这个方向；「你要走，孩子你就别想再见」这样一句，照那一句本身读，是宣告后果，带殖民这个方向。

第七篇：向外抬高对方的退出成本，带殖民这个方向；人已不站在答应过的地方，又让爱的外观替他撑着对方的安排，读作撂下的结构，这是离开的人对自己的读法；离开时替对方说「这些年什么也没损失」，滑向殖民的方向。

合起来看，这些方向借的，是婚姻里已经在或可能形成的件：背书，誓言，「我们」，家，分工，付出，孩子，长年的安排；有些并非每段婚姻都有，也不因此成为婚姻的组分。这几件本身不带方向。同一纸证，可以只是向那个制度公开；同一句誓言，可以只是说的人今天的站；同一个孩子，是另一个目的。方向在它们被拿来做什么的那一次作用上读，成没成，看对方此后怎样理解、怎样做。

关系里不朝殖民去，本来就不容易；婚姻里绑定的件多，背书、誓言、家，以及可能有的分工与孩子，每一件都是一处可以被拿来做别的事、滑向殖民的地方。所以关系都是混合的：混合是常态，一直在 15DD 上的，只是理想型；28 理想型不证明何时何地都彼此不疑的那一种没有，那一种这个系列不接（第二节）。这个系列读的是方向，一次滑向照那一次读，不据此判一段婚姻出了射程；出射程，照第一篇 MA9 读。这个系列不因此说婚姻危险，也不因此劝人结婚或不结婚；它只读。

第七节 余项

几件事，这个系列登记，不结。

一，「家」能否另行成立为一个承担者。意义论在这一处留着不判（意义论 P3 Y27）；这个系列只立两个人的小圈子不成第三个承担者，「我们」「家」不收下、也不替下各人的意义，家本身能否另行成立，不判（第二篇、第五篇、第六篇）。

二，两个人何时何地都彼此不疑的那一种，不接；只登记，不证明它没有（第二节）。

三，一方带进婚姻的孩子。再婚时一方已有的孩子，来到另一方的生活里，不经两人一起的孕育；夫妻之间的那一面，第六篇的几件大体可以照读，可两人与孩子之间的起点不同，各人认下了什么、从什么时候认下，这个系列没有读。

四，一方的自我关系与两人之间的来往怎样缠在一起。一个人照着一句从外面来的话对另一个人说了多年，那一句此刻是他自己的，还是外来的说法经他的嘴在运；这一问在他自己的昨天与今天之间，第四篇只读到两人之间的那一段。

五，爱的片刻在一段婚姻里稀到什么程度，这个系列就不再读它。爱按片刻读，从主体这一侧看，从外面数不出片刻；第七篇不给线，也给不出线。

六，孩子选不了最初的条件，这一处对两个大人意味着什么。第五篇只读到条件朝哪个方向去，不发应当；孩子那一侧，归亲子论。

七，吉尔伯特的共同承诺。第一篇接她在婚姻这一层的一说，答在各人的站、已经留下的作用与背书的公开手续上。关系论与她对过以后写明：「这说明它保留了什么，不等于已经解释了吉尔伯特的全部规范特征」。29 这一处限制带到婚姻这一层，这个系列没有补。

第八节 全系列命题

各篇的命题，照各篇正文的命题原句合列，只在句末补句号；论证与限定在各篇正文。

第一篇 眷属

MA1　这个系列读的婚姻，以两方在 15DD 方向上为前提。在这个前提下，婚姻由三件合成：有爱；两人各自的承诺；制度背书。三件各有各的作用，不能互相替代：两人之间的爱，是这个系列读的婚姻里少不了的一件，完全没有了，那段婚姻就出了射程，它也是排他的来处；承诺是各人答应的言行与站，约束靠站与已经留下的作用，违背要入账；背书把这一段向制度公开，换取承认性权利，带上那个制度的规则。三件的读法也不同：爱从主体这一侧看；承诺从外面看言行与它留下的结构；背书看那个制度的规则。登记与誓言可以在同一场里，分开记：登记是向制度公开，不作承诺的言行读。

MA2　15DD 方向是方向，不是状态。人都是混合的，层按一次运作记，回落照读。对象层所读的，是两方都在这个方向上的婚姻；操作层每一方只确认自己这一端，不判对方在哪一层；另一端以对方第一人称的认领与站出现，我不认证它；若后来确认对象并未成立，这个系列退出这一件，不倒判对方那一端。从外面不认证两端的爱、认领与层位，不据此给整段婚姻定性；对已经可核的结构条件，仍可说明这个系列的前提在该处成不成立，不倒判任何一端的内心。

MA3　婚姻要有爱，要的是两人之间的爱：两人之间完全没有了爱，不论还需要这段婚姻，还是只凭各自的承诺维持着，都不再是这个系列读的婚姻；这个系列看结构，不读心，也不批判。两人都爱孩子而彼此之间没有爱，也不在这个系列读的婚姻里，对孩子的爱替不了两人之间的爱。婚姻里的爱怎样运作，照爱情论读。

MA4　婚姻不是一次承诺，是每天重新做出的同一个承诺：每一天站在那里的，都是今天的人。承诺锁不住明天，约束人靠站与已经留下的作用；不读作承诺每天失效、每天重订。

MA5　制度背书，是婚姻双方把这段婚姻向制度公开，承认制度的权力，换取承认性权利。符合那个制度的规则，就算；不符合的，那个制度没有背书。规则本身认而不判；这一面只登记，不立论。背书算不算，照那个制度的规则；那段婚姻在不在这个系列里，是另一件。登记的背书存在，也不替它的规则作正当性背书。

MA6　背书替不了本人的承诺，也替不了爱。拿背书替对方认领，例如「领了证，你就是我的人」，滑向殖民的方向。

MA7　背书让承诺与退出都有了公开的一面；它也可以是为还没有来的人创造的条件之一。

MA8　婚姻的排他来自爱：15DD 向的爱本来就排他。承诺把它认下并说出来；承诺的约束，从外面看动作与结构：答应的言行，此后的言行有没有越过答应过的界，以及已经在对方那里留下的安排。一时的动心是心里的事，不判；不违背承诺，不带殖民方向；站在不在，是他那一端的事。背书把它公开。两方的排他是两个单向的排他碰上了，不是交换来的，也不是约定生出来的。排的是爱的那一份；认不排他，把排他推到认那一层，是偏爱。

MA9　三种婚姻不在这个系列里，读法各不相同。纯交易的婚姻，两方都只把对方当作手段：这是理想型，从外面只见线索，只条件式地说。没有了两人之间的爱还维持的婚姻：爱从主体这一侧看，从外面读不出，也只条件式地说；这个系列不批判。退出被封死的婚姻，无论是构封的、另一方封的，还是本人拿誓言把自己封住的，都不在。构封的、另一方封的，在结构上把人还原为手段：看结构，读两人之间落成的结构，不读那个制度的规则；本人此时是不是仍站在答应过的地方，另记。本人拿誓言把自己封住的，在运行上封死了自己改变的可能，在资格上位置仍在他那里：只在确使退出实际不可达时计入，是第一人称的事，只条件式地说，读作锁定，不叫自我殖民；撑着那道锁的若是外来的说法，读作被殖民。后两种发生在婚后的，那段婚姻自那时起出射程，不追溯；出射程的是这一件，不倒判两人各自的方向与爱，也不倒判此前。

第二篇 各自

MA10　你是你，我是我，然后才有我们。婚姻里一说「我们」，分两支：一起做的事，与相认；哪一支发生，逐件另核。两支都不先于两人，也不高于两人，不立法，不终审各自的法；两人说定的规则，约束力分别经过各自的法，或者来自双方已经承认的程序。各人的承诺仍按各人的认领、站与已经留下的作用分别记；它不构成第三支「我们」：在一起做的事里，它是各人认下的那一份，与相认同场时，也不由「我们」替任何一方承担。

MA11　意义的归属在各人，方向的出口可以在自己之外。两人的方向可以在同一件共同做的事上交错，各自的关系仍锚在各自身上，不合成一个；「我们」「家」不收下、也不替下各人的意义。「家」能否另行成立为一个承担者，不判。

MA12　认对方也是目的：对方有对方的不得不，我只认它在，读不出它的内容；不替对方做他能自己做的决定；对方说出的感受，可以不理解，不否定它确实有。

MA13　再亲密的婚姻也有进不去的地方：对方的内在、独处、外部关系、过去，是对方自己的。

MA14　婚姻里的不疑两面都在：不疑自己是目的，也不疑对方是目的。两面都是各人一次次重新做出的选择，不是婚姻到达的状态；从外面只读言行与结构，不读心。

MA15　从这一件上读到的殖民方向：以亲密之名进入；以「这个家」「为了我们」之名替一方判她的法，即拿「我们」「家」去收下并替下一方的方向，这是借构对人的殖民方向，婚姻和别的制度一样，生不出一个把各人收成一个的「我们」；以「我们相爱，所以你得同意」为由终审一件一起做的事，是把相认借作裁断；「为了这个家，你得……」实际借的是哪一支，按那一次的用法核，不凭句式分派；把自己的意义交给对方保管，再向对方要。

第三篇 成就

MA16　成就对方，是让对方成长成对方自己。成就就是涵育，是方向；成没成，在对方那一端。仅凭我的成功评价就替对方认定他的构成已经完成，就不再是成就。

MA17　我成就你，不等你先成就我。你的方向是你的，我只能为它腾出空间；它在你那一端完成，不在我这里结账。两个这样的单向碰上了，才是互相，不是交换来的；碰不上，各人那一份照旧在，互相不是一段婚姻要达到的状态。

MA18　成就主要有两种方式：创造条件，与追问。追问把问题留给对方，不替对方下结论；替对方下了结论，就是拿我的方向当对方的尺子。

MA19　互相不要求同时，不要求对等，不记账：不把互相成就记成待对方偿还的交换账；承诺、贡献与已经留下的作用照记。分工协作不是交换，因为并非互相的要求。互补不是问题，互相要求对方跟自己一样，是拿我的方向当对方的尺子。

MA20　对方从我这里得着的，一经他自己的法认领，在构成归属上就是他的，不成为我替他完成的构成；共同的财物、贡献、功劳与责任各有各的账，不由这一条结算。在不侵害其他主体位置的边界内，对方可以走向我没有预料、不同意、甚至不再需要我的方向；这一句是能力式的，不是事实断言。互相成就有双向的形状，不认证 16DD。

MA21　从这一件上读到的殖民方向：替对方定他该成为什么；把所给条件换算成对对方第一人称构成成果的占有，「你今天的一切都是我给的」即是，真实的贡献照记，这一句占的是对方成为自己的那一步。把成就写成交换，是回落。把自己那一份交了出去，是迁就，不是成就：它没有站住，从结构上看没有向对方去的殖民方向；他自己这一侧怎样，从外面看不出来，不判。另有向外施压的，照那一次作用另记；不凭多年让步认定迁就。

第四篇 岁月

MA22　长期的成长：两个人各自的昨天、今天、明天落在同一段来往里；人会变，变了以后一起重新选择。一起重新选择有两种样子：两条法都不变而形成一项新的安排，或一方、双方各自修订，两人再一起确认新的做法合得上。

MA23　拿当年的对方量现在的对方，说出口替对方定他是谁，滑向殖民的方向。

MA24　分工是两人一起认下的现在安排，可以重新认领，可以改；它的约束力分别经过两人各自的法。分工滑成指向对方的固定要求，被当作对方改不得的、被挂到对方是谁上、或在习惯里被偷换，就有殖民的倾向：说出口给分工定性，滑向殖民的方向；在运行上使对方改不了，带殖民这个方向。一方自己把分工当作改不得的，是他的自我关系，从结构上看没有向外的方向，不在这一倾向之内。

MA25　交换的片刻是回落，常见，有时是气话。不凭这一次给整段婚姻定性；这一次若已说出口，把自己的付出写成对方须偿还的账，方向照这一次作用读；它此后是否固定成持续的要求，另按沉积读。

MA26　表达照三种根据分别读：自己的法里成立的要求，双方明确安排的角色要求，只是关系期待；单凭期待，不倒推义务或职责。把期待挂到对方是谁、在乎什么上，并据此替对方作判，滑向殖民的方向。

MA27　冲突可能成为关系中凿的来路，这一可能与它的代价一起读。冲突一再发生之后，止点可以成了常事，拖着也可以成了常态；长期无冲突不作涵育的证据。可以诊断的是这一次的这个操作，不是这个人；共识不追认既往。违反之后能不能回来、承认、修复，照两账并行读。

第五篇 孕育

MA28　孕育是两人一起为一个还没有来的人创造条件，是涵育在创造条件这一种上的样子；这一篇只读这一种。追问与后来者自己的回应，在已经有实际来往时另记，不以开始一起生活为唯一的界线。

MA29　孕育成为两人各自认下、不得不做的 C 时，读作共同做事：你为 A，我为 B，我们一起做 C。C 是一起为一个还没有来的人创造条件这件事，不是孩子，也不就是任何一方的 A；A、B 各是各的，不互相审。一方的 B 可以就是认可对方想要孩子的目的，愿意为它付出与支持，这也是他自己的不得不；某一个人的 B 是不是这一种，只有他那一端确认得了。

MA30　C 要两人都认下，各有一份自己做得了的，合起来做得成，不由任何一方独占其目的与裁断。独占在结构上的样子，是一方的 A 借了另一方的手，这件事怎么做由一方说了算，另一方参与决定、调整、撤回的路不在；那样就不是这里的 C。一方愿意照对方的安排去做，只要他参与、调整、撤回的路在，不算独占。一方不认下，就没有 C。一方替另一方认下，或说出口给对方改主意定性，滑向殖民的方向；把对方说不、改主意的路封住或弄得难走，带殖民这个方向；越过基础权利的，另判为侵。准备有没有发生、实际留下了什么条件、后来那个人来没来，分开记：没有来，不倒判此前的准备未曾发生，也不据此判任何一方有所欠缺；没有迎来孩子，不自动等于那一位空着。

MA31　新的主体也是目的。孩子是孕育迎来的另一个目的，不是这件事的产出；两人为他留下的是条件，不替他完成方向的认领。孩子作为目的的地位不由准备这件事创造；他自己的构成位置，也不因不能选择最初进入哪些条件而转归两人。条件还算不算支持，另照支持的否定边界核：不把两人的条件、判断或安排冒充他的构成，不封住他此后拒绝、修订、退出与改向的通道，并保留他能够不再围绕两人中的任何一个而展开的条件。他还不能拒绝、修订的那些年里，保护的是他既有的目的地位、价值来源不外置，与第一人称运行的开放条件；两人这一侧的意义谓词，不复制到他身上。他不只作为两人的延续、继后世的一环、养老的保障或留住对方的条件；「只作为」从两人的安排里读，一句话照那一句本身读；落到「只作为」，是把孩子只当作条件，带殖民方向，孩子那一侧交亲子论。

MA32　为领养做准备也算：还没有来的人，是还没有来到这两个人的生活里，不一定还不存在。

MA33　两个人的看向在同一个还没有来的人身上交错，各归各人，不合成一个家的意义。

第六篇 三个人

MA34　第三个人来了以后，婚姻论只读夫妻之间：共同养育在夫妻之间的那一面。孩子与父母之间，交亲子论；三人以上，两两记账。

MA35　孩子能够不再围绕我而展开，也能够不再围绕她而展开：检验逐人读。这是检验，不是孩子事实上必须离开；能够，限为构成独立，不是现实自足。

MA36　「为了孩子」不能替两个人立法；拿孩子当留住对方的锁，落到「只作为」时，是把孩子只当作条件，带殖民方向。「为了孩子」与「为了这个家」，被用来替一方判她的法时同形；它诉诸的是一起做的事、既有的安排，还是借相认作终审，逐件另核。

MA37　各人在共同养育里做下的那一份，不能换一个人来认；共同养育不以单方退出结清。

MA38　领养迎来第三个人以后，共同养育在夫妻之间的一面照本篇读；两人为他的到来一起做的准备，照第五篇读。

第七篇 去留

MA39　这一篇只读 15DD 向的婚姻怎样结束：一方或两方的爱结束；违背婚姻的承诺；离婚；丧偶。结束以后仍维持的那种婚姻，不读。

MA40　一方的爱没了而仍站在承诺上，照承诺读，不判为手段；那段婚姻已缺一方的爱，这个系列自那时起退出这一件，不追溯，不倒判那一方。爱有没有结束，是爱的人自己的事。此后两人怎样，这一条不排路。

MA41　人已不站在答应过的地方，又让爱的外观替他撑着对方的安排，读作撂下的结构；这是离开的人对自己的读法：看声明与那份答应在结构上对对方的影响，不以自己心里认没认账开脱；从外面不报撂下。违背婚姻的承诺分开记：撂下、虚假、未做到、受阻、共同改约；殖民方向只落在撂下与虚假，从外面只报未做到、受阻与共同改约，连同痕迹。违背不先认定爱已经移走，也不仅因出现新爱或婚外情，就倒认旧爱已经结束。离开要入账，还要有让对方能清楚理解的退出声明；入账、声明与已经留下的作用分开记，声明不以对方同意退出为条件。

MA42　退出的资格锁不住，只读退出权的基础分量；运行上够不够得着，另核，被封死的不在这个系列里。一方的留下在他自己的法里有没有根据，逐方读，只有他那一端确认得了，不按谁知道什么分。退出够得着，拿掉的只是锁；成本、拖延与被偷换的习惯，仍可能是他留下的凭借。知道对方可以离开，离开也是现实可行的选项，留下不再仅由无路可走定下；这仍不证明这一次留下的根据。退出的自由读在不疑自己是目的那一面：问的是关系有没有为自己的去留与重新选择留下运行条件；不从通道被封推出本人已不认自己是目的，也不从通道够得着认证他已经认领了留下或信任。退不出的人，他的位置不由外部取走；封死拿走的，是这一面在运行上的余地。他人也是目的那一面各自读，不因退出受阻自动取消。从外面只读可核的言行、条件与线索，不据此认证不疑。

MA43　退出声明让对方清楚理解本人退出，在关系一层；婚姻在背书制度里的解除及其公开手续，属退出权的承认分量，照那个制度的规则登记。两步是否同时完成，分开记。只未取得制度中的解除，不独自证明退出权的基础分量被封死；离开共同住所，也不独自证明退出已经够得着。「走得了」指本人实际能够退出这段来往、收回自己这一侧，照关系论核。构的权力抬高退出成本，认而不判；抬到人实际走不了，就是封死。一方借构去压对方，照人与人读：向外抬高对方的退出成本，带殖民这个方向；封死对方退出本身的，在基础权利一层另是侵，那段婚姻也出了这个系列。

MA44　退出不结清：共同养育与共同的事，不以单方退出结清；离开时替对方说「这些年什么也没损失」，滑向殖民的方向。

MA45　丧偶：背书那一面怎样，照那个制度的规则，认而不判；爱从主体这一侧看，死亡不等于爱情结束；人死之后有没有那边，不判。

第九节 收束

这一篇不立命题，不证明任何对象层的命题。它说的是：婚姻论在关系侧的下游，接在爱情论之后，读两个人的婚姻里只在婚姻里成立的那一部分；射程是两个人都在认对方也是目的的那个方向上的婚姻，不主张别的形式不是婚姻；方向是前提，婚姻由爱、两人各自的承诺与制度背书合成；主线是你是你，我是我，然后才有我们，两个人各自看向自己之外，看向对方，看向一个还没有来的人；把七篇串起来的，是每天重新做出的同一个承诺。各篇读到的殖民方向，借的是婚姻里已经在或可能形成的件；方向在被拿来做什么的那一次作用上读；人都是混合的，一次滑向照那一次读。
