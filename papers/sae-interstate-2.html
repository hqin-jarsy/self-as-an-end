Mutual Questioning
互问

Abstract

Among states that have no superior overhead, where do questions come from, and who cannot close them; and when the system itself acts, who can question it? This paper takes the Three-Borders Cooperation System as its example and answers both halves. The system's position is that of an external questioner: its determinations are public questions and records; it neither authorizes nor vetoes, and even a member that sends forces cannot close its questions. Making a covenant needs the consent of all parties; questioning existing matters does not; the party questioned recuses itself on the matter directed at it, and the questioning party does not decide alone that the case is closed. Closing one channel cannot cancel the questioning. The system does not enforce, and whether a question lands rests with each member; a member's obligation to answer comes from the covenants it has acknowledged, and "internal affairs" does not block questions. Treaty bodies admitting individual petitions fill the place where individuals cannot reach the procedures between states, and they are checked against the three conditions of a channel. The unified sentence of the Theory of the State is extended here, for the first time, to the system level, and its full text is given in §6: those whom the system's acts reach and who cannot avoid them are, as regards those acts, in the system's interior, whether or not they are within a member's territory; giving them a road by which the system itself can be reached is the system's own fifth ought; the accounts of the system and of the members are each checked by their own source. The system's arrangements can be chiseled; the three conditions are not withdrawn, and the arrangements are checked against the four points of War Treatise §16; decisions can be final, positions cannot. Four matters run through the paper: the system puts questions to the DRP about a decree that deprived people of citizenship; the families of the dead of Junction Town and the farming households of Wheat Plain petition the Petitions Committee against the UCSE; shopkeepers and villagers on the two banks of the Grey River petition over a common measure; people in the Eastfield camp petition against a sharing decision of the Conference of Members itself. The paper interfaces in place twice with Vattel's The Law of Nations: on the liberty and independence of nations, and on no state's having a right to interfere in the government of another; for Kant's publicity, it points to the interface the Theory of the State has already made. The paper has seven propositions (XZ9 to XZ15), each stated at the end of its section and listed together at the end.

Introduction

In the autumn of Year 9, with the war still being fought, the Democratic Republic of Purposes (DRP) issued a decree: those who left the country during the mobilization and did not return within six months would lose DRP citizenship, and the DRP would not register children born to them abroad. From that year on, the Nation Union of Telos (NUT) took in, by degrees, some forty thousand people who had left the DRP. In the Willow Ferry Reception Center in the Kingdom State of the United City-States of Ends (UCSE), three hundred ten children had been born by Year 12, and the "Citizenship" field on their birth certificates read "undetermined." Theory of the State Paper 3, §4, has read the situation of these children on the UCSE's side (N14). At that time there was not yet any place among the three states where they could put questions together.

In the spring of Year 13, the three states signed the Three-Borders Compact (hereafter the Compact) at Three Borders, a border town of the NUT. With it came the Three-Borders Cooperation System (hereafter the system). The system has a Conference of Members, in which each member has one vote; a standing Secretariat; several common procedures; and a committee that admits individual petitions. The Compact came into force in the winter of Year 13. Among the provisions the DRP acknowledged when it ratified the Compact is Article 4(3): members "do not push their own people onto other states by way of deprivation of citizenship." The Compact, the organs of the system and the laws of the several states are all examples set up by this series; this series does not claim that they are the best design, and they are cited so that there are facts to judge by.

In the summer of Year 14, the NUT requested that questions be put to the DRP under this provision, and the UCSE seconded the request. The Conference of Members opened questioning by 2 to 0, the DRP not taking part in the vote. The Secretary-General, who held DRP citizenship, was recused; the Deputy Secretary-General for Common Procedures acted in the Secretary-General's place on the documents. This Deputy Secretary-General held NUT citizenship. The DRP's permanent representative argued that, since the NUT was the requesting party, the Deputy Secretary-General should be recused as well; the Secretariat replied in writing: "Recusal under Article 8 refers to the case in which the official's own state of citizenship is the party questioned." The DRP's objection was entered in the record.

The letter of questions put three questions: how many people had lost citizenship under the decree; how many children born abroad had not been registered; and whether the DRP intended to register these children.

In the autumn of Year 14, the DRP's Foreign Ministry replied in writing: "The acquisition and loss of citizenship are internal affairs of the Republic. The decree was issued in the autumn of Year 9, before the Compact; Article 4(3) of the Compact is not retroactive." The reply attached a further passage that turned to the UCSE: "In the winter of Year 9 the UCSE revoked the citizenship of a commentator living in the NUT, leaving her holding no citizenship of any state; Article 14 of the UCSE's Membership Act still requires the parents of stateless children to have been lawfully resident for five years. The UCSE has not given these children citizenship either." The reply gave no numbers.

In the winter of Year 14, the Conference of Members adopted a record by 2 to 0, the DRP not taking part in the vote. The record contained the full text of the decree; the figures the NUT supplied: of the some forty thousand people in the NUT who had left the DRP, about eleven thousand had lost DRP citizenship under the decree, and about nine hundred children born in the NUT had not been registered; the figure the UCSE supplied: about four hundred twenty children had been born in the Willow Ferry Reception Center by Year 14; and the full text of the DRP's reply, together with the attached passage. The record was made public on the Secretariat's website. In Year 15, one newspaper in the NUT, two in the UCSE and one in the DRP capital each printed the record in full or in summary.

Two things followed. In the summer of Year 15, the UCSE's Chamber of Citizens amended Article 14 of the Membership Act by 171 votes to 120, deleting the condition that the parents have been lawfully resident for five years; by Year 17, three hundred eighty-nine of the children born in the Willow Ferry Reception Center had acquired UCSE citizenship. In the autumn of Year 15, the DRP's Foreign Ministry published new registration rules: a child born abroad could be registered if one parent applied in person at a DRP consular office in the NUT or the UCSE and "completed after the fact the exit formalities for the mobilization period"; the parents' own citizenship was not restored. By the end of Year 16, thirty-seven children had been registered under these rules.

On whom this matter falls must be made clear first. It falls on those who lost DRP citizenship under that decree, about eleven thousand of them in the NUT; and on their children born abroad and not registered by the DRP, about nine hundred in the NUT and about four hundred twenty at Willow Ferry. These are the people to be protected. The DRP is the member questioned, the address under whose name this account is booked; what answers is the position of the DRP's Foreign Ministry. The children at Willow Ferry are at the same time under an act of the UCSE: Article 14 of the Membership Act decides whether they can acquire UCSE citizenship. So the DRP's counter-question falls on the same children too (§4).

Which line is to be looked at is stated for two sides. On the DRP's side, it is Article 4(3), which the DRP acknowledged when it ratified the Compact. On the system's side, it is how far the system itself goes: it only asks and only records; it does not make citizenship law for the DRP, and it does not enforce; landing rests with each member.

Before the questioning, the decree had long been in force, and those who had lost citizenship and the unregistered children were already there; Article 14 of the UCSE's Membership Act still required the parents to have been lawfully resident for five years. On procedure, Article 7(b) of the Compact provides that putting questions on existing matters, opening procedures and adopting records require a majority of the members not concerned, and that the member questioned does not take part in the vote but has the right to make statements at every stage; Article 8 provides that the Secretary-General and the Deputy Secretaries-General are recused in matters concerning their own state of citizenship; Article 9 provides that all questions, replies and records are public.

In this matter there is no one above the three states. Clause 2 of Article 2 of the Compact reads: "This Compact establishes no organ above the members. No organ of the system may exercise force against a member or against persons within a member's territory." The Conference of Members cannot order the DRP to change its law, and the Secretariat cannot send people to register the children. Yet the matter is not a blank either: someone asked, someone answered, and what was answered and what was not were all recorded and laid out in the newspapers of the three states. Afterward, two of the states each changed one thing.

This paper asks about questioning of this kind. With no superior overhead, where do questions come from, and who cannot close them; on what grounds does the system question a member, and on what grounds a state outside the system; how do questions land inside member states? Conversely, the system itself also acts, and its acts fall on people: do those whom its acts reach have a road by which it can be reached, and can its arrangements be chiseled? The last clause of this series' main-line sentence says: it puts questions to its members, and every one of its own arrangements remains open to questioning (the Prequel, §5). This paper writes out both halves of that clause.

The paper proceeds in eight steps. §1 sets out the system's position; §2, that making covenants and questioning are kept apart, and that the party questioned recuses itself; §3, that closing one channel cannot cancel the questioning; §4, how questions land inside member states; §5, treaty bodies admitting individual petitions; §6, those whom the system's acts reach; §7, the system itself being questioned; §8, that the system's arrangements can be chiseled. The first four sections write of the system questioning others; the last four, of people questioning members and questioning the system. The interfaces with the tradition come in place, where the judgment falls.

§1 Questions Come from Outside

The Conference of Members cannot give orders. What, then, did it do in the DRP matter, and on what grounds?

Reading the word "last," the Theory of the State says: "'Last' refers to level: there is nothing above. It does not refer to exemption from questioning." And again: "That there is no one overhead shows only where the questions must come from; it does not show that the questioning has stopped." (the Theory of the State Prequel, §3) Over a single state there is no higher level, and those who question it are inside it and also beside it: its own people question it through its own channels, and peer states question it from outside. When many states are put together, there is likewise no higher level over them, and questions can come only from outside, from a group of peers. The system stands among this group: it is the place where the members put questions together and make records together. Its position is that of an external questioner.

What this position is not must be made clear first. The system's determination of line-crossing is public questions and records, not authorization: it constitutes no ground for any state to start a war, nor does it endorse any state's sending of forces. The system neither authorizes nor vetoes. Article 18 of the Compact writes this into its text: where there is an entry by armed force into a member or another state, the Conference of Members may make a record, "There is a colonizing entry"; clause 2 goes on: "This record is a public question and record; it constitutes no authorization, and no obligation on any member to deploy."

The DRP record is exactly such a thing. It contains the full text of the decree, the figures of the NUT and of the UCSE, and the DRP's reply and counter-question. Not one sentence requires the DRP to change its law, and not one declares the DRP in breach of its covenant. Whether to change rests with the DRP's own positions; how a change, once made, is questioned inside the DRP is written in §4.

Questioning reaches the side that sends forces too. The Stonegorge Commonwealth (Stonegorge) joined the system in the autumn of Year 14; the only sea route from the Mirror Sea to the outer ocean is the Stonegorge Strait, and both of its shores belong to Stonegorge. In the spring of Year 17, ships and forces of the Plateau Federation entered Stonegorge's territorial waters and Lamp Island in the strait, and the Conference of Members made a record under clause 1 of Article 18. After the UCSE sent forces, it submitted a written explanation to the Conference of Members under clause 3 of the same article, listing three grounds, one of which was that two UCSE freighters and thirty-one crew members had been detained in Stonegorge's territorial waters. On this ground the DRP's permanent representative said: "The detention of the freighters is not the same as an attack on the UCSE." Both were entered in the record. What kind of ground each of those grounds is, whether they suffice, and who determines that, are written in Paper 5, §4; here only one thing is taken: the side that sends forces is also under questioning. Theorem Four, in War Treatise §16, says exactly this: "the war-party has no privilege of not being questioned"; it also asks of third parties: "international institutions, neutral observers, humanitarian organizations, scholars, journalists: may they challenge the war-party's decisions without being treated as enemies?" (War Treatise §16) This theorem originally speaks of the war-party and its third parties; here the third party is replaced by the system, and the war-party by the member that sends forces (own undertaking). The system's questioning a member that sends forces is not hostility; the member that sends forces cannot close this question.

Conversely, if an arrangement in fact acquires force over the members, it is no longer the system spoken of here. Once there is a superior with force overhead, that is a level in the Theory of Government's sense, and it answers as that level answers (Theory of Government Paper 4, G26; Paper 7, XZ46).

It may be said that a system that can only ask and cannot act is empty, and that rather than look on, it would be better to let it authorize the use of armed force, at least in the face of a colonizing entry. The weight of this objection is in the cost: questions do not stop guns. But once authorization is given, the system has compulsory power. Force is substituting one's own judgment for the other party's judgment on its own affairs. Once authorization is given, the system's judgment becomes the members' ground for starting a war; yet under the War Treatise, starting a war requires that the sending side's own first face hold (War Treatise §13), and whether to start a war is the sending side's judgment on its own affairs; authorization moves it onto the system's vote. If the authorization is directed at matters within a state's territory, what is substituted also includes that state's judgment on its own affairs: how matters within its territory are to be handled passes to the system, which determines that they may be changed by armed force. With this layer added, the system is a superior, and no longer cooperation between one another. So the system of this series does not authorize.

Nor is questioning empty. It leaves an account visible from outside: who asked, who answered, what was answered and what was not (Paper 3). On the strength of this account, peers can each withdraw cooperation (Paper 4). Asking and recording themselves also fall within the stretch that the War Treatise says must be exhausted before a war is started: "the level of jiao (diplomacy, channels, third-party mediation, the procedures of international institutions, non-military pressure, the concluding and repairing of agreements)" (War Treatise §13). The system's asking and recording are a non-war route that, where available, ought to be brought into this test; whether it has been exhausted is checked by the routes actually available at the time and by the conditions of urgency, and going mechanically through every common procedure is not made into a further, uniform threshold.

It may also be said that not asking is what neutrality is: only by saying little does the system keep from being drawn in. But ceasing to ask is also an act. Speaking of bystanders in a network, Power Theory says: "At the network-topology layer, silence (with subject-position registration, without an operational counter-vector) is a zero-resistance transmitting node that objectively amplifies the power gradient." (Power Theory P3 §V.4) This originally speaks of a node in a network; here it is extended to the system (own undertaking): when the system does not ask in the face of a line-crossing, its silence saves the crossing side a share of effort. Conversely, the system's observation is likewise an act. The Theory of the State says that setting up an observation post on the other side's land requires first explaining the source of authority, the manner of entry, what may be done there and the arrangements for withdrawal, and then checking them (Theory of the State Paper 6, N34). When the system sends people to look, it likewise explains first and then checks; this is a step extended from a single state to the system (own undertaking).

One thing is missing at the system level, and it must be said plainly. Moral Law P8, writing of the default channel within a community, leaves a fallback road for the party that refuses: "if one party refuses ... to the 14DD court for handling." (Moral Law P8 §3.4.2) That is a court with compulsion. Between states there is no such layer overhead. What can serve as a fallback at the system level is only each state's own law and each state's own first face, together with checks (own undertaking). This is a condition of the position of external questioner, and it is written here.

The cost too is written down as it is. The system's position is that of an external questioner, and the cost is watching problems happen, unable to resolve them directly by armed force, able to use only indirect means. In the DRP matter, the record was adopted, the newspapers printed it, and two of the states each changed one thing; the DRP's new rules state that the parents who lost citizenship do not have their own citizenship restored. This is recorded as it is, and no road of compulsion is sought to make it up.

Here this paper speaks in three layers (how the three layers are spoken of is in the Prequel, §11). That the system is an external questioner, that its determinations are questions and records, and that it neither authorizes nor vetoes: this is the normative layer. The wording of Article 18 of the Compact and the vote required by Article 7(b) are examples at the rule layer. What wording a record uses, and whether a particular entry is recorded as a "colonizing entry" or as something else, are judged by the positions that vote in the Conference of Members, and the account falls on the members that vote: this is the judgment layer. On the Lamp Island occasion, the DRP argued that it should be recorded as an "armed entry" and abstained, and its words too were entered in the record.

The system's questions, answers and records are public (Compact, Article 9). How far publicity can go, Theory of the State Paper 5, §4, has already checked against the formula of publicity in Appendix II of Kant's Perpetual Peace (Zum ewigen Frieden): how, with no common judge, one can recognize that a practice toward the outside is not right. The conclusion there is that publicity can serve as a trace, not as a verdict. Here this paper points back to it and does not redo it. The DRP record, printed in the newspapers of the three states, is a trace of this kind: it lets people see, and judges for no one.

XZ9 The system's position is that of an external questioner. The system's determination of line-crossing is public questions and records, not authorization; it constitutes no ground for any state to start a war, nor does it endorse any state's sending of forces; the system neither authorizes nor vetoes. "Last" refers to level, not to exemption from questioning; that there is no one overhead shows only where the questions must come from, and does not show that the questioning has stopped (the Theory of the State Prequel, §3). The war-party has no privilege of not being questioned; third parties may challenge the war-party's decisions without being treated as enemies (War Treatise §16). With the system as external questioner, a state that sends forces cannot close this question (the War Treatise originally speaks of the war-party and third parties; here it is extended to the member that sends forces and the system; own undertaking).

§2 Making a Covenant Needs Consent; Questioning Existing Matters Does Not

Setting up new binding needs every party's consent; asking about something that has already happened need not wait for the party questioned to nod. The two are easily mixed up. Once they are mixed, either the system can ask about nothing at all, or it can make rules for others in the name of asking.

Article 7 of the Compact writes the two separately. Article 7(a): amending the Compact, adding common procedures, admitting new members and concluding new agreements in the system's name are new binding, and require the written consent of all members. Article 7(b): putting questions on existing matters, opening procedures and adopting records require a majority of the members not concerned; the member questioned does not take part in the vote on that matter, but has the right to make statements at every stage and to require that its statements be entered in the record.

The matter of the Gorge Water put the two side by side. The Gorge Water rises in Stonegorge, flows southwest into the NUT's Northern District, and joins the Cloud River above the High Gorge Reservoir; Stonegorge has a power station on the Gorge Water, built with a loan from a bank of the Plateau Federation, and Article 22 of the loan contract forbids providing the station's operating data to third parties without the lender's written consent. In the spring of Year 15, the Cloud River Basin Protocol drafted by the Secretariat was opened for signature; the UCSE and the NUT signed, and Stonegorge did not; under Article 7(a), the three-party protocol did not come into being. That summer a gate at the Gorge Water power station failed and water was released in an emergency; Stonegorge notified the NUT only forty minutes after the release began. Within two hours, the Gorgemouth gauging station on the NUT side measured the flow rising to nine times its normal level, the High Gorge Reservoir overflowed, two villages in the NUT's Northern District were flooded, and one person died. The NUT requested that questions be put to Stonegorge under Article 5 of the Compact. Article 5 is one Stonegorge had already acknowledged when it joined in the autumn of Year 14: where a member's acts may affect people within another member's territory, it gives notice beforehand; where there is no time to do so beforehand, it gives notice immediately afterward. The Conference of Members opened questioning by 3 to 0, Stonegorge not taking part in the vote. Stonegorge replied that the gate failure was an accident; that there had been "no time" for advance notice; and that Article 22 of the loan contract covers only operating data, not notice after the fact. The record was made public. On one and the same river, new binding needed its consent; questions on an existing obligation did not.

Paper 1, §3, has read this matter from the side of covenant. Here the side of questioning is looked at: the question falls on the people of the two flooded villages downstream; the line to be looked at is Article 5, which Stonegorge had already acknowledged; before this, Stonegorge had acknowledged Article 5 and had not acknowledged the protocol.

The two can be kept apart because they govern different things. A covenant governs what comes after: it adds for each party a binding the party did not have before, and without a party's consent there is no binding on that party (Paper 1, §1; XZ1). A question governs acts already done: the release had already happened, the obligation to give notice had long been acknowledged, and what is asked about is something already there. The party questioned may answer, may not answer, may answer insufficiently; it cannot close this question (Theory of the State Paper 1, §5; Theory of Government G36).

Once they are kept apart, a common failure comes into view: the party questioned holds the power to shut the door on the question directed at itself. A rule requiring everyone's agreement lets any one party block everything. That making covenants needs the consent of all is right; if questioning existing matters also needed the consent of all, the party questioned could shut the question with one vote, and questioning would become something it decides for itself whether to undergo.

So when a common procedure questions a member, the member recuses itself on the matter directed at it: it does not vote, and does not hold the power to close the case. That covenant and questioning are kept apart in this way, and that the party questioned recuses itself in this way, has no ready sentence upstream; it is a step this series undertakes itself (own undertaking). In the DRP matter, the DRP did not take part in the vote, and the Secretary-General holding DRP citizenship was recused; in the Gorge Water matter, Stonegorge did not take part in the vote. The form of recusal can differ, and Articles 7(b) and 8 of the Compact are only one way of writing it; what must be held is that, on that matter, the party questioned does not have in its hand the vote that shuts the door.

A sentence of the Conflict Treatise can be used here, but only half of it. Speaking of who has standing to question a person, it says: "I cannot be my own final adjudicator and declare in advance that some class of questioner is permanently without standing. This layer I cannot exempt. Once I have that power I have become my own court of last instance." (Conflict Treatise §15) This originally speaks of a person in the first person; here it is extended to the member state questioned (own undertaking): if a state could veto, within a common procedure, the questions directed at itself, it would become its own court of last instance. The same section has another half: "it is I who must remain open to questioning. This constraint gives me no standing to question the other." (ibid.) This half cannot supply the system's standing to put questions. That the system can ask rests on two other grounds. One is that external questioning cannot be cancelled (§3; XZ11). The other is the class of external body that Conflict Treatise §27 marks out: "But this holds only for a certain class of external body: a procedure both parties have acknowledged, a mechanism with lawful authority, or an institution with legitimate authority for the protection of basic rights." (Conflict Treatise §27) The DRP and Stonegorge both ratified the Compact, and Article 7(b) is a procedure they have acknowledged. That sentence originally speaks of two parties; here it is extended to many (own undertaking).

Recusal keeps the party questioned within bounds; the questioning party must be kept within bounds too. Questioning does not need the consent of the party questioned; but neither may the questioning party declare by itself that the questioning is over, that the answer was insufficient, and that the matter is thereupon recorded as a breach of covenant. The step of closing the case needs a position that the questioner does not monopolize; where there is no such position, the matter is recorded plainly as unsettled. The Theory of Government says: "Decisions can be final: a particular matter can be answered and closed. Positions cannot be final: no position may monopolize the power to shut the door" (Theory of Government Paper 6, G36). Institution Theory has said the same thing of the party under review: "Questioning must be independent, and the reason is that the party under review may not monopolize the decision on whether a challenge exists and whether it is over" (Institution Theory Paper 6, Z35a). This originally speaks of the party under review; here it is extended to the questioning party (own undertaking): the questioning party likewise may not monopolize the decision on whether the questioning is over.

The DRP record reproduces verbatim the sentence "not retroactive." Whether that sentence holds, the record did not judge on anyone's behalf. The decree was issued before the Compact; the statelessness the decree caused went on after the Compact came into force, and children were still being born. How these two are to be read together, the NUT may have its reading and the DRP has its own; what the Conference of Members adopted by 2 to 0 is a record, not a verdict. The Conflict Treatise says: "cues are not criteria, and traces are not verdicts" (Conflict Treatise §26). On this paper's reading, the question there is unsettled.

It may be said that states are free and independent, so that questioning a state should first have its consent. This objection has a long history. Emer de Vattel says in the Preliminaries of The Law of Nations that "each nation should be left in the peaceable enjoyment of that liberty which she inherits from nature" (Emer de Vattel, The Law of Nations, Preliminaries, §15, ed. J. Chitty, Philadelphia, 1883, read against two online texts; tier B). In the section immediately following, according to one online text, he says that each nation judges by itself what its conscience requires of it, and that other states cannot coerce it to act in any particular way; only where the other side holds a perfect right may it coerce (Preliminaries, §16; tier C, paraphrased only). In the section after that, he distinguishes perfect from imperfect rights: the former carry a right to coerce, the latter permit only requests (Preliminaries, §17, same source; tier C, paraphrased only). The texts relied on for §§15 to 17 of the Preliminaries are all English online transcriptions; neither the French original nor the pages of the 1883 printing has been checked in person, and checking against the transcriptions is not taken as checking against the original printed pages.

The common question is: how wide is the range within which a state is not swayed by other states. The question here is the same, and where they agree is in making covenants. That other states cannot coerce a state to act in a particular way lands, in this series, on a covenant's requiring the consent of all parties (XZ1): when Stonegorge does not sign the protocol, the protocol does not come into being for it, and no one signs on its behalf. When it comes to questioning, three things must first be kept apart: questioning, requesting performance, and compulsion. What Vattel's §16 speaks of is when a state may not be coerced; it does not cancel external questioning along with that; and §17 lists separately a right of request that carries no compulsion. This series therefore does not rely on these two sections to present "questioning does not need the consent of the party questioned" as a step he did not take, nor does it say that he allowed a state to be questioned only by itself. What this series adds is an unfolding: within common procedures the parties have acknowledged, it writes out item by item who can initiate, how the party questioned recuses itself, where the grounds for answering come from, and how the results connect into the interior (this section; §§3 and 4). A question is not new binding, and requires the state to do nothing; that it must answer comes from a separate covenant it has acknowledged (§4; XZ12). Nor does recusal mean that every party can veto: the Theory of Government says that "Control does not mean that every person affected has a veto" (Theory of Government Paper 6, G36a). Nor is being questioned being accused: "'Open to questioning' means that the channel is always open, for every government and at every time, and being questioned is no evidence of illegitimacy" (Theory of Government Paper 6, G35). So this is a division of scope, and it can be read side by side with his division of the right to coerce: Vattel guards a state against being coerced to do something; this paper guards the point that a state cannot close the questions directed at what it has already done. These sections of Vattel rest only on English online texts read against each other and, in part, on a single source; here they serve only as an in-place comparison, not as a refutation.

Back to the objection in the Introduction. The DRP wanted the Deputy Secretary-General holding NUT citizenship to be recused as well. The norm this series gives here is only two sentences: the party questioned recuses itself, and the questioner does not decide alone that the case is closed. Articles 7(b) and 8 of the Compact take recusal only as far as the citizenship of the party questioned; this is only one way of writing it, an example. Whether acting in the Secretary-General's place on the documents counts as part of holding the power to close the case, and whether a person from the requesting side may so act, are for specific rules to settle; in a given matter, they are judged by those in positions. On that occasion the Secretariat judged; the DRP's objection remains in the record, and it can be raised again later.

One point is left here: how far an answer must go to count as answering the matter asked about. Stonegorge answered the question about the release and still does not provide the operating data; the DRP answered "internal affairs" and "not retroactive," and gave no numbers. Whether an answer suffices is not decided by the questioner alone; where there is no position the questioner does not monopolize to determine it, it is recorded plainly as unsettled.

XZ10 Questioning does not need the consent of the party questioned; the party questioned recuses itself (own undertaking). A covenant requires the consent of all parties (XZ1); questioning existing matters does not need the consent of the party questioned: a question governs acts already done, and the party questioned cannot close it (Theory of the State Paper 1, §5; Theory of Government G36). Only when the two are kept apart can a common failure be told: the party questioned holding the power to shut the door on the question directed at itself. Requiring everyone's agreement lets any one party block everything: that making covenants needs consent is right; that questioning existing matters should also need consent is wrong. So when a common procedure questions a member, the member recuses itself on the matter directed at it: it does not vote, and does not hold the power to close the case; the form is not fixed. Of Conflict Treatise §15, only the half in which the party questioned does not hold the power to shut the door is taken: one cannot be one's own final adjudicator and declare in advance that some class of questioner is permanently without standing (originally said in the first person; here extended to relations between states; own undertaking). The system's standing to put questions rests on other grounds: external questioning cannot be cancelled (XZ11); and procedures the parties have acknowledged, as Conflict Treatise §27 says (originally said of two parties; here extended to many; own undertaking). Recusal does not mean that everyone can veto: control does not mean that every person affected has a veto (Theory of Government G36a). Questioning does not need the consent of the party questioned, and neither does the questioner decide alone that the case is closed: the step of closing the case needs a position that the questioner does not monopolize, or else the matter is registered plainly as unsettled; no position may monopolize the power to shut the door, the questioning party included (Theory of Government G36; own undertaking).

§3 Closing One Channel Cannot Cancel the Questioning

The party questioned does not take part in the vote, and questioning is opened all the same. Can it still walk away: exit that arrangement and let the questioning come to nothing?

At the level of a single state, the Theory of the State set up one sentence: "Here only one sentence is established (own undertaking): external questioning cannot be cancelled. This state may exit or switch an external arrangement; that is a change of structure, itself under questioning (Paper 5, N24); it can close one channel, but it cannot cancel the questioning of it by other states, by the people of other states, and by those affected" (Theory of the State Paper 1, §5). This paper takes up that sentence as it stands.

Extended to the system, it has two sides (own undertaking). One side is the members: when a member exits one of the system's arrangements, the questioning of it by the system and by the other members goes on as before, and its acts before the exit can still be questioned (Paper 7, XZ45, XZ47). The other side is the system itself: the system's arrangements are under this sentence too; it can close a particular road, but it cannot cancel the questioning of it by those whom its acts reach (§§6–8; XZ14, XZ15).

The DRP later ran into exactly this. From the winter of Year 9 to the spring of Year 11, the UCSE maintained an administration office in the Wheat Plain buffer zone, and the office selected eleven local residents to form a provisional self-government committee. At the withdrawal, nine of them left with the UCSE forces; the two who stayed were prosecuted by the DRP for "collaboration with the enemy," and in the winter of Year 13 were sentenced to twelve and nine years respectively, at trials not open to the public and with defense counsel appointed by the court. Article 14 of the Compact established a committee admitting individual petitions (written in full in §5), and in the autumn of Year 13 the DRP's People's Assembly, in a separate vote, accepted that article by 318 votes to 247. In the spring of Year 16, the two petitioned the Committee against the DRP through a lawyer. In the autumn of Year 17, the Committee asked the DRP to submit the trial records and to allow a member of the Committee to visit the two in prison. In the spring of Year 18, the DRP's Foreign Ministry, with the approval of the President of the Republic, declared under clause 5 of Article 14 that the DRP was exiting Article 14. The declaration read: "Article 14 has been used to interfere in the Republic's administration of justice. The Republic will make no further answer on this petition." Under clause 5, the declaration takes effect twelve months after it is made, and petitions already submitted before it takes effect are heard and concluded as usual. In the summer of Year 18, the Conference of Members recorded this declaration by 3 to 0, the DRP not taking part in the vote. In the winter of Year 18, the Committee gave its opinion on the case of the two, asking the DRP for a public retrial and to allow visits; the DRP did not reply within six months, and in the summer of Year 19 the Conference of Members recorded the fact that no reply had come. From the spring of Year 19 on, the Committee returned seven newly received petitions against the DRP, on the ground that they had been submitted after the declaration took effect and were not to be taken up.

How this matter is to be judged is in Paper 7, §§1 and 3. Here only the structure it lays out is taken. What exit closes is a channel's road from then on: the seven petitions after the spring of Year 19 could no longer come in. Questioning already begun still lands in the record: the case of the two was heard and concluded as usual, and the opinion was given as usual. Not answering is itself one more fact in the record. The DRP can keep new petitions from being taken up; it cannot make the case already under questioning in the spring of Year 18 disappear.

Two things must be kept apart (own undertaking). Not joining an arrangement, not cooperating, and exiting an arrangement are closing a channel; this is allowed. Pressing, punishing or retaliating against a questioner because the questioner has raised questions is trying to cancel the questioning; this crosses the line (as in the first of the places named in Paper 4, §5; XZ25). The former changes the structure between oneself and the questioning; the latter seeks to have the questions no longer raised. Among the matters this paper uses, there is no fact of anyone moving against a questioner; here this is set up only as a principle, and the example is in Paper 4, §6: prosecutions under the Anti-Division Act of people who commented on the system's arrangements.

What the party questioned cannot close includes two more things, outside it. The Theory of the State says: "what it cannot close is peers judging for themselves on this basis and withdrawing cooperation other than recognition (N26), and these questions entering the channels inside through this state's internal subjects (N37)" (Theory of the State Paper 7, §1). In the DRP matter, there is a trace of the second. Accepting Article 14 was voted on by the People's Assembly, but exiting it was approved by the President; sixty-one members of the People's Assembly jointly demanded that the People's Assembly vote on the exit, and the Presiding Board did not put it on the agenda. This demand is itself one appearance of that question inside the DRP; where it landed is in Paper 7.

It may be said that, since exit is allowed, "cannot be cancelled" is empty talk: a state that does not want to be questioned need only exit. The weight of this objection is that exit did close a road: all seven new petitions were returned. But what cannot be cancelled is the questioning, not any particular road. The side that exits is still questioned inside itself: "exit does not stand in for questioning." (Theory of Government Paper 4, G27) If it does not answer outwardly, that not answering is also questioned inside it: "Answering and not answering are themselves both open to questioning in this state, and are questioned in the channels inside (own undertaking)" (Theory of the State Paper 7, §3). Peers, too, can still each act on the record. With one road fewer, the questions still have other places to land.

One point is left here: the questioning cannot be cancelled, but the answer may not come. The question whose answer does not come stays in the record; how it is recorded and how it is read are handed to Paper 3. The two from Wheat Plain were still in custody in Year 20, one of them moved to the prison hospital; by what road their questions go from here is answered in Paper 7.

XZ11 External questioning cannot be cancelled (carried over from Theory of the State Paper 1, §5, its own undertaking taken up as it stands). A state may exit or switch an external arrangement; that is a change of structure, itself under questioning; it can close one channel, but it cannot cancel the questioning of it by other states, by the people of other states, and by those affected (Theory of the State Paper 1, §5). Extended to the system (own undertaking): when a member exits an arrangement, the questioning of it by the system and by the other members goes on as before (Paper 7, XZ45, XZ47); the system's own arrangements are under this sentence too (XZ14, XZ15). Closing a channel is kept apart from moving against a questioner: not joining, not cooperating, and exiting an arrangement are closing a channel, and are allowed; pressing, punishing or retaliating against a questioner because the questioner has raised questions is trying to cancel the questioning, and crosses the line (as in the first place in Paper 4, XZ25).

§4 Questions Land Inside Member States

After the record was adopted, the UCSE changed its own law and the DRP changed its registration rules. What is the relation between these two things and that record?

The record does not say, and nothing else on this point has been recorded. The UCSE's Chamber of Citizens amending Article 14 of the Membership Act by 171 votes to 120 was a vote cast by the UCSE's legislators in a UCSE procedure; the DRP's Foreign Ministry publishing new registration rules was a decision made by a DRP position under DRP procedure. Each was made by that state's own positions, and each is questioned inside that state. Whether the system's questions land rests with each member.

This is the direct consequence of the system's not enforcing. At the level of a single state, the Theory of the State spoke of this point about external routes: "But they have no enforcer of their own: with no force at a higher level above, whether an award lands once it is made depends on whether this state's positions carry it out." (Theory of the State Paper 1, §5) So too with the system's questions. To become a channel, a question must also connect to a road inside the member state: "A peer's question does not on its own count as a channel; it counts only once it connects to N37." (Theory of the State Paper 7, §3) N37 is the inward channel set up in Theory of the State Paper 7: the road by which a state's own people question that state.

In the DRP matter, several of the roads by which the question connected inside are visible. A newspaper in the DRP capital printed the record. The DRP's Foreign Ministry afterward changed the registration rules; whether the change goes far enough must be asked inside the DRP: the new rules require one parent to go in person to a DRP consular office in the NUT or the UCSE and to have "completed after the fact the exit formalities for the mobilization period," the parents' own citizenship is not restored, and by the end of Year 16 thirty-seven children had been registered. All of this can be asked in the channels inside the DRP, and can also be asked again by the system. The Theory of the State has, further, a sentence about two doors: "if what a peer asks about falls on people inside this state, not answering outwardly cannot stand in for answering inwardly: these are two doors." (ibid.) Those who lost citizenship are outside the DRP's territory; but under the unified sentence of the Theory of the State, as regards that decree they cannot avoid it, and they are still in the DRP's interior. The DRP's answering the system with "internal affairs" does not answer, on the DRP's behalf, to these people and their children.

Why must the DRP answer? That the system is an external questioner gives the system the position of asking; it does not directly give the party questioned an obligation to answer. The Theory of the State allows a state not to answer ordinary questions from peers: "This state may decline to answer ordinary diplomatic questions between peers that carry no separate obligation to answer; the requirements to answer and to implement that it bears under the treaties and common procedures it has acknowledged still follow N24 and N27" (Theory of the State Paper 7, §3). The obligation to answer must come from covenants acknowledged. The DRP's obligation arises from two things it acknowledged when it ratified the Compact: the commitment of Article 4(3), and the procedure of Article 7(b). In the autumn of Year 13, the DRP's People's Assembly ratified the Compact by 389 votes to 196. A covenant requires the consent of all parties (XZ1), and so the obligation to answer comes into being through the consent of all parties too; it is not derived from "the system is an external questioner" alone. That sentence of the Theory of the State originally speaks of a state toward peer states; here it is extended to a member toward the system (own undertaking).

The obligation of a member that sends forces to explain its grounds to the system comes about in the same way. Clause 3 of Article 18 of the Compact requires a deploying member to explain in writing, within seventy-two hours, the grounds of its deployment; the explanation the UCSE submitted in the spring of Year 17 was submitted under this clause (§1). This clause moves being questioned from beforehand to afterward; it does not withdraw being questioned. The Theory of Government said a sentence of the same shape about emergencies: an emergency "may move review and being questioned from beforehand to afterward, but it does not withdraw being questioned" (Theory of Government Paper 5, G30). This originally speaks of a level of government in an emergency; here it is extended to a member that sends forces (own undertaking). Having given the explanation to the system does not stand in for giving it to the state's own people either. The War Treatise calls the practice of concealing things from one's own side and cutting off internal questioning gui (deception): "What this treatise calls 'gui' ... is not any tactical concealment or camouflage before the enemy, but the war-technique that operates at the cost of oppressing one's own side's subjectivity and cutting off internal questioning" (War Treatise §12). To have spoken outwardly and said nothing inwardly does not count as having answered. Toward those whom the sending of forces reaches, the sending state answers following the unified sentence (state level, the Theory of the State Prequel, §8; see §6). The part of the coordination and preparation made in the system's name that this sending of forces then used, such as joint plans, joint exercises and prepositioned depots (Paper 5, §7), still answers to those it reaches following the unified sentence (system level, §6). That the sending state answers at the state level to those whom its sending of forces reaches does not relieve the system of what it owes for its own part; nor does the system's part bear, on the states' behalf, their starting of a war.

Toward a state outside the system, the Compact can supply no obligation to answer. The "sweeps" in the north of the Republic of the Sandbars (the Sandbars) began in the spring of Year 16; that summer, the Secretary-General wrote to the Sandbars' Whitewall government, asking about the number and locations of the "instruction camps" and about the state of the villages that had been burned. The Ministry of Foreign Affairs in Whitewall replied: "This is an internal affair of the Republic. The system is not the Republic's superior, and the Republic is not a member of the system." The letter and the reply were made public under Article 9 of the Compact and are kept at the Secretariat. This matter is read in Paper 6, §§1 and 6. Here only the principle is set up. A state outside the system has never acknowledged the Compact, and may decline to answer. Its not answering does not cancel the questioning (XZ11). The question and the non-answer both remain outside, as an account that can be seen; that account is handed to Paper 3.

The DRP's first words in answer were "internal affairs." Do they block the questioning? The Theory of the State says: "Not interfering is not the same as not asking, either." (Theory of the State Paper 5, §2) To interfere is to decide for it; to ask is only to require it to explain. The system does not enforce, and the Conference of Members has not written a single line of citizenship law for the DRP. How the DRP settles citizenship is still settled by the DRP's law. Speaking of handing disputes to an external procedure, the Conflict Treatise has said how much is handed over: "What is ceded is the procedural power of adjudication over disputes about action, not the constitutive power over one's own law." (Conflict Treatise §27) This originally speaks of two persons; here it is extended to a member and the system (own undertaking): in acknowledging Article 7(b), what the DRP handed over is the procedure of being questioned and answering on an existing matter, not the power to settle citizenship.

Moreover, this "internal affair" had reached outside from the start. The decree turned those who had left into people without citizenship within other states' territory, and left their children in other states' birth registers, the "Citizenship" field reading "undetermined." What Article 4(3) governs is precisely this effect of pushing people onto other states.

How is the DRP's counter-question to be read? It asks about the UCSE: the UCSE once revoked a commentator's citizenship, and the UCSE's citizenship law did not give the Willow Ferry children citizenship. This is a separate question to the UCSE, and one that can fairly be put; the revocation has already been checked in Theory of the State Paper 3, §3 (N14). But it cannot close the question to the DRP. The Theory of the State says: "a question is answered according to its object, an exchange is checked according to its own reasons. The two can be negotiated at the same time; one cannot be used to close out the other." (Theory of the State Paper 5, §4) That the two questions fall on the same children is no surprise. The Theory of the State says: "Between states there is no nesting, only overlap: one and the same person can, as regards different acts, be in the interior of two states (extension; own undertaking)." (Theory of the State Paper 5, §2) As regards the DRP's decree, the Willow Ferry children are in the DRP's interior; as regards Article 14 of the Membership Act, they are in the UCSE's interior. The DRP's account and the UCSE's account are each booked by their own acts. What relation the UCSE's later change of law bears to that counter-question is for the UCSE's own positions to say; nothing on this point has been recorded.

It may be said that citizenship is the most typical of internal affairs, and that the system's putting questions about citizenship is interference. This objection too has a history. Vattel says that "no state has the smallest right to interfere in the government of another" (The Law of Nations, bk. II, ch. 4, §54, same source; tier B). He says further, of a state's sovereign: "If he loads his subjects with taxes, and if he treats them with severity, the nation alone is concerned in the business" (same chapter, §55, same source).

The common question is: when a state treats its own people in some way, what other states may do. Where they agree is non-interference. That other states have no right to govern a state in its place, the Theory of the State has already set up as a proposition (N25); that this series' main-line sentence says the system does not enter other states to decide on behalf of the people there is the same point. Where they part is "the nation alone is concerned." Vattel speaks of how a state treats its subjects as a concern of that nation alone; this series does not let "internal affairs" exempt anything from questioning. The system can ask, can record, and can make public records among the members; it does not enter by force, nor does it make that state's law for it. The DRP matter has one more layer besides: the decree's effects do not fall only within the DRP's territory; those who lost citizenship and the children are in the NUT and in the UCSE. Even by Vattel's division, this matter is not the concern of that nation alone. This is a division of scope; for these two sections of Vattel only a reading against online texts has been obtained, and this is not offered as a refutation.

Whether the DRP's reply counts as having answered, this paper does not settle for anyone. That step is handed to the channels inside the DRP and to the account visible from outside, and it is not decided by the questioner alone either (§2). One entry is left here: the DRP gave no numbers. By the end of Year 16, thirty-seven children had been registered under the new rules; the other children, and the some eleven thousand people who lost citizenship, are recorded plainly as unsettled.

XZ12 The system's questions connect into member states; the obligation to answer comes from the Compact (carried over from Theory of the State N38; own undertaking). The system does not enforce, and whether its questions land rests with each member: a peer's question does not on its own count as a channel; it counts only once it connects to the channels inside (Theory of the State Paper 7, §3). The Theory of the State allows a state not to answer ordinary questions from peers, and the obligation to answer must come from covenants acknowledged: a state may decline to answer ordinary diplomatic questions between peers that carry no separate obligation to answer; the requirements to answer and to implement that it bears under the treaties and common procedures it has acknowledged still follow the covenants acknowledged (ibid.). So a member's obligation to answer to the system, including a state that sends forces explaining its own first face publicly to the system, is written into the Compact and comes into being through the consent of all parties; it is not derived from "the system is an external questioner" alone; toward those whom the sending of forces reaches, the sending state answers following the unified sentence (state level, the Theory of the State Prequel, §8; see §6); the part of the coordination and preparation made in the system's name that this sending of forces then used (joint plans, joint exercises, prepositioned depots) still answers to those it reaches following the unified sentence (system level, §6). When a member answers with "internal affairs," not interfering is not the same as not asking (Theory of the State Paper 5, §2); a question is answered according to its object, an exchange is checked according to its own reasons (Theory of the State Paper 5, §4).

§5 Treaty Bodies Admitting Individual Petitions

Up to here, the questions have all been states questioning states. When the system questions the DRP, it asks what the DRP has done to its people; those people themselves are, on this road, only the people asked about. Can they hand in questions themselves?

The Theory of the State came this far. The Cloud River arbitral tribunal ruled on the volume of water between the UCSE and the NUT, and the Theory of the State wrote there: "But what it reviews are matters between the two states; the forty-three farming households cannot reach it, and above the three states there is no common court." (Theory of the State Paper 2, §5) Procedures between states are beyond the reach of individuals. The Theory of the State went on: "Treaty mechanisms admitting individual petitions come close to a channel: internal subjects can initiate them themselves, and the treaty also prohibits retaliation; this state's undertaking is not to obstruct, not to retaliate, and to implement rulings under the treaties this state has acknowledged (N24); such a mechanism has no enforcer of its own, and whether its decisions land still rests with this state." (Theory of the State Paper 5, §4) At that time there was no such mechanism among the three states. What such a mechanism is as a channel, and how it is to be designed, the Theory of the State registered as a remainder and handed on to here. Nor is such a road without a footing between states. In its nation-state case, How Is Institution Possible (Foundational Paper 6; cited as F6) said that where states share one continent, external checks too are hard to invoke independently; and it at once limited itself: "sharing a continent does not by itself prove that every external check must fail to be independent." (F6 §3.1b)

Article 14 of the Compact set up such a body, the Three-Borders Petitions Committee, located at Three Borders. It is written out in full here, since the sections that follow all make use of it. The article has seven clauses:

  • Clause 1, composition: seven members of the Committee, elected by the Conference of Members by a three-quarters majority from a list of persons nominated by the members, for a term of six years; members of the Committee serve in their personal capacity and do not take part in deliberating on petitions against their own state of citizenship.
  • Clause 2: where a member's acts touch on the matters listed in Article 4 and Annex II (life, the person, detention without trial, citizenship, leaving and returning, movement, speech, the taking of property), those affected may petition after exhausting the remedies within that member state.
  • Clause 3: "Persons reached by a decision made under a common procedure may also petition."
  • Clause 4: the Committee gives opinions, not rulings; the member petitioned against replies in writing within six months, and the reply is public.
  • Clause 5: a member may declare its exit from this article; the declaration takes effect twelve months after it is made; petitions already submitted before it takes effect are heard and concluded as usual, and those submitted after it takes effect are not taken up.
  • Clause 6: the Committee takes up acts done after this article has come into force for the member concerned; acts done before it came into force whose effects continue after it came into force may be taken up.
  • Clause 7: "Members shall take no adverse measures against petitioners or their relatives on account of a petition."

The Committee was constituted in the spring of Year 14; of the seven, two hold the citizenship of no member. Each member's acceptance of this article was decided separately. The UCSE accepted it in the winter of Year 13, attaching a declaration: "The opinions of the Committee land according to the law of the UCSE." Article 14 came into force for the UCSE in the spring of Year 14.

The first petitions to reach the Committee were petitions against the UCSE.

The background is in the Theory of the State. In the winter of Year 9, the UCSE struck the railway junction at Junction Town in the DRP's Wheat Plain District, hitting the station's waiting hall; the DRP announced forty-one civilian dead, the UCSE's Office of Defense twenty-two; the Office of Defense's target memorandum read "Passenger train times not verified." In the same period, the Defense Force, in the buffer zone, requisitioned the stored grain in the granaries of the Wheat Plain District, and the receipts said "to be settled after the war" (Theory of the State Paper 6, §3). In Year 12, written statements from the families of the Junction Town dead went through the liaison office at Three Borders to the War Inquiry Commission of the UCSE's Chamber of Citizens; the Commission discussed whether to accept them, and in the end accepted them (Theory of the State Paper 7, §3). In the winter of Year 13, the Chamber of Citizens passed the War Victims' Compensation Fund Act by 152 votes to 140; the fund compensated only "UCSE citizens and persons lawfully resident within the UCSE."

In the spring of Year 14, Article 14 came into force for the UCSE. The families and the farming households first went all the way through the routes within the UCSE. Twenty-seven families of the Junction Town dead applied to the Fund Administration Office for compensation in the spring of Year 14 and were turned down, on the ground that they were not among the persons listed in the Fund Act; they complained to the Inspectorate, which replied that the complainants were neither UCSE citizens nor lawfully resident within the UCSE, and were not among those whose complaints it takes up. Three hundred eleven farming households of the Wheat Plain District applied to the Office of Defense in the summer of Year 14 for settlement of the receipts; the Office of Defense replied that "the method of settlement after the war has not yet been drawn up."

In the winter of Year 14, two petitions reached the Committee: one from the families, on the scope of the Fund Act and the disclosure of the target memorandum; one from the farming households, on the receipts not having been settled. Both were submitted by lawyers in Rivermouth City engaged by the families and by the farming households respectively, and the petitions set out the routes they had already taken within the UCSE. After they were submitted, the DRP's Foreign Ministry wrote to the Secretariat: "The Republic supports the petitions of its nationals."

The member of the Committee holding UCSE citizenship did not take part in the deliberations, and six deliberated. In the spring of Year 15, the Committee decided to take up two items of the families' petition, the scope of the Fund Act and the target memorandum, and the whole of the farming households' petition; the ground was clause 6: the receipts had not been settled, and the Fund Act applied after Article 14 came into force, their effects continuing after it came into force. The decision added: "Within the UCSE, the petitioners have no other available remedy." The strike itself took place before Article 14 came into force and was not taken up; the decision read: "This Committee does not determine whether that strike accorded with the law of war."

In the winter of Year 15, the Committee gave its opinion: it asked the UCSE to explain on what basis the Fund Act excludes civilians whom the UCSE's acts reach and who are outside the UCSE's territory; asked the UCSE to settle the granary receipts; and asked the UCSE to let the families see the parts of the target memorandum bearing on the deaths of their relatives. In the summer of Year 16, the UCSE's Office of External Affairs replied in writing: "The UCSE respects the opinions of the Committee; landing follows the law of the UCSE." In the same season, the Chamber of Citizens elected in the autumn of Year 15 amended the Fund Act by 158 votes to 139, adding "civilians outside the territory whom the UCSE's acts reach." The Office of Defense settled the receipts at Year 9 grain prices; a representative of the farming households said that, "at Year 9 prices, it amounts to paying thirty percent less." The Office of Defense refused to disclose the target memorandum, on the ground that it "touches on intelligence sources," and gave the families a redacted summary. In Year 17, nineteen of the twenty-seven families received compensation from the fund; eight were turned down, the Fund Administration Office's reason being that "whether the dead were civilians cannot be determined." The relatives of these eight families are not among the twenty-two the Office of Defense announced, and are among the forty-one the DRP announced.

This matter falls on the twenty-seven families and the three hundred eleven farming households. They hold DRP citizenship and are outside the UCSE's territory; the strike, the requisitioning of grain and the Fund Act are all acts of the UCSE that fall on them, and they cannot avoid them. The line to be looked at is the fifth ought: whether there is a road by which questions can reach the UCSE, independent, actually able to be initiated, and bringing no retaliation for initiating it; and what the UCSE undertook under Article 14, which it had accepted. Before this, they had only one road: the families' statements went through the liaison office at Three Borders to the War Inquiry Commission, and whether they were passed on and whether they were accepted both lay with others. The Theory of the State has spoken to the weight of such a road: "A route that gets through only if others are willing cannot yet certify that 'can actually initiate' has been met" (Theory of the State Paper 7, §3). And the Fund Act compensated only UCSE citizens and those lawfully resident.

Step one: check against the three conditions of a channel. Institution Theory has written these three: "the channel meets three conditions: it is independent of the party questioned, internal subjects can actually initiate it, and initiating it brings no retaliation." (Institution Theory Paper 6, Z34) This originally speaks of a construct and its internal subjects; here it is extended to a treaty body and those whom members' acts reach (own undertaking).

First, independence. Members of the Committee serve in their personal capacity and are elected by the Conference of Members by a three-quarters majority; the member holding UCSE citizenship does not take part in deliberating on petitions against the UCSE, and this case was deliberated by six. The vote on whether to take it up was not in the hands of the party questioned.

Next, whether it can actually be initiated. The petitioners are the families and the farming households themselves, and the lawyers submitted the petitions on their instructions; the DRP Foreign Ministry's letter came after submission, and the petitions depended neither on the DRP passing them on nor on whether the liaison office was willing to pass them on. Clause 2 requires that domestic remedies be exhausted before petitioning. This requirement has its reason: it gives the party questioned's own positions the chance to answer first. But it can also become a lock, if what counts as "exhausted" is for the party questioned to say. In this case, the Fund Administration Office turned the families down, the Inspectorate did not take up their complaint, and the Office of Defense said that the method had not yet been drawn up; what determined that there was no other available domestic remedy was the Committee, not the UCSE. This item passes the check.

Last, no retaliation for initiating it. Clause 7 states that members shall take no adverse measures against petitioners or their relatives on account of a petition; this clause is an example at the rule layer. In this matter, whether the petitioners and their relatives met with adverse treatment has not been recorded. So this item is checked only as far as the text being there; how things actually went is written plainly as not recorded, and is not written as having been checked.

Step two: state this channel clearly by the four items. The Theory of the State requires: "For every channel it must be stated who initiates it, which object it affects, whether the party questioned can close it, and how the account is booked when it fails" (Theory of the State Paper 7, §1). The Theory of Government puts it more strictly: "Of each, three things must be statable: who can activate it; what actual efficacy it has on reasons, actions, rules, or the holding of office; how the party questioned cannot cancel it on its own. Where these cannot be stated, that function is there in name only." (Theory of Government Paper 6, G36a) Following these two places, it is written in four items:

  • Who initiates it: those affected, themselves.
  • Which object it affects, and with what actual efficacy: the UCSE's acts that touch on the matters in Annex II, here the taking of property, and life; the Committee's opinion asks the UCSE to explain, to settle and to let the families see, and the UCSE must reply in writing within six months, the reply being public.
  • Whether the party questioned can close it: the UCSE cannot withdraw this petition on its own. All it can do is exit the whole article under clause 5; the exit takes effect only after twelve months, and petitions already submitted are heard and concluded as usual (§3).
  • How the account is booked when it fails: where the UCSE does not reply, or the opinion does not land, this is booked to the UCSE and to the position that made that decision, and the record is public.

Step three: landing. Once the opinion was given, what it became on landing was the work of the UCSE's own positions (§4; XZ12). The Chamber of Citizens amended the Fund Act; the Office of Defense settled the receipts at Year 9 grain prices; the target memorandum was given only as a redacted summary. Each of the three was done by a position of the UCSE, and each is questioned inside the UCSE: the farming households' representative's words, "paying thirty percent less," are one such question. A member's undertaking toward this road is written following Theory of the State N27: not to obstruct, not to retaliate, and to implement under the covenants acknowledged. One point must be put precisely here. That sentence of the Theory of the State says "implement rulings"; the committee in the example gives only opinions, and what the acknowledged covenant requires of the UCSE is a written reply and publicity. So this paper writes it as: to implement the decisions of treaty bodies under the covenants acknowledged, whether those decisions are rulings or opinions being as the covenant has it.

The line of time separates two kinds of question. The strike itself took place before Article 14 came into force; the Committee did not take it up, and stated that it does not determine whether that strike accorded with the law of war; what it took up is the part whose effects continue after Article 14 came into force: the receipts and the Fund Act. The question of the strike itself did not vanish on that account: it is in the UCSE's own War Inquiry Commission, and in the channels written of in Theory of the State Paper 7; it is only not on this road.

It may be said that a committee that only gives opinions and cannot enforce them is a rubber stamp. The weight of this objection is that an opinion can indeed be politely received and then left to lie. But whether a channel counts does not turn on whether it has an enforcer of its own; the Theory of the State has already said that external routes have no enforcers of their own (this paper, §4). What must be looked at is whether what it leaves behind stands up to checking: whether those who ask are there, whether the questions reach the place where decisions are made, whether those who ask are protected, and whether decisions change because of it (§8). In this case, after the opinion, the Office of External Affairs answered in writing; the Chamber of Citizens amended the Fund Act in the same season; the receipts were settled; the summary was given. All of these left traces visible from outside. What relation they bear to the opinion, and how the two sentences "respects the opinions of the Committee" and "land according to the law of the UCSE" are to be read together, is questioned inside the UCSE.

What this paper gives is only the three conditions, the four items and the members' undertaking. How many tiers to set up, whether it is a court or a committee, how many members it has, how long the time limit for replies is, are handed to specific theories of institutions; Article 14 of the Compact is only one way of writing it, an example. How much of an opinion lands is judged by the UCSE's positions, and the account falls on those positions.

Several points are left here. Eight families were turned down because "whether the dead were civilians cannot be determined"; no third party has ever made a determination between the two numbers, forty-one and twenty-two, and these eight families' question falls between the two numbers. The parts of the target memorandum not given out remain out of reach even after questions from outside have connected inside the UCSE. Of the thirty percent less paid by settling at Year 9 grain prices, the farming households have spoken; who answered has not been recorded. The strike itself is not on this road.

XZ13 Treaty bodies admitting individual petitions as channels (carried over from Theory of the State N27; own undertaking). Treaty mechanisms admitting individual petitions come close to a channel: internal subjects can initiate them themselves, and the treaty also prohibits retaliation; such a mechanism has no enforcer of its own, and whether its decisions land still rests with the state (Theory of the State Paper 5, §4). What it fills is the place where arbitration between two states is beyond the reach of individuals (Theory of the State Paper 2, §5). The design sets up functions only: following the three conditions of a channel, it is independent of the party questioned, internal subjects can actually initiate it, and initiating it brings no retaliation (Institution Theory Z34); it does not depend on the willingness of an intermediary, since a route that gets through only if others are willing cannot yet certify that it can actually be initiated (Theory of the State Paper 7, §3); for each function it can be stated who can activate it, what actual efficacy it has, and how the party questioned cannot cancel it on its own (Theory of Government G36a). The members' undertaking follows Theory of the State N27: not to obstruct, not to retaliate, and to implement the decisions of treaty bodies under the covenants acknowledged (whether rulings or opinions, as the covenant has it). How many tiers, and whether a court or a committee, is handed to specific theories of institutions.

§6 Those Whom the System's Acts Reach

In the preceding sections, those questioned were all states: the system questions members, and people question members. But the system itself also acts, and its acts fall on people. Where are those people?

In the summer of Year 16, after the sweeps and detentions in the north of the Republic of the Sandbars had begun, the Conference of Members adopted Common Measure No. 2 by 4 to 0, for a term of one year: (1) an embargo on arms and "dual-use goods" to the Republic of the Sandbars; (2) a freeze on the personal assets, in each member, of the members of the Stability Committee and the commanders of the Stability Squads; (3) a halt to commercial traffic at all the ferry crossings of the Grey River, persons and humanitarian supplies excepted. Humanitarian supplies pass on the license of each member's competent authorities. Article 11 of the Compact provides that common measures are implemented by each member under its own law, that they must include a humanitarian exemption, and that those affected by a measure may petition under Article 14.

The lower Grey River is the boundary river between the Republic of the Sandbars and Stonegorge, the west bank belonging to the Sandbars and the east bank to Stonegorge. The market in the town of Grey Ferry on the east bank had been shared by both banks, held three times a week, and the villages on the west bank bought most of their salt, fuel and medicine there. After item (3) was implemented, the market closed. The licensing review period at Stonegorge's Commerce Office averaged forty-one days in the autumn of Year 16; water-purification agents were listed as "dual-use goods" and refused licenses. In the winter of Year 16, a doctor at a clinic on the river in West Shoal Province reported that the supply of cold-chain medicines had been cut off for two months, and that the clinic had cut its opening to two days a week; the Grey Ferry chamber of commerce said that thirty percent of the four hundred twelve shopkeepers in the town had closed.

That same winter, two petitions reached the Petitions Committee. The Grey Ferry chamber of commerce, on behalf of one hundred twenty shopkeepers, petitioned over Stonegorge's implementation of item (3); the Committee set aside the item on compensation, on the ground that the remedies within Stonegorge had not been exhausted, and took up the rest. The doctor at that clinic in West Shoal Province and fifty-eight villagers petitioned over the Commerce Office's licensing review and the classification as "dual-use goods." These petitioners are outside the system, in the Sandbars. The Committee decided by 4 to 3 to take the petition up: "Clause 2 of Article 14 speaks of 'those affected,' and does not limit them to people within members' territory." The minority opinion: "People outside the system are not within the Compact; their questions should be put by the Republic of the Sandbars."

In the spring of Year 17, the Committee gave its opinion: it asked Stonegorge to shorten the licensing review period to fifteen days or less; asked Stonegorge and the Conference of Members to explain why water-purification agents were listed as "dual-use goods"; and asked that a humanitarian market at Grey Ferry be considered. In the same season, the Conference of Members amended Measure No. 2 by 4 to 0: the review period was capped at fifteen days; water-purification agents were removed from the "dual-use goods"; and a humanitarian market was to open at Grey Ferry twice a week, selling salt, cooking fuel and medicine. Stonegorge's Commerce Office complied.

This matter falls on people in two places: the shopkeepers of Grey Ferry, within Stonegorge's territory; and the doctor and villagers of the riverside clinic in West Shoal Province, outside the system. All of them are within the reach of item (3) of Measure No. 2 and of the "dual-use goods" classification, and cannot avoid them; yet what the measure targets is the Stability Committee and the Stability Squads, not them. Two lines are to be looked at: the first ought, that the part of the measure falling on people does not cross the line, and that the humanitarian part is cut out, and cut out so that it lands; and the fifth ought, that these people have a road by which the system itself can be reached. The situation before this was: item (3) had halted commercial traffic, persons and humanitarian supplies excepted; the licensing review averaged forty-one days; water-purification agents were refused licenses.

In whose interior are they? The Theory of the State answered this at the level of a single state. Its unified sentence says: "Those whom this state's acts reach and who have no way to avoid them by exiting are, as regards those acts, in this state's interior; the five oughts apply each on its own conditions"; and its latter half reads: "the first and fifth oughts (a channel that is independent, within reach, and brings no retaliation for its use) and the 'not sealing the channels' half of the fourth do not vary with relational thickness; the trigger of the second does not vary with it, and its means are bounded by this state's authority at that place" (the Theory of the State Prequel, §8).

The subject term of this sentence is "this state." To extend it to the system level, the whole sentence is extended, the subject term is changed, and how far the system's means at that place go is stated (own undertaking). Extending it in this way first requires that the system count as a construct, its acts organizing these people's lives; this is answered further in §7. The whole series writes it out in full here for the first time, and later papers point back here when they use it:

Those whom the system's acts reach and who have no way to avoid them by exiting are, as regards those acts, in the system's interior; the five oughts apply each on its own conditions: the first ought (the system's acts cross no line), the fifth (a road within reach by which the system itself can be questioned) and the "not sealing the channels" half of the fourth do not vary with relational thickness; the trigger of the second does not vary with it, and its means are bounded by the system's authority at that place, going only as far as questioning, recording, withdrawing cooperation and asking the member where it lands to guard the line; the third ought, and the "leaving room" half of the fourth, go by relational thickness: where those reached are in member states, the conditions for cultivation lie within those states; where those reached are outside the system, the system only creates conditions for cultivation. This confers no membership.

The system's acts are actions taken in the system's name, made through a common procedure, and acting on people: lists entered under a common procedure; notices and lists the system issues; the part of the coordination and preparation of joint defense made in the system's name; the allocation of admissions; the decisions of treaty bodies. Sending forces and joint action themselves are acts of each participating state (Paper 5, XZ30, XZ32). Measure No. 2 was adopted by the Conference of Members through a common procedure, and it is an act of the system; it is also implemented by each member under its own law, and on the stretch of the Grey River it is implemented by Stonegorge's Commerce Office, so on that stretch it is also an act of Stonegorge.

Read the two banks of the Grey River by this sentence. The people in both places are, as regards these acts of the system, in the system's interior, whether or not they are within a member's territory. One and the same person can also be in two interiors at once: as regards item (3), the shopkeepers of Grey Ferry are within the system; and since item (3) is implemented by Stonegorge under its own law, they are within Stonegorge too. This is the overlap spoken of in §4, extended from between two states to between the system and its members (own undertaking).

The minority opinion says that the west-bank villagers' questions should be put by the Sandbars. But the Sandbars' government is precisely the side the measure targets. Under the unified sentence, those reached must have a road within reach; a road that can pass only through the side the measure targets cannot be their only road.

Where each of the five oughts falls in this matter is written in four items.

  • The second ought: the trigger is not reduced, and the means are bounded by the system's authority. The measure targets the line-crossing of the Stability Committee and the Stability Squads; the system's means go only as far as withdrawing cooperation, questioning and recording, and do not enter the Sandbars. Halting commercial traffic on the Grey River is each member withdrawing its own share of dealings. Force is substituting one's own judgment for the other party's judgment on its own affairs. There is no such substitution here: how matters in the north of the Sandbars are handled is still judged in the Sandbars; what each member withdraws is its own cooperation. Withdrawing cooperation is a check, not force; this is set up in Paper 4, §2 (XZ22).
  • The first and fifth oughts, and the "not sealing the channels" half of the fourth: these do not vary with relational thickness. The west-bank villagers' relation to the system is very thin, and for them these oughts are not reduced in the least. What item (3) halted was commercial traffic; persons were not among what was halted, and the ferry crossings were not closed to people. The measure fell on the shopkeepers of Grey Ferry and on the clinic on the west bank, and people came to the Committee by way of Article 14; the Committee gave its opinion, the Conference of Members amended the measure by 4 to 0, and Stonegorge's Commerce Office, where it lands, complied. The three amendments all fall under this item: the review period capped at fifteen days, water-purification agents removed from the "dual-use goods," and a humanitarian market at Grey Ferry twice a week. This is the system correcting its own act: the part of the measure falling on people does not cross the line, and the humanitarian part is cut out, and cut out so that it lands; salt, fuel and medicine reach the hands of the people on both banks, and the channels are not sealed.
  • The third ought, and the "leaving room" half of the fourth: these go by relational thickness. The shopkeepers of Grey Ferry are in a member state, and the conditions for their cultivation are provided by Stonegorge according to its relation with them; the system does not do this in Stonegorge's place. The people on the west bank are outside the system, and the system only creates conditions for their cultivation; how they live their lives is still for them to decide.
  • This confers no membership: the Committee's taking up the west-bank villagers' petition does not make them the people of any member, nor does it give the system authority to govern the Sandbars.

It may be said that people outside the system are not within the Compact, and that the Committee, in taking them up, went beyond the Compact. This is the minority opinion, and the weight of this objection is that the Compact is indeed only a covenant among the members, and people outside the system never signed it. But the Compact draws the members; the unified sentence draws what acts reach. Wherever the system's acts fall, the road for questions must follow. Following them there gives the people there no membership, and gives the system no authority to govern there; it gives them only a road within reach of the system.

That to be reached is to be inside, and that the road must follow, is the norm this paper gives; the wording of clause 2 of Article 14, "those affected," is one way of writing it into a rule. Where reach is drawn still has to be judged in each matter: this time it was drawn by the Committee by 4 to 3, and the account falls on the members of the Committee who made the decision to take the petition up.

One entry is left here. Before the amendment, cold-chain medicines were cut off for two months, the clinic cut its opening to two days a week, and thirty percent of the shopkeepers in the town closed; the account of those months stands as recorded, and how it is recorded and how it is read are handed to Paper 3. How the humanitarian part is cut out, and cut out so that it lands, is in Paper 4, §4 (XZ24). How the accounts of the system and of Stonegorge are booked separately, and how the system's own road is its own fifth ought, is in the next section.

XZ14 is set up in full in §7.

§7 The System Itself Is Questioned

What the people on the two banks of the Grey River asked about was how Stonegorge implemented a measure, and in passing they asked the Conference of Members too. What if what is asked about is a decision the Conference of Members itself has made?

People from the north of the Sandbars fled across the Grey River, and Stonegorge received them; after the camp at Grey Ferry was twice shelled from the opposite bank, it moved east to Eastfield in the winter of Year 16. Article 12 of the Compact set up a sharing of admission: the members share the costs and the places for transfer, calculated by the formula in the annex; Article 12 itself states that "sharing does not decide for those who arrive where they are to settle."

In the spring of Year 17, the Conference of Members made Sharing Decision No. 3 by 4 to 0: thirty thousand people were to be transferred from the Eastfield camp that year, with places allotted as sixteen thousand to the UCSE, eight thousand to the DRP and six thousand to the NUT (the NUT having already taken in about one hundred ninety thousand people, which was deducted under the formula). Those eligible for transfer were people who had been registered in the camp for six months; to which state they would go was drawn by lot, household by household; "those drawn who refuse the result of the lot lose eligibility for transfer that year and remain in the camp." Adult children counted as households of their own. The Admission-Sharing Office's explanation was: "The lot allocates places, not people; those drawn may refuse."

In the camp, forty-six households, two hundred fourteen people, who had relatives in the NUT's Northern District were drawn for the UCSE or the DRP. Thirty-one of these households refused, and lost their eligibility for that year. In nine households, the adult children and the parents were drawn for different states.

In the summer of Year 17, one hundred three of these two hundred fourteen people petitioned the Committee under clause 3 of Article 14 against Sharing Decision No. 3 itself. The Deputy Secretary-General for Common Procedures issued a written opinion on behalf of the Secretariat: "A 'decision made under a common procedure' in clause 3 of Article 14 means a decision made by the members under a common procedure, not a decision of the Conference of Members itself; the Conference of Members is responsible for its own decisions." The Committee decided by 5 to 2 to take the petition up: "Clause 3 does not exclude the decisions of the Conference of Members." The minority opinion: "The Conference of Members is a conference of the members; to question the Conference of Members is to question the members, and petitions should be brought against each member separately."

In the winter of Year 17, the Committee gave its opinion: it asked the Conference of Members to explain how the clause "refusing the result of the lot means losing eligibility for transfer" can coexist with Article 12 of the Compact, "sharing does not decide for those who arrive where they are to settle"; and asked the Conference of Members to take families into account in allocation. In the spring of Year 18, the Conference of Members made Decision No. 4 by 4 to 0: adult children may join their parents as one household; those who refuse the result of the lot keep their eligibility for the following year. Brothers and sisters are not joined into one household. The reply was issued in the name of the Conference of Members, stating how each member voted, as Article 3 of the Compact requires. Stonegorge's permanent representative said at the meeting: "The camp is in Stonegorge, and those who stay are in Stonegorge too."

This matter falls on those forty-six households, two hundred fourteen people: they are within the reach of Sharing Decision No. 3 and cannot avoid it; thirty-one households lost that year's eligibility because they refused; nine households were split between different states. Two lines are to be looked at. One is the fifth ought: whether there is a road by which the Conference of Members itself can be reached. The other is the first ought: the system's allocation does not cross the line; Article 12 of the Compact itself states that sharing does not decide for those who arrive where they are to settle. Before this: Sharing Decision No. 3 had been made by 4 to 0, with lots drawn by household, refusal meaning the loss of that year's eligibility, and adult children counted as households of their own; clause 3 of Article 14 allowed those reached by a decision made under a common procedure to petition.

Step one: who can be questioned. The Secretariat's reading would place the Conference of Members' own decisions outside this road. The Committee did not follow it. A decision of the Conference of Members is precisely an act of the system: made in the system's name, through a common procedure, and falling on people. Those it reaches must have a road by which it can be reached. Whether this road is open is decided neither by the Conference of Members being questioned nor by its Secretariat; the party questioned does not hold the power to shut the door on the question directed at itself (§2; XZ10). The Secretariat's written opinion is one position's opinion; it was entered in the record, and it did not block the taking up; whether to take the petition up was decided by the Committee by 5 to 2, and the account for this judgment falls on the members of the Committee who made the decision.

This road, stated clearly by the four items: those reached initiate it themselves; its object is the decision of the Conference of Members; the Conference of Members cannot close this case on its own, and amending the Compact can change the structure but cannot change the three conditions this road must meet (§8; XZ15); if there is no answer and no change, the account is booked to the system, and also to each member that voted.

From this one thing can be said: for the system, a mechanism of the kind that admits individual petitions is not merely a better design; it is the system's own fifth ought. Speaking of multilateral sanctions, the Theory of the State has spoken of a state's share in a common procedure: "This state's share in the common procedure includes advocating and supporting that a road be left for those affected" (Theory of the State Paper 5, §5). That speaks of a participating state. At the level of the system, leaving those affected a road by which the system can be reached is the common procedure's own share (own undertaking).

Step two: where the account is booked. The minority opinion says that to question the Conference of Members is to question the members. This is half right. Between two levels of government, the Theory of Government set up a sentence: "The accounts of two constructs are each checked by their own source and are not transferred from one to the other (own undertaking)." (Theory of Government Paper 4, G25) This originally speaks of two levels of government within one state; here it is extended to the system and its members (own undertaking). The account of Sharing Decision No. 3 is in the system; it is also on the four members that voted and on their positions. Article 3 of the Compact requires documents issued in the system's name to state how each member voted precisely so that this share of the account can land on someone. Questioning the Conference of Members and questioning each member separately are two roads; they stand side by side, and neither replaces the other. Questioning each member asks why each member voted as it did; questioning the Conference of Members asks about this decision itself.

The same holds for the matter on the two banks of the Grey River. When the Committee asked Stonegorge to shorten the review period, it was asking about Stonegorge's share in implementing the measure under its own law; when it asked Stonegorge and the Conference of Members to explain the classification of water-purification agents, it was asking each of the two about its own part. The accounts are booked separately.

Step three: whether the system counts as a construct. To say that those whom the system's acts reach are in the system's interior presupposes that the system is a construct, something that organizes these people's lives. Institution Theory draws the interior by entanglement: "reach of influence is not reach of authority, and whether the construct is organizing their lives is judged by the actual relation" (Institution Theory Paper 1, Z4). Whether the system is a construct in this sense, Institution Theory did not say; this series undertakes it. Judged by the actual relation, where these people in the Eastfield camp go is organized by a decision of the Conference of Members; the lot, the way households are drawn up and the consequences of refusing all fall on their days. As regards these acts, the system is organizing their lives.

One more sentence must be added (own undertaking). The notices and lists the system issues fall on people, and people must have a road by which to ask. If a state uses the system's notices or lists to demand that another member send people back, the system does not help; not pushing back at the door holds as before (Paper 6, XZ38).

It may be said that the Committee was set up by the system itself, so that questioning the system is the left hand questioning the right. The weight of this objection is that the Committee is indeed an article of the Compact, and is located at the seat of the system. But whether a channel is independent is judged by structure, not by whose name it hangs under. The Theory of Government says: "Capture is judged by structure, not by the name of a body or where it sits" (Theory of Government Paper 6, G38). Judged by structure: members of the Committee serve in their personal capacity, are elected by the Conference of Members by a three-quarters majority, and do not hear petitions against their own state of citizenship; the Secretariat, on the side questioned, issued an opinion seeking to put this case outside, and it did not block the taking up. Whether this case was blocked, and whether it changed the decision, must still be checked against the four points of War Treatise §16, in the next section.

One point is left here, and it is not judged in this section. What to make of the arrangement itself, lots drawn by household and refusal meaning the loss of that year's eligibility, turns on whether the room not to go to a particular state is actually usable; that norm is set up in Paper 6, §4 (XZ39), and whether the room is actually usable in a given matter is left to the judgment of those in positions. This section judges only the road and the account. How the system's own fifth ought is realized, and whether a committee is its only form, is registered as a remainder and handed to The Close (Paper 8, XZ56).

XZ14 The system's own fifth ought (own undertaking). The system's acts are: lists entered under a common procedure, notices and lists the system issues, the part of the coordination and preparation of joint defense made in the system's name, the allocation of admissions, and the decisions of treaty bodies; sending forces and joint action themselves are acts of each participating state (XZ30, XZ32). The unified sentence of the Theory of the State (the Theory of the State Prequel, §8) is extended to the system level, the whole sentence extended, the subject term changed, and how far the system's means at that place go stated: those whom the system's acts reach and who have no way to avoid them by exiting are, as regards those acts, in the system's interior; the five oughts apply each on its own conditions: the first ought (the system's acts cross no line), the fifth (a road within reach by which the system itself can be questioned) and the "not sealing the channels" half of the fourth do not vary with relational thickness; the trigger of the second does not vary with it, and its means are bounded by the system's authority at that place, going only as far as questioning, recording, withdrawing cooperation and asking the member where it lands to guard the line; the third ought, and the "leaving room" half of the fourth, go by relational thickness: where those reached are in member states, the conditions for cultivation lie within those states; where those reached are outside the system, the system only creates conditions for cultivation. This confers no membership. The accounts of the system and of the member states that implement its acts are each checked by their own source and are not transferred from one to the other (Theory of Government G25; own undertaking). Hence a mechanism of the kind that admits individual petitions is not merely a better design but the system's own fifth ought; Theory of the State N29 says that a state's share in a common procedure includes advocating that a road be left for those affected, and at the level of the system this is the common procedure's own share. Whether the system is a construct in the sense of Institution Theory Z4 is undertaken by this series. The notices and lists the system issues fall on people, and people must have a road by which to ask; where a state borrows them to demand that people be sent back, the system does not help (not pushing back at the door holds as before; Paper 6, XZ38).

§8 The System's Arrangements Can Be Chiseled

Once there is a road, might the road be only for show? The system can set up a committee, let people petition, receive the opinions, and then carry on exactly as before. Being open to questioning requires that questioning be able to change something.

F6 has set up a proposition: "An institution premised on non dubito must have a chiseling channel satisfying three structural conditions: independent of what is chiseled; actually initiable by members; initiation not met with retaliation." (F6 §3.2, Proposition IV) This originally speaks of an institution and its members; here it is extended to the system level (own undertaking), and the three conditions read: the channel for chiseling the system's arrangements is independent of the place being questioned; members and those whom the system's acts reach can actually initiate it; and initiating it brings no retaliation.

The system might also find itself a reason for exemption from questioning: that it leans more toward the 15DD direction, that its aims are right, that it has already reformed. Institution Theory keeps out every reason of this kind: an institution "may not close off the conditions of questioning and correction on the grounds that it claims to cultivate, that it has already reformed, or that its aims are right; structures can change, but the three conditions cannot be withdrawn." (Institution Theory Paper 6, Z37) The system can amend its Compact, and can make the Committee into something else; the three conditions cannot be withdrawn.

Step one: check the three conditions against the system itself. In the Eastfield case, what issued an opinion seeking to put the Conference of Members' decisions outside clause 3 was the Secretariat, an organ of the side questioned itself; the Committee did not follow it (§7). Independence is judged by structure, not by name or where a body sits. The step of taking up the case was not held in the hands of the place questioned.

Step two: check against the four points. Speaking of whether a war-party allows itself to be questioned, the War Treatise says that "a real allowance must be embodied in structure: questioners exist, questioning reaches the decision core, questioners are protected, questioning actually affects decisions. Each of these four leaves traces visible from outside." It has also spoken first of the danger of such mechanisms: "But in actual operation these mechanisms may be no more than rubber stamps." (War Treatise §16) This originally speaks of the war-party and those who question it; here it is extended to the system and those who question it (own undertaking). The Eastfield case, looked at point by point by these four:

  • Questioners exist: the Committee is there, and the one hundred three petitioners are there.
  • Questioning reaches the decision core: the opinion came back to the Conference of Members; in the spring of Year 18, the Conference of Members made Decision No. 4 by 4 to 0.
  • Questioners are protected: the no-retaliation of clause 7 of Article 14 is an example at the rule layer; whether the petitioners met with adverse treatment has not been recorded. This point is checked only as far as the text being there.
  • Questioning actually affects decisions: Decision No. 4 was issued as the reply; adult children may join their parents' household, and those who refuse keep their eligibility for the following year.

The War Treatise further says: "Only when all four layers pass does Theorem Four truly pass. If any one layer gives way, Theorem Four fails." (ibid.) To say it as it is: in the Eastfield case, three of the four points left traces visible from outside; of the point on protection, only the text can be seen. There is no record of its giving way, and no record of its having been checked. The case on the two banks of the Grey River can be checked the same way: the three things the Committee asked for, the review period, the water-purification agents and the humanitarian market, match the Conference of Members' amendments one by one.

Step three: decisions can be final; positions cannot. Decision No. 4 is final in this case: that year's transfers were carried out under it. But it can still be questioned again afterward. Of the arbitration between two states, the Theory of the State has said: "The decision is final in the case; the positions that implement it are not final, each lying under a channel it cannot itself close" (Theory of the State Paper 5, §1). Nor does the Committee's opinion take the highest effect: it asks the Conference of Members to explain and to take families into account, and does not make decisions in the Conference of Members' place. Brothers and sisters are still not joined into one household. How far the change must go to count as having answered is decided neither by the Conference of Members alone nor by the Committee alone; where there is no position that neither side monopolizes to determine it, it is recorded plainly as unsettled (§2; XZ10).

It may be said that under pressure from outside the system must hold together, and that questioning of its own arrangements must be kept within measure, or it will be divided from outside. The weight of this objection is that the system does indeed face pressure from outside, and that among the members there will indeed be some who use questioning to pursue their own ends. But to press down questioning of one's own arrangements so as not to be divided is exactly the mirror the War Treatise speaks of: "if one's own side, to guard against the adversary, suppresses one's own space of questioning, one has already structurally become the adversary's mirror (the adversary originally does not allow questioning either)." (War Treatise §20) This originally speaks of a side at war; here it is extended to the system (own undertaking). How mirroring at the system level is recognized and judged is in Paper 4, §6 (XZ26); here only one thing is set up: the three conditions are not withdrawn because of pressure from outside.

That the three conditions are not withdrawn is the norm; how the Committee is composed, and how its voting thresholds and reply deadlines are set, are structure that can be changed. How far a change must go to be enough is judged by positions; in the Eastfield case, the account falls on the Conference of Members that made Decision No. 4 and on each member's vote.

Two larger questions are left here. One is how being open to questioning is realized between states as scale changes. Law Series P4 lists the modes of realization at each scale: "dyadic law relies on exit, group law on questioning delegates, national law on multi-branch separation of powers, interstellar law returns to exit and symmetric negotiation." (Law Series P4 §7) States cannot exit from one another, and they set up no superior with separated powers; how this is realized between them, the upstream has not given. This paper has answered part of it with XZ10 to XZ15, and the complete form is left to Paper 8. The other is a question Institution Theory left: "What the same passage goes on to ask, namely whether mutual recognition and mutual examination can grow into common legislation, this series does not answer." (Institution Theory Paper 6, Z39) Mutual questioning has come to the edge of this question; this paper does not answer it, and hands it to The Close (Paper 8, XZ55).

XZ15 The system's arrangements can be chiseled (carried over from Institution Theory Z37; own undertaking). An institution premised on non dubito must have a chiseling channel satisfying three structural conditions: independent of what is chiseled; actually initiable by members; initiation not met with retaliation (F6 §3.2, Proposition IV). Extended to the system level: the channel for chiseling the system's arrangements is independent of the place being questioned, members and those whom the system's acts reach can actually initiate it, and initiating it brings no retaliation. The system does not exempt itself from questioning by claiming "15DD predominance": it may not close off the conditions of questioning and correction on the grounds that it claims to cultivate, that it has already reformed, or that its aims are right; structures can change, but the three conditions cannot be withdrawn (Institution Theory Z37). In actual operation these mechanisms may be no more than rubber stamps, so they are checked against the four points of War Treatise §16: questioners exist, questioning reaches the decision core, questioners are protected, and questioning actually affects decisions (originally said of the war-party; here extended to the system itself; own undertaking). Capture is judged by structure, not by the name of a body or where it sits (Theory of Government G38). The decisions of treaty bodies do not take the highest effect, and are read by "decisions can be final; positions cannot be final" (Theory of Government G36).

Where This Paper Stops

This paper has proved these things. With no superior overhead, questions come from outside, from a group of peers; the system's position is that of an external questioner, its determinations are public questions and records, it neither authorizes nor vetoes, and even a member that sends forces cannot close its questions. Making a covenant needs the consent of all parties; questioning existing matters does not; the party questioned recuses itself on the matter directed at it, and the questioning party does not decide alone that the case is closed. Closing one channel cannot cancel the questioning; moving against a questioner is trying to cancel it. The system does not enforce, and whether a question lands rests with each member; a member's obligation to answer comes from the covenants it has acknowledged, and "internal affairs" does not block questions. Treaty bodies admitting individual petitions fill the place where individuals cannot reach the procedures between states, and they are checked against the three conditions of a channel and the four items. The unified sentence has been extended to the system level: those whom the system's acts reach and who cannot avoid them are, as regards those acts, in the system's interior, whether or not they are within a member's territory; giving them a road by which the system itself can be reached is the system's own fifth ought; the accounts of the system and of the members are each checked by their own source. The system's arrangements can be chiseled; the three conditions are not withdrawn, and the arrangements are checked against the four points of War Treatise §16; decisions can be final, positions cannot.

What it has not proved is handed elsewhere. How many tiers a treaty body has, whether it is a court or a committee, and the details of its procedure, are handed to specific theories of institutions; Article 14 of the Compact and the Petitions Committee are only examples, and this series does not claim that they are the best design. How "open to questioning" is realized between states as scale changes, in its complete form, is left to Paper 8. How the channels inside each state are designed is in Theory of the State Paper 7. How the account is recorded and read once questions have been asked is handed to Paper 3; withdrawing cooperation by the account is in Paper 4; questions left unsettled at exit, and the road for those whom a state's acts reach after its expulsion, are in Paper 7. Whether, in a sharing like Eastfield's with lots drawn by household, the room not to go to a particular state is actually usable, is in Paper 6.

Several costs are written here as they are. At the system level there is no fallback court; the questioning cannot be cancelled, but the answer may not come; the system watches problems happen and cannot resolve them directly by armed force. In the DRP matter, the parents who lost citizenship do not have their own citizenship restored; for those eight families of Junction Town, the question falls between two numbers; the two from Wheat Plain were still in custody in Year 20.

Of interfaces with the tradition, this paper has made only two, in place, both with Vattel: the liberty and independence of nations, which lands on a covenant's requiring the consent of all parties and parts from this series on questioning existing matters; and no state's having a right to interfere in the government of another, which parts from this series on "internal affairs" not exempting anything from questioning. These two interfaces with Vattel rest only on English online translations read against each other, and one of them on a single source, so they serve only as in-place comparisons. Kant's publicity has already been interfaced in Theory of the State Paper 5, §4, and this paper points back to it.

The Seven Propositions of This Paper

XZ9 The system's position is that of an external questioner. The system's determination of line-crossing is public questions and records, not authorization; it constitutes no ground for any state to start a war, nor does it endorse any state's sending of forces; the system neither authorizes nor vetoes. "Last" refers to level, not to exemption from questioning; that there is no one overhead shows only where the questions must come from, and does not show that the questioning has stopped (the Theory of the State Prequel, §3). The war-party has no privilege of not being questioned; third parties may challenge the war-party's decisions without being treated as enemies (War Treatise §16). With the system as external questioner, a state that sends forces cannot close this question (the War Treatise originally speaks of the war-party and third parties; here it is extended to the member that sends forces and the system; own undertaking).

XZ10 Questioning does not need the consent of the party questioned; the party questioned recuses itself (own undertaking). A covenant requires the consent of all parties (XZ1); questioning existing matters does not need the consent of the party questioned: a question governs acts already done, and the party questioned cannot close it (Theory of the State Paper 1, §5; Theory of Government G36). Only when the two are kept apart can a common failure be told: the party questioned holding the power to shut the door on the question directed at itself. Requiring everyone's agreement lets any one party block everything: that making covenants needs consent is right; that questioning existing matters should also need consent is wrong. So when a common procedure questions a member, the member recuses itself on the matter directed at it: it does not vote, and does not hold the power to close the case; the form is not fixed. Of Conflict Treatise §15, only the half in which the party questioned does not hold the power to shut the door is taken: one cannot be one's own final adjudicator and declare in advance that some class of questioner is permanently without standing (originally said in the first person; here extended to relations between states; own undertaking). The system's standing to put questions rests on other grounds: external questioning cannot be cancelled (XZ11); and procedures the parties have acknowledged, as Conflict Treatise §27 says (originally said of two parties; here extended to many; own undertaking). Recusal does not mean that everyone can veto: control does not mean that every person affected has a veto (Theory of Government G36a). Questioning does not need the consent of the party questioned, and neither does the questioner decide alone that the case is closed: the step of closing the case needs a position that the questioner does not monopolize, or else the matter is registered plainly as unsettled; no position may monopolize the power to shut the door, the questioning party included (Theory of Government G36; own undertaking).

XZ11 External questioning cannot be cancelled (carried over from Theory of the State Paper 1, §5, its own undertaking taken up as it stands). A state may exit or switch an external arrangement; that is a change of structure, itself under questioning; it can close one channel, but it cannot cancel the questioning of it by other states, by the people of other states, and by those affected (Theory of the State Paper 1, §5). Extended to the system (own undertaking): when a member exits an arrangement, the questioning of it by the system and by the other members goes on as before (Paper 7, XZ45, XZ47); the system's own arrangements are under this sentence too (XZ14, XZ15). Closing a channel is kept apart from moving against a questioner: not joining, not cooperating, and exiting an arrangement are closing a channel, and are allowed; pressing, punishing or retaliating against a questioner because the questioner has raised questions is trying to cancel the questioning, and crosses the line (as in the first place in Paper 4, XZ25).

XZ12 The system's questions connect into member states; the obligation to answer comes from the Compact (carried over from Theory of the State N38; own undertaking). The system does not enforce, and whether its questions land rests with each member: a peer's question does not on its own count as a channel; it counts only once it connects to the channels inside (Theory of the State Paper 7, §3). The Theory of the State allows a state not to answer ordinary questions from peers, and the obligation to answer must come from covenants acknowledged: a state may decline to answer ordinary diplomatic questions between peers that carry no separate obligation to answer; the requirements to answer and to implement that it bears under the treaties and common procedures it has acknowledged still follow the covenants acknowledged (ibid.). So a member's obligation to answer to the system, including a state that sends forces explaining its own first face publicly to the system, is written into the Compact and comes into being through the consent of all parties; it is not derived from "the system is an external questioner" alone; toward those whom the sending of forces reaches, the sending state answers following the unified sentence (state level, the Theory of the State Prequel, §8; see §6); the part of the coordination and preparation made in the system's name that this sending of forces then used (joint plans, joint exercises, prepositioned depots) still answers to those it reaches following the unified sentence (system level, §6). When a member answers with "internal affairs," not interfering is not the same as not asking (Theory of the State Paper 5, §2); a question is answered according to its object, an exchange is checked according to its own reasons (Theory of the State Paper 5, §4).

XZ13 Treaty bodies admitting individual petitions as channels (carried over from Theory of the State N27; own undertaking). Treaty mechanisms admitting individual petitions come close to a channel: internal subjects can initiate them themselves, and the treaty also prohibits retaliation; such a mechanism has no enforcer of its own, and whether its decisions land still rests with the state (Theory of the State Paper 5, §4). What it fills is the place where arbitration between two states is beyond the reach of individuals (Theory of the State Paper 2, §5). The design sets up functions only: following the three conditions of a channel, it is independent of the party questioned, internal subjects can actually initiate it, and initiating it brings no retaliation (Institution Theory Z34); it does not depend on the willingness of an intermediary, since a route that gets through only if others are willing cannot yet certify that it can actually be initiated (Theory of the State Paper 7, §3); for each function it can be stated who can activate it, what actual efficacy it has, and how the party questioned cannot cancel it on its own (Theory of Government G36a). The members' undertaking follows Theory of the State N27: not to obstruct, not to retaliate, and to implement the decisions of treaty bodies under the covenants acknowledged (whether rulings or opinions, as the covenant has it). How many tiers, and whether a court or a committee, is handed to specific theories of institutions.

XZ14 The system's own fifth ought (own undertaking). The system's acts are: lists entered under a common procedure, notices and lists the system issues, the part of the coordination and preparation of joint defense made in the system's name, the allocation of admissions, and the decisions of treaty bodies; sending forces and joint action themselves are acts of each participating state (XZ30, XZ32). The unified sentence of the Theory of the State (the Theory of the State Prequel, §8) is extended to the system level, the whole sentence extended, the subject term changed, and how far the system's means at that place go stated: those whom the system's acts reach and who have no way to avoid them by exiting are, as regards those acts, in the system's interior; the five oughts apply each on its own conditions: the first ought (the system's acts cross no line), the fifth (a road within reach by which the system itself can be questioned) and the "not sealing the channels" half of the fourth do not vary with relational thickness; the trigger of the second does not vary with it, and its means are bounded by the system's authority at that place, going only as far as questioning, recording, withdrawing cooperation and asking the member where it lands to guard the line; the third ought, and the "leaving room" half of the fourth, go by relational thickness: where those reached are in member states, the conditions for cultivation lie within those states; where those reached are outside the system, the system only creates conditions for cultivation. This confers no membership. The accounts of the system and of the member states that implement its acts are each checked by their own source and are not transferred from one to the other (Theory of Government G25; own undertaking). Hence a mechanism of the kind that admits individual petitions is not merely a better design but the system's own fifth ought; Theory of the State N29 says that a state's share in a common procedure includes advocating that a road be left for those affected, and at the level of the system this is the common procedure's own share. Whether the system is a construct in the sense of Institution Theory Z4 is undertaken by this series. The notices and lists the system issues fall on people, and people must have a road by which to ask; where a state borrows them to demand that people be sent back, the system does not help (not pushing back at the door holds as before; Paper 6, XZ38).

XZ15 The system's arrangements can be chiseled (carried over from Institution Theory Z37; own undertaking). An institution premised on non dubito must have a chiseling channel satisfying three structural conditions: independent of what is chiseled; actually initiable by members; initiation not met with retaliation (F6 §3.2, Proposition IV). Extended to the system level: the channel for chiseling the system's arrangements is independent of the place being questioned, members and those whom the system's acts reach can actually initiate it, and initiating it brings no retaliation. The system does not exempt itself from questioning by claiming "15DD predominance": it may not close off the conditions of questioning and correction on the grounds that it claims to cultivate, that it has already reformed, or that its aims are right; structures can change, but the three conditions cannot be withdrawn (Institution Theory Z37). In actual operation these mechanisms may be no more than rubber stamps, so they are checked against the four points of War Treatise §16: questioners exist, questioning reaches the decision core, questioners are protected, and questioning actually affects decisions (originally said of the war-party; here extended to the system itself; own undertaking). Capture is judged by structure, not by the name of a body or where it sits (Theory of Government G38). The decisions of treaty bodies do not take the highest effect, and are read by "decisions can be final; positions cannot be final" (Theory of Government G36).

摘要

一群头上没有上级的国家之间，问从哪里来，谁关不上它；体系自己作出施为时，又有谁能问它。这一篇以三界协作体系（the Three-Borders Cooperation System）为例子，答这两半。体系的位置是外部追问者：它的认定是公开的问与记录，不授权，不否决，出兵的成员也关不上它的问。立约要各方同意，问已有的事不要；被问的一方在指向自己的那一件上回避，问的一方也不独定结案。关上一条通道，取消不了问。体系不强制执行，问落不落地在各成员；成员作答的义务从它已认的约来，"内政"不挡问。许个人申诉的条约机构，补的是个人够不着国与国之间程序的那一处，照通道的三项条件核。国家论的统一句在这里第一次推到体系一级，全文见第六节：体系施为所及、避不开的人，就这些施为而言在体系的内部，不论在不在成员境内；给他们一条到得了体系本身的路，是体系自己的第五条；体系与成员的账各按来源核。体系的安排可被修凿，三项条件不撤，照战争论 §16 的四条核；决定可以终局，位置不能终局。全篇以四件事贯穿：体系就 DRP 一道剥夺籍的法令向它发问；枢镇死者的家属与麦原的农户向申诉委员会申诉 UCSE；灰河两岸的商家与村民就一项共同措施申诉；东坪营地的人就一项分担决定申诉成员会议本身。随文与瓦特尔《万国法》对接两处，各民族的自由与独立，别国无权干预一国的统治；康德的公开性指向国家论已有的对接。全篇七条（XZ9 至 XZ15），各节末写出，篇末合列。

第 14 年夏，NUT 请求依这一项向 DRP 发问，UCSE 附议。成员会议以 2 比 0 开启发问，DRP 不参加表决。秘书长持 DRP 籍，回避；分管共同程序的副秘书长代行文书。这位副秘书长持 NUT 籍。DRP 常驻代表提出，NUT 是提请的一方，副秘书长也应回避；秘书处书面答复："第 8 条的回避，指本人国籍国是被问的一方。"DRP 的异议载入记录。

第 14 年秋，DRP 外务部书面答复："籍的取得与丧失是共和国的内政。法令发布于第 9 年秋，早于宪约；宪约第 4 条第（三）项不溯及既往。"答复另附一段，转过来说 UCSE："UCSE 第 9 年冬撤销了一位住在 NUT 的评论者的籍，使她不持任何国籍；UCSE 的《成员籍法》第 14 条至今要求无国籍儿童的父母合法居留满五年。UCSE 自己也没有给这些孩子籍。"答复没有给出人数。

第 14 年冬，成员会议以 2 比 0 通过记录，DRP 不参加表决。记录载了法令全文；载了 NUT 提供的数字：从 DRP 出走、在 NUT 的约四万人中，依法令丧失 DRP 籍的约一万一千人，在 NUT 出生、没有被登记的孩子约九百人；载了 UCSE 提供的数字：柳渡接收中心到第 14 年出生的孩子约四百二十人；还载了 DRP 的答复全文，连同附的那一段。记录在秘书处的网站上公开。第 15 年，NUT 一家报纸、UCSE 两家报纸、DRP 首都一家报纸，各刊出了记录的全文或摘要。

此后有两件事。第 15 年夏，UCSE 民众院以 171 票对 120 票修改《成员籍法》第 14 条，删去父母合法居留满五年的条件；到第 17 年，柳渡接收中心出生的孩子中有三百八十九人取得 UCSE 籍。第 15 年秋，DRP 外务部公布新的登记办法：在境外出生的子女，父母一方亲自到 DRP 在 NUT 或 UCSE 的领事处申请、并"补办动员期间的出境手续"的，可以登记；父母本人的籍不恢复。到第 16 年底，依这一办法登记的孩子三十七人。

这件事落在谁身上，要先说清。它落在依那道法令丧失 DRP 籍的人身上，在 NUT 的约一万一千人；落在他们在境外出生、没有被 DRP 登记的孩子身上，在 NUT 的约九百人，在柳渡的约四百二十人。这些人是要被保障的人。DRP 是被问的成员，是这一笔账记在它名下的地址；作答的是 DRP 外务部这个位置。柳渡的孩子同时在 UCSE 的一项施为之下：《成员籍法》第 14 条决定他们能不能取得 UCSE 籍。所以 DRP 那一段反问，也落在同一批孩子身上（第四节）。

要看的是哪一条线，分两侧说。在 DRP 一侧，是它批准宪约时认下的第 4 条第（三）项。在体系一侧，是体系自己做到哪里为止：它只问，只记，不替 DRP 定籍法，不强制执行；落地在各成员。

发问以前，法令早已施行，失籍的人与没有登记的孩子都已经在那里；UCSE 的《成员籍法》第 14 条仍要父母合法居留满五年。程序上，宪约第 7 条（乙）规定，对已有的事发问、开启程序、通过记录，以未涉事成员的过半数，被问的成员不参加表决，但有权在每一阶段陈述；第 8 条规定秘书长与副秘书长在涉及本人国籍国的事项上回避；第 9 条规定一切发问、答复与记录公开。

这件事里，三国头上没有谁。宪约第 2 条第 2 款写着："本宪约不设凌驾于成员之上的机关。体系的任何机关不得对成员、对成员境内的人执行强力。"成员会议不能命令 DRP 改法，秘书处不能派人去给孩子登记。可这件事也不是一片空白：有人问了，有人答了，答了什么、没答什么，都记了下来，摆在三国的报纸上。后来两国各改了一样东西。

这一篇问的就是这样的问。头上没有上级，问从哪里来，谁关不上它；体系凭什么问一个成员，凭什么问体系外的一国；问怎样落进成员国的内部。反过来，体系自己也作出施为，落在人身上：它施为所及的人有没有一条到得了它的路，它的安排能不能被修凿。这个系列的主线句，末一截说的是，它向成员发问，它的每一项安排也仍可被追问（前置篇第五节）。这一篇把这一截的两半都写出来。

全篇分八步。第一节写体系的位置；第二节写立约与问分开，被问的一方回避；第三节写关上一条通道取消不了问；第四节写问怎样落进成员国的内部；第五节写许个人申诉的条约机构；第六节写体系施为所及的人；第七节写体系自己被问；第八节写体系的安排可被修凿。前四节写体系问别人，后四节写人问成员、问体系。与传统的对接随文，放在判断落下的地方。

第一节　问从外面来

成员会议不能下命令。那它在 DRP 那件事里做了什么，凭什么做？

国家论读"最后"一词时说：「"最后"指层级：上面没有了。它不指免问。」又说：「头上没有人，只说明问要从哪里来，不说明问已经停了。」（国家论前置篇第三节）一国头上没有更高一级，问它的人在它里面，也在它旁边：它自己的人经它自己的通道问它，平级的别国从外面问它。许多国家放在一起，头上同样没有更高一级，问就只能从外面来，从平级的一群来。体系站在这一群之间：它是成员们一起发问、一起作记录的地方。它的位置，是外部追问者。

这一位置先要说清它不是什么。体系对越线的认定，是公开的问与记录，不是授权：它不构成任何一国开战的理由，也不替哪一国的出兵背书。体系不授权，也不否决。宪约第 18 条把这一层写进了条文：遇以武力进入成员或别国的事，成员会议可以作出记录，"有一项殖民性的进入"；第 2 款接着说："此项记录是公开的问与记录，不构成授权，不构成任何成员出动的义务。"

DRP 那份记录正是这样一份东西。它载了法令全文，载了 NUT 与 UCSE 各自的数字，载了 DRP 的答复与反问。没有一句要 DRP 改法，也没有一句宣布 DRP 违约。改不改，落在 DRP 自己的位置上；改了以后怎样在 DRP 内部受问，在第四节写。

问也及于出兵的一方。石峡邦（the Stonegorge Commonwealth）第 14 年秋加入体系，镜海通外洋的唯一一条海路是石峡水道，两岸都属石峡邦。第 17 年春，高原联邦（the Plateau Federation）的舰艇与部队进入了石峡邦的领海与水道中的灯岛，成员会议依第 18 条第 1 款作了记录。UCSE 出兵以后，依同条第 3 款向成员会议交了书面说明，列了三条根据，其中一条是 UCSE 的两艘货轮与三十一名船员在石峡邦领海内被扣。DRP 常驻代表就这一条说："货轮被扣，不等于 UCSE 受到攻击。"两样都载入记录。那几条根据各是哪一种根据、够不够由谁认定，在第五篇第四节写；这里只取一件：出兵的一方也在问之下。战争论 §16 的第四条定理说的就是这一层：「战争方没有不被追问的特权」；它又问第三方，「国际机构、中立的观察者、人道组织、学者、记者，是否可以质疑战争方的决策，而不被当作敌人？」（战争论 §16）这条定理原说战争方与它的第三方，这里把第三方换作体系，把战争方换作出兵的成员（自担）。体系问出兵的成员，不是敌意；出兵的成员关不上这一问。

反过来，一项安排若事实上取得了对成员的强力，它就不再是这里说的体系。头上有了带强力的上级，那是政府论的一级，照那一级作答（政府论第四篇 G26；第七篇 XZ46）。

有人会说，只能问、不能做的体系是空的；与其看着，不如许它授权动武，至少在殖民性的进入面前。这一说的分量在代价上：问挡不住炮。可是授权一出，体系就有了强制力。强力，是用自己的判断力替代对方就它自己的事的判断。授权一出，体系的判断就成了各成员开战的理由；可开战照战争论，须出兵一方自己的第一面成立（战争论 §13），开不开战，本是出兵一方就它自己的事的判断，授权把它移到了体系的票上。授权若指向一国境内的事，替代的还有那一国就它自己的事的判断：它境内的事怎样办，改由体系判定可以用武力去改。有了这一层，体系就是一个上级，不再是彼此之间的协作。所以本系列的体系不授权。

问也不空。它留下一笔外面看得见的账：谁问了，谁答了，答了什么，没答什么（第三篇）。平级者可以照这笔账，各自收回配合（第四篇）。问与记本身，也在战争论所说开战以前须试尽的那一段之内：「交的层面（外交、通道、第三方斡旋、国际机构的程序、非军事的施压、协议的缔结与修复）」（战争论 §13）。体系的问与记，是可用时应当纳入这一检验的非战路径；是否已经试尽，按当时实际可用的途径与紧迫的条件核，不把机械地走完每一项共同程序当作另一道统一的门槛。

还有人会说，不问才是中立：体系少说话，才不被卷进去。可是停问也是一项施为。权力论讲网络里的旁观者时说：「在网络拓扑层, 沉默 (有本体登记, 无操作反向向量) 是零阻力传导节点, 客观放大权力梯度。」（权力论第三篇 §V.4）原说网络里的一个节点，这里推到体系（自担）：体系在一项越线面前不问，它的沉默替越线的一方省了一份力。反过来，体系的观察同样是施为。国家论说，在对方土地上设观察点，要先说明权源、进入的方式、能做什么与可撤的安排，再核（国家论第六篇 N34）。体系派人去看，也照此先说明、再核，这是从一国推到体系的一步（自担）。

体系一级还缺一样东西，要明说。道德律第八篇写一个共同体里的默认通道，给拒绝的一方留了一条兜底的路：「如果一方拒绝……将其移交14DD法庭处理。」（道德律第八篇 §3.4.2）那是一个带强制的法庭。到了国与国之间，头上没有这一层。体系一级能兜底的，只有各国自己的法、各国自己的第一面，加上牵制（自担）。这是外部追问者这一位置的条件，写在这里。

代价也照实写。体系的位置是外部追问者，代价就是看着问题发生，不能直接以武力解决，只能用间接的手段。DRP 那件事里，记录通过了，报纸刊出了，两国各改了一样东西；DRP 的新办法写明，失籍的父母本人，籍不恢复。这一笔照实记下，不另找强制的路去补。

这里分三层说话（三层的说法见前置篇第十一节）。体系是外部追问者，它的认定是问与记录，不授权、不否决，这是规范层。宪约第 18 条的写法、第 7 条（乙）的票数，是规则层的例子。一份记录用什么措辞，某一次进入记作"殖民性的进入"还是别的，由成员会议里投票的位置判断，账落在投票的成员身上，这是判断层。灯岛那一次，DRP 主张记作"武装进入"，弃了权，它的话也载入了记录。

体系的问、答与记录公开（宪约第 9 条）。公开能做到哪里，国家论第五篇第四节已拿康德《永久和平论》附录二的公开性公式对过：没有共同的裁判者时，怎样认出一项对外的做法不对。那里的结论是，公开性可以作一处痕迹，不作判决。这里指过去，不重做。DRP 那份记录被三国的报纸刊出，就是一处这样的痕迹：它让人看见，不替谁判。

XZ9　体系的位置是外部追问者。体系对越线的认定是公开的问与记录，不是授权，不构成任何一国开战的理由，也不替哪一国的出兵背书；体系不授权，不否决。"最后"指层级，不指免问；头上没有人，只说明问要从哪里来，不说明问已经停了（国家论前置篇第三节）。战争方没有不被追问的特权；第三方可以质疑战争方的决策，而不被当作敌人（战争论 §16）。体系作外部追问者，出兵的一国关不上这一问（战争论原说战争方与第三方，这里推到出兵的成员与体系，自担）。

第二节　立约要同意，问已有的事不要

立一项新的约束，要每一方同意；问一件已经发生的事，却不必等被问的一方点头。两样容易混在一起。混了，体系要么什么都问不了，要么能借问的名义给人立规矩。

宪约第 7 条把两样分开写了。（甲）项：修订宪约，增设共同程序，接纳新成员，以体系名义订立新的协定，这些新的约束，须全体成员书面同意。（乙）项：对已有的事发问、开启程序、通过记录，以未涉事成员的过半数；被问的成员不参加该事项的表决，但有权在每一阶段陈述，并要求把它的陈述载入记录。

峡水那件事把两样摆在了一处。峡水发源于石峡邦，向西南流入 NUT 北区，在高峡水库以上汇入云河；石峡邦在峡水上有一座电站，由高原联邦的一家银行贷款修建，贷款合同第 22 条不许未经贷款方书面同意向第三方提供电站的运行数据。第 15 年春，秘书处起草的《云河流域议定书》开签署会，UCSE、NUT 签了，石峡邦没有签；照第 7 条（甲），三方的议定书没有成立。同年夏，峡水电站一处闸门故障，紧急泄洪；石峡邦在泄洪开始后四十分钟才通知 NUT。两小时内，NUT 一侧的峡口站测到流量升到平时的九倍，高峡水库溢洪，NUT 北区两个村被淹，一人死亡。NUT 请求依宪约第 5 条向石峡邦发问。第 5 条是石峡邦第 14 年秋加入时已经认下的：一成员的施为可能影响别的成员境内的人的，事先通报；来不及事先的，事后即刻通报。成员会议以 3 比 0 开启发问，石峡邦不参加表决。石峡邦答复：闸门故障是意外；事先通报"来不及"；贷款合同第 22 条只管运行数据，不管事后通报。记录公开。同一条河上，新的约束要它同意，已有义务上的问不要。

这件事在第一篇第三节从约的一侧读过。这里看问的一侧：问落在下游被淹的两个村的人身上；要看的是石峡邦已经认下的第 5 条这条线；在此之前，石峡邦认了第 5 条，没有认那份议定书。

两样分得开，是因为它们管的东西不同。约管的是以后：它给各方加一项原来没有的约束，没有哪一方的同意，就没有那一方的约束（第一篇第一节，XZ1）。问管的是已有的施为：泄洪已经发生，通报的义务早已认下，问的是一件已经在那里的事。被问的一方可以答，可以不答，可以答得不够；它关不上这一问（国家论第一篇第五节；政府论 G36）。

分开以后，就看得出一种常见的失灵：被问的一方在指向自己的那一问上，握着关门之权。全体一致的规则，让任何一方都能挡住一切。立约要全体同意，是对的；问已有的事也要全体同意，被问的一方一票就能把问关掉，问就成了它自己决定要不要受的东西。

所以共同程序问到一个成员时，它在指向自己的那一件上回避：不投票，不握结案之权。约与问这样分开、被问的一方这样回避，上游没有现成的一句，是本系列自己担的一步（自担）。DRP 那件事里，DRP 不参加表决，持 DRP 籍的秘书长回避；峡水一事，石峡邦不参加表决。回避的形态可以不同，宪约第 7 条（乙）与第 8 条只是一种写法；要守住的是，在那一件事上，被问的一方手里没有关门的那一票。

冲突论有一句可以在这里用，但只能用一半。它在讲谁有资格追问一个人时说：「我不能以自己为最终裁判，预先宣布某一类追问者永远没有资格。这一层我不能豁免。一旦我取得了这个权限，我就成了自己的终审。」（冲突论 §15）原说第一人称的人，这里推到被问的成员国（自担）：一国若能在共同程序里否决指向自己的问，它就成了自己的终审。同一节还有另一半：「受问是我受问。 这一条不给我追问对方的资格。」（同上）这一半给不出体系发问的资格。体系能问，根据另有两处。一处是外部追问不可被取消（第三节，XZ11）。一处是冲突论 §27 划出的那一类外部：「但这只对某一类外部成立：双方已经承认的程序，依法有权的机制，或者为保护基础权利而有正当权限的制度。」（冲突论 §27）DRP 与石峡邦都批准了宪约，第 7 条（乙）是它们已经承认的程序。这一句原说两方，这里推到多方（自担）。

回避管住了被问的一方，还要管住问的一方。问不须被问者同意；可问的一方也不能自己宣布问完了、答得不够、这件事就此记作违约。结案的那一步，要有一个不由问者独占的位置；没有这样的位置，就照实记作未结。政府论说：「决定可以终局：具体事项可以答完、结案。位置不能终局：没有一个位置可以独占关门之权」（政府论第六篇 G36）。制度论在被审的一方那里说过同一件事：「追问须独立，理由在被审的一方不能独占对质疑是否存在、是否结束的决定」（制度论第六篇 Z35a）。原说被审的一方，这里推到发问的一方（自担）：问的一方同样不能独占问是否已经结束。

DRP 的记录照录了"不溯及既往"那一句。这一句成不成立，记录没有替谁判。法令发布在宪约之前；法令造成的无籍，在宪约生效以后仍在持续，孩子仍在出生。这两样怎样合起来读，NUT 可以有它的读法，DRP 有它的读法；成员会议以 2 比 0 通过的是一份记录，不是一份判决。冲突论说：「线索不是判准，痕迹也不是判决」（冲突论 §26）。照本篇的读法，这一问在那里是未结的。

有人会说，各国自由独立，问它也该先得它同意。这一说有很长的来历。瓦特尔在《万国法》的导论里说，「每一个民族都应当得以安然享有它得自自然的那份自由」（each nation should be left in the peaceable enjoyment of that liberty which she inherits from nature；Emer de Vattel，《万国法》导论第 15 节，据 J. Chitty 编注英译 1883 年费城本，经两处网上本对读，B 档）。紧接的一节，据一处网上本，他说每一民族独自判断它的良知要它做什么，别国不能强迫它照某种方式行事；只有对方握着完全的权利时，才可以强迫（导论第 16 节，C 档，只转述）。再下一节，他分开完全的权利与不完全的权利：前者伴有强迫之权，后者只许请求（导论第 17 节，同据，C 档，只转述）。导论第 15 至 17 节所据都是英文的网上转录本；法文原本与 1883 年印本的页面没有亲核，转录本上的核对不当作原刊页的核对。

共同的问题是：一国在多大的范围里不受别国左右。这里同题，同的地方在立约。别国不能强迫一国照某种方式行事，在本系列落在约须各方同意上（XZ1）：石峡邦不签议定书，议定书就对它不成立，没有谁替它签。到了问，先要分开三件：问，请求履行，强制。瓦特尔第 16 节讲的是什么时候不能强迫一国，并没有把外部的问一并取消；第 17 节又把不伴强迫的请求之权另列。本系列因此不凭这两节，把"问不须被问者同意"说成他没有走的一步，也不说他只许一国自己问自己。本系列多出来的，是一项展开：在各方已认的共同程序里，把谁能发动、被问的一方怎样回避、答复的依据从哪里来、结果怎样接进内部，逐项写出（本节；第三、四节）。问不是新的约束，不要它做什么；它要作答，另有它已认的约（第四节，XZ12）。回避也不等于每一方都能否决：政府论说，「控制不等于每个受影响者都有否决权」（政府论第六篇 G36a）。被问也不是被指控：「"可被追问"指通道常开，对一切政府、一切时候，受问不是不正当的证据」（政府论第六篇 G35）。所以这里是范围的分工，可以与他对强迫之权的划分对读：瓦特尔守的是一国不被强迫去做什么，本篇守的是一国关不上指向它已做之事的问。瓦特尔这几节只取到英文网上本的对读与单一来源，这里只作随文对照，不作驳倒。

回到引言里那一处异议。DRP 要持 NUT 籍的副秘书长也回避。本系列在这里给的规范只有两句：被问的一方回避，问者不独定结案。宪约第 7 条（乙）与第 8 条把回避写到被问一方的籍为止，这只是一种写法，是例子。代行文书算不算握着结案之权的一部分，提请一方的人能不能代行，要由具体的规则去定；到了一件事上，由位置上的人判断。那一次秘书处判了，DRP 的异议照留在记录里，此后还能再问。

这里留下一处：答到哪里，才算答到了被问的那件事。石峡邦答了泄洪那一问，运行数据仍不提供；DRP 答了"内政"与"不溯及既往"，没有给人数。答够没有，不由问者独定；没有一个不由问者独占的位置来认定的，照实记作未结。

XZ10　问不须被问者同意；被问的一方回避（自担）。约须各方同意（XZ1）；问已有的事，不须被问的一方同意：问管的是已有的施为，被问的一方关不上（国家论第一篇第五节；政府论 G36）。两样分开，才分得出一种常见的失灵：被问的一方在指向自己的那一问上握着关门之权。全体一致使任何一方都能挡住一切：立约要同意是对的；问已有的事也要同意，就错了。所以共同程序问到一个成员时，它在指向自己的那一件上回避：不投票，不握结案之权；形态不定。冲突论 §15 只取被问者不握关门之权这一半：不能以自己为最终裁判，预先宣布某一类追问者永远没有资格（原说第一人称，这里推到国与国，自担）。体系发问的资格另有根据：外部追问不可被取消（XZ11），与冲突论 §27 所说各方已经承认的程序（原说两方，这里推到多方，自担）。回避不等于人人可否决：控制不等于每个受影响者都有否决权（政府论 G36a）。问不须被问者同意，问者也不独定结案：结案的那一步，要有一个不由问者独占的位置，或照实登为未结；没有一个位置可以独占关门之权，问的一方也不例外（政府论 G36，自担）。

第三节　关上一条通道，取消不了问

被问的一方不参加表决，问照样开启。它还能不能走开：退出那一项安排，让问落空？

国家论在一国一级立过一句：「这里只立一句（自担）：外部追问不可被取消。本国可以退出或改接一项外部安排，那是构造的改动，本身在追问之下（第五篇 N24）；它关得上一条通道，取消不了别国、别国的人与受影响的人对它的追问」（国家论第一篇第五节）。本篇照接这一句。

推到体系，有两面（自担）。一面是成员：一个成员退出体系的某一项安排，体系与别的成员对它的问照旧，它退出以前的施为照样可以问（第七篇 XZ45、XZ47）。另一面是体系自己：体系的安排同样在这一句之下，它关得上某一条路，取消不了它施为所及的人对它的问（第六至八节，XZ14、XZ15）。

DRP 后来正碰到这件事。第 9 年冬到第 11 年春，UCSE 在麦原的缓冲带设过一个管理处，管理处挑选了十一位当地居民，组成临时自治委员会。撤军时，九人随 UCSE 的部队离开；留下的二人被 DRP 以"协敌"起诉，第 13 年冬分别被判十二年与九年，审理不公开，辩护人由法院指定。宪约第 14 条设了一个许个人申诉的委员会（第五节详写），DRP 人民议会第 13 年秋另行表决，以 318 票对 247 票接受了这一条。第 16 年春，二人经律师向委员会申诉 DRP。第 17 年秋，委员会请 DRP 提交审理记录，并许一位委员到监所探视二人。第 18 年春，DRP 外务部经 DRP 主席批准，依第 14 条第 5 款声明退出第 14 条。声明书写："第 14 条被用来干涉共和国的司法。共和国不再就这件申诉作答。"依第 5 款，声明满十二个月生效，生效以前已提出的申诉照常审结。第 18 年夏，成员会议以 3 比 0 记录了这一声明，DRP 不参加表决。第 18 年冬，委员会就二人一件作出意见，请 DRP 公开复审，并许探视；DRP 在六个月内没有答复，第 19 年夏，成员会议记录了没有答复这一件事。第 19 年春以后，委员会把新收到的七件对 DRP 的申诉退回，理由是生效以后提出的，不受理。

这件事怎样判，在第七篇第一、三节。这里只取它摆出来的结构。退出关上的，是一条通道此后的路：第 19 年春以后的七件，进不来了。已经开始的问，照样落进记录：二人那一件照常审结，意见照常作出。不答，本身也是记录里的一件事。DRP 能让新的申诉不被受理，不能让第 18 年春已经在问的那一件消失。

两样要分开（自担）。不加入一项安排，不合作，退出一项安排，是关上一条通道；这是许的。因为对方提出了追问，就对问者施压、处罚或报复，是想取消问；这越过了线（照第四篇第五节 XZ25 所说的第一处）。前一样改的是自己与问之间的构造，后一样要的是问不再被提出。本篇用到的几件事里，没有对问者下手的事实；这一样在这里只立原则，例子在第四篇第六节：《反分化法》下对评论体系安排的人的起诉。

被问的一方关不上的，还有两样在外面。国家论说：「它关不上的，是平级者据此自行判断、收回承认之外的配合（N26），以及这些问经本国的内部主体进入内部的通道（N37）」（国家论第七篇第一节）。DRP 那件事里，第二样有一处痕迹。接受第 14 条是人民议会表决的，退出却由主席批准；六十一位议员联名要求人民议会就退出表决，主席团没有排入议程。这一要求本身，就是那一问在 DRP 内部的一次出现；它落到了哪里，在第七篇。

有人会说，既然许退出，"不可取消"就是空话：一国想不受问，退出就是了。这一说的分量在于，退出确实关上了一条路，七件新申诉都被退回了。可取消不了的是问，不是某一条路。退出的一方在它自己的内部照旧受问：「退出不替追问。」（政府论第四篇 G27）它对外不答，那一不答也在它的内部受问：「答与不答本身，都在本国可被追问，在内部的通道里受问（自担）」（国家论第七篇第三节）。平级者也照样可以照记录各自行事。路少了一条，问还有别的地方落。

这里留下一处：问取消不了，答可以不来。不来的那一问留在记录里，怎样记、怎样读，交第三篇。麦原那二人到第 20 年仍在关押，其中一人移到了监所医院；他们的问此后经什么路，在第七篇答。

XZ11　外部追问不可被取消（承国家论第一篇第五节，自担照接）。一国可以退出或改接一项外部安排，那是构造的改动，本身在追问之下；它关得上一条通道，取消不了别国、别国的人与受影响的人对它的追问（国家论第一篇第五节）。推到体系（自担）：成员退出一项安排，体系与别的成员对它的问照旧（第七篇 XZ45、XZ47）；体系自己的安排同样在这一句之下（XZ14、XZ15）。关上一条通道与对问者下手分开：不加入、不合作、退出一项安排，是关上一条通道，许；因其提出追问而对问者施压、处罚或报复，是想取消问，越过（照第四篇 XZ25 的第一处）。

第四节　问落进成员国内部

记录通过以后，UCSE 改了自己的法，DRP 改了登记办法。这两件事与那份记录是什么关系？

记录没有说，这一点也没有别的记载。UCSE 民众院以 171 票对 120 票修改《成员籍法》第 14 条，是 UCSE 的议员在 UCSE 的程序里投的票；DRP 外务部公布新的登记办法，是 DRP 的一个位置照 DRP 的程序作的决定。两件各由该国自己的位置作出，各在该国内部受问。体系的问落不落地，落在各成员。

这是体系不强制执行的直接结果。国家论在一国一级说过外部路径的这一处：「可它们没有自己的执行者：头上没有更高一级的强力，裁决作出以后，落不落地，取决于本国的位置执行不执行。」（国家论第一篇第五节）体系的问也是这样。它要成为一条通道，还得接上成员国内部的路：「平级者的问单独算不上一条通道，接上 N37 才算。」（国家论第七篇第三节）N37 是国家论第七篇立的对内通道，一国自己的人问这一国的路。

DRP 那件事里，问接进内部的路有几处看得见。DRP 首都的一家报纸刊出了记录。DRP 外务部此后改了登记办法；改得够不够，要在 DRP 内部问：新办法要父母一方亲自到 DRP 在 NUT 或 UCSE 的领事处，要"补办动员期间的出境手续"，父母本人的籍不恢复，到第 16 年底登记的孩子三十七人。这些都可以在 DRP 内部的通道里问，也可以由体系再问。国家论还有一句两扇门的话：「平级者问到的事若落在本国内部的人身上，对外的不答，就不能替对内的答：那是两扇门。」（同上）失籍的人在 DRP 境外；可照国家论的统一句，就那道法令而言，他们避不开它，仍在 DRP 的内部。DRP 对体系答一句"内政"，不替它对这些人与他们的孩子作答。

DRP 为什么要答？体系是外部追问者，这一点给了体系问的位置，不直接给出被问者作答的义务。国家论许一国不答一般的平级发问：「对没有另行作答义务的一般平级外交发问，本国可以不答；已认条约与共同程序所负的作答、落实要求，仍照 N24、N27」（国家论第七篇第三节）。作答的义务要从已认的约来。DRP 的义务，出在它批准宪约时认下的两处：第 4 条第（三）项的承诺，与第 7 条（乙）的程序。第 13 年秋，DRP 人民议会以 389 票对 196 票批准了宪约。约须各方同意（XZ1），作答的义务也就经各方同意而有，不单从"体系是外部追问者"推出。国家论这一句原说一国对平级的别国，这里推到成员对体系（自担）。

出兵的成员向体系说明自己的根据，义务也是这样来的。宪约第 18 条第 3 款要出动的成员在七十二小时内书面说明本国出动的根据；UCSE 第 17 年春交的那份说明，就是依这一款交的（第一节）。这一款把受问从事前挪到了事后，没有撤掉受问。政府论在紧急状态里说过同形的一句：「可以把事前审查与事前受问改到事后，但不撤受问」（政府论第五篇 G30）。原说一级政府在紧急之时，这里推到出兵的成员（自担）。说明给了体系，也不替它说明给本国的人。战争论把对己方隐瞒、切断内部追问的做法叫作诡：「本文所谓"诡"，不是一切战术隐蔽或敌前伪装，而是以压制己方主体性、切断内部追问为代价的战争技术」（战争论 §12）。向外说了、对内不说，算不上答了。对出兵所及的人，出兵国照统一句作答（一国一级，国家论前置篇第八节；见第六节）。以体系名义作出的协调与预备，又被这次出兵用到的那一部分，比如联合计划、联合演练与预置的仓库（第五篇第七节），所及的人仍照统一句（体系一级，第六节）。出兵所及的人照一国一级作答，不免去体系为它自己那一部分所负的；体系那一部分，也不替各国担开战。

对体系外的一国，宪约给不出作答的义务。沙洲共和国（the Republic of the Sandbars）北部的"清查"第 16 年春开始；同年夏，秘书长致函沙洲的白垣政府，询问"教导营"的人数与所在、被烧各村的情形。白垣外交部复函："这是共和国的内政。体系不是共和国的上级，共和国也不是体系的成员。"信与复函依宪约第 9 条公开，存秘书处。这件事在第六篇第一、六节读。这里只立原则。体系外一国没有认过宪约，可以不答。它不答，问不因此取消（XZ11）。问与不答都留在外面，成了一笔看得见的账，这笔账交第三篇。

DRP 的第一句答语是"内政"。这一句挡不挡问？国家论说：「不干涉，也不等于不问。」（国家论第五篇第二节）干涉是替它决定；问只要它说明。体系不强制执行，成员会议也没有替 DRP 写一条籍法。DRP 怎样定籍，仍由 DRP 的法定。冲突论讲把争议交给外部程序时，说过交出去的有多少：「让渡的是行动争议的程序裁决权，不是自己之法的构成权。」（冲突论 §27）原说两个人，这里推到成员与体系（自担）：DRP 认下第 7 条（乙），交出的是就一件已有的事受问、作答的程序，不是定籍的权。

而且这一件"内政"，本来就伸到了外面。那道法令把出境的人变成了别国境内无籍的人，把他们的孩子留在别国的出生登记里，国籍一栏写着"未定"。第 4 条第（三）项管的，正是这种推给别国的效果。

DRP 那一段反问怎样读？它问的是 UCSE：UCSE 撤过一位评论者的籍，UCSE 的籍法没有给柳渡的孩子籍。这是对 UCSE 的另一问，问得着；撤籍那一件，国家论第三篇第三节已经核过（N14）。可它了结不了对 DRP 的那一问。国家论说：「问照它的对象答，交换照它自己的理由核。两件可以同时谈，不能拿一件去了结另一件。」（国家论第五篇第四节）两问落在同一批孩子身上，并不奇怪。国家论说：「到了国与国之间，没有嵌套，只剩交叠：同一个人，可以就不同的施为，在两个国家的内部（推及自担）。」（国家论第五篇第二节）柳渡的孩子就 DRP 的法令在 DRP 的内部，就《成员籍法》第 14 条在 UCSE 的内部。DRP 的账与 UCSE 的账，各按各的施为记。UCSE 此后改法，与那一段反问是什么关系，由 UCSE 自己的位置说；这一点没有记载。

有人会说，籍是最典型的内政；体系就籍发问，就是干涉。这一说同样有来历。瓦特尔说，「没有一个国家有丝毫的权利干预另一国的统治」（no state has the smallest right to interfere in the government of another；《万国法》第二卷第四章第 54 节，同据，B 档）。他又说，一国的君主「若对臣民课以重税，若待他们严苛，那只关这个民族自己的事」（If he loads his subjects with taxes, and if he treats them with severity, the nation alone is concerned in the business；同章第 55 节，同据）。

共同的问题是：一国怎样待它自己的人，别国能做什么。同的地方在不干预。别国无权替一国统治，国家论已立为一条（N25）；本系列的主线句说不进入别国替人做主，也是这一条。分的地方在"只关那一民族"。瓦特尔把一国怎样待它的臣民，说成只关那一民族；本系列不以"内政"免问。体系可以问，可以记，可以在成员之间作出公开的记录；它不以强力进入，也不替那一国定它的法。DRP 这一件还多出一层：法令的效果不止落在 DRP 境内，失籍的人与孩子在 NUT，在 UCSE。即使照瓦特尔的分法，这件事也不只关那一民族。这是范围的分工；瓦特尔这两节只取到网上本的对读，不作驳倒。

DRP 的答复算不算答到了，本篇不替谁定。这一步交 DRP 内部的通道，交外面看得见的账，也不由问者独定（第二节）。这里留下一笔：DRP 没有给出人数。到第 16 年底，依新办法登记的孩子三十七人；其余的孩子，与那约一万一千位失籍的人，照实记作未结。

XZ12　体系的问接进成员国内部；作答义务来自宪约（承国家论 N38；自担）。体系不强制执行，问落不落地在各成员：平级者的问单独算不上一条通道，接上内部的通道才算（国家论第七篇第三节）。国家论许一国不答一般的平级发问，作答的义务要来自已认的约：没有另行作答义务的一般平级外交发问，一国可以不答；已认条约与共同程序所负的作答、落实要求，仍照已认的约（同上）。所以成员对体系的作答义务，包括出兵的一国向体系公开说明它自己的第一面，写进宪约，经各方同意而有，不单从"体系是外部追问者"推出；对出兵所及的人，出兵国照统一句作答（一国一级，国家论前置篇第八节；见第六节）；以体系名义作出的协调与预备、又被这次出兵用到的那一部分（联合计划、联合演练、预置的仓库），所及的人仍照统一句（体系一级，第六节）。成员以"内政"作答时，不干涉也不等于不问（国家论第五篇第二节）；问照它的对象答，交换照它自己的理由核（国家论第五篇第四节）。

第五节　许个人申诉的条约机构

到这里，问都是国家问国家。体系问 DRP，问的是 DRP 对它的人做了什么；那些人自己，在这条路上只是被问到的人。他们能不能自己把问交上去？

国家论走到过这里。云河的仲裁庭裁了 UCSE 与 NUT 之间的水量，国家论在那里写道：「可它审的是两国之间的事；四十三户农户够不着它，三国之上也没有共同的法院。」（国家论第二篇第五节）国与国之间的程序，个人够不着。国家论接着说：「许个人申诉的条约机制接近通道：内部主体自己可以发动，条约也禁止报复；本国的承担是不阻碍、不报复、照本国已认的条约落实判决（N24）；它没有自己的执行者，决定落不落地，仍在本国。」（国家论第五篇第四节）那时三国之间没有这类机制。它作为通道是什么性质、怎样设计，国家论登为余项，交到这里。这样一条路在国与国之间也不是没有立足处。《制度如何可能》在民族国家一例里说过，共享一块大陆，外部的制约也难以独立调用；随即自限：「共享大陆本身，也不证明一切外部制约都不能独立。」（《制度如何可能》§3.1(b)）

宪约第 14 条设了这样一个机构，三界申诉委员会（the Three-Borders Petitions Committee），设在三界镇。这里把它写全，后面几节都要用到。这一条有七款：

  • 首款，组成：委员七人，由成员会议从各成员提名的名单中以四分之三选出，任期六年；委员以个人身份任职，不参加对其国籍国的申诉的审议。
  • 第 2 款：成员的施为触及第 4 条与附件二所列事项（生命，人身，不经审判的拘押，籍，离开与返回，迁徙，言论，财产的征收），受其影响的人用尽该成员国内的救济以后，可以申诉。
  • 第 3 款："依共同程序作出的决定所及的人，也可以申诉。"
  • 第 4 款：委员会作出意见，不是判决；被申诉的成员六个月内书面答复，答复公开。
  • 第 5 款：成员可以声明退出本条，声明满十二个月生效；生效以前已提出的申诉照常审结，生效以后提出的，不受理。
  • 第 6 款：委员会受理本条对该成员生效以后的施为；生效以前的施为、其效果延续到生效以后的，可以受理。
  • 第 7 款："成员不因申诉对申诉人及其亲属采取不利措施。"

委员会第 14 年春组成，七人中两人不持任何成员的籍。各成员接受这一条，是另行决定的。UCSE 第 13 年冬接受，附一项声明："委员会意见的落地，依 UCSE 的法律。"第 14 条对 UCSE 自第 14 年春生效。

第一批交到委员会的申诉，申诉的是 UCSE。

背景在国家论里。第 9 年冬，UCSE 打击 DRP 麦原区的枢镇铁路枢纽，命中车站候车厅；DRP 公布死亡的平民四十一人，UCSE 防卫署公布二十二人；防卫署的目标备忘录写着"客车时刻未核实"。同一时期，防卫军在缓冲带征用麦原区粮仓的存粮，收据上写"战后结算"（国家论第六篇第三节）。第 12 年，枢镇死者家属的书面陈述经三界镇的联络处，转到 UCSE 民众院的战争调查委员会；委员会讨论过收不收，最后收了（国家论第七篇第三节）。第 13 年冬，民众院以 152 票对 140 票通过《战争受害者补偿基金法》，基金只补偿"UCSE 籍成员与在 UCSE 境内合法居留的人"。

第 14 年春，第 14 条对 UCSE 生效。家属与农户先在 UCSE 国内走了一遍。枢镇死者的二十七户家属，第 14 年春向基金管理处申请补偿，被驳回，理由是他们不属基金法所列的人；他们向监察署投诉，监察署答复，投诉人不是 UCSE 籍成员，也不在 UCSE 境内合法居留，不在受理之列。麦原区三百一十一户农户，第 14 年夏向防卫署申请结算收据，防卫署答复"战后结算的办法尚未制定"。

第 14 年冬，两件申诉送到委员会：家属一件，就基金法的范围与目标备忘录的公开；农户一件，就收据没有结算。两件由家属与农户各自委托的河口城律师递交，申诉书载明他们在 UCSE 国内走过的路。递交以后，DRP 外务部致函秘书处："共和国支持其国民的申诉。"

持 UCSE 籍的委员不参加审议，六人审议。第 15 年春，委员会决定受理家属一件中基金法范围与目标备忘录两项，农户一件的全部；理由是第 6 款：收据没有结算，基金法在第 14 条生效以后适用，效果延续到生效以后。决定书另写："在 UCSE 国内，申诉人没有别的可用的救济。"打击本身发生在生效以前，不受理；决定书写："本委员会不认定那一次打击是否合乎战争的法。"

第 15 年冬，委员会作出意见：请 UCSE 说明，基金法把 UCSE 施为所及、身在 UCSE 境外的平民排除在外，依据是什么；请 UCSE 结算粮仓收据；请 UCSE 让家属看到目标备忘录中与其亲属死亡有关的部分。第 16 年夏，UCSE 外事署书面答复："UCSE 尊重委员会的意见；落地依 UCSE 的法律。"同一季，第 15 年秋选出的民众院以 158 票对 139 票修改基金法，把"UCSE 的施为所及的境外平民"列入。防卫署按第 9 年的粮价结算了收据；农户代表说，"按第 9 年的价，等于少给了三成"。防卫署拒绝公开目标备忘录，理由是"涉及情报来源"，向家属提供了一份删节的摘要。第 17 年，二十七户中十九户从基金得到补偿；八户被驳回，基金管理处的理由是"死者是否平民，无法认定"。这八户的亲属，不在防卫署公布的二十二人之列，在 DRP 公布的四十一人之列。

这件事落在二十七户家属与三百一十一户农户身上。他们持 DRP 籍，身在 UCSE 境外；打击、征粮与基金法都是 UCSE 的施为，落在他们身上，他们避不开。要看的是第五条这条线：有没有一条到得了 UCSE 的问的路，独立，实际可以发动，发动不遭报复；以及 UCSE 在它已经接受的第 14 条下承担了什么。在此之前，他们只有一条路：家属的陈述经三界镇的联络处转到战争调查委员会，转不转、收不收，都在别人。国家论说过这样一条路的分量：「只靠这一条须别人愿意才通的路，尚不能认证"实际可以发动"已满足」（国家论第七篇第三节）。基金法又只补偿 UCSE 籍与合法居留的人。

第一步，照通道的三项条件核。制度论写过这三项：「通道满足三项条件：独立于被追问的一方，内部主体实际可以发动，发动不被报复。」（制度论第六篇 Z34）原说一个构与它的内部主体，这里推到条约机构与成员施为所及的人（自担）。

先看独立。委员以个人身份任职，由成员会议以四分之三选出；持 UCSE 籍的委员不参加对 UCSE 的审议，这一件是六人审的。受理与否的那一票，不在被问的一方手里。

再看实际可以发动。申诉人是家属与农户本人，律师受他们委托递交；DRP 外务部的函在递交以后，申诉不靠 DRP 转递，也不靠联络处愿不愿意转。第 2 款要求申诉以前用尽国内的救济。这一要求有它的理由：让被问的一方自己的位置有机会作答在前。可它也可能变成一把锁，若"用尽"由被问的一方说了算。这一件里，基金管理处驳回，监察署不受理，防卫署说办法尚未制定；认定国内没有别的可用救济的，是委员会，不是 UCSE。这一项核得过。

最后是发动不遭报复。第 7 款写着，成员不因申诉对申诉人及其亲属采取不利措施；这一款是规则层的例子。这件事里，申诉人与他们的亲属有没有遭到不利对待，没有记载。所以这一项只核到条文写在那里为止；实际怎样，照实写作没有记载，不写成已经核过。

第二步，照四件写清这条通道。国家论要求，「每一条通道都要写清：谁发动，影响哪一对象，被问的一方能不能关上，失灵时怎样归账」（国家论第七篇第一节）。政府论说得更严：「每一项都要说得出三件：谁能启动；对理由、行动、规则或任职位置有什么实际效力；被问的一方怎样不能独自取消它。说不出的，那一项在那里只是名目。」（政府论第六篇 G36a）照这两处，分四项写：

  • 谁发动：受影响的人自己。
  • 影响哪一对象，有什么实际效力：UCSE 触及附件二事项的施为，这里是财产的征收，与生命；委员会的意见请 UCSE 说明、结算、让家属看到，UCSE 须在六个月内书面答复，答复公开。
  • 被问的一方能不能关上：UCSE 不能独自撤掉这一件申诉。它能做的，只有依第 5 款整条退出；退出要满十二个月才生效，已提出的申诉照常审结（第三节）。
  • 失灵时怎样归账：UCSE 不答复、不落地的，照记在 UCSE 与作出那一决定的位置上，记录公开。

第三步，落地。意见作出以后，落成了什么，是 UCSE 自己的位置做的（第四节，XZ12）。民众院改了基金法；防卫署按第 9 年的粮价结算了收据；目标备忘录只给了删节的摘要。三件各由 UCSE 的一个位置作出，各在 UCSE 内部受问：农户代表那一句"少给了三成"，就是一次这样的问。成员对这条路的承担，照国家论 N27 写：不阻碍，不报复，照已认的约落实。这里要写准一处。国家论那一句说"落实判决"；例子里的委员会只作意见，已认的约要 UCSE 做的，是书面答复与公开。所以本篇写作：照已认的约落实条约机构的决定，是判决还是意见，照约。

时间这一条线，分开了两种问。打击本身发生在第 14 条生效以前，委员会不受理，并写明不认定那一次打击是否合乎战争的法；受理的，是效果延续到生效以后的那一部分，收据与基金法。打击本身的问没有因此消失：它在 UCSE 自己的战争调查委员会里，在国家论第七篇写过的那些通道里；只是不在这条路上。

有人会说，一个只作意见、不能执行的委员会，是一枚橡皮图章。这一说的分量在于，意见确实可以被礼貌地收下，然后搁着。可一条通道作不作数，不看它有没有自己的执行者；国家论早已说过，外部路径都没有自己的执行者（本篇第四节）。要看的是它留下的东西经不经得起核：问的人在不在，问到没到作决定的地方，问的人受不受保护，决定有没有因此改变（第八节）。这一件里，意见之后，外事署书面作答；民众院同一季改了基金法；收据结算了；摘要给了。这些都留下了外面看得见的痕迹。它们与意见是什么关系，"尊重委员会的意见"与"落地依 UCSE 的法律"两句怎样合起来读，在 UCSE 内部受问。

本篇给的，只是三项条件、四件与成员的承担。设几级，是法庭还是委员会，委员几人，答复期限多长，交具体的制度论；宪约第 14 条只是一种写法，是例子。意见落多少地，由 UCSE 的位置判断，账落在那些位置上。

这里留下几处。八户因为"死者是否平民，无法认定"被驳回；四十一与二十二两个数，没有一个第三方认定过，这八户的问就落在两个数之间。目标备忘录里没有给出的部分，外面的问接进 UCSE 内部以后，仍然够不着。按第 9 年粮价结算少给的那三成，农户说了，谁答了，没有记载。打击本身，不在这条路上。

XZ13　许个人申诉的条约机构作为通道（承国家论 N27；自担）。许个人申诉的条约机制接近通道：内部主体自己可以发动，条约也禁止报复；它没有自己的执行者，决定落不落地，仍在本国（国家论第五篇第四节）。它补的是两国之间的仲裁个人够不着的那一处（国家论第二篇第五节）。设计只立功能：照通道的三项条件，独立于被追问的一方，内部主体实际可以发动，发动不被报复（制度论 Z34）；不靠转递者的意愿，只靠一条须别人愿意才通的路，尚不能认证实际可以发动（国家论第七篇第三节）；每一项说得出谁能启动、有什么实际效力、被问的一方怎样不能独自取消（政府论 G36a）。成员的承担照国家论 N27：不阻碍，不报复，照已认的约落实条约机构的决定（是判决还是意见，照约）。几级、是法庭还是委员会，交具体的制度论。

第六节　体系施为所及的人

前面几节，被问的都是国家：体系问成员，人问成员。可体系自己也作出施为，落在人身上。那些人在哪里？

第 16 年夏，沙洲共和国北部的清查与拘押开始以后，成员会议以 4 比 0 通过第 2 号共同措施，期限一年：（一）对沙洲共和国禁运武器与"两用物资"；（二）冻结安定委员会成员与安定队指挥官个人在各成员的资产；（三）灰河各渡口停止商货往来，人员与人道物资除外。人道物资经各成员的主管机关许可放行。宪约第 11 条规定，共同措施由各成员依本国法律实施，须设人道豁免，受措施影响的人可以依第 14 条申诉。

灰河下游是沙洲共和国与石峡邦的界河，西岸属沙洲，东岸属石峡邦。东岸灰渡镇的集市原是两岸共用的，每周三次，西岸各村的盐、燃料与药品多从这里买。第（三）项实施以后，集市关闭。石峡邦商务处的许可审理期，第 16 年秋平均四十一日；净水药剂被列为"两用物资"，不予许可。第 16 年冬，西洲省沿河一处诊所的医生报告，冷链药品断供两个月，诊所改为每周开两日；灰渡镇的商会说，镇上四百一十二户商家中三成歇业。

同一个冬天，两件申诉送到申诉委员会。灰渡镇商会代表一百二十户商家，就石峡邦对第（三）项的实施申诉；委员会以石峡邦国内的救济没有用尽为由，搁置其中的赔偿一项，受理其余。西洲省那处诊所的医生与五十八位村民，就石峡邦商务处的许可审理与"两用物资"的认定申诉。这些申诉人在体系外，身在沙洲。委员会以 4 比 3 决定受理："第 14 条第 2 款说的是'受其影响的人'，没有限于成员境内的人。"少数意见："体系外的人不在宪约之内；他们的问，应由沙洲共和国去问。"

第 17 年春，委员会作出意见：请石峡邦把许可的审理期缩到十五日以内；请石峡邦与成员会议说明净水药剂何以列为"两用物资"；请考虑在灰渡设人道的集市。同一季，成员会议以 4 比 0 修订第 2 号措施：审理期以十五日为限；净水药剂从"两用物资"中删去；灰渡每周两次开人道集市，卖盐、炊用燃料与药品。石峡邦商务处照办。

这件事落在两处人身上：灰渡镇的商家，在石峡邦境内；西洲省沿河诊所的医生与村民，在体系外。他们都在第 2 号措施第（三）项与"两用物资"认定所及之内，避不开；措施针对的却是安定委员会与安定队，不是他们。要看的是两条线：第一条，措施落在人身上的那一部分不越线，人道的那一部分要切出来、切得落地；第五条，这些人有一条到得了体系本身的路。在此之前的情形是：第（三）项停了商货，人员与人道物资除外，许可审理平均四十一日，净水药剂不予许可。

他们在谁的内部？国家论在一国一级回答过这一问。它的统一句说：「本国施为所及、无从以退出避开的，就这些施为而言在本国内部；五条应当照各自条件适用」；后半是：「第一条、第五条（通道独立、够得着、用了不遭报复）与第四条的不封通道，不随关系厚度减；第二条的触发不随它减，手段以本国在该处的权限为界」（国家论前置篇第八节）。

这一句的主词是"本国"。推到体系一级，照全句推，换主词，并写明体系在该处的手段只到哪里（自担）。这样推，先要体系算得上一个构，它的施为组织着这些人的生活；这一点第七节再答。全系列在这里第一次把它写全，后面各篇用到时指回这里：

体系施为所及、无从以退出避开的人，就这些施为而言在体系的内部；五条应当照各自条件适用：第一条（体系的施为不越线）、第五条（一条够得着体系本身的问的路）与第四条的不封通道，不随关系厚度减；第二条的触发不随它减，手段以体系在该处的权限为界，只到问、记、收回配合与请落地的成员守线；第三条与第四条的留出空间，按关系厚度：所及的人在成员国，涵育的条件在成员国；在体系外，体系只为涵育创造条件。这不给成员资格。

体系的施为，指以体系名义、经共同程序作出、作用到人的行动：共同程序列入的名单，体系发出的通报与名单，联合防御的协调与预备以体系名义作出的那一部分，接纳的分配，条约机构的决定。出兵与联合行动本身，是各参与国的施为（第五篇 XZ30、XZ32）。第 2 号措施是成员会议经共同程序通过的，它是体系的施为；它又由各成员依本国法律实施，在灰河一段由石峡邦商务处实施，那一段也是石峡邦的施为。

照这一句读灰河两岸。两处的人都在体系这几项施为的内部，在不在成员境内不论。同一个人还可以同时在两处内部：灰渡的商家就第（三）项在体系之内；第（三）项由石峡邦依本国法律实施，他们也在石峡邦之内。这是第四节说过的交叠，从两国之间推到体系与它的成员之间（自担）。

少数意见说，西岸村民的问应由沙洲去问。可沙洲的政府，正是措施所针对的那一方。照统一句，所及的人须有一条够得着的路；一条只能经被措施针对的那一方转递的路，不能是他们唯一的路。

五条在这件事里各落在哪里，分四项写。

  • 第二条：触发不减，手段以体系的权限为界。措施针对安定委员会与安定队的越线；体系的手段只到收回配合、问与记，不进入沙洲。停掉灰河的商货，是各成员收回自己那一份往来。强力，是用自己的判断力替代对方就它自己的事的判断。这里没有这一替代：沙洲北部的事怎样办，判断仍在沙洲；各成员收回的，是它们自己的配合。收回配合是制约，不是强力；这一条在第四篇第二节立（XZ22）。
  • 第一条、第五条与第四条的不封通道：不随关系厚度减。西岸的村民与体系的关系很薄，这几条在他们那里一点不减。第（三）项停的是商货，人员不在停止之列，渡口没有对人关上。措施落在灰渡的商家与西岸的诊所身上，人经第 14 条走到了委员会；委员会作出意见，成员会议以 4 比 0 修订，由落地的石峡邦商务处照办。修订的三件都落在这一项：审理期以十五日为限，净水药剂从"两用物资"中删去，灰渡每周两次开人道集市。这是体系纠正它自己的施为：措施落在人身上的那一部分不越线，人道的部分切出来、切得落地；盐、燃料与药品到得了两岸的人手里，通道不被封上。
  • 第三条与第四条的留出空间：按关系厚度。灰渡的商家在成员国，他们涵育的条件由石峡邦按它与他们的关系提供，体系不替石峡邦做。西岸的人在体系外，体系只为他们的涵育创造条件；日子怎样过，仍由他们自己定。
  • 这不给成员资格：委员会受理西岸村民的申诉，不使他们成为任何一个成员的人，也不使体系取得管沙洲的权。

有人会说，体系外的人不在宪约之内，委员会受理他们，越出了宪约。这是少数意见，它的分量在于，宪约确实只是成员之间的约，体系外的人没有签过它。可是宪约划的是成员；统一句划的是施为所及。体系的施为落到哪里，问的路就要跟到哪里。跟到那里，不给那里的人成员资格，也不给体系管那里的权，只给他们一条够得着体系的路。

所及即在内、路要跟到，是本篇给的规范；第 14 条第 2 款写作"受其影响的人"，是把它写进规则的一种办法。所及划到哪里，到了一件事上仍要判断：这一次由委员会以 4 比 3 划定，账落在作出受理决定的委员身上。

这里留下一笔。修订以前，冷链药品断供两个月，诊所改为每周开两日，镇上三成商家歇业；那几个月的账照实记着，怎样记、怎样读，交第三篇。人道的部分怎样切出、怎样切得落地，在第四篇第四节（XZ24）。体系与石峡邦的账怎样分开记，体系自己的那一条路怎样是它自己的第五条，在下一节。

XZ14 的全条，立在第七节。

第七节　体系自己被问

灰河两岸的人问的是石峡邦怎样实施一项措施，顺带问到了成员会议。若问的就是成员会议自己作出的一项决定呢？

沙洲北部的人逃过灰河，石峡邦接住了他们；灰渡的营地两次遭到对岸的炮击以后，第 16 年冬东迁到东坪。宪约第 12 条设了一项接纳的分担：各成员分担经费与转送名额，按附件的公式计；第 12 条自己写着，"分担不替来到的人决定落在哪里"。

第 17 年春，成员会议以 4 比 0 作第 3 号分担决定：当年从东坪营地转送三万人，名额 UCSE 一万六千、DRP 八千、NUT 六千（NUT 已接纳约十九万人，按公式抵扣）。转送对象是在营地登记满六个月的人；去往哪一国，以户为单位抽签；"被抽到的人拒绝抽签结果的，当年失去转送资格，留在营地"。成年子女单独为一户。接纳分担办公室的说明是："抽签分的是名额，不是人；被抽到的人可以拒绝。"

营地里有四十六户、二百一十四人在 NUT 北区有亲属，被抽到去 UCSE 或 DRP。其中三十一户拒绝，失去了当年的资格。九户的成年子女与父母，被抽到了不同的国家。

第 17 年夏，这二百一十四人中的一百零三人依第 14 条第 3 款，向委员会申诉第 3 号决定本身。分管共同程序的副秘书长代表秘书处出具书面意见："第 14 条第 3 款所说的'依共同程序作出的决定'，指各成员依共同程序作出的决定，不指成员会议自己的决定；成员会议的决定由成员会议负责。"委员会以 5 比 2 决定受理："第 3 款没有把成员会议的决定除外。"少数意见："成员会议是各成员的会议；问成员会议，就是问各成员，应当分别申诉各成员。"

第 17 年冬，委员会作出意见：请成员会议说明，"拒绝抽签结果即失去转送资格"一条与宪约第 12 条"分担不替来到的人决定落在哪里"怎样并存；请成员会议在分配时顾及家庭。第 18 年春，成员会议以 4 比 0 作第 4 号决定：成年子女可以与父母合为一户；拒绝抽签结果的人保留下一年的资格。兄弟姐妹不合户。答复以成员会议的名义发出，照宪约第 3 条载明各成员的投票。石峡邦常驻代表在会上说："营地在石峡邦，留下来的人也在石峡邦。"

这件事落在那四十六户、二百一十四人身上：他们在第 3 号决定所及之内，避不开；三十一户因为拒绝，失去了当年的资格；九户被分到了不同的国家。要看的是两条线。一条是第五条：有没有一条到得了成员会议本身的路。一条是第一条：体系的分配不越线；宪约第 12 条自己写着，分担不替来到的人决定落在哪里。在此之前：第 3 号决定以 4 比 0 作出，以户抽签，拒绝即失当年资格，成年子女单独为一户；第 14 条第 3 款许依共同程序作出的决定所及的人申诉。

第一步，谁能被问。秘书处的读法，会把成员会议自己的决定排在这条路之外。委员会没有照它。成员会议的决定，正是体系的施为：以体系名义、经共同程序作出、落在人身上。它所及的人，须有一条到得了它的路。这条路开不开，不由被问的成员会议定，也不由它的秘书处定；被问的一方不握指向自己的那一问的关门之权（第二节，XZ10）。秘书处的书面意见是一个位置的意见，载入了记录，没有挡住受理；受理与否由委员会以 5 比 2 判定，这一判断的账落在作出决定的委员身上。

照四件写清这条路：所及的人自己发动；对象是成员会议的决定；成员会议不能独自关上这一件，修约可以改构造，改不掉这条路要满足的三项条件（第八节，XZ15）；不答、不改，账记在体系，也记在投票的各成员身上。

由此可以说一件事：许个人申诉一类的机制，对体系不只是一项更好的设计，它是体系自己的第五条。国家论讲多边制裁时说过一国在共同程序里的那一份：「本国在共同程序里的那一份，包括主张并支持给受影响的人留一条路」（国家论第五篇第五节）。那说的是一个参与国。到了体系一层，给受影响的人留一条到得了体系的路，是共同程序自己的一份（自担）。

第二步，账记在哪里。少数意见说，问成员会议，就是问各成员。这一说对了一半。政府论在两级政府之间立过一句：「两个构的账各按来源核，不互相转记（自担）。」（政府论第四篇 G25）原说一国之内的两级政府，这里推到体系与它的成员（自担）。第 3 号决定的账在体系；它也在投票的四个成员与它们的位置上。宪约第 3 条要以体系名义发出的文书载明各成员的投票，就是为了让这一份账落得到人。问成员会议与分别问各成员，是两条路，并存，不互相替代。问各成员，问的是各成员为什么这样投；问成员会议，问的是这一项决定本身。

灰河两岸那件事同理。委员会请石峡邦缩短审理期，问的是石峡邦依本国法律实施措施的那一份；请石峡邦与成员会议说明净水药剂的认定，是两处各问各的。账分开记。

第三步，体系算不算一个构。说体系施为所及的人在体系的内部，前提是体系是一个构，一个组织着这些人生活的东西。制度论按纠缠划内部：「影响所及不等于权限所及，构是否在组织他们的生活，按实际关系判」（制度论第一篇 Z4）。体系是不是这一意义上的构，制度论没有说，由本系列担。按实际关系看，东坪营地里这些人往哪里去，是成员会议的一项决定组织的；抽签、户的划法、拒绝的后果，都落在他们的日子上。就这些施为而言，体系在组织他们的生活。

还要补一句（自担）。体系发出的通报与名单落在人身上，人须有路可问。若有一国借体系的通报或名单，向别的成员要人回去，体系不帮；门口不推回，照旧（第六篇 XZ38）。

有人会说，委员会是体系自己设的，问体系，是左手问右手。这一说的分量在于，委员会确实是宪约里的一条，设在体系的驻地。可是一条通道独立不独立，看结构，不看它挂在谁的名下。政府论说：「判捕获看结构，不看机构的名称与设在哪里」（政府论第六篇 G38）。照结构看：委员以个人身份任职，由成员会议以四分之三选出，不审对其国籍国的申诉；被问一方的秘书处出具了要把这一件排出去的意见，没有挡住受理。这一件挡没挡住、改没改动决定，还要照战争论 §16 的四条核，在下一节。

这里留下一处，不在本节判。以户抽签、拒绝即失当年资格，这一安排本身怎样，要看不去某一国的余地是不是实际可用；这一规范在第六篇第四节立（XZ39），一件事里算不算实际可用，交位置上的判断。本节只判路与账。体系自己的第五条怎样实现，委员会是不是唯一的形态，登为余项，交收束（XZ56）。

XZ14　体系自己的第五条（自担）。体系的施为，指共同程序列入的名单、体系发出的通报与名单、联合防御的协调与预备以体系名义作出的那一部分、接纳的分配、条约机构的决定；出兵与联合行动本身是各参与国的施为（XZ30、XZ32）。国家论的统一句（国家论前置篇第八节）推到体系一级，照全句推，换主词，并写明体系在该处的手段只到哪里：体系施为所及、无从以退出避开的人，就这些施为而言在体系的内部；五条应当照各自条件适用：第一条（体系的施为不越线）、第五条（一条够得着体系本身的问的路）与第四条的不封通道，不随关系厚度减；第二条的触发不随它减，手段以体系在该处的权限为界，只到问、记、收回配合与请落地的成员守线；第三条与第四条的留出空间，按关系厚度：所及的人在成员国，涵育的条件在成员国；在体系外，体系只为涵育创造条件。这不给成员资格。体系的账与执行它的成员国的账各按来源核，不互相转记（政府论 G25，自担）。由此，许个人申诉一类的机制不只是更好的设计，是体系自己的第五条；国家论 N29 说本国在共同程序里的那一份包括主张给受影响的人留一条路，到了体系一层，这是共同程序自己的一份。体系是不是制度论 Z4 意义上的构，由本系列担。体系发出的通报与名单落在人身上，人须有路可问；被一国借来要人回去的，体系不帮（门口不推回照旧，第六篇 XZ38）。

第八节　体系的安排可被修凿

有了一条路，路会不会只是摆设？体系可以设一个委员会，让人申诉，收下意见，然后一切照旧。可被追问，要追问改得动一些东西。

《制度如何可能》立过一条：「一个以不疑为前提的制度，其修凿通道须满足三项结构条件：独立于被凿者；成员可实际发动；发动不被报复。」（《制度如何可能》§3.2，命题 IV）原说一个制度与它的成员，这里推到体系一级（自担），三项条件写作：修凿体系安排的通道，独立于被问的那一处；成员与体系施为所及的人实际可以发动；发动不被报复。

体系还可能给自己找一种免问的理由：它更偏 15DD 的方向，它的目的是对的，它已经改过了。制度论把这一类理由都挡在了外面：「不得以自称涵育、已经改革或目的正确为由关闭追问与修正的条件；构造可以改，三项条件不能撤。」（制度论第六篇 Z37）体系可以改它的宪约，可以把委员会改成别的样子；三项条件不能撤。

第一步，三项条件对着体系自己核。东坪那一件里，出意见要把成员会议的决定排出第 3 款的，是秘书处，是被问那一方自己的机关；委员会没有照它（第七节）。独立看结构，不看名称与设在哪里。受理的那一步，没有握在被问的一处手里。

第二步，照四条核。战争论讲一个战争方允不允许被追问时说：「真实的允许必须在结构上体现为追问者存在、追问到达决策核心、追问者受保护、追问真的影响决策。这四条每一条都留下外部可见的痕迹。」它也先说了这些机制的危险：「但这些机制在真实运作中可能只是橡皮图章。」（战争论 §16）原说战争方与追问它的人，这里推到体系与问它的人（自担）。东坪那一件，照这四条一条一条看：

  • 追问者存在：委员会在，一百零三位申诉人在。
  • 追问到达决策核心：意见回到了成员会议；第 18 年春，成员会议以 4 比 0 作第 4 号决定。
  • 追问者受保护：第 14 条第 7 款不报复，是规则层的例子；申诉人有没有遭到不利对待，没有记载。这一条只核到条文写在那里为止。
  • 追问真的影响决策：第 4 号决定以答复的名义发出，成年子女可以与父母合户，拒绝的人保留下一年的资格。

战争论又说：「四层全部通过，定理四才算真正通过。任何一层失守，定理四就失败。」（同上）照实说，东坪这一件，四条里有三条留下了外面看得见的痕迹；受保护那一条，只看得到条文。它没有失守的记载，也没有核过的记载。灰河两岸那一件也可以这样核：委员会所请的三件，审理期、净水药剂、人道集市，与成员会议的修订一一对上。

第三步，决定可以终局，位置不能终局。第 4 号决定在本案是终局的：那一年的转送照它办。可它此后仍可再问。国家论在两国的仲裁那里说过：「决定在本案终局；落实它的位置不终局，各在一条自己关不上的通道之下」（国家论第五篇第一节）。委员会的意见也不取最高效力：它请成员会议说明、请它顾及家庭，不替成员会议作决定。兄弟姐妹仍不合户。改到哪里算答到了，不由成员会议独定，也不由委员会独定；没有一个不由双方独占的位置来认定，就照实记作未结（第二节，XZ10）。

有人会说，体系在外部施压之下要团结；对自己安排的追问要有分寸，否则会被外面分化。这一说的分量在于，体系确实面对着体系外的压力，成员之间也确实会有人借追问打自己的算盘。可是为了不被分化而压低对自己安排的追问，正是战争论所说的镜像：「如果己方为了防备对面而压制自己的追问空间，己方已经在结构上变成对面的镜像（对面本来就不允许被追问）。」（战争论 §20）原说交战的一方，这里推到体系（自担）。体系一级的镜像怎样认、怎样判，在第四篇第六节（XZ26）；这里只立一件：三项条件不因外面的压力撤。

三项条件不撤，是规范；委员会怎样组成，票数与答复期限怎样定，是可以改的构造。改到哪里才够，由位置判断；东坪这一件，账落在作出第 4 号决定的成员会议与各成员的票上。

这里留下两处更大的问。一处是可被追问在国与国之间随尺度怎样实现。法学第四篇列过各个尺度的实现方式：「两人靠退出，群体靠追问代表，国家靠多权分立，星际又回到退出和对称协商。」（法学第四篇 §7）国与国之间彼此退不出，又不立分权的上级，这一条怎样实现，上游没有给。本篇以 XZ10 至 XZ15 答了一部分，完整的形态留给第八篇。另一处是制度论留下的一问：「同一句后半所问，即互认与互审能否长成共同的立法，这个系列不答。」（制度论第六篇 Z39）互问走到了这一问的边上；本篇不答，交收束（XZ55）。

XZ15　体系的安排可被修凿（承制度论 Z37；自担）。一个以不疑为前提的制度，其修凿通道须满足三项结构条件：独立于被凿者；成员可实际发动；发动不被报复（《制度如何可能》§3.2，命题 IV）。推到体系一级：修凿体系安排的通道，独立于被问的那一处，成员与体系施为所及的人实际可以发动，发动不被报复。体系不以自称"15DD 主导"免问：不得以自称涵育、已经改革或目的正确为由关闭追问与修正的条件；构造可以改，三项条件不能撤（制度论 Z37）。这些机制在真实运作中可能只是橡皮图章，所以照战争论 §16 的四条核：追问者存在，到达决策核心，受保护，真的影响决策（原说战争方，这里推到体系自身，自担）。捕获按结构判，不看机构的名称与设在哪里（政府论 G38）。条约机构的决定不取最高效力，照"决定可以终局，位置不能终局"读（政府论 G36）。

这一篇停在哪里

这一篇证了这些。头上没有上级，问从外面来，从平级的一群来；体系的位置是外部追问者，它的认定是公开的问与记录，不授权，不否决，出兵的成员也关不上它的问。立约要各方同意，问已有的事不要；被问的一方在指向自己的那一件上回避，问的一方也不独定结案。关上一条通道，取消不了问；对问者下手，是想取消问。体系不强制执行，问落不落地在各成员；成员作答的义务从它已认的约来，"内政"不挡问。许个人申诉的条约机构补的是个人够不着国与国之间程序的那一处，照通道的三项条件与四件核。统一句推到了体系一级：体系施为所及、避不开的人，就这些施为而言在体系的内部，不论在不在成员境内；给他们一条到得了体系本身的路，是体系自己的第五条；体系与成员的账各按来源核。体系的安排可被修凿，三项条件不撤，照战争论 §16 的四条核；决定可以终局，位置不能终局。

它没有证的，交到别处。条约机构几级、是法庭还是委员会、程序的细则，交具体的制度论；宪约第 14 条与申诉委员会只是例子，本系列不主张它们是最好的设计。"可被追问"在国与国之间随尺度怎样实现，完整的形态留给第八篇。各国内部的通道怎样设计，在国家论第七篇。问了以后账怎样记、怎样读，交第三篇；照账收回配合，在第四篇；退出时未结的问，开除以后那一国施为所及的人的路，在第七篇。东坪那种以户抽签的分担，不去某一国的余地是否实际可用，在第六篇。

有几处代价照实写在这里。体系一级没有兜底的法庭；问取消不了，答却可以不来；体系看着问题发生，不能以武力直接解决。DRP 那件事里，失籍的父母本人籍不恢复；枢镇那八户，问落在两个数之间；麦原那二人，到第 20 年仍在关押。

与传统的对接，本篇只做了随文的两处，都与瓦特尔：各民族的自由与独立，落在约须各方同意上，分在问已有的事；别国无权干预一国的统治，分在"内政"不免问。瓦特尔这两处只据网上英译本对读，其中一处只有单一来源，所以只作随文对照。康德的公开性，国家论第五篇第四节已对过，本篇指过去。

本篇命题（合列）

XZ9　体系的位置是外部追问者。体系对越线的认定是公开的问与记录，不是授权，不构成任何一国开战的理由，也不替哪一国的出兵背书；体系不授权，不否决。"最后"指层级，不指免问；头上没有人，只说明问要从哪里来，不说明问已经停了（国家论前置篇第三节）。战争方没有不被追问的特权；第三方可以质疑战争方的决策，而不被当作敌人（战争论 §16）。体系作外部追问者，出兵的一国关不上这一问（战争论原说战争方与第三方，这里推到出兵的成员与体系，自担）。

XZ10　问不须被问者同意；被问的一方回避（自担）。约须各方同意（XZ1）；问已有的事，不须被问的一方同意：问管的是已有的施为，被问的一方关不上（国家论第一篇第五节；政府论 G36）。两样分开，才分得出一种常见的失灵：被问的一方在指向自己的那一问上握着关门之权。全体一致使任何一方都能挡住一切：立约要同意是对的；问已有的事也要同意，就错了。所以共同程序问到一个成员时，它在指向自己的那一件上回避：不投票，不握结案之权；形态不定。冲突论 §15 只取被问者不握关门之权这一半：不能以自己为最终裁判，预先宣布某一类追问者永远没有资格（原说第一人称，这里推到国与国，自担）。体系发问的资格另有根据：外部追问不可被取消（XZ11），与冲突论 §27 所说各方已经承认的程序（原说两方，这里推到多方，自担）。回避不等于人人可否决：控制不等于每个受影响者都有否决权（政府论 G36a）。问不须被问者同意，问者也不独定结案：结案的那一步，要有一个不由问者独占的位置，或照实登为未结；没有一个位置可以独占关门之权，问的一方也不例外（政府论 G36，自担）。

XZ11　外部追问不可被取消（承国家论第一篇第五节，自担照接）。一国可以退出或改接一项外部安排，那是构造的改动，本身在追问之下；它关得上一条通道，取消不了别国、别国的人与受影响的人对它的追问（国家论第一篇第五节）。推到体系（自担）：成员退出一项安排，体系与别的成员对它的问照旧（第七篇 XZ45、XZ47）；体系自己的安排同样在这一句之下（XZ14、XZ15）。关上一条通道与对问者下手分开：不加入、不合作、退出一项安排，是关上一条通道，许；因其提出追问而对问者施压、处罚或报复，是想取消问，越过（照第四篇 XZ25 的第一处）。

XZ12　体系的问接进成员国内部；作答义务来自宪约（承国家论 N38；自担）。体系不强制执行，问落不落地在各成员：平级者的问单独算不上一条通道，接上内部的通道才算（国家论第七篇第三节）。国家论许一国不答一般的平级发问，作答的义务要来自已认的约：没有另行作答义务的一般平级外交发问，一国可以不答；已认条约与共同程序所负的作答、落实要求，仍照已认的约（同上）。所以成员对体系的作答义务，包括出兵的一国向体系公开说明它自己的第一面，写进宪约，经各方同意而有，不单从"体系是外部追问者"推出；对出兵所及的人，出兵国照统一句作答（一国一级，国家论前置篇第八节；见第六节）；以体系名义作出的协调与预备、又被这次出兵用到的那一部分（联合计划、联合演练、预置的仓库），所及的人仍照统一句（体系一级，第六节）。成员以"内政"作答时，不干涉也不等于不问（国家论第五篇第二节）；问照它的对象答，交换照它自己的理由核（国家论第五篇第四节）。

XZ13　许个人申诉的条约机构作为通道（承国家论 N27；自担）。许个人申诉的条约机制接近通道：内部主体自己可以发动，条约也禁止报复；它没有自己的执行者，决定落不落地，仍在本国（国家论第五篇第四节）。它补的是两国之间的仲裁个人够不着的那一处（国家论第二篇第五节）。设计只立功能：照通道的三项条件，独立于被追问的一方，内部主体实际可以发动，发动不被报复（制度论 Z34）；不靠转递者的意愿，只靠一条须别人愿意才通的路，尚不能认证实际可以发动（国家论第七篇第三节）；每一项说得出谁能启动、有什么实际效力、被问的一方怎样不能独自取消（政府论 G36a）。成员的承担照国家论 N27：不阻碍，不报复，照已认的约落实条约机构的决定（是判决还是意见，照约）。几级、是法庭还是委员会，交具体的制度论。

XZ14　体系自己的第五条（自担）。体系的施为，指共同程序列入的名单、体系发出的通报与名单、联合防御的协调与预备以体系名义作出的那一部分、接纳的分配、条约机构的决定；出兵与联合行动本身是各参与国的施为（XZ30、XZ32）。国家论的统一句（国家论前置篇第八节）推到体系一级，照全句推，换主词，并写明体系在该处的手段只到哪里：体系施为所及、无从以退出避开的人，就这些施为而言在体系的内部；五条应当照各自条件适用：第一条（体系的施为不越线）、第五条（一条够得着体系本身的问的路）与第四条的不封通道，不随关系厚度减；第二条的触发不随它减，手段以体系在该处的权限为界，只到问、记、收回配合与请落地的成员守线；第三条与第四条的留出空间，按关系厚度：所及的人在成员国，涵育的条件在成员国；在体系外，体系只为涵育创造条件。这不给成员资格。体系的账与执行它的成员国的账各按来源核，不互相转记（政府论 G25，自担）。由此，许个人申诉一类的机制不只是更好的设计，是体系自己的第五条；国家论 N29 说本国在共同程序里的那一份包括主张给受影响的人留一条路，到了体系一层，这是共同程序自己的一份。体系是不是制度论 Z4 意义上的构，由本系列担。体系发出的通报与名单落在人身上，人须有路可问；被一国借来要人回去的，体系不帮（门口不推回照旧，第六篇 XZ38）。

XZ15　体系的安排可被修凿（承制度论 Z37；自担）。一个以不疑为前提的制度，其修凿通道须满足三项结构条件：独立于被凿者；成员可实际发动；发动不被报复（《制度如何可能》§3.2，命题 IV）。推到体系一级：修凿体系安排的通道，独立于被问的那一处，成员与体系施为所及的人实际可以发动，发动不被报复。体系不以自称"15DD 主导"免问：不得以自称涵育、已经改革或目的正确为由关闭追问与修正的条件；构造可以改，三项条件不能撤（制度论 Z37）。这些机制在真实运作中可能只是橡皮图章，所以照战争论 §16 的四条核：追问者存在，到达决策核心，受保护，真的影响决策（原说战争方，这里推到体系自身，自担）。捕获按结构判，不看机构的名称与设在哪里（政府论 G38）。条约机构的决定不取最高效力，照"决定可以终局，位置不能终局"读（政府论 G36）。
