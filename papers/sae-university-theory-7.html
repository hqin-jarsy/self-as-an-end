SAE Theory of the University · Paper 7

University Governance
大学的治理

Han Qin (秦汉) · Independent Researcher · 2026
DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.24293757 · Full PDF on Zenodo · CC BY 4.0
Abstract

The first six papers each stated who makes a decision and who bears its consequences. This paper brings them together and asks one last thing: who sets these rules, and how are the positions that set rules questioned? It begins with Oxford's Congregation: the university's ultimate legislative body, in which twenty members can submit a resolution on any topic and two members can oppose a legislative proposal. Its members are academics and staff above a certain grade; students are not among them. How, then, do those who are not members, yet bear the consequences of these rules, initiate questioning that reaches their problems? The paper first brings each "who decides" handed over by the first six papers down to positions of accountability, and then states four propositions. The positions that set rules can be questioned, through channels that meet three conditions, with students, staff, and those kept out by a door they cannot get around all on the inside. Academic judgment is completed by positions with the corresponding competence that bear the relevant accountability, and can still be questioned; the right to question does not confer the power to replace academic conclusions, and the positions of academic judgment do not monopolize shutting the door either. Leaving space for leading: it is not written into rules, and it is not assessed. The university itself is also a construct: decisions can be final, positions cannot. The whole series is brought together here.

Writing Declaration: This paper was co-drafted with Claude (Anthropic). All intellectual decisions, framework design, and final editorial judgments were made by the author.

Introduction

On Oxford's governance website there is a page titled "What is Congregation?" It says: "Congregation is the ultimate legislative body of the University of Oxford." It considers major questions of policy put to it by Council or by members, elects members of Council and other bodies, and approves changes to statutes and regulations. Members have several ways of initiating: "20 members of Congregation may submit a resolution on any topic"; two members can oppose a legislative proposal or propose an amendment; one member can ask a question about the university's policy or administration at a meeting during term.

Another page sets out membership: Congregation consists of all of Oxford's academic staff and of academic-related staff of higher grades; those listed include members of the faculties, the principal officers of the university, the heads of colleges and societies, staff in university departments in posts at grade 8 and above, and others. Students are not among those listed.

The opening asks one question only: how do those who are not members, yet bear the consequences of these rules, initiate questioning that reaches their problems?

These two pages prove only one point: members have channels through which they can initiate and change rules, written into the rules. Whether those who are not members have other channels, such as the student union, complaints and appeals procedures, or outside adjudicating bodies, these two pages do not say, and this paper has not checked them one by one; this question is not a judgment on Oxford. How it is actually questioned is checked separately.

The first six papers each stated who makes a decision and who bears its consequences; this paper brings them together. The paper has six sections. §1 takes up, item by item, the "who decides" handed over by the first six papers; §2 says the positions that set rules can be questioned; §3, the accountability of academic judgment and its being questioned; §4, leaving space for leading; §5, the advising relationship and the positions that bear the accountability of advising; §6, the university itself is also a construct. Sections 2, 3, 4 and 6 each state one proposition at the end, and all are listed together at the end of the paper. Last comes where this series stops.

§1 The "Who Decides" Handed Over by the First Six Papers

Each proposition the first six papers stated said which kind of position is to do a thing, and on what basis. This section gathers them into one table. The table names only kinds of accountability, not the names and composition of bodies: which committee, which office, is at the rule layer.

There is a division of labor here that Paper 5 already fixed: Paper 5 deals with the power of evaluation and its checks; this paper deals with the positions that set rules and the positions of academic judgment. Setting rules, dividing resources and checking are not evaluation, and are all power: by Power Theory, Paper 1's definition, they change the paths open to others; they are subject to checks on the grounds of the two sentences of the Prequel of Political Economy, power itself is not the problem and the arrangements that check it are themselves open to questioning.

Each row lands on a position that can be named; each position can be questioned. The next section says how.

§2 The Positions That Set Rules Can Be Questioned

The positions that set the rules of teaching, evaluation, research and funding, and the positions that set the establishment, such as the division into departments and home units, are all open to questioning. The channels of questioning meet three conditions, in Institution Theory's wording: "it is independent of the party questioned, internal subjects can actually initiate it, and initiating it brings no retaliation" (Institution Theory Z34). The party questioned has to answer the very matter it was asked about.

Who is on the inside? Institution Theory says: "The interior is drawn by entanglement: those in the entanglement the construct handles, together with those its acts reach who have no way to avoid it by exiting, make up the interior" (Institution Theory Z4). Brought down to the university: students and staff are inside; they are in the entanglement these rules handle. There is also another kind of person, kept out by the door of a degree or an evaluation, yet unable to get around that door: the Theory of Disciplines says, "those the door reaches and who cannot get around it are inside by the second branch" (Theory of Disciplines, S46). Political Economy speaks of the same kind: when a degree becomes a door others cannot get around, those the door reaches and who cannot get around it can question the door (Political Economy E31).

The question of the introduction here takes the shape of an answer. Membership is one channel; it is not the only one. Those who are not members need a channel that meets the three conditions, and the channel has to reach their problems: it can be initiated, it has actual efficacy, and the party questioned cannot cancel it on its own (Political Economy E16). What the channel looks like is a matter for the rule layer; this paper does not say it has to be a seat in Congregation.

The party questioned changes. From the advisor to the department, from the department to the administration, to the board of trustees, or to the position of appeal itself: independence has to be checked again against the party actually questioned. A position of appeal that is independent of the advisor is not necessarily independent of the administration that set it up.

Setting rules is also power. The Prequel of Political Economy says: "the arrangements that check it are themselves open to questioning." The positions that set rules are subject to checks; the arrangements that check them are themselves open to questioning. When the university adopts automated arrangements of evaluation or allocation, it answers by Paper 5's proposition (DX24).

The Office of the Independent Adjudicator for Higher Education (OIA) in England and Wales, cited in Paper 2, is outside the universities; students can go there after completing internal procedures; it does not review academic judgment itself, but can review the marking process. This is an independent channel outside the university; that it does not review academic judgment shows exactly one of the two lines of boundary in §3.

Several practices are offered only as suggestions. A position for appeal and mediation that students, professors and staff can all go to, and that does not belong to the party complained against. How informal consultation, mediation and referral are combined with formal appeal is left to the rule layer. A position of the ombuds kind, by the International Ombudsman Association's Standards of Practice revised in October 2009, "does not participate in any formal investigative or adjudicative procedures"; "does not make binding decisions, mandate policies, or formally adjudicate issues"; communications to it "are not notice to the organization"; and it "supplements, but does not replace, any formal channels."1 So the mere existence of such a position cannot prove that the related objections already have adequate channels of handling and answering; what it can handle, how it refers, and who answers for formal decisions have to be checked separately.

The strongest opponent will say: if every position of decision and review can still be questioned, will the same dispute never end, and who will dare make decisions that need timely execution? The answer: particular matters can be closed by procedures that have grounds. Institution Theory says: "what persists is the conditions of the channel and of the burden of explanation, not the endless re-asking of the same matter." (Institution Theory Z35a) That positions can be questioned does not mean that the same request can be started again and again without condition. Nor does this proposition prescribe a structure of governance; timely execution and being open to questioning do not cancel each other.

Another says: outside accountability, governments and accrediting bodies, is enough. The answer: outside accountability is also a construct, and is also questioned; channels that internal subjects can actually initiate are still needed.

DX34 The positions that set rules can be questioned. The positions that set the rules of teaching, evaluation, research and funding, and the establishment (the division into departments and home units), are questioned through channels that meet three conditions: independent of the party questioned, actually open to initiation by internal subjects, and bringing no retaliation on those who initiate them; the party questioned has to answer the very matter it was asked about. The interior is drawn by entanglement: students and staff are inside, and so are those the door reaches and who cannot get around it (those kept out by the door of a degree or an evaluation). When the party questioned changes from the advisor to the department, the administration, the board of trustees or the position of appeal itself, independence is checked again against the party actually questioned. Setting rules is also power, subject to checks, and the arrangements that check it are themselves questioned. When the university adopts automated arrangements of evaluation or allocation, DX24 applies.

§3 The Accountability of Academic Judgment, and Its Being Questioned

Whether a thesis passes, what a course teaches, whether the method of a study holds up: these are academic judgments. Who makes them?

The Theory of Government says: "Rules can enter a judgment, yet applying, weighing and taking up remain the work of the one who judges; rules cannot do judgment in anyone's place." (Theory of Government G3) Action Theory says the same thing (Action Theory D6). Extended to academic matters: academic judgment is completed by positions with the corresponding competence that bear the relevant accountability. The qualification "with the corresponding competence" is added by this series: the two upstream sentences speak of those present, and say nothing of competence; academic judgment needs it, and that is this series' own undertaking. Competence is recognized by the peer credentials of the discipline; this recognition too can be questioned.

The positions that make judgments can still be questioned: the division of labor, the grounds and the consequences can all be asked about. There are two lines of boundary here.

The first: the right to question does not automatically confer the power to replace academic conclusions. A student can ask about the grounds of a mark and can appeal on procedure; he does not thereby acquire the power to change the mark to the one he wants. The outside adjudicating office of §2, which does not review academic judgment itself, is one form of this line.

The second: primary authority to make academic judgments does not confer the power to monopolize intake, review or the ending of questioning either. The Theory of Government says: "no position may monopolize the power to shut the door" (Theory of Government G36). That the faculty has primary authority to make academic judgments about the curriculum does not mean that objections to the curriculum can only be taken in by the faculty itself, reviewed by itself, and closed by its own say.

Between the two lines there is one more point. What students know of the conditions of learning, and what staff know of the conditions of carrying things out, are not excluded by the words "that is a matter of teaching." When a course's workload crushes a year's cohort, students know it first; when an examination arrangement cannot work in the laboratory, staff know it first. What they know needs a way to come in. Nor does it follow that every matter is turned into a vote of all.

The 1966 Statement on Government of Colleges and Universities, jointly formulated by the American Association of University Professors, the American Council on Education and the Association of Governing Boards, is an example at the rule layer. It says the faculty has primary responsibility for "such fundamental areas as curriculum, subject matter and methods of instruction"; the original sentence does not end there, and goes on to list research, faculty status, and those aspects of student life which relate to the educational process. The board retains the power of review or final decision, but this power "should be exercised adversely only in exceptional circumstances," and the reasons are to be communicated to the faculty. When students wish to take part responsibly in governance, their desire "should be recognized as a claim to opportunity." This is a normative text, used as an example, not taken as a division of labor already derived upstream in this series.

Paper 5 left one point: how outside opinion and internal rulings divide their labor. Read by the two lines of boundary: independent opinion of the external examiner kind can ask whether the rules were followed, and can give views on academic judgment; it does not thereby acquire the power to replace academic conclusions. What reaches the conclusion is still the position within the institution that bears the corresponding accountability; how it responds to outside opinion is at the rule layer.

Paper 4 left a layer of Kant, which connects here, as a limited analogy only. Kant wanted the university to have one place which, in its teaching, is not under the commands of the government, one that "keine Befehle zu geben, aber doch alle zu beurtheilen die Freiheit habe": that gives no commands, yet has the freedom to judge all (Akademie-Ausgabe VII, 19–20). Where they point the same way: allowing criticism does not thereby confer the authority to command; this points the same way as the first line of boundary. Who bears academic conclusions, this series argues separately on its own conditions of accountability and competence, not by Kant. Where they part: Kant gave this place to one faculty only; this series places every position under a channel it cannot close itself, the positions of academic judgment included. The relation between university and government, this series does not write.

The strongest opponent will say: student participation will erode academic standards. The answer: the two lines of boundary have already said that the right to question does not confer the power to replace academic conclusions; conversely, the positions of academic judgment do not monopolize shutting the door either.

§4 Leaving Space for Leading

Education left an open question: leading needs adults themselves to stand there, which cannot be mass-produced; "How can education reform leave space at the institutional level for what cannot be institutionalized?" The Prequel said that Paper 1 lays the premises and Paper 7 answers. The answer is given here.

The premises are in Paper 1. Leading is neither a power of the teaching position nor its accountability: it can happen only by personal demonstration, it is not registered as an accountability, and it is not assessed (DX6). The teaching position's own judgment is not stood in for by rules either (DX7). The advisor gives leading one place where it can be shown by personal demonstration (DX3), and leading is not on that account registered as the advisor's accountability.

So what the positions that set rules can do is one not-doing and one doing. What they do not do: they do not write leading into the rules of appointment, promotion and course evaluation, and do not assess it. Suppose an appointment rule lists "demonstrating by personal example, so that students see a direction" as an item, asks students to rate it on a five-point scale, and counts it toward promotion: this rule lists leading as a performance to be delivered; the conduct the ratings record cannot vouch that anyone thereby avowed a direction. Behavioral items in course evaluation such as "whether students are respected" are different: they are accountabilities of the teaching position, can be checked, and do not count as leading. What they do: they leave conditions for the teaching position to show its own judgment and bearing, without rules standing in for it; time, latitude, the part of a course not filled up by rubrics.

Whether anyone thereby sees a direction is not made an output of the university, is not inspected and accepted, and is not guaranteed by these arrangements. In the matter of whether anyone thereby sees a direction, what the university bears is the conditions it gives; it cannot guarantee the other's step.

§5 The Advising Relationship and the Positions That Bear the Accountability of Advising

The earlier papers handed over two things, which are placed here. This section states no proposition of its own.

The first: the arrangement, changing and continuation of advising relationships land on a position that can be named. A student who wants to change advisors knows whom to go to; who states when a new advisor can take over; when the arrangement is unavailable, the position responsible for arranging it answers. MIT's transitional support, cited in Paper 2, with a coordinator in each department, is one form of this.

The second: the advising work this arrangement needs is counted in the workload of the position that bears this accountability. Reducing other duties and adding staff are both means the rule layer can use. Not leaving the work needed to unlimited bearing by those who take it on: the normative claim of this sentence belongs to Paper 1 (DX3), and its funding side is in Paper 6. The Prequel of Cultivation Theory says (following Action Theory, Paper 4, D23): "Self-preservation rests first of all on one's also being an end: not cultivating to excess, and not wearing oneself out in a relationship one cannot cultivate." That speaks of one person; extended to the university, it means the university does not wear out those who give. Those who bear this accountability can be professors, and can also be employed graduate students (Paper 2).

There is one more layer. Institution Theory says: "An institution provides conditions for the cultivation of its internal subjects according to relational thickness; it is concerned with conditions, not with outcomes." (Institution Theory Z23) The people inside a university include professors and staff, not only students. Action Theory says: "The one who cultivates can also be cultivated; the two sides are not fixed as giver and receiver" (Action Theory D37). The teacher judges in a course, and also practices in it. From this, Paper 1's saying that the teaching position's own judgment is not stood in for by rules (DX7), and Paper 6's saying that an entrance that does not demand conversion is left among the gates where resources are divided (DX32), each gain one more root: they are also conditions the university gives to those who teach.

§6 The University Itself Is Also a Construct

All along, this series has spoken of other constructs: departments, committees, the advisor's position, the gates where resources are divided. Last, it has to speak of the university itself.

The university is not its own end. It is a construct, and has a remainder too; there is no one final master design. A university that has finished one round of revising its rules is not thereby spared from revising them again; however thorough a scheme of governance is, it will leave places uncovered when it is used.

The Theory of Government says: "Decisions can be final: a particular matter can be answered and closed. Positions cannot be final: no position may monopolize the power to shut the door" (Theory of Government G36). Institution Theory says: "An institution cannot acquire the standing of permanent exemption from questioning" (Institution Theory Z37). The Theory of Disciplines, of its seats, said a sentence of the same shape: "questioning needs no unquestionable end point." (Theory of Disciplines, S52) Brought down to the university: particular matters, an appeal, an assessment, a revision of a rule, can be answered and closed; no position, whether the board of trustees, the faculty or the office of appeals, may monopolize the power to shut the door. Under new evidence and new questions, it can be questioned again.

The whole series is brought together here. Every ought the first six papers stated lands on some position that can be questioned: the teaching position, the advisor's position, the positions of evaluation, the positions that divide resources, the positions that set rules. Every position stands under some channel it cannot close itself. This is what the series means by saying that the university itself is also a construct.

DX37 The university itself is also a construct. The university is not its own end, and has a remainder too; there is no one final master design. Decisions can be final; positions cannot.

Where This Series Stops

What it has not written: how boards of trustees are composed; whether tenure ought to be kept or abolished; the legal status of student organizations and unions; the form of positions of appeal, which is offered only as suggestion; the relation between university and government.

Handed downstream: the design of admission qualifications, admission and certification is handed to later dedicated papers, and the item handed over by Political Economy goes with it; student-to-teacher ratios in primary and secondary schools, compulsory enrollment and public provision are handed to a later Theory of Schools; particular forms of governance are at the rule layer; how those present judge is in Action Theory; institutions organizing games, the item left by the Overview of Exercises and Games of Judgment, still awaits separate treatment.

This series began from one sentence: the university fosters judgment; knowledge is the material of judgment, not the end of the university. Written out over seven papers, it has not given an ideal university. What it gives is a set of conditions, together with where each condition lands, who answers for it, and who can question it.

摘要

前六篇各自写明了谁作决定，谁承担后果。这一篇把它们收回来，问最后一件事：这些规则由谁定，定规则的位置怎样被问。它从牛津大学的 Congregation 起：这是该校最高的立法机构，二十名成员可以就任何题目提出决议，两名成员可以反对一项立法提案。成员是学者与一定职级以上的职员；学生不在其列。那么，不具成员资格，却承受这些规则后果的人，怎样发动足以处理他们问题的追问？全篇先把前六篇交来的"谁定"逐项落到职责的位置上，再立四条。定规则的位置可被追问，通道满足三项条件，学生，职员，以及被门拒在外面又绕不开的人都在内。学术判断由具备相应能力，承担相关职责的位置完成，仍可受问；追问权不授予替代学术结论的权力，学术判断的位置也不独占关门。为引领留出空间：不把它写进规则，不考核它。大学自己也是构：决定可以终局，位置不能终局。全系列在这里收回。

写作声明：本文与 Claude（Anthropic）共同起草，所有思想决策、框架设计和最终编辑判断由作者做出。

引言

牛津大学治理网站上有一页，题为"什么是 Congregation"。它说：「Congregation is the ultimate legislative body of the University of Oxford」，Congregation 是牛津大学最高的立法机构。它审议理事会或成员提交的重大政策问题，选举理事会等机构的成员，批准章程与规则的修改。成员有几条发动的路：「20 members of Congregation may submit a resolution on any topic」，二十名成员可以就任何题目提出决议；两名成员可以反对一项立法提案，或提出修正；一名成员可以在学期中的会议上，就大学的政策或行政提一个问题。

另一页写成员资格：Congregation 由牛津的全体学术人员，与较高职级的学术相关人员组成；所列的有各学院的成员，大学的主要职员，各学院与学会的院长，大学部门里八级及以上职位的职员，等等。学生不在所列之内。

开头只问一句：不具成员资格，却承受这些规则后果的人，怎样发动足以处理他们问题的追问？

这两页只证到一处：成员有能发动，能改规则的通道，写在规则里。成员之外的人有没有别的通道，比如学生会，投诉与申诉的程序，校外的裁判机构，这两页没有写，这一篇也没有逐一核；这一问不是在判牛津。实际怎样被问，另核。

前六篇各自写明了谁作决定，谁承担后果；这一篇把它们收回来。全篇六节。第一节逐项收回前六篇交来的"谁定"；第二节说定规则的位置可被追问；第三节说学术判断的职责与受问；第四节说为引领留出空间；第五节说导师关系与承担指导职责的位置；第六节说大学自己也是构。第二，三，四，六节节末各立一条，篇末合列。最后写这个系列停在哪里。

第一节　前六篇交来的"谁定"

前六篇每立一条，都说了一件事该由哪一类位置作，依什么作。这一节把它们收在一张表里。表里只写职责的类别，不写机构的名称与组成：哪一个委员会，哪一个办公室，在规则层。

这里有一处分工，第五篇已经定下：第五篇管评价的权力与它的制衡；这一篇管定规则的位置，与学术判断的位置。定规则，分资源，核查，都不是评价，也都是权力：照权力论第一篇那一定义，它们改变别人可走的路；它们受制衡，根据是政治经济学前置篇那两句，权力本身不是问题，制衡它的安排自己也可被追问。

第二节　定规则的位置可被追问

定教学，评价，科研，经费规则的位置，定建制，比如系的划分与归口的位置，都受追问。追问的通道满足三项条件，照制度论的写法：「独立于被追问的一方，内部主体实际可以发动，发动不被报复」（制度论 Z34）。被问的一方，须答到被问的那件事上。

谁在内部？制度论说：「内部按纠缠划：构所处理的纠缠里的人，与其施为所及、又无从以退出避开它的人，合为内部」（制度论 Z4）。落到大学：学生与职员在内；他们是这些规则所处理的纠缠里的人。还有一类人，被学位或评价的门拒在外面，又绕不开这扇门：学科论说，「门所及又绕不开的人，照第二支在内」（学科论 S46）。政治经济学说的也是这一类：一张学位成了别人绕不过去的门时，门所及又绕不开它的人，可以就门发问（政治经济学 E31）。

引言那一问，在这里有了回答的形状。成员资格是一条通道；它不是唯一的一条。不具成员资格的人，要有一条满足三项条件的通道，而且这条通道要够得着他们的问题：能发动，有实际效力，被问的一方不能独自取消它（政治经济学 E16）。通道长什么样，是规则层的事；这一篇不说它必须是 Congregation 的席位。

被问者会换。从导师换成系，从系换成行政，换成董事会，或者换成申诉的位置本身：独立，要对着实际被问的那一方重核。一处申诉的位置，对导师是独立的，对设立它的行政未必是。

定规则也是权力。政治经济学前置篇说：「制衡它的安排，自己也可被追问」。定规则的位置受制衡；制衡它的安排，自己也受问。大学采用自动化的评价或分配安排，照第五篇那一条作答（DX24）。

英国的高等教育独立裁判办公室（OIA），第二篇引过：它在院校之外，学生走完校内程序以后，可以去那里；它不审学术判断本身，可以审评分的过程。这是一处校外的独立通道；它不审学术判断，正好示出第三节那两句界线里的一句。

几种做法，只作建议。一处申诉与调解的位置，学生，教授，职员都可以去，不属于被申诉的一方。非正式的咨询，调解，转介与正式的申诉怎样组合，留给规则层。申诉调解员（ombuds）一类的位置，照国际申诉调解员协会 2009 年 10 月修订的《执业标准》，它「does not participate in any formal investigative or adjudicative procedures」，不参与正式的调查与裁决程序；「does not make binding decisions, mandate policies, or formally adjudicate issues」，不作有约束力的决定，不强制推行政策，不正式裁断；对它说的话「are not notice to the organization」，不算已经通知了机构；它「supplements, but does not replace, any formal channels」，补充而不取代正式的通道1。所以，单凭设有这一类位置，不能证明相关的异议已经有了充分的处理与作答的通道；要另核它能处理什么，怎样转介，正式的决定由谁作答。

最强的反方会说：每个决定与复核的位置都还可以被问，同一件争议会不会永远结束不了，谁还敢作需要及时执行的决定？回答：具体事项可以依有根据的程序结案。制度论说：「持续的是通道与说明之责的条件，不是同一件事无限重问。」（制度论 Z35a）位置可问，不等于同一请求无条件地反复启动。这一条也不规定治理的结构；及时执行与可被问，不相抵。

还有一种说：外部的问责，政府，认证机构，已经足够。回答：外部的问责也是构，也受问；内部主体实际可以发动的通道，仍要有。

DX34　定规则的位置可被追问。 定教学，评价，科研，经费规则与建制（系的划分与归口）的位置，受满足三项条件的通道追问：独立于被追问的一方，内部主体实际可以发动，发动不被报复；被问的一方须答到被问的那件事上。内部按纠缠划，学生与职员在内，门所及又绕不开的人（被学位，评价之门拒在外面的人）也在内。被问者从导师换成系，行政，董事会或申诉位置本身，独立对着实际被问的一方重核。定规则也是权力，受制衡，制衡它的安排自己也受问。大学采用自动化的评价或分配安排，照 DX24。

第三节　学术判断的职责与受问

一份论文合不合格，一门课教什么，一项研究的方法站不站得住：这些是学术判断。谁来作？

政府论说：「规则可以进入判断，适用、取舍与承接仍须由判断的人完成；规则代不了判断。」（政府论 G3）行动论说的是同一件事（行动论 D6）。推到学术上：学术判断由具备相应能力，承担相关职责的位置完成。"具备相应能力"这一限定，是这个系列添的：上游那两句说的是在场的人，没有能力这一项；学术判断要有这一项，由这个系列承担。能力照学科的同行资格认；这一认定，也可以受问。

作判断的位置，仍可受问：分工，根据与后果，都可以问。这里有两句界线。

第一句：追问权不自动授予替代学术结论的权力。一个学生可以问一份评分的根据，可以就程序申诉；他不因此取得把分数改成自己想要的那个的权力。第二节那个校外的裁判办公室，不审学术判断本身，就是这一句的一种样子。

第二句：主要的学术判断权，也不授予独占受理，复核与结束追问的权力。政府论说：「没有一个位置可以独占关门之权」（政府论 G36）。教授会对课程有主要的判断权，不等于对课程的异议只能由教授会自己受理，自己复核，自己说了算结束。

两句之间，还有一处。学生对学习条件的所知，职员对执行条件的所知，不因"那是教的事"几个字被排除。一门课的作业量压垮了一届学生，学生最先知道；一项考试安排在实验室里行不通，职员最先知道。他们的所知要有地方进来。这也不推出把所有事项改成全员表决。

美国大学教授协会，美国教育理事会与大学董事会协会 1966 年联合拟定的《学院与大学治理声明》，是规则层的一个样例。它说，教授对「such fundamental areas as curriculum, subject matter and methods of instruction」负有主要的责任：课程，教学内容与教学方法这些基本的领域；原句到这里没有完，后面还列了研究，教授的身份，以及学生生活中与教育过程相关的那些方面。董事会保有复核或最终决定之权，但这一权力「should be exercised adversely only in exceptional circumstances」，只在例外的情形下才作出与教授相反的决定，并要把理由告知教授。学生希望负责地参与治理时，他们的愿望「should be recognized as a claim to opportunity」，应当被视作对机会的正当要求。这是一份规范文本，作样例，不当作这个系列的上游已经推出的分工。

第五篇留下一处：外部意见与内部裁决怎样分工。照这两句界线读：外部考评人一类的独立意见，可以就规则有没有照办发问，也可以就学术判断提出意见；它不因此取得替代学术结论的权力。作出结论的，仍是院校里承担相应职责的位置；它怎样回应外部意见，在规则层。

第四篇留下康德的一层，在这里接上，只作有限的类比。康德要大学里有一处，在教导上不受政府的命令，「keine Befehle zu geben, aber doch alle zu beurtheilen die Freiheit habe」：不发命令，却有评判一切的自由（学院版第七卷第 19 至 20 页）。同向的是：容许批评，并不因此授予发命令的权限；这与第一句界线同向。学术结论由谁承担，这个系列另据自己的职责与能力条件立论，不靠康德。分开的一处：康德把这一处只给一个学院；这个系列让每一个位置都在一条它自己关不上的通道之下，学术判断的位置也在内。大学与政府的关系，这个系列不写。

最强的反方会说：学生参与，会侵蚀学术标准。回答：两句界线已经说了，追问权不授予替代学术结论的权力；反过来，学术判断的位置也不独占关门。

DX35　学术判断的职责与受问。 学术判断由具备相应能力，承担相关职责的位置完成；分工，根据与后果仍可受问。两句界线：追问权不自动授予替代学术结论的权力；主要的学术判断权也不授予独占受理，复核与结束追问的权力。学生对学习条件的所知，职员对执行条件的所知，不因"教的事"三个字被排除；也不因此把所有事项改成全员表决。

第四节　为引领留出空间

教育篇留过一个开放的问题：引领要大人自己站在那里，这不可批量生产；「教育改革如何在制度层面为不可制度化的引领留出空间？」前置篇说，第一篇给前提，第七篇作答。这里作答。

前提在第一篇。引领不是教的位置上的权力，也不是它的职责：只能以身示范，不登记成职责，不考核（DX6）。教的位置自己的判断，也不被规则顶替（DX7）。导师给了引领一处能以身示范的地方（DX3），不因此把引领登记成导师的职责。

所以，定规则的位置能做的，是一件不做与一件做。不做的是：不把引领写进聘任，晋升与课程评估的规则，也不考核它。设一份聘任规则，把"以身示范，让学生看到方向"列为一项，请学生按五级打分，计入晋升：这项规则把引领列成了须交付的绩效；评分所记录的表现，不能代证有人因此认下方向。课程评估里"是否尊重学生"一类的行为项不同：那是教的位置的职责，可以核，不算引领。做的是：为教的位置显出自己的判断与承担，留出条件，不以规则顶替；时间，余地，不被细则填满的那一部分课。

有没有人因此看到方向，不作大学的产出，也不验收，不由这些安排担保。在有没有人因此看到方向这一件事上，大学承担的是它给出的条件，不能担保对方那一步。

DX36　为引领留出空间。 定规则的位置不把引领写进聘任，晋升与课程评估的规则，也不考核它；为教的位置显出自己的判断与承担留出条件，不以规则顶替；有没有人因此看到方向，不作大学的产出，也不验收，不由这些安排担保。

第五节　导师关系与承担指导职责的位置

前几篇交来两件事，在这里落位。这一节不另立命题。

第一件：导师关系的安排，更换与接续，落到一处说得出的位置。学生要换导师，知道去找谁；新导师何时能接上，谁说明；安排不可用时，由负责安排的位置作答。第二篇引过麻省理工学院的过渡支持，各系设有协调人，是一种样子。

第二件：这项安排所需的指导工作，计入承担这一职责的位置的工作量。减少别的职责，增加人手，都是规则层可以用的办法。不把所需工作留给承担者无限承担：这一句的规范归第一篇（DX3），经费一侧在第六篇。涵育论前置篇说（据行动论第四篇 D23）：「自保首先因为自己也是目的：不过度涵育，不在涵育不了的关系里把自己耗尽」。那里说的是一个人；推到大学，是大学不把给的人耗尽。承担这一职责的，可以是教授，也可以是受雇的研究生（第二篇）。

还有一层。制度论说：「制度按关系厚度，为它的内部主体的涵育提供条件；只管条件，不管结果。」（制度论 Z23）大学内部的人，含教授与职员，不只含学生。行动论说：「涵育者也可以被涵育；双方不固定为施予者与接受者」（行动论 D37）。教的人在一门课里判，也在一门课里练。由此，第一篇说教的位置自己的判断不被规则顶替（DX7），第六篇说分资源的闸门里留一处不要求换算的入口（DX32），都多了一条根：它们也是大学为教的人给的条件。

第六节　大学自己也是构

这个系列一路在说别的构：系，委员会，导师的位置，分资源的闸门。最后要说大学自己。

大学不以自身为目的。它是一个构，也有余项；没有一次终局的总设计。一所大学改完一轮规则，不等于从此无需再改；一份治理的方案写得再周全，也会在用的时候留下没有覆盖到的地方。

政府论说：「决定可以终局：具体事项可以答完、结案。位置不能终局：没有一个位置可以独占关门之权」（政府论 G36）。制度论说：「制度不能取得永久免问的地位」（制度论 Z37）。学科论在它的席位上说过同形的一句：「追问不需要一个不可问的终点。」（学科论 S52）落到大学：具体的事项，一份申诉，一次评定，一项规则的修订，可以答完，结案；没有哪一个位置，董事会也好，教授会也好，申诉办公室也好，可以独占关门之权。新的证据，新的问题之下，仍可再受问。

全系列在这里收回。前六篇立的每一条应当，都落在某一个可以被问的位置上：教的位置，导师的位置，评价的位置，分资源的位置，定规则的位置。每一个位置都在某一条它自己关不上的通道之下。这就是这个系列说的，大学自己也是构。

DX37　大学自己也是构。 大学不以自身为目的，也有余项；没有一次终局的总设计。决定可以终局，位置不能终局。

这个系列停在哪里

它没有写的：董事会怎样组成；终身教职的存废；学生组织与工会的法律地位；申诉位置的形态，只作建议；大学与政府的关系。

交给下游的：入学资格，录取与认证的设计，交给日后的专论，政治经济学交来的那一项一并归它；中小学的师生比，义务入学与公共供给，交给日后的学校论；具体的治理形态，在规则层；在场的人怎样判断，在行动论；机构办游戏，《判断力练习与游戏》总论留下的那一项，仍待另论。

这个系列从一句话起：大学培养判断力；知识是判断的材料，不是大学的目的。七篇写下来，它没有给出一所理想的大学。它给的是一组条件，与每一条条件落在哪里，谁就它作答，谁可以问。

本篇命题（合列）

DX34　定规则的位置可被追问。 定教学，评价，科研，经费规则与建制（系的划分与归口）的位置，受满足三项条件的通道追问：独立于被追问的一方，内部主体实际可以发动，发动不被报复；被问的一方须答到被问的那件事上。内部按纠缠划，学生与职员在内，门所及又绕不开的人也在内。被问者换了层级，独立对着实际被问的一方重核。定规则也是权力，受制衡，制衡它的安排自己也受问。大学采用自动化的评价或分配安排，照 DX24。（规范；承制度论 Z34，Z4；学科论 S46，门所及又绕不开的人照第二支在内；政治经济学 E31，E16，前置篇"制衡它的安排，自己也可被追问"；DX24）

DX35　学术判断的职责与受问。 学术判断由具备相应能力，承担相关职责的位置完成；分工，根据与后果仍可受问。两句界线：追问权不自动授予替代学术结论的权力；主要的学术判断权也不授予独占受理，复核与结束追问的权力。学生对学习条件的所知，职员对执行条件的所知，不因"教的事"三个字被排除；也不因此把所有事项改成全员表决。（规范；前半推及行动论 D6，政府论 G3，原说在场的人的判断，主词换作学术位置，本系列承担；"具备相应能力"一限定是本系列新添，本系列承担；后半承政府论 G36；美国 1966 年的治理声明作规则层样例）

DX36　为引领留出空间。 定规则的位置不把引领写进聘任，晋升与课程评估的规则，也不考核它；为教的位置显出自己的判断与承担留出条件，不以规则顶替；有没有人因此看到方向，不作大学的产出，也不验收，不由这些安排担保。（规范；答教育篇开放问题五，本系列承担；前提在 DX6，DX7）

DX37　大学自己也是构。 大学不以自身为目的，也有余项；没有一次终局的总设计。决定可以终局，位置不能终局。（规范；承政府论 G36，制度论 Z35a，Z37；与学科论 S52 同形）
