Abstract

This paper asks whether "a person has no equivalent" stops at a company's boundary. On April 24, 2013, the Rana Plaza building in Bangladesh collapsed and more than eleven hundred garment workers died; the five factories in the building made clothes for the American and EU markets. The dead were not the brands' employees; they were people on the partners' side. This paper's answer is: it does not stop; it goes all the way down the chain. A partner is another company, a construct; behind the construct are people. When a company writes oughts toward a partner, it is construct to construct; the people on the partner's side are identified separately by Institution Theory's two branches, and the rules and appeals the partner construct faces are examined separately under the Specialist Paper. A company buys what partners do, and the price falls on what is done; it does not press costs, layer by layer, into the Base Layer of the people on the partners' side; the longer the chain, the more clearly it must be written who is accountable, and accountability cannot be subcontracted away; the power-face that shows in a cooperative relation is subject to checks, and the more a partner depends on the company and the less it can get around it, the stronger the checks and the more explanation and routes of questioning the company owes it (GS10). The Bangladesh accord on fire and building safety, Germany's Supply Chain Due Diligence Act and Germany's ban on subcontracting in the meat industry show how the rule layer gives "all the way down the chain" efficacy, and also show that efficacy must be examined by the three questions. The UK Groceries Code Adjudicator's finding against Tesco and the EU's Late Payment Directive speak of paying as agreed and on time; the EU's Platform-to-Business Regulation speaks of a marketplace's rules being stated, changes announced in advance, differences in treatment of the platform's own offerings set out, and appeals having a door. Crossways Market telling sellers "you can choose not to sell here" does not answer what it was asked. Finally the paper speaks of direction: growing together. The paper has one proposition (GS10), stated at the end of §4 and listed again at the end.

Introduction

On April 24, 2013, the Rana Plaza building in Bangladesh collapsed, killing "more than 1,100 garment workers." There were five factories in the building, making clothes for the American and EU markets (Congressional Research Service, Report R43085, 2014-02-06).

These garment workers were not the brands' employees. They worked in another company, which made clothes for the brands. On the account of the earlier papers, the brands' employees are inside the brands, and the brands owe them explanation and routes of questioning; but what of the people in the building?

This paper asks whether "a person has no equivalent" stops at a company's boundary. Its answer is: it does not stop; it goes all the way down the chain. §1 says who partners are; §2, that a person has no equivalent all the way down the chain; §3, paying as agreed and on time; §4, partners in the marketplace; §5 speaks of direction.

The reading follows the Prequel: the construct takes the ought, the position takes accountability, the person takes his own law.

§1 Who Partners Are

A partner is another company: one that supplies, one that manufactures on contract, one that takes on a contract, one that sells on a platform. A partner is a construct; behind the construct are people, the partner's investors, the partner's employees.

When a company writes oughts toward a partner, it is construct to construct. Toward the people on the partner's side this series writes no oughts; each has his own law. But "writing no oughts toward them" does not mean the company owes them nothing. Here the partner construct and the people on the partner's side must be looked at separately (own undertaking).

Whether the people on the partner's side are inside the company is identified separately by Institution Theory's two branches: "those in the entanglement the construct handles, together with those its acts reach who have no way to avoid it by exiting, make up the interior" (Institution Theory Z4). The orders a brand places, the prices it sets and the delivery dates it sets organize how much and how long the people in a contract factory work; whether those people are within the entanglement the brand handles is judged by the actual relation, not by which company is written on their badges. Which of the brand's acts fall on them, and whether they have no way to avoid those acts by exiting, are also examined as they actually are.

What the partner construct faces is another layer. A large buyer, a platform, makes rules for many partners: how prices are set, how rankings are ranked, how settlement is handled, how appeals are dealt with. How these rules are read follows the Specialist Paper's requirement on positions that cannot be got around: "The scope of these rules is drawn by the institution, not fixed and changed by the rule-making party on its own," and the rules made "can be questioned, and the channel of questioning is functionally independent of the rule-making position" (A Way Out, a Way Forward, E41a).

How deeply a partner construct depends on a company is looked at through the parts that can be examined: how large a share of its income comes from this one company; the dedicated investments made for it; the contract term and the conditions of termination; what changing channels would cost. What these four items help judge is the partner construct's dependence, and the explanation and routes of questioning the company owes it: the more it depends, the more is owed. There is an example in the Kingdom State. On Crossways Market, Apple City's online marketplace, about thirty percent of the sellers in the state sell only on it, which is the income item among the four; of the twenty-six workshops in Bluekiln, one left Crossways in Year 22 and sold only in town and in its small shop on Echo Wall, and a year later its turnover was thirty percent of what it had been.

In a cooperative relation, the larger party has a power-face over the smaller party's livelihood. Political Economy writes that the power-face "is compared by its particular effects: whom it converges, down to which dimension, how many must pass through it, how great the cost of walking away," and that "The larger an economic position's power-face, the stronger the checks on it" (Political Economy Paper 3, E14). Where the partner cannot get around it, that party is already near a chokepoint (Political Economy Paper 2, E7), and the checks must be stronger still.

§2 A Person Has No Equivalent, All the Way Down the Chain

Normative layer. A company buys what partners do, and the price falls on what is done (the main-line sentence of Paper 2, GS5, extended to partners). Two things follow.

First, costs are not pressed, layer by layer, into the Base Layer of the people on the partner's side: safety, rest, housing, pay. Where the price is pressed so low that the work can be done only by taking away the Base Layer of the people on that side, the line is crossed (own undertaking). The ground has two parts: if the people on the partner's side are inside the company by the two branches, the company's acts cross no line against them (Institution Theory Z4, Z9); the conversions a company makes with its accumulation are not conversion into domination over people's Base Layer (Political Economy Paper 4, E21). At what point a price counts as "doable only by taking away the Base Layer," this paper gives no threshold; it is left as a remainder for Paper 0.

Second, the longer the chain, the more clearly it must be written who is accountable. Accountability cannot be subcontracted away. Political Economy writes: "the construct's account, the position's accountability and individual responsibility with independent grounds are examined separately: the construct's name does not shield the last, and the construct's failure is not automatically transferred onto any one person" (Political Economy Paper 1, E2). When work is subcontracted layer after layer, and each layer says "that is the next company's business," at the end of the chain there is no position that can be questioned. This sentence extends E2 to the chain of subcontracting (own undertaking): each layer of the chain has its own account, and the buyer's share does not vanish because a few companies stand in between.

It judges structure, not persons. What is looked at is the structure of prices, terms and subcontracting; what the people doing the purchasing have in mind is not asked.

Examples at the rule layer. Three examples, each showing one way of writing it, and each showing one thing: the efficacy of the rule layer must be examined by the three questions. The three questions are: "who can activate it; what actual efficacy it has on reasons, actions, rules, or the holding of office; how the party questioned cannot cancel it on its own." (Political Economy Paper 3, E16)

After Rana Plaza, a group of brands, retailers and trade unions signed a legally binding agreement to fund fire and building safety improvements: the participating retailers committed to five years of independent factory safety inspections (Congressional Research Service R43085). The accord says of itself that it has an "inspections and remediation program led by specialist, independent engineers," that workers have "an independent complaints mechanism," and that there are "provisions to ensure remediation is financially feasible"; the International Labour Organization serves as neutral chair; from 2021 it became an international accord (International Accord, "About us"). Read by the three questions: who can activate it, workers can file complaints; what actual efficacy it has, what has been examined so far is that the accord is legally binding, while which remediation, ruling or enforcement a worker's complaint actually triggers this paper has not examined; how the party questioned cannot cancel it on its own, the inspections are independent of the factories inspected, but this does not by itself answer the question; whether brands and factories can terminate the arrangement or refuse remediation depends on the accord's mechanisms of enforcement, dispute resolution and exit, which this paper has not examined, and it gives no answer.

Germany's Supply Chain Due Diligence Act (LkSG), §3(1), provides that enterprises are obliged to observe, in an appropriate manner, the human rights and environmental due diligence obligations within their supply chains ("Unternehmen sind dazu verpflichtet, in ihren Lieferketten die in diesem Abschnitt festgelegten menschenrechtlichen und umweltbezogenen Sorgfaltspflichten in angemessener Weise zu beachten"). Paragraph 3 of the same section provides that a breach of the obligations under this Act does not give rise to civil liability ("Eine Verletzung der Pflichten aus diesem Gesetz begründet keine zivilrechtliche Haftung."), and that civil liability established independently of this Act remains unaffected ("Eine unabhängig von diesem Gesetz begründete zivilrechtliche Haftung bleibt unberührt.") (LkSG §3; my translations). Here the rule layer has itself drawn the range of its efficacy: the obligations are written onto the chain, while civil recourse set in motion by the Act itself is not written in. This is not read as a general exemption; where efficacy falls is examined by the three questions.

Germany's ban on subcontracting in the meat industry goes further. Section 6a(2) of the Act to Safeguard Workers' Rights in the Meat Industry (GSA Fleisch) provides that, in slaughtering, including the cutting of carcasses, and in meat processing, the owner may let workers work only within employment relationships with himself ("Der Inhaber darf im Bereich der Schlachtung einschließlich der Zerlegung von Schlachtkörpern sowie im Bereich der Fleischverarbeitung Arbeitnehmerinnen und Arbeitnehmer nur im Rahmen von mit ihm bestehenden Arbeitsverhältnissen tätig werden lassen."); third parties may not have people work in these areas either, nor may agency labor be brought in. The legislative reasons set out in the Federal Constitutional Court's decision of January 27, 2026 are that whole stages of production had been subcontracted to different contractors, sometimes forming a chain of subcontracting without clear responsibility; that the mass Covid infections at large meat plants had brought problems of occupational safety, housing, social insurance and wages into view; and that "against this background it is necessary to arrive at clear responsibilities in the meat industry" ("Vor diesem Hintergrund ist es erforderlich, im Bereich der Fleischindustrie zu klaren Verantwortlichkeiten zu kommen."). The Court rejected a constitutional complaint against the ban ("Die Verfassungsbeschwerde wird zurückgewiesen."), finding that the part of the ban covering slaughtering, including the cutting of carcasses, that is, the first case in the first sentence of paragraph 2, together with the second sentence (the owner may not let self-employed persons work in these areas), is compatible with the occupational freedom of the commissioning meat companies (BVerfG, Beschluss vom 27. Januar 2026, 1 BvR 2637/21; my translations). Here the rule layer cut the chain of subcontracting outright; it is used only as an example, with no claim that other industries ought to do the same.

Judgment layer. Who looks at which layer of the chain, and how far down they look; when a line-crossing is found, who stops, who pays; who decides, who is accountable.

§3 Paying as Agreed and on Time

Normative layer. Toward partners, too, it is one and the same story (Paper 1, GS2). Agreements are not changed unilaterally, deductions are not made after the fact, payment is on time. For a small partner, payment terms are its livelihood: if payment for goods arrives three months late, it may be unable to pay wages.

Examples at the rule layer. On January 26, 2016, the UK Groceries Code Adjudicator published the findings of her investigation of Tesco: Tesco "seriously breached a legally-binding Groceries Supply Code of Practice." Two practices were found: unilaterally deducting money from sums owed to suppliers; and unreasonably delaying payment for long periods, in some cases deliberately. In one instance a supplier was owed a "multi-million pound sum," which was repaid only more than two years after the wrongful deductions began. The investigation covered June 25, 2013 to February 5, 2015. What was judged were these practices; it is stated as it stands that the Adjudicator also said: "Tesco has also kept me informed of changes it is making to deal with the issues." (gov.uk, "Tesco breached code," 2016-01-26)

Article 3(5) of the EU's Late Payment Directive: Member States shall ensure that the period for payment fixed in contracts between undertakings "does not exceed 60 calendar days, unless otherwise expressly agreed in the contract and provided it is not grossly unfair to the creditor" (Directive 2011/7/EU, Art. 3(5)).

Judgment layer. Who adjudicates disputes; whether a partner can ask, and ask without fear of losing its orders. This last matters: if a small supplier may lose its orders the moment it speaks up, it will not speak up, and "as agreed" will not land. Those who ask are not penalized for asking (Institution Theory Z34).

§4 Partners in the Marketplace: Platforms and Sellers

There is one kind of partner relation that is especially close: a platform and those who sell on it. The platform is the marketplace and makes the marketplace's rules; sometimes it also sells in the marketplace itself.

Normative layer. The marketplace's rules can be stated and can be questioned; changes to the rules are announced first; how the platform's own offerings and other sellers are treated differently is made clear (as with the grounds for ranking in Paper 3); appeals are independent of the party questioned.

"You can choose not to sell here" does not answer what was asked. Crossways Market's seller terms have three items: a seller's listed price on Crossways may not be higher than its listed price in any other channel; only sellers who ship through Crossways logistics can enter the "Express" section; the commission was raised in Year 22 from twelve percent to fifteen percent. Crossways said: "Rates are set by the market. A seller who is not satisfied can choose not to sell here."

What the sellers asked was how these rules are set and why they were changed. "You can choose not to sell here" answers something else: whether sellers can leave. The more a partner depends on the company, the more explanation and routes of questioning the company owes it; being able to leave is a baseline owed to partners, not a reason for the company to refuse to answer (own undertaking). Moreover, about thirty percent of the state's sellers sell only on Crossways, and one workshop's turnover was thirty percent of what it had been a year after it left: for some sellers, this route of leaving is not light. This does not mean that the route of exit is gone; a fall in turnover does not mean there is no exit. What it says is that a sentence "you can leave" cannot stand in for a reason.

Examples at the rule layer. The EU's Platform-to-Business Regulation (P2B) writes the points above into rules: a platform changing its terms must give notice on a durable medium, with a notice period of "at least 15 days" (Art. 3(2)); its terms must set out "the main parameters determining ranking and the reasons for the relative importance of those main parameters" (Art. 5(1)); they must set out any differentiated treatment of the platform itself or of sellers it controls (Art. 7(1)); it must have an internal complaint-handling system, "easily accessible and free of charge," dealing with complaints within a reasonable time (Art. 11(1)) (Regulation (EU) 2019/1150).

Contrast. In Year 23, Crossways set up a Seller Appeals Committee with seven members: five appointed by management, two elected by and from among the Gold Sellers. The committee's decisions give no public reasons. Read by the three questions: who can activate it, sellers can; what actual efficacy its decisions have, the case does not record; whether the party questioned can cancel it on its own, whether management can overturn decisions or refuse to deliver on them, the case does not record either. Only one thing bearing on the third question is recorded: in Year 25, the two members elected by and from among the Gold Sellers were changed to appointment by management. Political Economy has written: "A complaints arrangement whose majority is appointed by the party questioned is examined for independence against the party actually questioned, under Institution Theory Z34" (Political Economy Paper 3, E16). Appointment entirely in management's hands, and reasons not made public, are places to keep examining; they do not by themselves prove that review has been sealed off.

Handed on. Whether ranking the platform's own offerings first is unfair competition, and whether the marketplace and the platform's own sales should be separated, are handed to a later theory of the market.

GS10 A person has no equivalent, all the way down the chain. A company buys what partners do, and the price falls on what is done; it does not press costs, layer by layer, into the Base Layer of the people on the partners' side. The longer the chain, the more clearly it must be written who is accountable; accountability cannot be subcontracted away. The power-face that shows in a cooperative relation is subject to checks; the more a partner depends on the company and the less it can get around it, the stronger the checks, and the more explanation and routes of questioning the company owes it (per Political Economy E2, E7, E14, E21, A Way Out, a Way Forward, E41a; Institution Theory Z4, Z6, Z9; own undertaking).

§5 Direction: Growing Together

The first four sections spoke of the baseline. This section speaks of direction.

Give partners conditions: steady orders, predictable payment, sharing of data that is useful to them. From Year 23, Crossways Market opened to each seller its own sales and visitor data. In one and the same company, practices that need questioning and practices that give conditions exist side by side; a direction is not a label.

Help partners grow, without letting them grow into dependence: do not require exclusivity, do not prevent them from also selling elsewhere. The more a partner can sell elsewhere, the more it can leave; and only when it can leave is its cooperation with the company its own choice.

The discipline follows the earlier papers: contribution is a direction, not a duty; outcomes are not taken as rules.

Where This Paper Stops

This paper has laid down one proposition: a person has no equivalent, all the way down the chain (GS10).

What it has not proved: competition between companies, ranking the platform's own offerings first, and breakup, which are handed to a later theory of the market; the particular design of supply chain legislation; what a reasonable price is.

Handed to the later papers: those whom the company's acts reach who have no way to avoid them by exiting, in Paper 5; how the channels of appeal and self-correction are independent, in Paper 6, §2.

Remainders left: at what point a price counts as taking away the Base Layer of the people on the partner's side has no threshold, and is left to Paper 0 (§2); the actual efficacy of workers' complaints under the Bangladesh accord, and its exit mechanisms, this paper has not examined (§2); which of the people on the partner's side are inside the brand is judged item by item by the actual relation, without a general table (§1).

摘要

这一篇问"人没有等价物"停不停在公司的边界上。2013 年 4 月 24 日，孟加拉的拉纳广场倒塌，死了一千一百多名制衣工人；楼里的五家工厂给美国与欧盟市场做衣服。死去的人不是那些品牌的员工，是伙伴那边的人。这一篇的回答是：不停，它沿着链往下走。伙伴是另一家公司，是构；构后面是人。公司对伙伴写应当，是构对构；伙伴那边的人照制度论的两支另认，伙伴构面对的规则与申诉照专论另核。公司买的是伙伴做的事，价格落在事上；不把成本一层层压进伙伴那边的人的基础层；链越长，越要写清谁负责，责任不能被分包分没了；合作关系里显出的权力面受制衡，伙伴越依赖、越绕不过去，制衡越强，公司欠它的说明与问路越多（GS10）。孟加拉的消防与建筑安全协议、德国的供应链尽职调查法、德国肉类业的外包禁令，示范规则层怎样让"沿着链往下"有实效，也示范实效要照三问去核。英国食品杂货守则裁判官对 Tesco 的认定与欧盟的逾期付款指令，说的是照约定、按时付钱；欧盟的平台对企业条例，说的是场上的规则说得出、改了先讲、自营的差别写明、申诉有门。通衢市集对卖家说"可以不在这里卖"，答不到被问的那件事。最后说方向：一起长。全篇一条（GS10），写在第四节末，篇末合列。

引言

2013 年 4 月 24 日，孟加拉的拉纳广场（Rana Plaza）大楼倒塌。一千一百多名制衣工人死亡（"more than 1,100 garment workers"）。楼里有五家工厂，给美国与欧盟市场做衣服（美国国会研究处，报告 R43085，2014-02-06）。

这些制衣工人，不是那些品牌的员工。他们在另一家公司里做事，那家公司替品牌做衣服。照前几篇的写法，品牌的员工在品牌的内部，品牌欠他们说明与问路；那么楼里的人呢？

这一篇问："人没有等价物"停不停在公司的边界上。这一篇的回答是：不停，它沿着链往下走。第一节说伙伴是谁；第二节说人没有等价物沿着链往下；第三节说照约定、按时付钱；第四节说场上的伙伴；第五节说方向。

读法照前置篇：构用应当，位置用职责，人用自己的法。

第一节　伙伴是谁

伙伴是另一家公司：供货的，代工的，承包的，在平台上卖东西的。伙伴是构；构后面是人，伙伴的出资人，伙伴的员工。

公司对伙伴写应当，是构对构。对伙伴那边的人，这个系列不写应当，他们各有自己的法。可是"不对他们写应当"，不等于公司对他们没有什么可欠。这里要把伙伴构与伙伴一侧的人分开看（自担）。

伙伴一侧的人在不在公司的内部，照制度论的两支分别识别：「构所处理的纠缠里的人，与其施为所及、又无从以退出避开它的人，合为内部」（制度论 Z4）。一家品牌下的订单、定的价、定的交期，组织着代工厂里的人做多少工、做多久；那些人是否在品牌所处理的纠缠里，按实际关系判，不按他们的工牌写着哪一家判。品牌的哪些施为落在他们身上、他们又无从以退出避开，也照实核。

伙伴构所面对的，是另一层。一个大的买方、一个平台，为许多伙伴立了规：价格怎样定，排序怎样排，结算怎样办，申诉怎样处理。这些规怎样读，照专论对绕不过去的位置的要求：「这些规的范围由制度划，不由立规的一方独自定、独自改」，所立的规「可以被问，问的通道在功能上独立于它」（专论 E41a）。

伙伴构对一家公司依赖多深，照能核的部分看：收入有多大一块靠这一家；为这一家做的专用投资；合同期与解约的条件；换渠道要付的代价。这四项帮着判断的，是伙伴构的依赖，以及公司欠它的说明与问路：越依赖，欠得越多。王国州里有一个例子。苹果城的线上市集通衢市集（Crossways Market），州内的卖家约三成只在它上面卖，这是四项里收入的那一项；青窑镇的二十六家作坊里，有一家第 22 年退出通衢，只在镇上和回音壁上的小店里卖，一年后营业额剩原来的三成。

合作关系里，大的一方在小的一方的生计上有了权力面。政治经济学写：权力面「按具体的作用比较：它收敛谁，收敛到哪一个维度，多少人要经过它，走开的代价多大」，「经济位置的权力面越大，对它的制衡越强」（政治经济学第三篇 E14）。伙伴绕不过去的时候，那一方已近关口（政治经济学第二篇 E7），制衡更要强。

第二节　人没有等价物沿着链往下

规范层。 公司买的是伙伴做的事，价格落在事上（第二篇 GS5 的主线句推到伙伴）。由此有两件。

第一件，不把成本一层层压进伙伴那边的人的基础层：安全，休息，住处，工钱。价格压到只有拿走那边人的基础层才做得成，就越了线（自担）。根据有两段：伙伴一侧的人若照两支在公司的内部，公司的施为对他们不越线（制度论 Z4、Z9）；公司以积累所作的兑换，不兑换为对人的基础层的支配（政治经济学第四篇 E21）。价格压到哪一点算"只有拿走基础层才做得成"，这一篇没有阈值，交 Paper 0 的余项。

第二件，链越长，越要写清谁负责。责任不能被分包分没了。政治经济学写：「构的账、位置的职责与个人有独立根据的责任分别核，构的名义不遮蔽后者，构的失责也不自动转记给某个人」（政治经济学第一篇 E2）。一层层外包，每一层都说"那是下一家的事"，链的尽头就没有一个能被问的位置。这一句把 E2 推到分包的链上（自担）：链上每一层有每一层的账，买方的那一份不因为中间隔了几家就消失。

判结构，不判人。 看的是价格、条款、分包的结构；不问采购的人怎么想。

规则层的例子。 三个例子，各示范一种写法，也各示范一件事：规则层的实效，要照三问去核。三问是：「谁能启动；对理由、行动、规则或任职位置有什么实际效力；被问的一方怎样不能独自取消它。」（政治经济学第三篇 E16）

拉纳广场之后，一批品牌、零售商与工会签了一份有法律约束力的协议，资助消防与建筑的改造：参加的零售商承诺为期五年的独立工厂安全检查（美国国会研究处 R43085）。协议自述，检查与整改由独立的专业工程师主持（"inspections and remediation program led by specialist, independent engineers"），工人有一个独立的申诉机制（"an independent complaints mechanism"），并有条款确保整改在财务上做得到（"provisions to ensure remediation is financially feasible"）；国际劳工组织任中立的主席；2021 年起改为国际协议（International Accord，"About us"）。照三问读：谁能启动，工人能申诉；有什么实际效力，目前核到的是协议有法律约束力，工人的申诉具体触发哪一种整改、裁决或执行，本文没有核到；被问的一方怎样不能独自取消它，检查独立于被查的工厂，这一点不独自回答这一问，品牌与工厂能不能终止安排、拒绝整改，要看协议的执行、争议解决与退出的机制，本文没有核到，不作回答。

德国的《供应链尽职调查法》（LkSG）第 3 条第 1 款写，企业要在它们的供应链里，以适当的方式遵守人权与环境方面的尽职义务（"Unternehmen sind dazu verpflichtet, in ihren Lieferketten die in diesem Abschnitt festgelegten menschenrechtlichen und umweltbezogenen Sorgfaltspflichten in angemessener Weise zu beachten"）。同条第 3 款又写：违反本法的义务，不创设民事责任（"Eine Verletzung der Pflichten aus diesem Gesetz begründet keine zivilrechtliche Haftung."）；依本法以外的根据成立的民事责任，不受影响（"Eine unabhängig von diesem Gesetz begründete zivilrechtliche Haftung bleibt unberührt."）（LkSG §3）。规则层在这里自己划了实效的范围：义务写到了链上，由这部法本身启动的民事追究没有写进来。这不读作全面的免责；实效落在哪里，照三问去核。

德国肉类业的外包禁令走得更远。《保障肉类业劳动者权利法》（GSA Fleisch）第 6a 条第 2 款写：在屠宰（含屠体分割）与肉类加工领域，经营者只能让与他自己有劳动关系的劳动者做事（"Der Inhaber darf im Bereich der Schlachtung einschließlich der Zerlegung von Schlachtkörpern sowie im Bereich der Fleischverarbeitung Arbeitnehmerinnen und Arbeitnehmer nur im Rahmen von mit ihm bestehenden Arbeitsverhältnissen tätig werden lassen."）；第三方也不得在这些领域让人做事，不得派遣劳务进来。联邦宪法法院 2026 年 1 月 27 日的决定所述的立法理由是：整道工序外包给不同的承包者，有时成了一条分包链，没有清楚的责任；大型肉厂新冠的大规模感染，把劳动安全、住处、社会保险与工资的问题显了出来；"在这一背景下，有必要在肉类业达成清楚的责任"（"Vor diesem Hintergrund ist es erforderlich, im Bereich der Fleischindustrie zu klaren Verantwortlichkeiten zu kommen."）。法院驳回了一项针对这一禁令的宪法诉愿（"Die Verfassungsbeschwerde wird zurückgewiesen."），认定这一禁令在屠宰（含屠体分割）领域的部分，即第 2 款第 1 句的第一种情形，以及第 2 句（经营者不得让自雇者在这些领域做事），与委托方肉类企业的职业自由相容（BVerfG, Beschluss vom 27. Januar 2026, 1 BvR 2637/21）。规则层在这里直接切掉了分包的链；这里只作例子，不主张别的行业都该这样做。

判断层。 链上哪一层由谁看，看到多远；发现越线时，谁停，谁出钱；谁定，谁负责。

第三节　照约定，按时付钱

规范层。 对伙伴，也是同一个故事（第一篇 GS2）。不单方面改约定，不事后扣款，按时付钱。对一家小伙伴，账期就是生计：货款晚到三个月，它可能就发不出工钱。

规则层的例子。 英国的食品杂货守则裁判官（Groceries Code Adjudicator）2016 年 1 月 26 日公布对 Tesco 的调查结论：Tesco 严重违反了有法律约束力的《食品杂货供应守则》（"seriously breached a legally-binding Groceries Supply Code of Practice"）。认定的做法有两样：单方面从应付供应商的款项里扣钱；不合理地长期拖延付款，有的是故意拖延。其中一例，一家供应商被欠了数百万英镑（"multi-million pound sum"），错扣开始两年多以后才还上。调查期为 2013 年 6 月 25 日至 2015 年 2 月 5 日。判的是这几项做法；照实写明，裁判官也说，Tesco 一直在告知她它为处理这些问题正在作的改动（"Tesco has also kept me informed of changes it is making to deal with the issues."）（gov.uk，"Tesco breached code"，2016-01-26）。

欧盟的《逾期付款指令》第 3 条第 5 款：成员国应确保，企业之间合同所定的付款期不超过 60 个日历日，除非合同另有明确约定，且对债权人不构成严重不公（"does not exceed 60 calendar days, unless otherwise expressly agreed in the contract and provided it is not grossly unfair to the creditor"）（Directive 2011/7/EU, Art. 3(5)）。

判断层。 争议由谁裁；伙伴能不能去问，问了不怕丢掉订单。最后这一件要紧：一家小供应商若一开口就可能失去订单，它就不会开口，照约定这一条也就落不了地。问的人不因问而受罚（制度论 Z34）。

第四节　场上的伙伴：平台与卖家

有一种伙伴关系格外近：一个平台与在上面卖东西的人。平台是场，它立场上的规；有时它自己也在场里卖。

规范层。 场的规则说得出，也可以被问；改规则，先讲；自营与别的卖家怎样区别对待，讲明（第三篇的排序依据同此）；申诉独立于被问的一方。

"可以不在这里卖"答不到被问的那件事。 通衢市集的卖家条款有三条：在通衢上的标价不得高于在其他任何渠道的标价；只有用通衢物流发货的卖家能进"快送"栏；佣金第 22 年由一成二提到一成五。通衢说："费率是市场定的。卖家不满意，可以不在这里卖。"

卖家问的，是这几条规怎样定、为什么改。"可以不在这里卖"答的是另一件事：卖家能不能走。伙伴越依赖，公司欠它的说明与问路越多；离得开是对伙伴的底线，不是公司拒绝回答的理由（自担）。何况州内约三成的卖家只在通衢卖，一家作坊退出一年后营业额剩三成：这一句离开的路，对一部分卖家来说并不轻。这不是说退出的路已经没有了；营业额减少，不等于没有退出。它说的是：一句"可以走"，替不了一句理由。

规则层的例子。 欧盟的《平台对企业条例》（P2B）把上面几件写成了规则：平台改条款，要在耐用载体上通知，通知期至少 15 天（"at least 15 days"）（Art. 3(2)）；在条款里写明决定排序的主要参数，以及这些参数为什么相对更重要（"the main parameters determining ranking and the reasons for the relative importance of those main parameters"）（Art. 5(1)）；写明对平台自己或其控制的卖家的区别对待（Art. 7(1)）；设内部申诉系统，易于使用、免费（"easily accessible and free of charge"），在合理时限内处理（Art. 11(1)）（Regulation (EU) 2019/1150）。

对照。 第 23 年，通衢设了一个"卖家申诉委员会"，七名委员：五名由管理层任命，两名由金牌卖家互选。委员会的决定不公开理由。照三问读：谁能启动，卖家能；决定有什么实际效力，这一例没有记下；被问的一方能不能独自取消它，管理层能不能撤销决定、拒不兑现，这一例也没有记下。只记下了一件关第三问的事：第 25 年，那两名由金牌卖家互选的委员改由管理层任命。政治经济学写过：「由被问的一方任命多数的申诉安排，独立照制度论 Z34 对着实际被问的一方核」（政治经济学第三篇 E16）。任命全在管理层手里、理由不公开，是要继续核的地方；它们不独自证明审查已被封死。

交出去的。 自营排在前面算不算不正当竞争，场与自营要不要分开，交日后的市场论。

GS10　人没有等价物沿着链往下。公司买的是伙伴做的事，价格落在事上；不把成本一层层压进伙伴那边的人的基础层。链越长，越要写清谁负责；责任不能被分包分没了。合作关系里显出的权力面受制衡；伙伴越依赖、越绕不过去，制衡越强，公司欠它的说明与问路越多（承政治经济学 E2、E7、E14、E21，专论 E41a；制度论 Z4、Z6、Z9；自担）。

第五节　方向：一起长

前四节说的是底线。这一节说方向。

给伙伴条件：稳定的订单，可预期的付款，共享对它有用的数据。通衢市集第 23 年起，向卖家开放各自的销量与访客数据。同一家公司里，需要追问的做法与给条件的做法并存；方向不是标签。

帮伙伴长大，不让它长成依赖：不要求独家，不妨碍它也在别处卖。一家伙伴越能在别处卖，越离得开；离得开，它与公司的合作才是它自己选的。

纪律照前几篇：贡献是方向，不是义务；结果不当规则。

这一篇停在哪里

这一篇立了一条：人没有等价物沿着链往下（GS10）。

它没有证明的：公司与公司之间的竞争、自营排前、拆分，交日后的市场论；供应链立法的具体设计；合理的价格是多少。

交给后面各篇的：公司施为所及、无从以退出避开的人，在第五篇；申诉与自我修正的通道怎样独立，在第六篇第二节。

留下的余项：价格压到哪一点算拿走了伙伴那边人的基础层，没有阈值，交 Paper 0（第二节）；孟加拉协议里工人申诉的实际效力与退出机制，本文没有核到（第二节）；伙伴一侧的人哪些在品牌的内部，按实际关系逐项判，没有一张总表（第一节）。

本篇命题（合列）

GS10　人没有等价物沿着链往下。公司买的是伙伴做的事，价格落在事上；不把成本一层层压进伙伴那边的人的基础层。链越长，越要写清谁负责；责任不能被分包分没了。合作关系里显出的权力面受制衡；伙伴越依赖、越绕不过去，制衡越强，公司欠它的说明与问路越多（承政治经济学 E2、E7、E14、E21，专论 E41a；制度论 Z4、Z6、Z9；自担）。
