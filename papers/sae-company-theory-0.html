Theory of the Company and the Tradition: The Price Falls on What Is Done
公司论与传统：价格落在事上

Han Qin (秦汉) · Independent Researcher · 2026
DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.24045743 · Full PDF on Zenodo · CC BY 4.0
Abstract

The Prequel and the six papers of this series have been written. They ask how a company whose consensus includes "a human being is an end" treats its investors, its employees, its users, its partners and the people its acts reach. The answer is gathered into one main line: a person has no equivalent; a company buys what people do, not people; the price falls on what is done, not on the person. This paper lays down no propositions, and changes no sentence of the seven papers. It does four things. First it steps outside the seven papers and has this machine give an account of itself: what it asks and what it does not, how its sentence form and its three layers are arranged, how it divides the work with upstream. Next it interfaces with the tradition, at two depths: the schools whose originals have been examined are carried as far as the examination reaches: Kant, Dodge v. Ford Motor Co., Friedman and the Business Roundtable go through all four steps; the Mackey–Friedman debate, Bebchuk and Tallarita, the British Academy's "Future of the Corporation" programme and Ciepley go through all four steps on the abstracts or pages examined; Freeman, and Hart and Zingales, examined only in one sentence or an abstract, go as far as the third step; the schools reached only through secondary material, Coase, Jensen and Meckling, Berle and Means, Anderson and Ferreras, go as far as the common question and the surface of contact. Several differences fall on two lines: how the purpose is formulated and how the line is put into effect; whether the argument relies on "winning." One more lies outside the two lines: where the line comes from. The second half of the main-line sentence this paper reads as having Kant's Academy edition IV 435 on its source side, and it states the distance between this reading and Kant's own line. Finally it settles accounts: how the items handed down from upstream land, what is adjacent and not taken up, what is handed downstream, and the six slots of remainders.

Writing Declaration: This paper was co-drafted with Claude (Anthropic). All intellectual decisions, framework design, and final editorial judgments were made by the author.

Introduction

This paper was written only after the seven papers were finished. A conversation is to be had once one's own object stands: only someone who knows what he himself is saying can tell where what others say differs from it. Each of the seven papers used real cases at the places where its judgments landed; those are facts, not interfacing; how this series interfaces with existing theories of the company has from the start been reserved for this paper.

This paper lays down no propositions, makes no new demands on the reader, and changes no sentence of the seven papers. When it cites this series, it gives the paper and the proposition number; when it cites the schools, it says only what each holds and where, and gives the smallest source that can be examined. The schools are interlocutors, not premises of this series; this paper does not write of this series correcting anyone, nor of anyone anticipating this series. Its structure follows Political Economy Paper 0: the machine's account of itself, method, interfacing school by school, handovers and remainders.

Outside texts are cited in four tiers, following Political Economy Paper 0:

  • Tier A: the sentence and its location have been confirmed in a reliable original; it is quoted as it stands, with the original and the location given;
  • Tier B: the quoted sentence has been examined in the original relied on, but the edition, the exact location or the full text has not yet been confirmed; it is quoted, with what it rests on and what has not been examined noted;
  • Tier C: it rests only on reliable secondary literature; it is only paraphrased, with what it rests on stated, and without quotation marks;
  • Cited at second hand: it rests only on what others have quoted; it follows the text relied on, is marked as cited at second hand, goes as far as the third step, and no objection is drafted;
  • What could not be confirmed is not cited.

Works written in English are quoted as they stand; German is given in my translation, with the original in parentheses. What is examined is the original given in parentheses, or, for works in English, the English as quoted.

§1 What Kind of Machine This Is

§1.1 What It Asks

How a company whose consensus includes "a human being is an end" treats its investors, its employees, its users, its partners and the people its acts reach: the baseline is not crossing the line, not treating anyone as something that can be traded off; the direction is giving conditions, so that the road each of these people goes on is wider; profit is a condition and a return, not the ultimate criterion; and the arrangements that keep this direction are themselves open to questioning.

It does not ask whether companies should exist, which form of ownership is best, or how high profit should be. It writes only about companies oriented toward the Kingdom of Ends; the direction of the Kingdom of Means serves only as a contrast (the Prequel, §6).

§1.2 Sentence Form and the Three Layers

The construct takes the ought, the position takes accountability, the person takes his own law: a company is a construct, and this series writes oughts for it; directors, managers and the CEO are in positions, and the series writes their accountability; employees, users and investors each have their own law, and the series writes no oughts for them.

The three layers are a method: the normative layer gives direction and the baseline; the rule layer only makes suggestions and may give examples; the judgment layer makes clear who decides and who is accountable; there is always a remainder. It judges the operation, not the person; it speaks only of structure, not of right and wrong, which does not mean that there is no right and wrong, but that no moral verdict is passed on a person or a company (the Prequel, §7).

§1.3 The Root and the Main Line

The main-line sentence: a person has no equivalent: a company buys what people do, not people; the price falls on what is done, not on the person.

Each of the seven papers lands it in one place:

  • Investment: shares are not ownership of people (GS1); the baseline is made clear before investment, not weighed in a vote (GS3).
  • Working Together: a company buys what is done, not people (GS5); a way out, a way forward, and a person's own affairs are his to decide (GS6).
  • Service: no locking in, no manipulation, no deception (GS8); health and safety are not traded for profit (GS8a); a model is judged by its execution, not by its name (GS9).
  • Partners: a person has no equivalent, all the way down the chain (GS10).
  • Society: the baseline is not crossing the line; contribution is a direction (GS11).
  • Keeping the Direction: the arrangements that keep the direction do not presuppose consensus (GS12); judging hard times, and answering for the judgment (GS13).

§1.4 The Division of Labor with Upstream

What Institution Theory, Political Economy and its Specialist Paper have already proved, this series does not prove again; it only brings it down to a company's relations with five parties: the interior is drawn by entanglement, in two branches (Institution Theory Z4); a construct's acts cross no line, which is not discounted under any condition (Institution Theory Z9); channels are independent of the party questioned, can be initiated, and initiating them brings no retaliation (Institution Theory Z34); power lies in the position that can mobilize accumulation (Political Economy Paper 1, E2); chokepoints and the sole position of supply at the life-substrate (Paper 2, E7, E11a); checks increase with the power-face, and the three questions (Paper 3, E14, E16); the two gates and exchange outside them (Paper 4, E21–E24); the rate of return is not the ultimate criterion (Paper 5, E28); support does not acquire the authority to decide a person's direction (Paper 6, E37); a way out, a way forward, and a person's own affairs (A Way Out, a Way Forward, E40, E41).

§2 The Method of Interfacing

§2.1 The Four Steps

Each conversation takes four steps: first, a common question that can be examined is sought; next, whether the two speak of the same thing; next, how far agreement goes, and where the two part; last, the strongest objection from the other side is written, with this series' reply. The objection is drafted by this paper, marked as drafted, and as far as possible made to touch the other side's strength.

§2.2 Two Depths

Where the original has been examined, the conversation is carried as far as the examination reaches. Where the full text or the original judgment has been examined, all four steps are taken: Kant, Dodge v. Ford Motor Co., Friedman, the Business Roundtable. Where only an abstract, an organization's page or a few passages of a debate's original text have been examined, the four steps are taken on those sentences, and it is stated that beyond them this paper does not answer for the other side: the Mackey–Friedman debate, Bebchuk and Tallarita, the British Academy, Ciepley. Where only one sentence or an abstract has been examined and the material is not enough to draft a strong objection, the conversation goes as far as the third step, and where it stops is noted: Freeman, Hart and Zingales.

Where only secondary material is reached, the conversation goes as far as the common question and the surface of contact: Coase, Jensen and Meckling, Berle and Means, Anderson, Ferreras. In these units, this paper writes what its paraphrase rests on, where the surface of contact lies, and which question is to be decided once the original has been examined; it does not write a settled conclusion of "meeting" or "parting," nor does it use quotation marks.

§2.3 The Differences Fall on Two Lines

Once the conversations are written out, several differences fall on the same two lines. The two lines are this paper's generalization, not any school's own division, nor a classification of all the differences.

The first line: how the purpose is formulated, and how the line is put into effect. The schools dispute both the purpose of the company, "for shareholders," "for stakeholders," "for people and planet," and how purpose is put into effect as incentives, accountability and institutional arrangements. Along this line this paper asks: whatever the formulation of purpose, where is the actual line, who can set questioning in motion, and what efficacy the channel has (Political Economy Paper 5, E27, says that a goal's name and a statement do not by themselves confirm a reading; Paper 3, E16). This is this paper's way of asking; it is not a claim that the other side disputes only names.

The second line: whether the argument relies on "winning." Some defenses say that treating every party well also pays in the long run. The normative layer of this series does not rest its argument on "winning" (the Prequel, §3): the line is a line even when it does not pay.

One point outside the two lines: where the line comes from. At several places where the two part, the difference lies neither in how the purpose is written and put into effect, nor in whether it pays, but in the ground of the line: whether it is investors' preferences and mandate, or something already there before investment and outside any vote. This point is registered in the sixth slot of §7.

§3 The Root: Price and Dignity

§3.1 Kant (A)

The common question. What can be traded off.

The original. The Prequel, §5 cited these sentences. Academy edition IV 434: "In the kingdom of ends everything has either a price or a dignity. What has a price can have something else put in its place as its equivalent; what on the other hand is raised above all price, and therefore admits of no equivalent, has a dignity." ("Im Reiche der Zwecke hat alles entweder einen Preis, oder eine Würde. Was einen Preis hat, an dessen Stelle kann auch etwas anderes als Äquivalent gesetzt werden; was dagegen über allen Preis erhaben ist, mithin kein Äquivalent verstattet, das hat eine Würde.") IV 435, lines 7–9, says what it is that has dignity: "Hence morality, and humanity insofar as it is capable of morality, is that which alone has dignity." ("Also ist Sittlichkeit und die Menschheit, so fern sie derselben fähig ist, dasjenige, was allein Würde hat.") Immediately afterwards: "Skill and diligence in work have a market price; wit, lively imagination and humor have an affective price; on the other hand, fidelity in promising and benevolence from principles (not from instinct) have an inner worth." ("Geschicklichkeit und Fleiß im Arbeiten haben einen Marktpreis; Witz, lebhafte Einbildungskraft und Launen einen Affectionspreis; dagegen Treue im Versprechen, Wohlwollen aus Grundsätzen (nicht aus Instinct) haben einen innern Werth.") (my translation; per the Korpora transcription of the Academy edition)

Where they meet. The predicate of "a person has no equivalent" is taken from "kein Äquivalent verstattet" in IV 434; the subject is taken from the sentence at IV 435, lines 7–9: what has dignity is morality, and humanity insofar as it is capable of morality. This series' "person" does not carry the antecedent "insofar as it is capable of morality," and is read as every person (own undertaking). The Second Enlightenment Paper 1 reads the same two pages: "A person has no equivalent: this layer carries on from Kant." (The Second Enlightenment Paper 1, §4) IV 435 then puts market price on "skill and diligence in work." Kant's line runs between a person's talents and a person's morality; this series takes up the distinction between price and dignity and carries it into the relation between a company and labor, reading it as running between what a person does and the person: a company buys what people do, and the price does not fall on the person; how far the authority acquired by employment extends is developed in Paper 2 (own undertaking). This is what "the source side" means; it is not a claim that Kant already wrote the main-line sentence.

Where they part. Kant's Kingdom of Ends is a community of rational beings; this series takes it as a direction, not as a state to be signed off (Institution Theory Z39). A company itself is not a member of the Kingdom of Ends; it has no first person (the Prequel, §1). That a company's assets or shares are priced does not mean that the people in it are priced; to buy or sell a company is not to buy or sell the people in it (Paper 2, GS5, GS6; mergers are handed to a later theory of the market) (own undertaking). Political Economy Paper 0 has already written one place where it departs from Kant: eligibility to vote is not conditioned on economic independence (Political Economy Paper 0, §3.2); this paper points to it and does not rewrite it.

The strongest objection from the other side (drafted by this paper, from within Kant). By IV 435, what has a market price is skill and diligence, talents in the person himself, not what the person does; the price has already fallen on a part of the person. Your saying "the price does not fall on the person" runs exactly against this sentence.

Reply. This series grants that the line in IV 435 runs between talents and morality. But what IV 435 writes is, in the first place, "im Arbeiten," skill and diligence in work; what this series reads is precisely the use of talents in work: what a company buys is the stretch in which talents are applied to a task, not the talents themselves, still less the person. So what can be adjusted is what people do, posts, working hours, a part of income; the Base Layer is not traded away, and a way out and a way forward are not taken away (Paper 2, GS5, GS6). Time can be sold; the one who sells his time is not thereby bought (own undertaking). This reading stands some distance from Kant's letter: at "what is done," it comes close to "im Arbeiten"; at the subject term, where it drops the antecedent "capable of morality," it stands farther off. This paper registers this as it stands, and does not say that this is what Kant meant.

§4 For Whom a Company Is Run

The Political Economy Specialist Paper handed "for whom a company should be run" to the theory of the company (A Way Out, a Way Forward, "Where This Paper Stops"). This section receives it.

§4.1 Dodge v. Ford Motor Co. (A)

The original. In Dodge v. Ford Motor Co., the Supreme Court of Michigan wrote in 1919: "A business corporation is organized and carried on primarily for the profit of the stockholders. The powers of the directors are to be employed for that end. The discretion of directors is to be exercised in the choice of means to attain that end and does not extend to a change in the end itself, to the reduction of profits or to the nondistribution of profits among stockholders in order to devote them to other purposes." (Dodge v. Ford Motor Co., 204 Mich. 459, 507 (1919); 170 N.W. 668, 684; per the text of the opinion in Harvard Law School's Caselaw Access Project) On the same page it also wrote: "There is committed to the discretion of directors, a discretion to be exercised in good faith, the infinite details of business, including the wages which shall be paid to employees, the number of hours they shall work, the conditions under which labor shall be carried on, and the prices for which products shall be offered to the public." And: "it is not within the lawful powers of a board of directors to shape and conduct the affairs of a corporation for the merely incidental benefit of shareholders and for the primary purpose of benefiting others" (ibid., 507).

The common question. For whom directors' powers are used.

Where they meet. Directors' powers are not their own; they are to be used for some end: the position takes accountability. The opinion writes one further layer: directors' discretion lies in the means, and does not extend to changing the end itself. On this point this series runs in the same direction: the direction is not changed by fiduciary positions such as directors and managers on their own (Paper 1, GS3; Paper 6, GS12; own undertaking). Nor does the opinion say that directors may never make a company earn less: wages, hours, labor conditions and prices remain within directors' good-faith discretion.

Where they part. For whom. This series answers "for people": for those who work in the company, for those who invest, for those who use what it makes, for those its acts reach; and it does not rank the parties (the Prequel, §1).

The strongest objection from the other side (drafted by this paper). By the opinion, conducting a company for the primary purpose of benefiting others is not within the lawful powers of the board. You say that the line is made clear before investment and that investors accepted it; but by this prior acceptance alone, do directors acquire the authority to conduct the company in such a direction?

Reply. This series' line is not a discretion directors exercise under a given end; it is the company's direction itself, made clear before investment (Paper 1, GS3). In a company oriented toward the Kingdom of Ends, investors accepted on putting their money in that profit is a condition and a return, not the ultimate criterion. So guarding the line is not directors changing the end on their own; it is the investors who change the direction, and once they do, the company is out of this series' scope (Paper 1, §3). The layer the opinion asks about is whether directors can change the end on their own, and this series agrees that they cannot (own undertaking). But "made clear before investment" answers only whether directors have changed an existing end on their own; it does not by itself answer whether, in a particular jurisdiction and corporate form, such an end can be lawfully fixed, or what authority directors can acquire. This paper does not derive that legal conclusion from investors' prior acceptance. Existing formulations at the rule layer can serve as examples: under section 172(2) of the UK Companies Act, where the purposes of the company include purposes other than the benefit of its members, directors' duty is read by those purposes; Delaware's public benefit corporation writes a public benefit into its certificate and requires directors to balance among several parties (subsection (1) in Paper 1, §2; public benefit corporations in Paper 1, §3). These two show that the law can permit such a formulation, not that every jurisdiction permits it. There is also a layer that does not depend on direction: the lines that Institution Theory and Political Economy draw for every company are there before the purpose of any one company, and are not cancelled by any one company's purpose clause (the Prequel, §6).

§4.2 Friedman (A)

The original. In The New York Times Magazine of September 13, 1970, Friedman said that a corporate executive is "an employe of the owners of the business," and is to conduct the business in accordance with their desires, "which generally will be to make as much money as possible while conforming to the basic rules of the society, both those embodied in law and those embodied in ethical custom"; further down in the same paragraph: "Of course, in some cases his employers may have a different objective." At the end of the article: there is one and only one social responsibility of business, to use its resources and engage in activities designed to increase its profits "so long as it stays within the rules of the game, which is to say, engages in open and free competition without deception or fraud" (The New York Times Magazine, 1970-09-13, Section SM, p. 17, per the New York Times archive page).

The division of labor with Political Economy Paper 0. There the four steps have already been taken from the economic side: profit stays within the rules of the game; they part over where the boundary comes from (Political Economy Paper 0, §6.2). Here only two points are added from the company side.

Where they meet. "Without deception or fraud": this series says no deception, as agreed (Paper 1, GS2). Nor is this series' line settled by the executive personally: it is made clear before investment (Paper 1, GS3), and is not handed to the executive to choose a social goal for himself.

Where they part. Friedman explains the executive's responsibility by the owners' mandate. This series asks one step further: what authority the control that comes with shares can acquire over the other parties, and what limits it (Paper 1, GS1). Whether the boundary is handed to the rules of the day follows Political Economy Paper 0 and is not rewritten.

The strongest objection from the other side, and the reply. As in Political Economy Paper 0, §6.2; not rewritten.

§4.3 Mackey and Friedman, 2005 (B)

The original. In October 2005, Reason magazine published a debate. Whole Foods' founder Mackey said: "Making high profits is the means to the end of fulfilling Whole Foods' core business mission." Friedman answered: "The differences between John Mackey and me regarding the social responsibility of business are for the most part rhetorical." Speaking of founders setting up donations with their own money, he said: "They were clearly within their rights in doing so." (reason.com, 2005-10-01; the original has been examined; no page numbers)

The common question. Whether profit is a means or an end.

Where they meet (with Mackey). Profit is a condition, not the ultimate criterion (the Prequel, §3; Political Economy Paper 5, E28).

Where they part. After saying that the differences between the two are for the most part rhetorical, Friedman went on: "Strip off the camouflage, and it turns out we are in essential agreement. Moreover, his company, Whole Foods Market, behaves in accordance with the principles I spelled out in my 1970 New York Times Magazine article." In the next paragraph he said: "Had it devoted any significant fraction of its resources to exercising a social responsibility unrelated to the bottom line, it would be out of business by now or would have been taken over." This exchange falls on the second line, and the reading is this paper's (own undertaking): the principle of the 1970 article is increasing profits within the rules of the game; if the reasons for treating every party well are put here, then in the part justified by making money, the two converge. This series' line does not rest on that sentence: the line is a line even when it does not pay (Paper 1, GS3; Paper 3, GS8a). This paper does not say on Mackey's behalf whether he relies on "winning"; it reads only these sentences.

One more thing must be made clear. "Stated in advance and accepted by investors" is not where this series parts from Friedman: further down the same paragraph of the 1970 article he grants that owners may have a different objective, and in 2005 he too said that founders setting up donations with their own money were "clearly within their rights." Where they part is the ground of the line: this series' line is not grounded in investors' preferences or in the outcome of profit (Paper 1, GS3). Investors may change the direction, and once they do, the company leaves this series; what is not cancelled along with it are the lines Institution Theory and Political Economy draw for every company (the Prequel, §6) (own undertaking). This point lies outside the two lines: where the line comes from.

The strongest objection from the other side (drafted by this paper, on the side of execution). The line is written in the articles, but when it is applied to decision after decision, it is still executives who judge hard times and judge the Base Layer; executive discretion comes back, only under another name.

Reply. That is why Paper 6 does not hand keeping the direction to executives' agreement: the position that judges gives reasons and submits to review, and if the judgment is wrong bears the consequences that correspond to the position (Paper 6, GS13); self-correcting channels are independent of the party questioned, and those who ask are not penalized for asking (Paper 6, GS12). The discretion is still there; the discretion is questioned.

§4.4 Freeman (B)

The original. In chapter 3 of Strategic Management: A Stakeholder Approach, Freeman writes that "there are groups and individuals who can affect, or are affected by, the achievement of an organization's mission." (Cambridge chapter page; page number not confirmed)

The common question. Who is counted in.

Where they meet. Those affected are brought into consideration ("are affected by").

Where they part (only as far as this one sentence allows). This series examines separately how the people concerned enter into a relation of being inside (the two branches of Institution Theory Z4), and how the difficulty of leaving affects the strength of explanation and of checks (the Prequel, §7). That "who is counted in" and "the degree of a relation of being inside" are questions on the same layer has not been shown; this one sentence is not enough to judge where the two ways of identifying people part, and that is settled once the original book has been examined. Nor does this paper place, on Freeman's behalf, whether what he speaks of is a method of management or a normative baseline.

Having examined only one sentence, this paper drafts no objection.

§4.5 The Business Roundtable, 2019 (A)

The original. On August 19, 2019, the American Business Roundtable issued its Statement on the Purpose of a Corporation, signed by 181 CEOs: "While each of our individual companies serves its own corporate purpose, we share a fundamental commitment to all of our stakeholders." Its five commitments fall on customers, employees, suppliers, communities and shareholders; the item on employees writes "We foster diversity and inclusion, dignity and respect."; and at the end it says: "Each of our stakeholders is essential." (businessroundtable.org)

The common question. To which parties a company has commitments.

Where they meet. The five parties almost coincide with this series' five: investors, employees, users, partners, society. The item on employees writes dignity.

Where they part. Where it lands. In this series, consensus is identified by where it lands: written into the articles and decision procedures, able to be invoked when decisions are made, able to be used to question; where the line is, and whether the channels of questioning have efficacy, are examined by the three questions (the Prequel, §5; Paper 6, GS12). A statement by itself has not yet answered these questions (own undertaking). What is judged is the statement as a document, not the companies that signed it.

The strongest objection from the other side (drafted by this paper). The Prequel counts one sentence said publicly by Barry-Wehmiller's CEO as a landing; why does a statement signed by one hundred and eighty-one CEOs not count?

Reply. Both can be invoked and used to question. Whether either has independent and effective channels of questioning must still be examined for both; the difference already visible here is whether it is tied to a particular decision. Barry-Wehmiller's sentence is tied to the furlough arrangement of 2009; the statement by itself is not tied to any decision. So the Prequel says only that the sentence "becomes one that can be used to question the company," not that it has already answered the three questions; and this paper speaks only of the statement itself, not of the signatory companies all lacking corresponding arrangements.

§4.6 Bebchuk and Tallarita (B)

The original. In The Illusory Promise of Stakeholder Governance (2020), Bebchuk and Tallarita conclude: "Stakeholderism, we conclude, is an inadequate and substantially counterproductive approach to addressing stakeholder concerns." "By making corporate leaders less accountable and more insulated from shareholder oversight," it "would impede or delay reforms that could bring real, meaningful protection to stakeholders"; "Stakeholderism should be rejected, including and especially by those who take stakeholder interests seriously." (Cornell Law Review 106, abstract; the body of the article not opened)

The common question. Who questions the company.

Where they meet. Merely handing the various parties to management's discretion leaves them unable to question, which comes to nothing. This is exactly the point of Paper 6: the channels of questioning must be independent of the party questioned (Paper 6, GS12) (own undertaking).

Where they part. Their conclusion is to reject stakeholder governance. This series does not choose a name between "for shareholders" and "for every party" (the first line). Its answer lies in channels: employees' voice (Paper 2, GS7), users being able to refuse and to leave (Paper 3, GS8, GS9), those who ask not being penalized for asking (Paper 6, GS12), the baseline not being weighed in a vote (Paper 1, GS3). Those who ask are not only shareholders. Beyond the abstract, how they design outside protections for the various parties this paper has not examined, and does not write.

The strongest objection from the other side (drafted by this paper). You draw lines for all five parties, but management still carries them out; the more lines, the more management can use one party to block another, and the less it is questioned.

Reply. This series' most direct answer is that lines do not go onto the scale (Paper 1, GS3): lines are not weighed against one another, and management cannot use one party's line to block another's; where one party can be used to block another is only above the line, at the layer where the various parties' interests are traded off, and there the answer is again given by the channels of questioning, examined by the three questions: who can activate it, what efficacy it has, how the party questioned cannot cancel it on its own (Political Economy Paper 3, E16). More lines do not replace the channels of questioning; once channels are set up, whether they have efficacy in conflict must still be examined. Examined in this way, this series' own example shows a gap: the right to sue in a Delaware public benefit corporation is given only to stockholders who meet the threshold (Paper 6, §2).

§4.7 The British Academy, "Future of the Corporation" (B)

The original. In 2019 the British Academy's "Future of the Corporation" programme published its Principles for Purposeful Business: "the purpose of business is to solve the problems of people and planet profitably, and not profit from causing problems." The first of the eight principles is that corporate law should place purpose at its center (thebritishacademy.ac.uk, publication page; the body of the report not opened).

The common question. What a company's purpose is written as.

Where they meet. "Profitably": profit is a condition (the Prequel, §3). "Not profit from causing problems": costs are not pushed onto others (Paper 5, GS11). "Corporate law placing purpose at its center" runs in the same direction as Paper 1's "the baseline is made clear before investment" (Paper 1, GS3).

Where they part. "People and planet": this series says only that a company exists for people. The planet is not a person; the environment comes in through the people the company's acts reach (Paper 1's taking from Patagonia only the structure; Paper 5) (own undertaking). The first line: whatever the purpose is written as, this paper asks where the line is and what efficacy the channels have. This end rests on the organization's page, and does not answer for the complete argument of any author in the programme.

The strongest objection from the other side (drafted by this paper). Not writing the planet lets pass the harms that do not fall on any one person: the disappearance of a species, an uninhabited sea.

Reply. This series comes in only through people: society is identified item by item by "having no way to avoid a given act by exiting" (Paper 5, §1), and the environment comes in through the people the company's acts reach. Harms that do not fall on any one person this series does not cover, and this is registered as it stands in the second slot of §7. How people who come later are counted under "having no way to avoid it by exiting" Paper 5 has not written either, and this is registered in the fourth slot.

§4.8 Hart and Zingales (B)

The original. In the abstract of "Companies Should Maximize Shareholder Welfare Not Market Value" (2017), Hart and Zingales write that "shareholders are prosocial and externalities are not perfectly separable from production decisions"; "We argue that maximization of shareholder welfare is not the same as maximization of market value." (Journal of Law, Finance, and Accounting 2(2): 247–274, abstract)

The common question. Whether all investors want is money.

Where they meet. Investors are first of all people, each with ends of his own (Paper 1, §1).

Where they part. §5.2 Jensen and Meckling (cited at second hand)

§5.4 Ciepley (B)

The original. In the abstract of "Beyond Public and Private: Toward a Political Theory of the Corporation" (2013), Ciepley writes: "Corporations are government-like in their powers, and government grants them both their external 'personhood' and their internal governing authority." "They are thus not simply private." Yet "they are privately organized and financed and therefore not simply public."; corporations need to be placed in a separate category, "neither public nor private, but 'corporate'". He also says that "The liberal effort to assimilate them, treating them as contractually constituted associations of private property owners, endows them with rights they ought not have" (American Political Science Review 107(1): 139–158, abstract).

The common question. Where a company's power comes from, and what constrains it.

Where they meet. A company has a power-face, and must be subject to checks (Paper 1, GS1; Paper 4, GS10).

Where they part. Ciepley reads it from the government's grant; this series reads it from the power-face: whether or not there is a grant, wherever the power-face is, the checks go (Political Economy Paper 3, E14) (own undertaking). The two also come close at one point: Political Economy says that the power of capital comes from "the narrative of possession is carried by society, and others register it as possession" (Political Economy Paper 1, E2), which, in its "coming from outside," is close to "grant." This paper has examined only the abstract, and does not settle here how near or far the two are. What rights companies ought to have this series does not enter into.

The strongest objection from the other side (drafted by this paper). Without asking about the grant, one cannot say on what ground a state makes rules for companies.

Reply. That question belongs between states and companies, and is handed to a later theory of the market. This series asks only how one company treats five parties; its oughts for the company do not presuppose whether a state has granted anything, nor does it answer for the state on what ground it makes rules.

第一节　这是一台什么样的机器

一 · 它问的是什么

一家以"人是目的"为共识的公司，怎样待出资人、员工、用户、合作伙伴和它的施为所及的人：底线是不越线，不把任何人当作可以抵换的东西；方向是给条件，让这些人各自往下走的路更宽；利润是条件与回报，不作最终标准；守住这个方向的安排，自己也可被追问。

它不问公司该不该存在，不问哪一种所有权形式最好，不问利润该多高。它只写目的王国方向的公司；手段王国方向只作对照（前置篇第六节）。

二 · 句式与三层

构用应当，位置用职责，人用自己的法：公司是构，本系列对它写应当；董事、经理、CEO 在位置上，写职责；员工、用户、出资人各有自己的法，本系列不对他们写应当。

三层是方法：规范层给方向与底线；规则层只作建议、可以举例；判断层写清谁定、谁负责；永远有余项。判操作，不判人；只谈结构，不谈对错，不是说没有对错，是不给一个人、一家公司下道德的判词（前置篇第七节）。

三 · 根与主线

主线句：人没有等价物：公司买的是人做的事，不是人；价格落在事上，不落在人上。

七篇各落一处：

  • 出资：股份不是对人的所有（GS1）；底线在出资之前讲明，不在表决里称量（GS3）。
  • 共事：公司买的是事，不是人（GS5）；离得开，有的选，他自己的事由他自己定（GS6）。
  • 服务：不锁死，不操纵，不欺瞒（GS8）；不以健康与安全换利润（GS8a）；模式不判名称，判执行（GS9）。
  • 伙伴：人没有等价物沿着链往下（GS10）。
  • 社会：底线是不越线，贡献是方向（GS11）。
  • 守向：守住方向的安排，不以共识为前提（GS12）；难关的判断与负责（GS13）。

四 · 与上游的分工

制度论、政治经济学与它的专论已经证过的，这个系列不重证，只落到一家公司对五方的关系上：内部按纠缠划，两支（制度论 Z4）；构的施为不越线，不随任何条件打折（制度论 Z9）；通道独立于被问的一方，可以发动，发动不被报复（制度论 Z34）；权力在能调动积累的位置（政治经济学第一篇 E2）；关口与生命基底处唯一的供给位置（第二篇 E7、E11a）；制衡随权力面递增，三问（第三篇 E14、E16）；两道闸与闸以外的交换（第四篇 E21–E24）；回报率不作最终标准（第五篇 E28）；支持不取得替人定方向的权限（第六篇 E37）；离得开，有的选，一个人自己的事（专论 E40、E41）。

第二节　对接的方法

一 · 四步

每一场对话走四步：先找可核的共同问题；再看两家说的是不是同一件事；再说同意到哪里为止，从哪里分开；最后写对方一侧最强的一条反驳，与本系列的回应。反驳由本篇代拟，标明代拟，尽量触到对方的强处。

二 · 两种深度

原文核到的，按核到的范围推进。核到全文或判词原文的，四步走完：康德、道奇诉福特汽车公司案、弗里德曼、商业圆桌会议。只核到摘要、组织页面或辩论原文的几段，按核到的那几句走完四步，并写明那几句以外不替对方作答：麦基与弗里德曼的辩论、别布丘克与塔拉里塔、英国学院、奇普利。只核到一句或摘要、材料不足以代拟一条强反驳的，走到第三步，注明止步处：弗里曼、哈特与津加莱斯。

只到二手材料的，走到共同的问题与接触面为止：科斯、詹森与梅克林、伯利与米恩斯、安德森、费雷拉斯。这些单元里，本篇写明据什么转述、接触面在哪里、原书核到以后要决定的是哪一问；不写"同在""分在"的定论，也不加引号。

三 · 差异落在两条线上

对话写下来，有几处差异落在同样的两条线上。两条线是本篇的归纳，不是哪一家自己的分法，也不是全部差异的分类。

第一条，目的的表述与线的落实。 各家既争公司的目的，"为股东""为利益相关者""为人与地球"，也争目的怎样落实为激励、问责与制度安排。本篇沿这条线追问：无论哪一种目的表述，实际的线在哪里，谁能启动追问，通道有什么效力（政治经济学第五篇 E27 说，目标的名称与一句声明不独自确认读数；第三篇 E16）。这是本篇的问法，不是说对方只争名称。

第二条，靠不靠"会赢"。 有的辩护说，善待各方长远看也赚钱。这个系列的规范层不靠"会赢"立论（前置篇第三节）：线在不赚钱的时候也是线。

两条线以外的一处：线从哪里来。 有几处分开的地方，不在目的怎样写、怎样落实，也不在赚不赚钱，在线的根据：是出资人的偏好与委托，还是在出资之前、在表决之外就在。这一处登在第七节第六格。

第三节　根：价格与尊严

一 · 康德（A）

共同的问题。 什么可以被抵换。

原文。 前置篇第五节引过这几句。学院版 IV 434：「在目的王国里，一切东西或者有一个价格，或者有一种尊严。有价格的东西，也可以用别的东西作为等价物放在它的位置上；与此相反，超乎一切价格之上、因而不容许任何等价物的东西，有一种尊严。」（"Im Reiche der Zwecke hat alles entweder einen Preis, oder eine Würde. Was einen Preis hat, an dessen Stelle kann auch etwas anderes als Äquivalent gesetzt werden; was dagegen über allen Preis erhaben ist, mithin kein Äquivalent verstattet, das hat eine Würde."）IV 435 第 7–9 行说有尊严的是什么：「所以，道德性以及能有道德性的人性，是唯一有尊严的东西。」（"Also ist Sittlichkeit und die Menschheit, so fern sie derselben fähig ist, dasjenige, was allein Würde hat."）紧接着：「工作中的技巧与勤勉有一个市场价格；机智、活泼的想象与情趣有一个情感价格；与此相反，守诺的忠实、出于原则（不是出于本能）的善意，有一种内在的价值。」（"Geschicklichkeit und Fleiß im Arbeiten haben einen Marktpreis; Witz, lebhafte Einbildungskraft und Launen einen Affectionspreis; dagegen Treue im Versprechen, Wohlwollen aus Grundsätzen (nicht aus Instinct) haben einen innern Werth."）（据 Korpora 学院版转录）

同在。 "人没有等价物"的谓语取自 IV 434 的"kein Äquivalent verstattet"；主词取自 IV 435 第 7–9 行那一句：有尊严的，是道德性与能有道德性的人性。这个系列的"人"不带"就它能有道德性而言"这一前件，读作每一个人（自担）。二次启蒙论第一篇读过同两页：「人没有等价物，这一层接着康德。」（二次启蒙论第一篇第四节）IV 435 接着把市场价格放在"工作中的技巧与勤勉"上。康德的线划在人的才能与人的道德性之间；这个系列承接价格与尊严之分，把它推进到公司与劳动的关系，读作人做的事与人之间：公司买的是人做的事，价格不落在人上；雇佣取得的权限到哪里为止，由第二篇展开（自担）。这就是"出处一侧"的意思，不是说主线句康德已经写了。

分在。 康德的目的王国是理性存在者的共同体；这个系列取它作方向，不作要验收的状态（制度论 Z39）。公司本身不是目的王国的成员，它没有第一人称（前置篇第一节）。公司的资产或股份被计价，不等于对其中的人计价；买卖一家公司，不买卖它里面的人（第二篇 GS5、GS6；并购交日后的市场论）（自担）。政治经济学 Paper 0 已写过一处离开康德的地方：投票的资格不以经济独立为条件（政治经济学 Paper 0 第三节第二小节）；本篇照指，不重写。

对方最强的反驳（本篇代拟，从康德内部）。 照 IV 435，有市场价格的是技巧与勤勉，那是人自己身上的才能，不是人做的事；价格已经落在了人的一部分上。你们说"价格不落在人上"，恰恰与这一句相反。

回应。 这个系列承认 IV 435 的线划在才能与道德性之间。可 IV 435 写的本来就是"im Arbeiten"，工作中的技巧与勤勉；这个系列读的，正是才能在工作里的使用：公司买的，是才能用在一件事上的那一段，不是才能本身，更不是人。所以可以调整的是人做的事，岗位、工时、收入的一部分；基础层不拿去兑换，离得开、有的选不拿走（第二篇 GS5、GS6）。时间可以卖，卖时间的人不因此被买下（自担）。这一读离康德的字面有一段距离：在"事"这一处，它与"im Arbeiten"靠得近；在主词那一处，它去掉了"能有道德性"的前件，离得远些。本篇照实登记，不说它就是康德的意思。

第四节　公司为谁经营

政治经济学的专论把"公司该为谁经营"交给了公司论（专论"这一篇停在哪里"）。这一节收这一件。

一 · 道奇诉福特汽车公司案（A）

原文。 1919 年密歇根州最高法院在道奇诉福特汽车公司案里写：「商业公司的组建与经营，主要是为了股东的利润。董事的权力，要为这一目的而用。董事的裁量，在于选择达成这一目的的手段，不及于改变目的本身，也不及于为其他目的而减少利润或不向股东分配利润。」（"A business corporation is organized and carried on primarily for the profit of the stockholders. The powers of the directors are to be employed for that end. The discretion of directors is to be exercised in the choice of means to attain that end and does not extend to a change in the end itself, to the reduction of profits or to the nondistribution of profits among stockholders in order to devote them to other purposes."）（Dodge v. Ford Motor Co., 204 Mich. 459, 507 (1919)；170 N.W. 668, 684；据哈佛法学院 Caselaw Access Project 所载判词原文）同页又写：「交给董事善意裁量的，是经营上无数的细节，包括付给员工的工资、他们工作的时数、劳动的条件，以及产品向公众售出的价格。」（"There is committed to the discretion of directors, a discretion to be exercised in good faith, the infinite details of business, including the wages which shall be paid to employees, the number of hours they shall work, the conditions under which labor shall be carried on, and the prices for which products shall be offered to the public."）以及：「以股东只得附带之利、以使他人受益为主要目的来安排与经营公司的事务，不在董事会的合法权限之内。」（"it is not within the lawful powers of a board of directors to shape and conduct the affairs of a corporation for the merely incidental benefit of shareholders and for the primary purpose of benefiting others"）（同上，507）

共同的问题。 董事的权力为谁而用。

同在。 董事的权力不是董事自己的，要为某个目的而用：位置用职责。判词还写了一层：董事的裁量在手段上，不及于改变目的本身。这个系列在这一点上与它同向：方向不由董事、经理这类受托的位置独自改（第一篇 GS3；第六篇 GS12；自担）。判词也没有说董事一概不能使公司少赚：工资、工时、劳动条件与售价，仍在董事善意的裁量里。

分在。 为谁。这个系列答"为人而存在"：为在里面做事的人，为出资的人，为用它所造之物的人，为它施为所及的人；不给各方排序（前置篇第一节）。

对方最强的反驳（本篇代拟）。 照判词，以使他人受益为主要目的来经营公司，不在董事会的合法权限之内。你们说线在出资之前讲明、出资人接受了；可仅凭这一先行接受，董事就取得了照这样的方向经营的权限吗？

回应。 这个系列的线不是董事在一个既定目的下行使的裁量，是公司的方向本身，在出资之前讲明（第一篇 GS3）。在目的王国方向的公司里，出资人投进来的时候就接受了：利润是条件与回报，不作最终标准。所以守线不是董事擅自改了目的；改方向的是出资人，改了，公司就出了这个系列的范围（第一篇第三节）。判词问的一层是董事能不能独自改目的，这个系列同意不能（自担）。可"出资前讲明"回答的，只是董事有没有擅自改既有的目的；它本身还没有回答，在具体的法域与公司形态下，这样的目的能不能依法确定，董事能取得什么权限。本篇不由出资人的先行接受推出这一法律结论。规则层已有的写法可作例子：英国《公司法》第 172 条第 2 款，公司的目的包括成员利益以外的目的时，董事的义务照那些目的读；特拉华的公益公司，把公益写进章程，要董事在几方之间求平衡（第 1 款见第一篇第二节，公益公司见第一篇第三节）。这两处说明法律可以准许这样写，不说明每一个法域都准许。另有一层不靠方向：制度论、政治经济学说给一切公司的线，在任何一家公司的目的之前就在，不由一家公司的目的条款取消（前置篇第六节）。

二 · 弗里德曼（A）

原文。 弗里德曼 1970 年 9 月 13 日在《纽约时报杂志》上说：公司经理是「企业所有者的雇员」（"an employe of the owners of the business"），要照他们的意愿经营企业，「这通常就是在遵守社会的基本规则——既有体现在法律中的，也有体现在伦理习俗中的——的同时，尽可能多地赚钱」（"which generally will be to make as much money as possible while conforming to the basic rules of the society, both those embodied in law and those embodied in ethical custom"）；同段下文：「当然，在某些情形下，他的雇主可能另有目标。」（"Of course, in some cases his employers may have a different objective."）文末：企业有且只有一项社会责任，运用它的资源、从事旨在增加利润的活动，「只要它守在游戏规则之内，也就是说，从事公开、自由、没有欺骗或欺诈的竞争」（"so long as it stays within the rules of the game, which is to say, engages in open and free competition without deception or fraud"）（The New York Times Magazine, 1970-09-13, Section SM, p. 17，据《纽约时报》存档页）。

与政治经济学 Paper 0 的分工。 那里已经从经济的一侧走完四步：利润守在游戏规则之内；分在边界从哪里来（政治经济学 Paper 0 第六节第二小节）。这里只补公司一侧的两处。

同在。 "without deception or fraud"：这个系列说不欺瞒，照约定（第一篇 GS2）。这个系列的线也不是经理个人认定的：它在出资之前讲明（第一篇 GS3），不交给经理去自选一个社会目标。

分在。 弗里德曼以所有者的委托说明经理的职责。这个系列多问一步：股份带来的控制，对其他当事人能取得什么权限，受什么限制（第一篇 GS1）。边界是不是交给一时的规则，照政治经济学 Paper 0，不重写。

对方最强的反驳与回应。 照政治经济学 Paper 0 第六节第二小节，不重写。

三 · 麦基与弗里德曼，2005（B）

原文。 2005 年 10 月，《理性》（Reason）杂志刊出一场辩论。全食超市（Whole Foods）的创办人麦基说：「赚取高额利润，是实现全食核心使命这一目的的手段。」（"Making high profits is the means to the end of fulfilling Whole Foods' core business mission."）弗里德曼答：「约翰·麦基与我在企业社会责任上的分歧，大部分是修辞上的。」（"The differences between John Mackey and me regarding the social responsibility of business are for the most part rhetorical."）说到创办人用自己的钱设捐赠，他说：「他们这样做，显然是在他们的权利之内。」（"They were clearly within their rights in doing so."）（reason.com，2005-10-01；原文已核，无页码）

共同的问题。 利润是手段还是目的。

同在（与麦基）。 利润是条件，不是最终标准（前置篇第三节；政治经济学第五篇 E28）。

分在。 弗里德曼说两人的分歧大部分在修辞以后，接着写：「剥去伪装，原来我们在本质上一致。而且，他的公司全食超市，正是照我在 1970 年《纽约时报杂志》那篇文章里阐述的原则行事。」（"Strip off the camouflage, and it turns out we are in essential agreement. Moreover, his company, Whole Foods Market, behaves in accordance with the principles I spelled out in my 1970 New York Times Magazine article."）下一段又说，全食若把相当一部分资源用于与盈亏无关的社会责任，「现在早已关门，或已被人收购」（"Had it devoted any significant fraction of its resources to exercising a social responsibility unrelated to the bottom line, it would be out of business by now or would have been taken over."）。这一场落在第二条线上，读法是本篇的（自担）：1970 年那篇的原则，是守在游戏规则之内增加利润；善待各方的理由若归到这里，在以赚钱作辩护的这一部分，两者趋同。这个系列的线不靠这一句：线在不赚钱的时候也是线（第一篇 GS3；第三篇 GS8a）。本篇不替麦基说他靠不靠"会赢"，只读这几句。

还有一处要说清。"事先讲明、出资人接受"，不是这个系列与弗里德曼分开的地方：1970 年那篇同段下文就承认所有者可以另有目标，2005 年他也说创办人用自己的钱设捐赠"显然在他们的权利之内"。分开的地方，在线的根据：这个系列的线，不以出资人的偏好或利润的结果为根据（第一篇 GS3）。出资人可以改方向，改了，公司就出了这个系列；不随之取消的，是制度论、政治经济学说给一切公司的线（前置篇第六节）（自担）。这一处落在两条线以外：线从哪里来。

对方最强的反驳（本篇代拟，执行一侧）。 线写在章程里，用到一项项决定上时，判难关、判基础层的还是经理；经理的裁量又回来了，只是换了一个名字。

回应。 所以第六篇不把守向交给经理的认同：作判断的位置说明理由、接受复核，判错了担与位置相应的后果（第六篇 GS13）；自我修正的通道独立于被问的一方，问的人不因问而受罚（第六篇 GS12）。裁量还在；裁量受问。

四 · 弗里曼（B）

原文。 弗里曼在《战略管理：利益相关者方法》第三章写：「有一些群体与个人，能够影响一个组织使命的达成，或受其影响。」（"there are groups and individuals who can affect, or are affected by, the achievement of an organization's mission."）（Cambridge 章节页；页码未确认）

共同的问题。 谁算进来。

同在。 把受影响的人纳入考量（"are affected by"）。

分在（只到这一句能说的）。 这个系列另核相关的人怎样进入内部的关系（制度论 Z4 两支），离开的难度怎样影响说明与制衡的强度（前置篇第七节）。"谁算进来"与"内部关系的程度"，还没有证明是同一层的问题；仅凭这一句，不足以判两套识别在哪里分开，原书核到再定。本篇也不替弗里曼定位他说的是管理的方法，还是规范的底线。

只核到一句，本篇不代拟反驳。

五 · 商业圆桌会议，2019（A）

原文。 2019 年 8 月 19 日，美国商业圆桌会议（Business Roundtable）发布《公司目的声明》，181 位 CEO 签署：「我们各家公司各有自己的公司目的，但我们共有一项对我们所有利益相关者的根本承诺。」（"While each of our individual companies serves its own corporate purpose, we share a fundamental commitment to all of our stakeholders."）五项承诺落在客户、员工、供应商、社区与股东上；员工一条写了「我们促进多样与包容、尊严与尊重」（"We foster diversity and inclusion, dignity and respect."）；末了说：「我们每一方利益相关者都不可或缺。」（"Each of our stakeholders is essential."）（businessroundtable.org）

共同的问题。 公司对哪几方有承诺。

同在。 五方与这个系列的五方几乎重合：出资人、员工、用户、伙伴、社会。员工一条写了尊严。

分在。 落处。这个系列的共识按落处认：写进章程与决策程序，做决定时可以被援引、可以拿来问；线在哪里，问的通道有没有实效，照三问核（前置篇第五节；第六篇 GS12）。一份声明本身，还没有回答这几问（自担）。判的是声明这一份文件，不判签署的各家公司。

对方最强的反驳（本篇代拟）。 前置篇把 Barry-Wehmiller 的 CEO 公开说的一句话算作落处；一份一百八十一位 CEO 签了名的声明，为什么不算？

回应。 两者都能被援引、拿来问。两者有没有独立而有效的问的通道，都还要核；这里已经看见的差别，是它是否系于一项具体的决定。Barry-Wehmiller 那一句系在 2009 年的休假安排上；声明本身没有系在哪一项决定上。所以前置篇只说那一句"成了可以拿来问公司的一句话"，不说它已经回答了三问；本篇也只说声明本身，不说签署的公司都没有相应的安排。

六 · 别布丘克与塔拉里塔（B）

原文。 别布丘克与塔拉里塔在《利益相关者治理的虚幻承诺》（2020）里的结论是：「我们的结论是，利益相关者主义是一种不足、并在相当程度上适得其反的处理利益相关者关切的方式。」（"Stakeholderism, we conclude, is an inadequate and substantially counterproductive approach to addressing stakeholder concerns."）它会使公司领导人「更少受问责、更隔绝于股东的监督」（"By making corporate leaders less accountable and more insulated from shareholder oversight"），并「阻碍或推迟能给利益相关者带来真实、有意义保护的改革」（"would impede or delay reforms that could bring real, meaningful protection to stakeholders"）；「利益相关者主义应当被拒绝，包括、尤其是被那些认真对待利益相关者利益的人拒绝。」（"Stakeholderism should be rejected, including and especially by those who take stakeholder interests seriously."）（Cornell Law Review 106，摘要；正文未开）

共同的问题。 谁来问公司。

同在。 只把各方交给管理层的裁量，问不到，等于没有。第六篇说的正是这一处：问的通道要独立于被问的一方（第六篇 GS12）（自担）。

分在。 他们的结论是拒绝利益相关者治理。这个系列不在"为股东"与"为各方"之间选一个名称（第一条线）。它的回答在通道：员工的声音（第二篇 GS7），用户能拒、能走（第三篇 GS8、GS9），问的人不因问而受罚（第六篇 GS12），底线不在表决里称量（第一篇 GS3）。问的人不只是股东。摘要以外，他们怎样设计对各方的外部保护，本篇未核，不写。

对方最强的反驳（本篇代拟）。 你们给五方都立了线，执行的还是管理层；线越多，管理层越能拿一方挡另一方，越不受问。

回应。 这个系列最直接的一答，是线不进天平（第一篇 GS3）：线与线之间不称量，管理层不能拿一方的线去挡另一方的线；能拿一方挡另一方的地方，只在线以上，各方利益怎样取舍的那一层，那里再以问的通道去答，照三问核：谁能启动，有什么实效，被问的一方怎样不能独自取消它（政治经济学第三篇 E16）。线多，并不替代问的通道；通道立了，也还要核它在冲突中有没有实效。这个系列自己的例子照此核，显出的是一个缺口：特拉华公益公司的诉权，只给达到门槛的股东（第六篇第二节）。

七 · 英国学院"公司的未来"（B）

原文。 英国学院（British Academy）"公司的未来"项目 2019 年发布《目的型企业原则》：「企业的目的，是以有利可图的方式解决人与地球的问题，而不是从制造问题中获利。」（"the purpose of business is to solve the problems of people and planet profitably, and not profit from causing problems."）八条原则的第一条，是公司法把目的置于中心（thebritishacademy.ac.uk，出版物页；报告正文未开）。

共同的问题。 公司的目的写成什么。

同在。 "profitably"：利润是条件（前置篇第三节）。"not profit from causing problems"：成本不推给别人（第五篇 GS11）。"公司法把目的置于中心"，与第一篇"底线在出资之前讲明"同向（第一篇 GS3）。

分在。 "people and planet"：这个系列只说为人而存在。地球不是人；环境经由施为所及的人进来（第一篇对巴塔哥尼亚只取结构的处理；第五篇）（自担）。第一条线：无论目的写成什么，本篇追问线在哪里、通道有什么效力。这一端据的是组织页面，不替项目里任何一位作者的完整论证作答。

对方最强的反驳（本篇代拟）。 不写地球，就把那些不落在任何一个人身上的损害放过去了：一个物种的消失，一片无人居住的海。

回应。 这个系列只从人进来：社会按"就某一项施为无从以退出避开"逐项认（第五篇第一节），环境经由施为所及的人进来。不落在任何一个人身上的损害，这个系列不覆盖，照实登在第七节第二格。后来的人怎样计入"无从以退出避开"，第五篇也没有写，登在第四格。

八 · 哈特与津加莱斯（B）

原文。 哈特与津加莱斯在《公司应当最大化股东福利，而非市场价值》（2017）的摘要里写：「股东是亲社会的，外部性与生产决策又不能完全分开」（"shareholders are prosocial and externalities are not perfectly separable from production decisions"）；「我们论证，最大化股东福利，不同于最大化市场价值。」（"We argue that maximization of shareholder welfare is not the same as maximization of market value."）（Journal of Law, Finance, and Accounting 2(2): 247–274，摘要）

共同的问题。 出资人要的只是钱吗。

同在。 出资人首先是人，各有自己的目的（第一篇第一节）。

分在。 他们从出资人的福利出发；这个系列的底线不从出资人的偏好来，它在出资之前讲明，在出资人的表决之外（第一篇 GS3）（自担）。这一处落在两条线以外：线从哪里来。摘要以外的设计未核，不写。

只据摘要，本篇不代拟反驳。

九 · 这一节停在哪里

各家既争目的，也争落实。这个系列不论目的写成什么，追问线在哪里，谁能问，问了有没有用。专论交来的"公司该为谁经营"，在这个系列的射程之内、与本篇已核文本相交的那一部分，在这里收件；英国学院一端据的是组织文本，不替一位作者的完整论证作答。

第五节　企业是什么，谁拥有

一 · 科斯（C）

据政治经济学 Paper 0 所据 Econlib《简明经济学百科》的科斯条目转述：科斯在《企业的性质》（1937）里问企业为什么存在，答案是使用市场有成本（政治经济学 Paper 0 第五节第二小节）。接触面：两家都问企业为什么存在。前置篇第一节写：几个人各自做不成、一起做得成的事，要有一个能持有资源、订立约定、承担后果的构。原书核到以后要决定：这一句与"使用市场有成本"是不是在答同一问；"为人而存在"是不是另一问。

二 · 詹森与梅克林（转引）

据 Gindis 2020 的转述：詹森与梅克林把企业界定为一种法律拟制，作个人之间一组合同关系的联结点。这里不加引号。接触面：公司不是人（前置篇第一节）。原书核到以后要决定：合同的联结能不能作应当的地址；这个系列对构写应当，构是归责的地址（政治经济学第一篇 E2）。

三 · 伯利与米恩斯（C）

照政治经济学 Paper 0 第五节第一小节，不重写。这个系列在那里多出的一句：控制是一个位置，受制衡（第一篇 GS1）。通衢的票在两个系列里是同一个例子。

四 · 奇普利（B）

原文。 奇普利在《越出公与私：走向公司的政治理论》（2013）的摘要里写：「公司在权力上类似政府，而政府授予它们对外的'人格'与对内的治理权威。」（"Corporations are government-like in their powers, and government grants them both their external “personhood” and their internal governing authority."）它们「因此并不单纯是私的」（"They are thus not simply private."），可它们「是私人组织、私人出资的，因此也不单纯是公的」（"Yet they are privately organized and financed and therefore not simply public."）；公司需要被放进一个单独的类别，「既非公、也非私，而是'公司的'」（"neither public nor private, but “corporate”"）。他又说，自由主义同化公司的努力，把公司当作私人财产所有者以契约组成的联合体，「赋予了它们不该有的权利」（"The liberal effort to assimilate them, treating them as contractually constituted associations of private property owners, endows them with rights they ought not have"）（American Political Science Review 107(1): 139–158，摘要）。

共同的问题。 公司的权力从哪里来，受什么约束。

同在。 公司有权力面，要受制衡（第一篇 GS1；第四篇 GS10）。

分在。 奇普利从政府的授予来读；这个系列从权力面来读：不论有无授予，权力面在哪里，制衡就到哪里（政治经济学第三篇 E14）（自担）。两家也有一处靠得近：政治经济学说资本的权力来自「关于占有的叙事被社会承载，别人把它登记为占有」（政治经济学第一篇 E2），在"由外而来"这一点上，与"授予"相近。本篇只核到摘要，不在这里定两者的远近。公司应当有哪些权利，这个系列不进。

对方最强的反驳（本篇代拟）。 不问授予，就说不清国家凭什么给公司立规。

回应。 那一问是国家与公司之间的事，交日后的市场论。这个系列只问一家公司怎样待五方；它对公司的应当，不以国家是否授予为前提，也不替国家回答它凭什么立规。

第六节　工作场所

一 · 安德森（C）

据普林斯顿大学出版社的书介转述：《私人政府》（2017）把许多工作场所读作专断的私人政府，并质疑自由市场使工人自由这一说法。政治经济学 Paper 0 另据 Estlund 2018 的书评转述：辞职的自由不足以保护雇员（政治经济学 Paper 0 第五节第三小节）。接触面在第二篇：离得开是底线，可离得开不够，员工的声音还要实（第二篇 GS6、GS7）。原书核到以后要决定："私人政府"这一读法，与这个系列"判做法，不给一家公司定性"，是不是在同一层上说话。

二 · 费雷拉斯（C）

据剑桥大学出版社的书介转述：《作为政治实体的企业》（2017）以"经济两院制"把企业看作政治实体；页面所录的一则评语，把这一提议概括为代表工人与资本所有者的两院机构。接触面：专论交来的"工作场所民主取什么形态"。这个系列把形式交规则层，只要求员工的声音要实（第二篇 GS7）；两院制是可选的一种形式。原书核到以后要决定：两院制对三问的答案，谁能启动，有什么实效，被问的一方能不能独自取消它。

四 · 本系列停下的地方

分六格登记，照政治经济学 Paper 0。六格区别的是未继续的性质，不是每个系列都要填满的六个空格。

第一格，这一次构自己留下的余项。 离开有多难，只算能核的部分，没有一把通约的尺（前置篇第七节）；价格压到哪一点算拿走了伙伴那边人的基础层，没有阈值（第四篇第二节）；难关是不是真的，这个系列不提供统一的事实检验程序，难关的证据仍须可核，认定可以复审（第六篇 GS13）。

第二格，辖域相邻的对象。 税；竞争，以及国家与公司；公司的权利；不落在任何一个人身上的损害（本篇第四节第七小节）。

第三格，已经到头的结论。 公司没有第一人称，写下共识不使它成为一个好人；守向的安排不保证守得住（第六篇第四节）。这是这一层能给的，不是还没找到办法。

第四格，研究候选。 守向的通道里，谁能启动：员工、用户、社会怎样有份（第六篇对特拉华 §367 的观察）；后来的人怎样计入施为所及（本篇第四节第七小节）；AI 怎样帮用户想清楚他真正想要什么（经济学第六篇，旁证）；康德的尊严带着"能有道德性"的前件，本系列的"人"不带；康德的线在才能与道德性之间，本系列读作事与人之间；这两段距离能不能从康德自己的文本里缩短（本篇第三节）。

第五格，已有明确地址的手递。 市场论；公司论的专论；税；具体的制度论，见本节第三小节。

第六格，对照各家才看出来的问题。 目的的表述与线的落实：各家既争目的，也争落实；这个系列不论目的写成什么，追问线与通道（第一条线）。"会赢"：善待各方若以"也赚钱"来辩护，在这一部分，与股东优先的一端趋同（第二条线）。线从哪里来：弗里德曼、哈特与津加莱斯两处的分处在这里，不在两条线上；这个系列的线不以出资人的偏好或利润的结果为根据（第一篇 GS3）。

这一篇停在哪里

本篇不立命题，没有改动七篇的任何一句。它把这个系列与已有的公司理论对接了一遍，照实登记了每一家核到哪一层：康德、道奇诉福特汽车公司案、弗里德曼、商业圆桌会议，原文核到；麦基与弗里德曼的辩论、别布丘克与塔拉里塔、英国学院、奇普利，核到辩论原文、摘要或组织页面；弗里曼、哈特与津加莱斯，核到一句或摘要；科斯、詹森与梅克林、伯利与米恩斯、安德森、费雷拉斯，只到二手材料或转引。

它没有做的：没有证明利益相关者治理与股东优先哪一个对，这个系列不在两个名称之间选；没有替任何一家作完整的回应，摘要以外的论证都没有进；伯利与多德 1931—32 年的论争、汉斯曼《企业的所有权》、布莱尔与斯托特的团队生产理论，本篇没有取到原文，不引。
