Abstract

When there is no superior to enforce, how is the account still at the address? This paper asks what the system and its members record, in whose name it is booked, and how to record without characterizing a state and without leaving out a single item; and how the account follows the address through exit, expulsion, re-accession and dissolution. The whole paper starts from one matter: in the winter of Year 17, the draft of the annual sharing report of the Secretariat of the Three-Borders Cooperation System contained a sentence saying what kind of state the DRP is, and saying that its not paying its share is an expression of its nature as a state; the Deputy Secretary-General for Records and Reports deleted the sentence and recorded instead the shares owed, the places not taken up, the full text of the DRP's reasons, the two thousand one hundred people whose case was pending, and another member's late payment. This paper answers: reputation is an account visible from outside, a distributed observation, and the system sets up no centralized identifying authority; records are attributed to positions and persons, the account under the system's name and the account of the member states are booked at both addresses, and a record is a trace, not a verdict; records record only acts, not the names of states, the same kind of act takes the same wording, and the test is that, with the acting party replaced and not one other word changed, the sentence still holds when applied to the same act by another state; deception is discounted, and saying it openly wins over time; mutual recognition in ordinary times is the foundation of cooperation, mutual recognition is not permission, and the system does not achieve mutual trust on the members' behalf; the account follows the address, and does not vanish with exit, expulsion, re-accession or dissolution. Also used are the system's questions about the DRP's citizenship decree and the record of them, the long-term fertilizer contract and the delayed consent, the wording of the two records on Lamp Island and the Outer Sandbars, and the two petitions Stonegorge left unanswered during its period of exit. In §1 the paper interfaces with the Second Definitive Article of Kant's Perpetual Peace: when there is no supreme legislator overhead, what does trust rest on? The test of publicity is pointed to Theory of the State Paper 5, §4, and not redone. The paper has six propositions (XZ16 to XZ21), each stated at the end of its section and listed together at the end.

Introduction

In the winter of Year 17, the Admission-Sharing Office of the Secretariat at Three Borders was drafting that year's annual sharing report.

Sharing is one of the common procedures of the Three-Borders Cooperation System. Each year the Conference of Members makes a sharing decision, and the members divide the funds and the transfer places among themselves by the formula in Annex III of the Three-Borders Compact (the Compact): population counts for forty percent, output for forty percent, and the number of people already admitted counts as an offset at twenty percent (Compact, Article 12). The articles of the Compact cited here, like the system's organs and the laws of the several states, are examples set up by this series; this series does not claim that they are the best design. The total sharing funds came to 960 million accounting units for Year 16 and 1.4 billion for Year 17; the shares of the Democratic Republic of Purposes (DRP) were 210 million and 310 million respectively. In the spring of Year 17, the Conference of Members made Sharing Decision No. 3 by 4 to 0: that year thirty thousand people were to be transferred from the Eastfield camp in the Stonegorge Commonwealth (Stonegorge), the DRP's places among them numbering eight thousand. Those living in the Eastfield camp are people who fled across the Grey River from the north of the Republic of the Sandbars (the Sandbars).

By the winter of Year 17, the DRP had paid neither year's share, and not one person had been received against its eight thousand places. The United City-States of Ends (UCSE) had paid its Year 16 share four months late; its own Admission-Sharing Appropriations Act was not passed until the winter of Year 16.

In the autumn of Year 17, the DRP's Foreign Ministry had sent the Secretariat a letter: "While the UCSE continues to freeze the assets of the Republic's state bodies as 'security for the Reedtown compensation,' the Republic will make no payment into a sharing fund to which the UCSE is the largest contributor. The sharing formula counts by population, and takes no account of the fiscal difficulties of the Republic's northern districts." That freeze was originally one item of the sanctions the UCSE imposed on the DRP in the spring of Year 9; after the other items were lifted in Year 11, it stayed on under this new name (Theory of the State Paper 5, §5). Besides the letter there was one more thing: about two thousand one hundred people from the Sandbars had made their own way by sea to ports in the south of the DRP; the DRP gave them temporary residence, and asked that they be counted toward its offset.

An analyst in the Admission-Sharing Office drafted the annual report. Section 3 of the draft contained a sentence: "The DRP is a 14DD state, and will not fulfill its obligations to those outside the system; its failure to pay its share is an expression of its nature as a state."

The Secretary-General, who held DRP citizenship, recused from this report. The Deputy Secretary-General for Records and Reports, who held UCSE citizenship, deleted the sentence under Article 4 of the Secretariat's Rules on Records. The wording of Article 4 is the same as that of Article 9 of the Compact: the Secretariat's reports "record the course of the act, the position that performed the act, and the documents on which it relied." In place of the deleted sentence was written: "The DRP's sharing funds for Years 16 and 17, 520 million accounting units in all: not paid. Its eight thousand transfer places for Year 17: not received. The reasons given in the DRP Foreign Ministry's letter of the autumn of Year 17 are appended (in full). About two thousand one hundred people from the Sandbars reached the DRP by sea, and the DRP gave them temporary residence; whether they are counted toward the offset awaits the decision of the Conference of Members. The UCSE's sharing funds for Year 16 were paid four months late." The revision was entered in the register of changes that the Rules on Records require, which every member can consult.

Before the report went out, the draft was published by a newspaper in the DRP capital. The DRP Foreign Ministry protested: "The Secretariat's people are characterizing the Republic." Stonegorge's permanent representative said in the Conference of Members: "Deleting that sentence lends a veneer to the side that does not pay." The Deputy Secretary-General answered: "The report records the money not paid and the people not received, without leaving out a single item." In the spring of Year 18, the Conference of Members adopted the record of the report by 3 to 0, the DRP not taking part in the vote; at the same time it decided that the two thousand one hundred would count at half toward the DRP's offset. By Year 20, the DRP had paid forty percent of its Year 16 share. In the spring of Year 18 the analyst was transferred to the Cloud River data station; the Secretariat gave the reason as "personal request."

The matter lands on people first. Behind the eight thousand places that Decision No. 3 allotted to the DRP are people in the Eastfield camp who have been registered for six full months and are waiting to be transferred; whether the roughly two thousand one hundred who reached the DRP by sea and were given temporary residence count toward the offset has to wait for the decision of the Conference of Members. The address the record faces is the DRP, a member, and the same report also records the UCSE; the position that made the decision not to pay is the DRP Foreign Ministry. The record has positions of its own as well: the analyst who drafted it, the Deputy Secretary-General who revised it, the Conference of Members that adopted it.

There are two lines to watch. One is the line of the record: the system's record records only the act, the position that performed it and the documents it relied on; it does not characterize a state, and it does not leave out a single item. The other lies on the DRP's side: sharing is something it acknowledged when it ratified the Compact; Sharing Decision No. 1 in the summer of Year 16 and Sharing Decision No. 3 in the spring of Year 17 both passed 4 to 0, and both times the DRP was among those in favor. Whether its reasons for not paying hold is not for the record to judge.

The system has no force; when the DRP does not pay, there is no one who can collect on the system's behalf. That does not mean the account is gone. This paper asks, starting from this matter: when there is no superior to enforce, how is the account still at the address? What is recorded, in whose name is it booked, and how is a state recorded without being characterized and without a single item left out? And how does the account follow the address through exit, expulsion, re-accession and dissolution?

The paper is called Mutual Trust, yet it does not begin from members who already trust one another. Trust here is a result, not a premise: whether members can trust one another depends on an account that is visible from outside, booked at an address that can be named, and able to bear being questioned. The paper goes in six steps. §1 writes that reputation is an account visible from outside, and interfaces there with Kant. §2 writes that records are attributed to positions and persons. §3 writes that records record acts, not the names of states; this is why that sentence was deleted. §4 writes that deception is discounted and that saying it openly wins over time. §5 writes that mutual recognition in ordinary times is the foundation of cooperation. §6 writes that the account follows the address, and does not vanish with exit, expulsion, re-accession or dissolution.

Conventions follow the Prequel of this series. Where judgment is required, this series speaks in three layers, the normative layer, the rule layer and the judgment layer (the Prequel, §11); where this paper uses them, it states which sentence is a norm, which item is a suggested rule or an example, and which step is left to those in positions. This paper writes oughts only for the system and its members; of states outside the system, it writes only how the system and its members deal with them.

§1 An Account Visible from Outside

What use is an account kept by a system that cannot give orders? Look first at a record made three years before the sharing report.

In the summer of Year 14, the system put questions to the DRP about a decree it had issued in the autumn of Year 9. The decree provided that those who left the country during mobilization and did not return within six months would lose DRP citizenship, and that the DRP would not register children born to them abroad. The DRP Foreign Ministry answered that the acquisition and loss of citizenship are the Republic's internal affairs, that the decree predates the Compact, and that Article 4(3) of the Compact does not apply retroactively; the answer also attached a further paragraph turning the question back on the UCSE, about the citizenship the UCSE itself had revoked and about its own citizenship law. In the winter of Year 14, the Conference of Members adopted a record by 2 to 0, the DRP not taking part in the vote. The course of this matter, and on whom it fell, have been set out at the opening of Paper 2; here only the stretch after the record was adopted is looked at.

The record was put on the Secretariat's website, where anyone can see it. In Year 15, a newspaper in the Nation Union of Telos (NUT), two newspapers in the UCSE and one in the DRP capital each published the full text of the record or a summary of it. That summer, the UCSE's Chamber of Citizens amended Article 14 of the Membership Act by 171 votes to 120, removing the condition that the parents of stateless children must have resided lawfully for five full years; by Year 17, three hundred eighty-nine of the children born at the Willow Ferry Reception Center had acquired UCSE citizenship. That autumn, the DRP Foreign Ministry published new registration rules: a child born abroad can be registered if one parent applies in person at a DRP consular office in the NUT or the UCSE and has "completed after the fact the exit formalities for the mobilization period"; the parents' own citizenship is not restored. By the end of Year 16, thirty-seven children had been registered under these rules.

What is the relation between these two things and that record? The record does not speak for anyone. It does not say that the UCSE changed its law because of it, does not say that the DRP's rules were an answer to it, and does not say whether the figure of thirty-seven is enough. Those who read the record read it for themselves.

But there is one thing nobody needs to say: the account is out in the open. Who asked what, who answered what, what went unanswered, in whose name it is booked: all of it is in the record; the record is on the website, and four newspapers have published its full text or a summary. The Theory of the State has said what recognition gives: "To recognize a state is not to endorse its acts: what is acknowledged is that its acts issue from its positions and that the account is booked in its name" (Theory of the State N26). Recognition leaves an address. The Theory of the State speaks of one state recognizing another; here it is extended to the system and its members: what the system's record does is set the account at this address out in the open, where every party can see it (own undertaking).

The War Treatise has said something close to this. It says that of the several judgments a war must pass, the war-party can itself claim to have passed the first three, and they are hard to check from outside; only one is different: "Whether questioning is allowed is directly observable from outside" (War Treatise §16). Whether there are people questioning, whether their questions reach the place where the decision is made, whether those who question are protected, whether anything was changed because of the questions: each of these leaves traces visible from outside. The War Treatise speaks of a war-party's allowing internal questioning. Here it is extended to the system and its members in ordinary times: whether a construct lets itself be questioned, and how it answers when questioned, is visible from outside (own undertaking). The extension holds because the reason for that sentence lies not in war but in the traces that questions and answers leave; in ordinary times, questions and answers leave only more traces.

This account visible from outside is what this paper calls reputation.

For reputation to count, it does not rely on anyone to enforce it. The system has no force: clause 2 of Article 2 of the Compact states that no organ of the system may exercise force against a member or against persons within a member's territory. With no superior to enforce, the account is still at the address; the peers who see the account can adjust their own cooperation by it. The Theory of the State, setting out the channels that cannot be closed, writes this point thus: "what it cannot close is peers judging for themselves on this basis and withdrawing cooperation other than recognition" (Theory of the State N36). How peers withdraw cooperation by the account is the business of Paper 4; this section establishes only the first half: the account is out in the open, and can be seen.

Why should an account set out in the open let states trust one another? This question must be taken to Kant. When there is no supreme legislator overhead, on what can a state rest its trust in its own right? Immanuel Kant asked this question in the Second Definitive Article of Perpetual Peace (Zum ewigen Frieden). He first says that when a people, in forming a state, sets over itself a supreme legislative, governing and judging power to settle its disputes peacefully, and on that ground says there shall be no war among them, this can be understood. He then says:

"But if this state says: 'There shall be no war between me and other states, although I recognize no supreme legislative power that secures my right to me and whose right I in turn secure,' then it cannot be understood at all on what I would then ground the trust in my right, unless it is the surrogate of the civil social union, namely free federalism." (my translation; Wenn aber dieser Staat sagt: »es soll kein Krieg zwischen mir und andern Staaten seyn, obgleich ich keine oberste gesetzgebende Gewalt erkenne, die mir mein, und der ich ihr Recht sichere,« so ist es gar nicht zu verstehen, worauf ich dann das Vertrauen zu meinem Rechte gründen wolle, wenn es nicht das Surrogat des bürgerlichen Gesellschaftbundes, nämlich der freye Föderalism ist; Zum ewigen Frieden, Second Section, Second Definitive Article, per the first edition, Nicolovius, 1795, Project Gutenberg EBook #46873; compared with the English translation by M. C. Smith, EBook #50922; tier A)

The common question is this: when there is no supreme legislator overhead, what do a state's trust in its own right, and states' trust in one another, rest on? This is exactly the question this paper asks. Kant says in the same article that this league need not be a state of nations (Theory of the State Paper 5, §7, has already checked this point); this series, likewise, sets up no superior with force over the states. Both hold that the absence of such a superior does not mean that nothing at all is needed: the securing of rights and trust in one another still require common arrangements. Kant's answer is free federalism. Within this shared boundary, this series unfolds what counts into consensus, checks and reputation (the Prequel, §7), and reads reputation as an account visible from outside.

One can draft the strongest objection on Kant's behalf: an account set out in the open is too thin; a state that does not care how others see it is not bound by it all the same; for trust to have something to rest on, a lasting league is indispensable. This series accepts half of this. The Three-Borders Cooperation System is itself formed by covenant, and without a covenant there would be no address at which to book an account; nor does reputation count on its own (see below, and Paper 4). What this series does not accept is placing the ground of trust in the form of a league. What it asks is what makes the account count within a system already formed; the answer lies in the account's being visible, booked at an address, and within reach of questions.

Where the two part must also be stated exactly. Kant calls free federalism the "surrogate" (Surrogat) of the civil social union; in the same article, he calls a world republic the positive idea, and the league its negative surrogate. How this series reads that ordering is left to Paper 8; here it takes up only the question Kant asked, and passes no judgment in advance. This is a division of scope under one and the same question, not a refutation.

Whose observation is reputation? One easy road is to make the system the judge: the Secretariat or the Conference of Members determines whether a state keeps faith and what kind of state it is, and every party treats it according to that determination. This series does not take that road. Moral Law, treating how to identify what condition a person is in, wrote down how this is done at the institutional level: "The institutional implementation is distributed observation through reputation networks, not centralized identifying authority." In the same place it also requires "multiple voices in the identification process to prevent dominance by a single perspective" (Moral Law P9 §5.3). There it speaks of identifying a person. Here it is extended to states: how a state's account is read is for each party that sees it to read for itself, and the system sets up no centralized identifying authority (own undertaking). The system's record is one place where this account is kept, and the account lies there; what is read out of it is not for the system alone to decide. As §3 will show, this is one of the reasons that sentence was deleted.

Where questions cannot reach, the account is out in the open all the same. In the spring of Year 14, Stonegorge applied to join. Article 7(a) of the Compact provides that admitting a new member requires the written consent of all members. The UCSE and the NUT gave their written consent that season; the DRP Foreign Ministry wrote to the Secretary-General: "The Republic's consent must be deliberated by the People's Assembly." In the summer of Year 14, the Plateau Federation and the DRP signed a long-term fertilizer contract: five years, at a price fifteen percent below the previous year's market price. The UCSE's permanent representative proposed putting questions to the DRP about the relation between this contract and the DRP's delay in consenting. The Secretary-General, holding DRP citizenship, recused, and the Deputy Secretary-General for Common Procedures acted in that place; the written opinion was: "Admitting a new member is a new covenant. Whether a member consents to a new covenant is that member's own affair, and is not among the 'existing matters' on which questions may be put under Article 7(b)." The UCSE withdrew the proposal. How new covenants are told apart from existing matters has already been set out in Paper 2, §2 (XZ10).

The question was withdrawn; the account was not. The timing and the price of the contract; the People's Assembly's consent to the admission in the autumn of Year 14, by 352 votes to 221; members from the DRP's northern districts saying that the contract "has nothing to do with the Stonegorge matter," and other members saying "the timing is no coincidence": all of this is out in the open, for anyone to see. Whether it was an exchange, the system does not judge on anyone's behalf; each party reads for itself, and bears what it reads. This is what distributed observation looks like when it lands on a matter: there is no center saying "this is an exchange" or "this is not an exchange," yet every party that has to deal with the DRP, or with the Plateau Federation, holds the same set of facts.

Why does reputation count? A state's reputation must be looked at from two sides, kept apart. One side is what its reputation is to itself: whether it cares. This paper does not discuss that side. A party leaning more toward the 14DD direction may not care about reputation, caring only for its own strength; a party leaning more toward the 15DD direction has no need to care about reputation, since it goes on treating people as ends; how others read its account is the business of those who read the account, and that is precisely the other side, which comes next. The other side is what its reputation is to the other parties: other states, the system, and the people of the several states see its account, and on that basis decide whether to trust its covenants, whether to give it cooperation, whether to put questions to it. When this paper says that reputation counts, it speaks only of this side (own undertaking). Whether a state has credit with other states depends on its acts, and its acts are out in the open. Power Theory, treating the holders of power within a state, has also drawn a boundary: reputation is not the sole source of power in modern politics (Power Theory P1 §III). Between states, likewise, reputation is not the whole of what counts.

It may be said that reputation works only on a party that cares about it. A party with great influence that leans more toward the 14DD direction may not care; and among several parties leaning more toward the 14DD direction, reputation can hold up even less. Moral Law has said: "14DDs cannot form mutually sustaining institutional structures with one another" (Moral Law P9 §2.5). The weight of this objection is real, but it speaks of the first side. The counting of reputation that this paper speaks of does not rely on the recorded party's caring: if a state does not care about its own reputation, other states still decide by its account whether to trust its covenants and whether to give it cooperation; if it cares only for its own strength, these decisions land on its strength, and that it does recognize. An account set out in the open becomes a cost only through the parties who read it adjusting what they do by it. Whether this mechanism actually works depends, further, on how the parties reading the account make their decisions by it. That several parties leaning more toward the 14DD direction cannot form mutually sustaining institutional structures does not mean that the parties make no use at all of public records in reckoning their interests; this paper does not derive any such universal negative from that sentence of Moral Law.

But this section has not said that reputation counts on its own. In a system with no superior, more than one thing counts: covenants are in Paper 1, questions in Paper 2, checks in Paper 4. Neither checks nor reputation require the other party first to acknowledge that persons are ends (Paper 4, XZ23). This section establishes only one thing: the account is visible. Being visible is the premise of the rest: without a visible account, by what would peers withdraw cooperation, and where would the questions begin? That sentence of Moral Law shows precisely that the system cannot count on members leaning more toward the 14DD direction to hold up cooperation by themselves; it must rely on the account's being set out in the open, so that every party has something in hand to check against.

This section leaves two points. Reputation is not measured: this paper gives no figure for what a state's account out in the open is worth, nor does it prove that the account works in every matter. A misreading does not change the account: whatever a reader reads out of a record, the item misread is booked to the reader, not to the record, and not to the party recorded.

XZ16 Reputation is an account visible from outside. To recognize a state is to acknowledge that its acts issue from its positions and that the account is booked in its name (Theory of the State N26); the system's record sets the account at this address out in the open (the Theory of the State originally speaks of one state recognizing another; here it is extended to the system and its members; own undertaking). Whether a construct lets itself be questioned, and how it answers when questioned, is visible from outside (War Treatise §16 originally speaks of a war-party's allowing questioning; here it is extended to the system and its members in ordinary times; own undertaking); this paper calls this account visible from outside reputation. When there is no superior to enforce, the account is still at the address, and peers judge for themselves by the account and adjust their cooperation (Theory of the State N36; for withdrawing cooperation, see Paper 4). Reputation is a distributed observation, with no centralized identifying authority (Moral Law P9 §5.3 originally speaks of identifying a person's condition; here it is extended to states; own undertaking); the system's record is one place where this account is kept, and does not read it on the parties' behalf. Reputation counts through the parties who see the account making their decisions by it: whether to trust the recorded party's covenants, whether to give it cooperation (own undertaking); whether the recorded party cares about its own reputation is not within this paper's discussion. Reputation is not the whole of what counts (Power Theory P1 §III). Reputation does not count on its own; it counts together with covenants, questions and checks (Paper 4, XZ23). Reputation is not measured; the account for a misreading is with the reader.

§2 Records Are Attributed to Positions and Persons

The report says that the DRP has "not paid." Who is "the DRP" here?

When a state does not pay, no one took the money out of a pocket and put it back again. Not paying is a decision: it is made by some position and spoken through some document. What the revised report says is: "The reasons given in the DRP Foreign Ministry's letter of the autumn of Year 17 are appended (in full)." The non-payment is attributed to that letter of the DRP Foreign Ministry, which is the position that made the decision and the document it relied on; the record did not dispose of the matter with the single name "the Republic." The same report also recorded the UCSE's late payment.

The Theory of the State has written down the minimum content of booking a state's account: "The minimum content of booking the account (own undertaking): decisions and reasons made public, records attributed to particular positions and persons; those affected can ask about it in an independent channel and receive an answer; a position at fault is subject to stricter confirmation in decisions of the same kind thereafter." (Theory of the State N39) There it speaks of a state toward those its acts reach. Here it is extended to the system and its members: when the system's record records a member's act, it likewise attributes it to the position and person that performed it (own undertaking).

Why attribute to positions? Because a state is only an address. Institution Theory says: "the construct is the address of attribution, not the subject of the act; the address of attribution and the actual actor do not hide each other." (Institution Theory Z7a) The same paper also says: "The construct's name does not shield an individual responsibility that has independent grounds, and the construct's failure is not automatically transferred onto any one person." (Institution Theory Z6) To record "the DRP has not paid" is to record the address; to record "the DRP Foreign Ministry's letter of the autumn of Year 17" is to record which position at that address did this, and on what basis. Both are needed. Record only the address, and those who ask do not know whom to ask; record only the position, and the address's obligation seems to fall on one person, so that when that person changes, the obligation leaves with them.

So too with the system's own name. Nothing made in the system's name was made by "the system" itself. Power Theory, treating the apparatus of power, says: "the apparatus does not exercise power; individuals exercise power by invoking the apparatus." (Power Theory P3 §V.5) There it speaks of the apparatus of power within a state; here it is extended to the system: decisions and documents made in the system's name are traced back to the positions and votes that made them (own undertaking). Article 3 of the Compact writes this as one example of a rule: every document issued in the system's name states the organ that made it, the votes cast and how each member voted. So the account under the system's name is with the system, and also with the member states and positions that voted for it and carry it out. The Theory of Government, treating two levels of government, says: "The accounts of two constructs are each checked by their own source and are not transferred from one to the other (own undertaking)." (Theory of Government G25) It originally speaks of two levels of government within a state; here it is extended to the system and its member states (own undertaking): the account is booked at both addresses, and neither transfers it to the other.

The record is itself an act. This entry in the sharing report has three positions: the analyst who drafted it; the Deputy Secretary-General who made the revision, because the Secretary-General, holding DRP citizenship, had recused; and the Conference of Members that adopted the record by 3 to 0, the DRP not taking part in the vote. The revision was made under Article 4 of the Rules on Records and entered in the register of changes, which every member can consult. Who changed it, on what basis, how it read before and how it reads after: all of it is there. Article 9 of the Compact and the Rules on Records are examples at the rule layer, not the only way to write this; what matters is that the record's own account also falls on positions, and that the places changed can be found. A record that can be quietly changed cannot be trusted in the accounts of others it records either (§4).

A record, again, is a trace, not a verdict. The Conflict Treatise, treating what can be seen of a conflict from outside, keeps cues, criteria and traces apart, and says: "cues are not criteria, and traces are not verdicts" (Conflict Treatise §26). It originally speaks of what a third person sees of a conflict between first persons; here it is extended to the system's record of its members (own undertaking): the record sets down how much the DRP owes and sets down its reasons; it does not judge whether those reasons hold.

The DRP's reasons point to another member: the UCSE still freezes the assets of the DRP's state bodies. The record appends the letter as it stands, neither cutting anything for the DRP nor answering anything for the UCSE. The freeze is an act of the UCSE; to question it, it is questioned separately, by its own object, and the party questioned is the UCSE (Paper 2, XZ12). When one member's reasons for non-performance point to another member, the record books the two accounts each at its own address, and does not set one against the other. Whether the two thousand one hundred are counted toward the offset is a decision of the Conference of Members: in the spring of Year 18 they were counted at half, and the decision and the votes are recorded as they are.

Look at this record by the criterion of force (the Prequel, §2): force is substituting one's own judgment for the other party's judgment on its own affairs. Whose judgment, and which, has this record substituted? Whether to pay, and on what reasons not to pay, is a judgment the DRP Foreign Ministry made on the DRP's affairs; the record sets down that judgment and its reasons as they stand, and has not altered them on the DRP's behalf. Whether the reasons hold, and how the two thousand one hundred are to be counted, are judgments of the Conference of Members; the record has not made those on the Conference's behalf either. What the record does is let both judgments fall on a visible address. The system does not enforce; it lands in each member; whether it has landed can be checked only once there is a record.

It may be said that attributing to positions and persons turns matters between states into personal matters, and lets the state hide behind persons. The DRP could say that it was one letter from the Foreign Ministry, and that another minister would mean another line; the system, too, could say that the revision was one Deputy Secretary-General's affair, and nothing to do with the members. The weight of this objection is that attribution to individuals can indeed be used to unload the address's account.

But what this section establishes is booking at both addresses, not rebooking. The DRP's account is with the DRP: 520 million not paid, eight thousand places not taken up; this item is booked in the DRP's name, and is not settled because the Foreign Ministry has changed hands. The account for that letter is with the Foreign Ministry, and the position that wrote it is questioned on it thereafter. The system's account is with the system; the account for the revision is with the Deputy Secretary-General and with the Conference of Members that adopted the record. Neither side settles the other's account. Those two sentences of Institution Theory say exactly that neither direction hides the other: the construct's name does not screen the individual, and the individual does not bear for the construct.

What is a norm and what is an example must be kept apart. Recording the act, the position that performed it and the documents it relied on is a requirement of the normative layer. The articles of the Rules on Records, the register of changes, and Articles 3 and 9 of the Compact are examples at the rule layer, which the parties can set otherwise as agreed. How finely an act is recorded is left to the judgment of the positions for records and reports, with changes registered; this step is at the judgment layer.

For the DRP's non-payment, the revised text recorded the sum, the places, the position that made the decision and the document it relied on; for the UCSE's late payment, it recorded only "paid four months late." Whether the transfer had anything to do with that sentence is not recorded, and this paper draws no inference. How the account moves when a position at the address changes hands is written in §6.

§3 Recording Acts, Not the Names of States

Why must that sentence be deleted?

First put it beside the sentences that remained. The deleted sentence (quoted in the Introduction) says three things: what kind of state the DRP is; that it will not fulfill its obligations to those outside the system; and that its not paying its share is an expression of its nature as a state. The sentences that remained say how much money was not paid, how many places were not taken up, which position made this decision and through which letter, what reasons the letter gave, that two thousand one hundred people were given temporary residence and that whether they count toward the offset is pending, and how late another member paid.

The deleted sentence is wrong in two places.

One is in what it speaks of. The sentences that remained speak of what a state did, on whom it fell, and on what documents; the deleted sentence speaks of what a state is. The former can be questioned: whether 520 million is the figure, whether not one of the eight thousand places has been taken up, whether the letter says this, whether two thousand one hundred people were given temporary residence. The DRP can answer, can ask back, can correct. The latter cannot be questioned: told that it "is" a certain kind of state, a state can only accept it or reject it. The DRP Foreign Ministry's response was exactly a rejection: "The Secretariat's people are characterizing the Republic."

The other is in what it infers. That sentence infers from the state's nature that it "will not fulfill its obligations to those outside the system," and then reads the non-payment as an "expression" of that nature. Yet the same report contains an act that does not fit it: about two thousand one hundred people from the Sandbars, people outside the system exactly, reached the DRP by sea, and the DRP gave them temporary residence. Afterward, by Year 20, it paid forty percent of its Year 16 share. When the next act is inferred from an identity, what is inferred does not fit that state's own acts.

The Theory of the State, treating leaning, set a discipline: "A leaning is a direction, not an identity." It also says: "That a state leans more toward the 14DD direction is no ground for presuming in advance that each of its acts is expansion or oppression; nor is a state's leaning more toward the 15DD direction a ground for presuming in advance that each of its acts is defense." (the Theory of the State Prequel, §7) That sentence breaks both: it writes a direction as an identity, and from the identity prejudges the next act.

There is one more layer. §1 said that the system sets up no centralized identifying authority. Had that sentence stayed in the report, an analyst in the Secretariat would have determined, in the system's name and on behalf of everyone who reads the report, what a state is.

So, by the line of recording acts, that sentence must be deleted. This is the reading at the normative layer. In that matter, the one who made the judgment was the Deputy Secretary-General for Records and Reports, who revised the text under Article 4 of the Rules on Records; the Conference of Members adopted the record by 3 to 0 in the spring of Year 18, the DRP not taking part in the vote; Stonegorge's permanent representative said at the meeting that it lent a veneer, and what the DRP Foreign Ministry protested against was the draft that appeared in the newspaper.

Rewritten as a statement of direction, that sentence still does not go into the record. Changed, say, to "the DRP's direction leans more to one side," it still does not go in. Direction is a diagnosis, and a diagnosis is made by acts (Paper 8, XZ53); the record records only acts, and what direction the parties reading the record read out of the acts is each one's own reading (§1). Of any state, this paper, too, writes only what it did.

The words of Stonegorge's permanent representative must be answered seriously: deleting that sentence lends a veneer to the side that does not pay. The weight of this objection is that if a record, afraid of offending a state, writes its non-performance down lightly, the account is falsified, and from then on no one need care about records.

Compare before and after. The only thing recorded less after the revision is that one characterization. The 520 million owed, the eight thousand places not taken up, the full text of the DRP's reasons: not one item is missing; and two things are recorded in addition: the UCSE's late payment, and the pending matter of the two thousand one hundred. The Deputy Secretary-General's answer was: "The report records the money not paid and the people not received, without leaving out a single item."

Recording acts is not recording less; it is booking the account where questions can reach it. Acts can be questioned, answered and corrected; a characterization can only be accepted or rejected. Record the characterization, and the DRP's response is reduced to protest, a protest against the characterization, while the 520 million recedes into the background. Record the acts, and the questions land on money and places: when the money will be paid, how the places will be taken up, how the matter its reasons point to is to be questioned separately. The forty percent of the Year 16 share paid by Year 20 is booked at the same address; the sixty percent of that year not paid, and the whole of the Year 17 share, are booked at the same address too.

The same question came up twice more in the system's records, this time about the wording for another kind of act.

In the spring of Year 17, ships and forces of the Plateau Federation entered the territorial waters of Stonegorge and Lamp Island, set up the "Lamp Island Sea-Lane Administration Post" on Lamp Island, required ships using the Southern Lane to register with that post, and detained two unregistered freighters. At the extraordinary session on the fourth day, the Conference of Members adopted a record under clause 1 of Article 18 of the Compact, stating "There is a colonizing entry"; the UCSE, the NUT and Stonegorge voted in favor, and the DRP abstained. The DRP's permanent representative said: "The record should read 'armed entry.' The word 'colonizing' is a characterization of the Plateau Federation." His words were entered in the record.

In the autumn of Year 17, two UCSE ships and a company landed on Great Outer Sandbar, in the Republic of the Sandbars, set up the "Outer Sandbars Provisional Administration Office," registered the island's residents and issued them residence cards, collected fishing fees and replaced the school textbooks. The Republic of the Sandbars is not in the system. In the summer of Year 18, the Conference of Members made a second record under the same clause, again writing "There is a colonizing entry"; the NUT and Stonegorge voted in favor, and the DRP abstained. The DRP's permanent representative said: "The Republic said in the matter of Lamp Island that the word 'colonizing' is a characterization, and that the record should read 'armed entry'; the same goes for the Outer Sandbars." Stonegorge's permanent representative said: "On Lamp Island it was called an administration post; on the Outer Sandbars it is called an administration office." The UCSE made a statement under Article 7(b), which was entered in the record: "No one there resisted, and no one died."

Whether the wording was right on these two occasions, this paper does not judge. To judge the wording, the act must be judged first: the Outer Sandbars occasion is judged in Paper 4, §7, and the Lamp Island occasion in Paper 5, §1; those two places also each state who judged that wording under the rules, and how. Here only the general half is answered: by what the wording is to be decided.

Wording follows the act, not the name of the state. The same kind of act takes the same wording, whether the one acting is a state outside the system or a member, and whichever state it is. Judgment falls on acts: who the acting party is does not change the judgment.

The DRP's words must be answered seriously too. It says that the word "colonizing" is itself a characterization. The weight of this objection is real: if a word carries a verdict on a state, writing it into the record is the same kind of error as the deleted sentence.

But "There is a colonizing entry" records an act: entering by armed force, setting up administration there, requiring the people there and passing ships to register. It says what happened on that occasion; it does not say what kind of state the entering party is. Where the two part can be seen by a test: replace the acting party, change not one other word, and the sentence still holds when applied to the same act by another state (own undertaking).

Try it on the deleted sentence. Replace "the DRP" with another state and leave the rest as it is, and the sentence still says what that state "is" and what it "will not" do. There is no act in it to check against; whether it holds of the other state has nothing to do with what that state did, and only to do with how the speaker sees that state. It fails the test, because it is not a sentence about acts at all.

Then try it on the record of the fourth day, testing only how it is written. The record first states where the ships and forces of the Plateau Federation entered, what they set up, whom they required to register and what ships they detained, and then writes "There is a colonizing entry." Replace "the Plateau Federation" with another state, leave the rest as it is, apply it to the same several things done by that other state, and the sentence is still a sentence about those several things. It is held up by acts, not by the name of a state.

This trial shows only that the sentence is a sentence about acts. More than one wording passes the test: "armed entry" is about acts as well. Which word fits those several acts requires judging the acts first, and is then judged by the Conference of Members under the rules; the test does not choose for it.

The test is content of the normative layer, not a criterion to be ticked off. It says what a sentence of record ought to look like; nor does it judge for anyone whether two acts are the same kind of act. On whether Lamp Island and the Outer Sandbars are the same kind, Stonegorge's "administration post" and "administration office," and the UCSE's "no one there resisted, and no one died," each point one way; those are the acts to be judged in Paper 4, §7, and Paper 5, §1.

In three layers. Recording only acts, with wording that bears the test above, is the normative layer. Article 4 of the Rules on Records, the register of changes, the vote under Article 7(b) of the Compact and the "right to require that its statements be entered in the record" are examples at the rule layer. Whether a particular wording writes "armed entry" or "colonizing entry," and how this is judged under the rules, is the judgment layer: it is judged by those in positions in the Conference of Members, and the reasons and dissent are entered in the record. On the Lamp Island occasion, the DRP's dissent was entered; on the Outer Sandbars occasion, the DRP's dissent and the UCSE's statement were both entered. This series does not judge on the Conference's behalf.

One point is left here. The record records acts, and those who read it may still infer identity from acts: reading that 520 million was not paid, someone will still say "just as expected, that kind of state." This layer is beyond the record's reach. What the record can see to is that it does not itself write so; how readers read is theirs, and the account for a misreading is with the reader (§1). The record of non-performance records shares, timing and reasons as they are, and attributes them to the position that made the decision (§2).

§4 Saying It Openly Wins over Time

Before the sharing report went out, the draft appeared in a newspaper in the DRP capital. This was a test for the record: the deleted sentence was now visible to everyone; would those who deleted it be said to be hiding something?

The Secretariat gave no further explanation, nor did it need to. The revision had been entered in the register of changes, which every member can check: how it read before, how it reads after, and that the basis was Article 4 of the Rules on Records. The draft and the revised text were both out in the open, and readers could compare them for themselves. The DRP Foreign Ministry protested against the draft; Stonegorge's permanent representative said the revision lent a veneer; both claims faced the same set of texts set out in the open.

Suppose that the revision had not been registered. Once the draft came out, what people would have seen is this: the report had a sentence, the sentence was gone when the report went out, and no one said who had deleted it or on what basis. Then no one could say what else the Secretariat had changed. With every report after that, readers would first have to ask: has this been changed?

The War Treatise has written of this kind of discounting. What it speaks of is yin-mou (scheming) between war-parties: yin-mou relies on the other's not knowing one's true intent, and may work in the short term, but "once seen through, it fails, and the other permanently keeps guard against all one's postures"; "All future cooperation, agreements, and signals get discounted." (War Treatise §12) It also says: "Real structure does not need maintenance (the real is automatically consistent with itself); falsehood requires continuous maintenance" (ibid.). The War Treatise speaks of what lies between war-parties. Here it is extended to multilateral covenants and signals in ordinary times (own undertaking): once a member's explanation to the system, the grounds it explains for sending forces, the share it owes, or its reasons for not paying, is seen through as false, all its later explanations are discounted; the system's own record is the same: once one change is seen through as a covert change, all later reports are discounted. The extension holds because the reason for the discounting lies not in war but in how, once one has been seen through, the other reads every word one says afterward; covenants and signals in ordinary times count precisely by being read in this way.

The register of changes spares the Secretariat from keeping up the pretense that "this report has never been revised": what is true needs no maintenance; what is false must be maintained without end. Whether the record itself is accurate, and whether the revision conforms to its rules, must still be open to questioning.

Now look at the members' side. The DRP does not pay its share, and its reasons are written in its letter, said openly: the UCSE's freeze, and a sharing formula that takes no account of the fiscal difficulties of the northern districts. The record appends the full text of the letter. Whether the reasons are good is for the readers and the Conference of Members to judge; saying it openly, in itself, gives the account somewhere to be checked against. What followed is all booked at the same address: the Conference of Members decided that the two thousand one hundred would count at half toward the offset; by Year 20 the DRP had paid forty percent of its Year 16 share.

The War Treatise pushes saying it openly to its extreme and calls it yang-mou (open strategy): "placing the plan in the open, and even so, the other cannot but follow"; "yang-mou's strength comes not from information asymmetry but from the structural position-differential itself" (War Treatise §12). The DRP's letter is not yang-mou; it only says things openly. But saying it openly and yang-mou share one thing: neither relies on the other's not knowing. Saying it openly puts reasons and stance where questions can reach them, and no longer depends on the other's not knowing to keep them up; whether what is said is true, and whether it is carried out later, must still be verified.

From here the qualified form of reputation and checks can be stated: state the bottom line openly, with real capacity behind it (Paper 4, XZ23). A member that will not pay says openly that it will not pay, and why; one that says it will send forces must have behind it the capacity to send them, and the determination its own state has already carried through. Deterrence is the same. The War Treatise says: "depth of cultivation is the foundation on which any deterrence stays credible over the long run." (War Treatise §10) It originally speaks of the deterrence of war-parties; here it is extended to what members and the system say outward (own undertaking): whether a word is credible over the long run rests on the depth of cultivation within the party that says it, not on how loudly it is said.

It may be said that saying it openly loses in the short term. In saying openly that it will not pay, the DRP as good as admits its non-performance; had it equivocated and put things off again and again, it would have left one handle fewer against itself. The weight of this objection is that in the short term this is indeed so: non-performance said openly lies out in the open word for word, while what is quietly put off appears in the record only as "not paid."

But the account is booked the same way. Whether the non-payment is said openly or kept quiet, the 520 million is booked in the DRP's name; not paid is not paid. The difference between the two lies not in this item but in what follows: the party that quietly delays has what it says later discounted; when the party that said it openly later says "forty percent has been paid," readers hold a claim they can check, and need not first guess what share it is admitting to; whether it really has paid must still be checked against the records of payment and receipt. The War Treatise says: "Yang-mou does not win in the short term; yang-mou wins in time" (War Treatise §12). Where saying it openly wins, too, can be seen only over time.

The measure must be made clear. Saying it openly is the direction of the qualified form; it sets up no separate obligation on members: what a member explains to the system, and how far, follows the covenants it has acknowledged (Paper 2, XZ12). Which of a member's explanations is seen through as false is judged by the peers who read it and by the channels within each state; the system only records, and does not judge who has lied. This step is at the judgment layer.

This section leaves a few points. This series does not judge whether the DRP's reasons hold (§2). Winning over time is a tendency of structure, not a guarantee: the party that tells the truth does not necessarily win in every matter; this is the same measure as the Theory of the State's not guaranteeing peace (Theory of the State Paper 5, §7).

The register of changes lets the revision bear being seen. Bearing being seen is not yet being right. Kant gave a test of publicity in Appendix II of Perpetual Peace; Theory of the State Paper 5, §4, has already checked against it, together with Kant's own statement that this principle is "merely negative," and the sentence saying that one cannot infer the converse, that maxims which can bear publicity are for that reason also just. This paper does not redo the test; it points there.

§5 Mutual Recognition in Ordinary Times

A member has not paid its share for two years, and gives another member's doings as its reason. Between the system and that member, what remains besides an account?

The War Treatise, speaking of peacetime, says that not fighting is also preparing for war, and that what is prepared is not military strength but the deepening of cultivation in peacetime. It lists several things, the first two of which are: "One, keep the channels of fa-mou and fa-jiao open." "Two, maintain mutual recognition of subjectivity." It also says: "When cultivation does not keep deepening in peacetime, the foundation in wartime is thin" (War Treatise §10; fa-mou: attacking through strategy; fa-jiao: attacking through diplomacy). It originally speaks of a society and its adversary in peacetime; here it is extended to a system of cooperation and its members in ordinary times (own undertaking): the channels of dealing stay open, and each still treats the other as a party that can be questioned and can also answer; this is the foundation of cooperation. If mutual recognition in ordinary times wears thin, then when the time comes to respond together, the foundation is thin too.

What mutual recognition is must be made clear first, or it will be read as goodwill, or as endorsement. The Conflict Treatise, treating the mutual acknowledgment of two persons where they are incommensurable, says that what is acknowledged is that "neither party has authority to cancel the other's undertaking on their behalf," and goes on: "Only the origin position is acknowledged; no permission for action is issued." "Acknowledgment does not mean their action may therefore be allowed, does not mean I must bear its consequences, does not mean public rules may not limit it, and does not cancel the harm or rights ledgers." (Conflict Treatise §25) The Theory of the State has already extended these sentences to states (Theory of the State N26); here they are extended again, to the system and its members (own undertaking). When the system and the members recognize the DRP, what they acknowledge is that its acts issue from its positions and that the account is booked in its name. This does not permit its non-payment, does not mean that the other members must bear the consequences of the non-payment, does not mean that the Compact and the sharing decisions cannot bind it, and does not cancel the account of the 520 million.

So mutual recognition stands alongside questioning, recording and withdrawing cooperation; they do not exclude one another. The system put questions to the DRP about its citizenship decree, and recorded its non-payment on sharing; if members adjust their cooperation with it by the account, that is the same. None of these is a refusal to recognize it; conversely, recognizing it is not letting these go.

One more thing must be made clear: the system does not presuppose that its members already trust one another. How Is Institution Possible (Foundational Paper 6; cited as F6), treating the premises of institutions, keeps the default toward persons apart from the presumption about a particular operation: what evidence can defeat is a presumption about a particular operation, and "On that basis authority may be limited, requirements refused, cooperation suspended, and protection of third parties initiated"; so its proviso "judges operations, not persons." The same place also writes: "the framework's non dubito does not achieve co-construction on the members' behalf." (F6 §2.1) The default there is toward persons; a state is not an end, and this series does not extend that layer of default to states. What is extended are two other things (own undertaking): what evidence defeats is a presumption about a particular act of a state, and it does not license the system to pass an overall judgment on a state on that basis, which is the same line as §3; and the system's framework does not achieve mutual trust on the members' behalf; what it can do is keep open the positions in which members can still question, answer and refuse one another.

What does mutual recognition in ordinary times look like when it lands on a matter? Go back to the questions put about the DRP's citizenship decree. When questioned, the DRP did not walk out. It answered: internal affairs, no retroactive effect. It wrote its counter-question to the UCSE into the answer: in the winter of Year 9 the UCSE had revoked the citizenship of a commentator living in the NUT, and Article 14 of the UCSE's Membership Act still required the parents of stateless children to have resided lawfully for five full years; "The UCSE has not given these children citizenship either." The record entered the answer in full, that paragraph included. The DRP also raised an objection on recusal, saying that the Deputy Secretary-General holding NUT citizenship should have recused as well; the objection, too, was entered in the record. In the summer of Year 15, the UCSE changed its own law. The party questioned asked back, and the party asking was questioned in turn; the channel was open at both ends. This is just what mutual recognition in ordinary times is doing.

The sharing matter is the same. The DRP Foreign Ministry protested that "The Secretariat's people are characterizing the Republic." Deleting the characterization treats the DRP still as a construct whose acts issue from positions, and which can be questioned and can answer (§3); the DRP's protest, too, was spoken within the same channel. Afterward it paid forty percent of its Year 16 share, and the Conference of Members counted the two thousand one hundred at half toward the offset. Both sides are still within this channel.

What does failure look like? In the same section, the War Treatise wrote of what an alliance coming apart in peacetime looks like: "Mutual recognition among the Alliance's members gradually weakened, and interaction among subjects within it diminished." That sentence was originally said of an alliance in a fictional world; this paper does not use that example, and takes only the sentence, as a diagnosis: failure need not be an exit; it can be mutual recognition wearing thinner little by little, fewer questions asked, fewer answered, members drifting away one by one.

It may be said that to speak of mutual recognition toward a member that does not pay, and gives another matter as its reason, is indulgence. The weight of this objection is that if mutual recognition meant letting it off, every further payment by the other members would be one more loss to them.

But mutual recognition is not permission, and does not cancel the account. The account is booked as before, the 520 million in full; cooperation can be withdrawn by the account, and that is a check, not force (Paper 4). Mutual recognition sees to only two things: the channel is not closed, and the other is still treated as a party that can answer. Only a member treated as a party that can answer can be questioned at all; treat it as a party already characterized, and the questions have no object.

This section leaves two points. Mutual recognition is written as a direction, and its depth is not measured; this paper gives no scale for how far members must drift apart before it counts as failure, and leaves that to judgment in positions. Nor does this paper guarantee that mutual recognition will not weaken: a visible account and an open channel are a foundation, not a guarantee.

§6 The Account Follows the Address

Addresses change. A member can exit, can be suspended or expelled, can apply to rejoin; the system itself can be dissolved. When the status of an address changes, what becomes of the account at that address?

This paper's answer is that none of these rewrites the record. Acts before exit remain on record. Re-accession does not wash the account clean; acts during the period of exit, too, remain on record and remain open to questions. Expulsion does not write off the account. On dissolution, the records are handed, matter by matter, to the member states concerned for safekeeping, and do not vanish along with the system.

The Conflict Treatise, treating mutual acknowledgment between two persons, wrote of the side of leaving: "Where shared resources, third-party responsibility or procedural obligation remain, departure does not close those accounts and can only pass to arrangement or arbitration." (Conflict Treatise §25) It originally speaks of two persons after each has gone their own way; here it is extended to a member's leaving the system (own undertaking): when a member exits, the acts it performed while in the system, the obligations arising from them, and the procedures already begun are not settled because it has gone. The clause in the same section, that acknowledgment "does not cancel the harm or rights ledgers," applies here as it stands. The Theory of Government says: "Decisions can be final: a particular matter can be answered and closed. Positions cannot be final: no position may monopolize the power to shut the door" (Theory of Government G36). Exit is a state's decision; what it settles is that state's position in the system from then on; it cannot by itself close the earlier accounts along with it.

The Compact writes this into several articles, all of them examples at the rule layer. Clause 3 of Article 25: "Exit does not affect obligations arising from acts before the exit, nor procedures already begun." Clause 4 of Article 27: "Suspension and expulsion do not release the member from obligations arising from its earlier acts." Article 30: before dissolution, follow-up arrangements are made, unsettled petitions are given a destination, the archives are handed, matter by matter, to the members concerned for safekeeping, and the sharing accounts are cleared. Article 27 does not fully agree with this series' direction: its suspension suspends only the right to vote, which is narrower than this series' direction; its conditions for expulsion are not limited to line-crossing, which is wider than this series' direction (Papers 4 and 7).

One matter shows where these words land. In the winter of Year 17, Stonegorge gave notice of exit under Article 25, to take effect in the winter of Year 18. During the notice period, the Three-Borders Petitions Committee twice put questions to Stonegorge on the petition concerning the rules on going out of the Eastfield camp, and Stonegorge answered neither time. This petition, and another petition against Stonegorge, the Committee heard and concluded as usual under Article 25, giving its opinions in Year 19; Stonegorge did not reply. In the autumn of Year 20, Stonegorge applied to rejoin. In the discussion, the UCSE's permanent representative listed the two petitions left unanswered during the period of exit as an item to be dealt with; Stonegorge's representative said that Stonegorge was willing to state, for every common measure from then on and item by item, whether it would take part, "and to reply to those two petitions."

The principle and this matter must be written separately. The principle is: questions not answered are not settled when exit takes effect (Paper 7, XZ47). How these facts are judged, and where Stonegorge's not answering during the notice period, and its not answering after the exit took effect, each land, is in Paper 7, §§1, 3 and 4; what conditions to set on re-accession in view of these two petitions is the business of Paper 7, XZ48. Here only one thing is looked at: as of the autumn of Year 20, those two petitions were still booked at the address of Stonegorge. The UCSE could list them because the record was there.

Positions at an address also change hands. Stonegorge changed its Chief of the Commonwealth twice in these years. In the autumn of Year 17 there were elections to the Commonwealth Chamber; the new majority chose a new Chief of the Commonwealth, and she said on taking office: "The system gave us a record, and Lamp Island is still in the Plateau Federation's hands." Stonegorge then gave notice of exit. In the spring of Year 20, after the Commonwealth Chamber passed a motion of no confidence, early elections were held, and a new majority again chose a new Chief of the Commonwealth; Stonegorge then applied to rejoin. The failure to answer during the notice period took place during the term of the Chief of the Commonwealth chosen in the autumn of Year 17; from which position's decision the non-answer came is not recorded. The one who said Stonegorge was willing to reply was the permanent representative at the time of the Year 20 application.

How is the account divided? The address's obligation stands as before: that the two petitions must be answered is a matter for the address of Stonegorge, and is not settled because the Chief of the Commonwealth has changed. A person's account goes with the person: if the non-answer came from the decision of some person in some position, that item is booked to that position and that person (§2). Suspension is the same: once a suspension is lifted, what is restored is that address's rights in the common procedures; what those in positions did during the suspension is not thereby settled. Theory of the State N39 attributes the account to positions and persons, speaking of what lies within a state; here it is extended to the members of the system, and to the times when a position at the address changes hands (own undertaking). The address does not change; what changes is the person in the position; so the address's account and the person's account must go separately. How these matters are judged is in Paper 7, §3.

The sharing matter is the same. By Year 20 the DRP had paid forty percent of its Year 16 share; the rest remains booked in the DRP's name. Whether those in the positions of the DRP Foreign Ministry changed in these years is not recorded; changed or not, the share is booked at the address, and the letter at the position that wrote it.

It may be said that re-accession is a new covenant, that a new covenant starts on a new page, and that old accounts should not be carried into it. The weight of this objection is that re-accession is indeed a new covenant: under Article 26 of the Compact, re-accession is a new admission under Article 7(a), requiring the consent of all parties, and each member may put forward conditions (Paper 1, XZ1; Paper 7, XZ48). The DRP's permanent representative said as much on the Stonegorge occasion: "No further conditions should be set; leaving is a right, and so is coming back."

But what a new covenant covenants for is what comes after. Settling one matter does not settle everything (Paper 1, XZ3): a new covenant settles this one matter, and does not rewrite what any party did before. Re-accession can be made without any conditions at all, and the two petitions remain booked in Stonegorge's name all the same: whether the account is there, and whether coming back requires conditions, are two separate things.

Here, too, things are said separately. That the account is not written off by exit, expulsion, re-accession or dissolution is a norm; Articles 25, 27 and 30 of the Compact are examples at the rule layer; what conditions to set on re-accession in view of old accounts is left to the judgment of the parties' political leaders, as agreed, and the account falls on the positions and persons that make the judgment (Paper 7, XZ48); this step is at the judgment layer.

This section leaves two points. That the account is booked is not the same as its having landed: the forty percent has been paid, the rest not yet; whether it has landed is recognized by covenant or by award, not decided by one party alone (Paper 4, XZ22). What cannot be provided for on dissolution is registered as a remainder, as it is, and it is not declared that there is no longer any shortfall (Paper 7, XZ51). How exit is reviewed, the floor for re-accession, the conditions for expulsion: the procedural side of all of these is in Paper 7.

XZ21 The account follows the address (carried over from Conflict Treatise §25 and Theory of Government G36). Exit, expulsion, re-accession and dissolution do not rewrite the record: acts before exit remain on record; re-accession does not wash the account clean, and acts during the period of exit remain on record and open to questions; expulsion does not write off the account; on dissolution, the records are handed, matter by matter, to the member states concerned for safekeeping. Recognition does not cancel the harm or rights ledgers; nor does leaving close shared resources, third-party responsibility or procedural obligation, which can only pass to arrangement or arbitration (Conflict Treatise §25 originally speaks of two persons; here it is extended to members and the system; own undertaking). Decisions can be final; positions cannot be final (Theory of Government G36). For the procedural side, see Paper 7. Read together with XZ17: when a position at the address changes hands, the address's obligation stands as before; a person's account goes with the person, and is booked to the position and person that made the decision; once a suspension is lifted, what is restored is that address's rights in the common procedures, and the person's account is not thereby settled (own undertaking).

Where This Paper Stops

This paper has proved only these things: when there is no superior to enforce, the account is still at the address. Reputation is an account visible from outside, a distributed observation, and the system sets up no centralized identifying authority. Records are attributed to positions and persons; the account under the system's name and the account of the member states are booked at both addresses; the record is itself an act, and changes to it are registered; a record is a trace, not a verdict. Records record only acts, not the names of states; the same kind of act takes the same wording, and the test is that, with the acting party replaced and not one other word changed, the sentence still holds when applied to the same act by another state. Deception is discounted; saying it openly wins over time. Mutual recognition in ordinary times is the foundation of cooperation; mutual recognition is not permission, and the system does not achieve mutual trust on the members' behalf. The account follows the address, and does not vanish with exit, expulsion, re-accession or dissolution; when a position at the address changes hands, the address's account and the person's account go separately.

What it has not proved: how reputation is measured; the test of publicity itself, which Theory of the State Paper 5, §4, has already checked against Kant, and to which this paper points. Whether the wording of the two records, on Lamp Island and on the Outer Sandbars, was right: this paper answers only by what it is to be decided; the two acts are judged in Paper 5, §1, and Paper 4, §7, and who judged the wording under the rules, and how, is also written in those two places. The procedural side of exit, expulsion, re-accession and dissolution is in Paper 7. Whether the account has landed, and how peers withdraw cooperation by the account, is in Paper 4. Kant reads the league as the negative surrogate of a world republic; how this series reads that ordering is in Paper 8.

The remainder it leaves: how readers read a record is beyond the record's reach, and the account for a misreading is with the reader. How far mutual recognition must weaken before it counts as failure is not measured, and is left to judgment in positions. Whether the DRP's reasons for not paying hold, this paper does not judge. For the UCSE's late payment, the sharing report recorded only the length of the delay, and left out the position and the document; this is pointed out as it is; whether the analyst's transfer had anything to do with that sentence is not recorded.

Once the account is visible, how peers withdraw cooperation by the account is taken up in Paper 4.

The Six Propositions of This Paper

XZ16 Reputation is an account visible from outside. To recognize a state is to acknowledge that its acts issue from its positions and that the account is booked in its name (Theory of the State N26); the system's record sets the account at this address out in the open (the Theory of the State originally speaks of one state recognizing another; here it is extended to the system and its members; own undertaking). Whether a construct lets itself be questioned, and how it answers when questioned, is visible from outside (War Treatise §16 originally speaks of a war-party's allowing questioning; here it is extended to the system and its members in ordinary times; own undertaking); this paper calls this account visible from outside reputation. When there is no superior to enforce, the account is still at the address, and peers judge for themselves by the account and adjust their cooperation (Theory of the State N36; for withdrawing cooperation, see Paper 4). Reputation is a distributed observation, with no centralized identifying authority (Moral Law P9 §5.3 originally speaks of identifying a person's condition; here it is extended to states; own undertaking); the system's record is one place where this account is kept, and does not read it on the parties' behalf. Reputation counts through the parties who see the account making their decisions by it: whether to trust the recorded party's covenants, whether to give it cooperation (own undertaking); whether the recorded party cares about its own reputation is not within this paper's discussion. Reputation is not the whole of what counts (Power Theory P1 §III). Reputation does not count on its own; it counts together with covenants, questions and checks (Paper 4, XZ23). Reputation is not measured; the account for a misreading is with the reader.

XZ17 Records are attributed to positions and persons (carried over from Theory of the State N39 and Institution Theory Z7a). Decisions and reasons are made public, and records are attributed to particular positions and persons (Theory of the State N39 originally speaks of a state; here it is extended to the system and its members; own undertaking). The construct is the address of attribution, not the subject of the act; the address and those who actually made the decision do not hide each other (Institution Theory Z7a). What is made in the system's name is traced back to the positions and votes that made it (Power Theory P3 §V.5; own undertaking); the account under the system's name is with the system, and also with the member states and positions that voted for it and carry it out; it is booked at both addresses, and neither transfers it to the other (Theory of Government G25 originally speaks of two levels of government; here it is extended to the system and its member states; own undertaking). The record is itself an act: its account falls on the positions that drafted, changed and adopted it, and changes are registered. A record is a trace, not a verdict (Conflict Treatise §26; own undertaking): it records acts and reasons, and does not judge whether the reasons hold; when the reasons for non-performance point to another member, the two accounts are each booked at their own address, and that other matter is questioned separately, by its own object. On what happens when a position at the address changes hands, read together with XZ21.

XZ18 Recording acts, not the names of states (carried over from the Theory of the State Prequel, §7). The system's reports and records write only the act, the position that performed it, the documents it relied on and on whom it falls, and do not write what kind of state a state is; they do not prejudge a state's next act by its leaning (the Theory of the State Prequel, §7: a leaning is a direction, not an identity). The same kind of act takes the same wording, whether the one acting is a member or a state outside the system, and whichever state it is. The test is this: replace the acting party, change not one other word, and the sentence still holds when applied to the same act by another state (own undertaking). Recording only acts, and this test, belong to the normative layer; the articles of rules on records, the register of changes and the entering of dissent in the record are examples at the rule layer; how a particular wording is judged under the rules is left to those in positions in the Conference of Members, and the reasons and dissent are entered in the record. The record of non-performance records shares, timing and reasons as they are, and attributes them to the position that made the decision.

XZ19 Deception is discounted; saying it openly wins over time (carried over from War Treatise §12). The War Treatise originally speaks of what lies between war-parties: once yin-mou is seen through, all later cooperation, agreements and signals are discounted; real structure needs no maintenance, and falsehood needs continuous maintenance; yang-mou does not win in the short term, but wins in time. Here it is extended to multilateral covenants and signals in ordinary times (own undertaking): once a member's explanation to the system (the grounds it explains for sending forces, its share, its reasons) is seen through as false, all its later explanations are discounted; the system's own record is the same: if changes go unregistered and one is seen through, all later reports are discounted. Stating the bottom line openly, with real capacity behind it, is the qualified form of reputation and checks (Paper 4, XZ23); whether a word is credible over the long run rests on the depth of cultivation, not on how loudly it is said (War Treatise §10; own undertaking). Saying it openly is a direction, and sets up no separate obligation on members; which explanation is seen through as false is judged by the peers who read it and by the channels within each state, and the system only records. Winning over time is a tendency of structure, not a guarantee.

XZ20 Mutual recognition in ordinary times is the foundation of cooperation (carried over from War Treatise §10). In ordinary times the channels of dealing are kept open and mutual recognition of subjectivity is maintained; when cultivation does not keep deepening in ordinary times, the foundation in wartime is thin (War Treatise §10 originally speaks of a society and its adversary in peacetime; here it is extended to the system and its members in ordinary times; own undertaking). Mutual recognition acknowledges only the origin position, issues no permission for action, and does not cancel the harm or rights ledgers (Conflict Treatise §25; Theory of the State N26 has already extended this to states; here it is extended to the system and its members; own undertaking); so mutual recognition stands alongside questioning, recording and withdrawing cooperation. The system does not presuppose that its members already trust one another: what evidence defeats is a presumption about a particular act of a state, not an overall judgment on a state; the system's framework does not achieve mutual trust on the members' behalf, and only keeps open the positions in which members can still question, answer and refuse one another (F6 §2.1 originally speaks of an institution and its subjects; here it is extended to the system and its member states; own undertaking). What failure in ordinary times looks like is mutual recognition weakening and members drifting away one by one (the diagnosis of War Treatise §10). Mutual recognition is written as a direction, and is not measured; how far the drifting apart must go to count as failure is left to judgment in positions.

XZ21 The account follows the address (carried over from Conflict Treatise §25 and Theory of Government G36). Exit, expulsion, re-accession and dissolution do not rewrite the record: acts before exit remain on record; re-accession does not wash the account clean, and acts during the period of exit remain on record and open to questions; expulsion does not write off the account; on dissolution, the records are handed, matter by matter, to the member states concerned for safekeeping. Recognition does not cancel the harm or rights ledgers; nor does leaving close shared resources, third-party responsibility or procedural obligation, which can only pass to arrangement or arbitration (Conflict Treatise §25 originally speaks of two persons; here it is extended to members and the system; own undertaking). Decisions can be final; positions cannot be final (Theory of Government G36). For the procedural side, see Paper 7. Read together with XZ17: when a position at the address changes hands, the address's obligation stands as before; a person's account goes with the person, and is booked to the position and person that made the decision; once a suspension is lifted, what is restored is that address's rights in the common procedures, and the person's account is not thereby settled (own undertaking).

这件事先落在人身上。第 3 号决定分给 DRP 的八千个名额，背后是东坪营地里登记满六个月、等着转送的人；经海路到了 DRP、得了暂住的约二千一百人，算不算进抵扣，要等成员会议决定。记录所对的地址是 DRP 这一成员，同一份报告也记了 UCSE；作出不交决定的位置，是 DRP 外务部。记录自己也有位置：起草它的分析员，删改它的副秘书长，通过它的成员会议。

要看的有两条线。一条是记录的线：体系的记录只记施为、作出施为的位置与所依的文书，不给一国定性，也不少记一笔。另一条在 DRP 那一边：分担是它批准宪约时认下的，第 16 年夏的第 1 号分担决定与第 17 年春的第 3 号分担决定都以 4 比 0 通过，DRP 都在赞成的一方。它不交的理由成不成立，不由记录来判。

体系没有强力，DRP 不交，没有谁能替体系去收。这不等于账就没了。这一篇从这件事问起：没有上级去执行时，账怎样仍在地址上？记什么，记在谁的名下，怎样不给一国定性也不少记一笔？账又怎样跟着地址，走过退出、开除、再加入与解散？

篇名叫"互信"，可这一篇不从成员彼此已经信得过写起。信任在这里是结果，不是前提：成员之间信不信得过，要靠一份外面看得见、记在说得出的地址上、经得起被问的账。分六步走。第一节写声誉是外部看得见的账，在那里与康德对接。第二节写记录归到位置与人。第三节写记录只记施为，不记国名，这是那一句被删的理由。第四节写欺骗被打折、明说在时间里赢。第五节写平时的互认是协作的底子。第六节写账跟着地址走，不随退出、开除、再加入或解散消失。

体例照本系列前置篇。这个系列在须判断的地方分规范层、规则层、判断层说话（前置篇第十一节）；本篇用到时，写明哪一句是规范，哪一项是建议的规则或例子，哪一步交给位置上的人。本篇只对体系与成员写应当；对体系外的国家，只写体系与成员怎样对它。

第一节　外面看得见的账

一个不能下命令的体系记下一份账，这份账有什么用？先看一份比分担报告早三年的记录。

第 14 年夏，体系就 DRP 第 9 年秋的一项法令向它发问。那项法令规定，动员期间出境、六个月内不归的人丧失 DRP 籍，他们在境外所生的子女，DRP 不予登记。DRP 外务部答：籍的取得与丧失是共和国的内政，法令早于宪约，宪约第 4 条第（三）项不溯及既往；答复另附一段，转过来问 UCSE 自己撤过的籍与它自己的籍法。第 14 年冬，成员会议以 2 比 0 通过记录，DRP 不参加表决。这件事的经过与它落在谁身上，第二篇开篇已经交代；这里只看记录通过以后的一段。

记录放在秘书处的网站上，谁都看得到。第 15 年，NUT 的一家报纸、UCSE 的两家报纸、DRP 首都的一家报纸，各刊出记录的全文或摘要。同年夏，UCSE 民众院以 171 票对 120 票修改《成员籍法》第 14 条，删去了无国籍儿童的父母须合法居留满五年的条件；到第 17 年，柳渡接收中心出生的孩子里有三百八十九人取得 UCSE 籍。同年秋，DRP 外务部公布新的登记办法：在境外出生的子女，父母一方亲自到 DRP 在 NUT 或 UCSE 的领事处申请、并"补办动员期间的出境手续"的，可以登记；父母本人的籍不恢复。到第 16 年底，依这一办法登记的孩子三十七人。

这两件事与那份记录是什么关系？记录不替谁说。它没有说 UCSE 是因它改了法，没有说 DRP 的办法是对它的答复，也没有说三十七这个数够不够。读记录的人，自己读。

可是有一样东西不必谁来说：账在外面。谁问了什么，谁答了什么，哪些没有答，记在谁的名下，记录里都有；记录放在网站上，四家报纸又刊出了它的全文或摘要。国家论讲过承认给出的是什么：「承认一个国家，不是为它的施为背书：认的是它的施为出自它的位置、账记在它的名下」（国家论 N26）。承认留下一个地址。国家论说的是一国承认另一国；这里推到体系与成员：体系的记录做的事，是把这个地址上的账摆到外面，让每一方都看得见（自担）。

战争论讲过一件相近的事。它说，一场战争要过的几条判断里，前三条战争方可以自己声称已经通过，外面很难核；只有一条不同：「追问是否被允许，这件事从外部直接可观察」（战争论 §16）。追问的人在不在，问到没到作决定的地方，问的人受不受保护，问了改没改什么，每一样都留下外面看得见的痕迹。战争论说的是战争方对内部追问的允许。这里推到平时的体系与成员：一个构许不许人问它，被问时怎样答，从外面看得见（自担）。推得过去，是因为那一句的理由不在战争上，在问与答留下的痕迹上；平时的问与答，留下的痕迹只会更多。

这份外面看得见的账，本篇称作声誉。

声誉要作数，靠的不是谁去执行它。体系没有强力：宪约第 2 条第 2 款写着，体系的任何机关不得对成员、对成员境内的人执行强力。没有上级去执行，账仍在地址上；看见账的平级者，可以照账调整自己的配合。国家论把这一点写成每一条通道都关不上的东西：「它关不上的，是平级者据此自行判断、收回承认之外的配合」（国家论 N36）。平级者怎样照账收回配合，是第四篇的事；这一节只立前一半：账在外面，看得见。

账摆在外面，为什么就能让各国彼此信得过？这一问要接到康德。头上没有最高的立法者时，一国凭什么信得过自己的权利？康德（Immanuel Kant）在《永久和平论》第二条正式条款里问过这一问。他先说，一个民族要结成一个国家，给自己立一个最高的立法、治理与裁判的权力，好和平地解决彼此的争执，由此说它们之间不应有战争，这是可以理解的。接着说：

「可是，如果这个国家说："我与别的国家之间不应有战争，虽然我不承认有一个最高的立法权力来保障我的权利，我也保障它的权利"，那就完全不能理解，我要把对我的权利的信任建立在什么上面，除非是公民社会联合的替代物，即自由的联盟制。」（Wenn aber dieser Staat sagt: »es soll kein Krieg zwischen mir und andern Staaten seyn, obgleich ich keine oberste gesetzgebende Gewalt erkenne, die mir mein, und der ich ihr Recht sichere,« so ist es gar nicht zu verstehen, worauf ich dann das Vertrauen zu meinem Rechte gründen wolle, wenn es nicht das Surrogat des bürgerlichen Gesellschaftbundes, nämlich der freye Föderalism ist；《永久和平论》第二节第二条正式条款，据 1795 年 Nicolovius 初版，Project Gutenberg 电子本 #46873；对照 M. C. Smith 英译，电子本 #50922，A 档）

共同的问题是：头上没有最高的立法者时，一国对自己权利的信任、各国彼此的信任，靠什么。这一篇问的正是这一个。康德在同一条里说，这个联盟不必是一个国际国家（国家论第五篇第七节已对过这一处）；这个系列也不在各国头上立带强力的上级。两边都认为，没有这个上级，不等于什么都不必有：权利的保障与彼此的信任，仍须有共同的安排。康德的答案是自由的联盟。这个系列在这一共同的边界里，把作数的东西展开为共识、牵制与声誉（前置篇第七节），把声誉读作外部看得见的账。

可以替康德拟一个最强的反驳：一份账摆在外面，太薄了；不在乎别人怎样看的国家，照样不受它约束；要让信任有所依托，非有一个持久的联盟不可。这一说本系列接一半。三界协作体系本身就是由约结成的，没有约，就没有可以记账的地址；声誉也不独自作数（见下文与第四篇）。本系列不接的，是把信任的根据放在联盟这一形式上。它问的是在一个已经结成的体系里，什么让账作数；答案落在账看得见、记在地址上、问得到。

分开的地方也要说准。康德把自由的联盟称作公民社会联合的"替代物"（Surrogat）；同一条里，他把世界共和国称作积极的理念，把联盟称作它的消极的替代物。这一排序本系列怎样读，放到第八篇；这里只接康德问的那一问，不先下判断。这是同一个问题之下范围的分工，不是驳倒。

声誉是谁的观察？一条容易走的路，是让体系来当裁判：由秘书处或成员会议判定一国守不守信、是哪一种国家，各方照这个判定待它。本系列不走这条路。道德律讲识别一个人处在哪一种状态时，写过它在机构上的做法：「机构性实施是通过声誉网络的分布式观察，而非中心化的识别权威。」同处还要求有「多元声音在识别过程中防止单一视角的支配」（道德律第九篇 §5.3）。那里说的是识别一个人。这里推到国与国：一国的账怎样读，由看见它的各方各自读，体系不设一个中心化的识别权威（自担）。体系的记录是这份账的一处，账摆在那里；从中读出什么，不由体系一家来定。第三节会看到，那一句被删，这是理由之一。

有的地方问到不了，账照样在外面。第 14 年春，石峡邦申请加入。宪约第 7 条（甲）规定，接纳新成员须全体成员书面同意。UCSE 与 NUT 当季书面同意；DRP 外务部致函秘书长："共和国的同意须经人民议会审议。"第 14 年夏，高原联邦与 DRP 签了一份化肥长约：五年，价格比上一年的市价低一成五。UCSE 常驻代表提议，就这份长约与 DRP 推迟同意之间的关系向 DRP 发问。秘书长持 DRP 籍回避，分管共同程序的副秘书长代行，书面意见是："接纳新成员是新约。一成员是否同意新约，是该成员自己的事，不在第 7 条（乙）可以发问的'已有的事'之内。"UCSE 撤回了提议。新约与已有的事怎样分，第二篇第二节已经写过（XZ10）。

问撤回了，账没有撤。长约签订的时点与价格；人民议会第 14 年秋以 352 票对 221 票同意接纳；DRP 北部各区的议员说这份长约"与石峡邦的事无关"，另有议员说"时间上不是巧合"：这些都在外面，谁都看得见。那是不是一笔交换，体系不替谁判；各方各自读，读出的东西各自担。分布的观察落到一件事上，就是这个样子：没有一个中心说"这是交换"或者"这不是交换"，可是每一个要与 DRP、与高原联邦打交道的一方，手里有同一组事实。

声誉为什么作数？一国的声誉要分开两面看。一面是它的声誉对它自己：它在不在乎。这一面本篇不讨论。更偏 14DD 方向的一方可能不在乎声誉，只在乎自己的实力；更偏 15DD 方向的一方用不着在乎声誉，它照把人当作目的去做；别人怎样读它的账，是看账一方的事，那正是下面要说的另一面。另一面是它的声誉对别的各方：别国、体系、各国的人看见它的账，据此决定信不信它的约，给不给它配合，要不要问它。本篇说声誉作数，只说这一面（自担）。一国在别国那里有没有信用，取决于它的施为，而它的施为摆在外面。权力论讲一国之内的权力者时也划过界：声誉不是现代政治中权力的唯一来源（权力论第一篇 §3）。在国与国之间，声誉同样不是作数的全部。

有人会说：声誉只对在乎它的一方起作用。影响力大、更偏 14DD 方向的一方未必在乎；更偏 14DD 方向的几方之间，靠声誉更撑不起什么。道德律说过：「14DD之间不能form相互支撑的机构性结构」（道德律第九篇 §2.5）。这一说的分量是真的，可它说的是前一面。本篇说的声誉作数，不靠被记的一方在乎：一国不在乎自己的声誉，别国照样据它的账决定信不信它的约、给不给它配合；它若只在乎自己的实力，这些决定落到它的实力上，它也认得。一份账摆在外面，靠的是看账的各方照它调整自己的做法，它才成为代价。这一机制是否实际起作用，还看读账的各方怎样据它作决定。更偏 14DD 方向的几方之间不能形成相互支撑的机构性结构，不等于各方完全不用公开的记录计算利害；本篇不从道德律那一句推出这样一个全称的否定。

可是这一节没有说声誉独自作数。没有上级的体系里，作数的不止一样：约在第一篇，问在第二篇，牵制在第四篇。牵制与声誉都不要求对方先认人是目的（第四篇 XZ23）。这一节只立一件事：账看得见。看得见是其余几样的前提：没有看得见的账，平级者照什么收回配合，问又从哪里问起？道德律那一句恰好说明，体系不能指望更偏 14DD 方向的成员自己撑起协作；它要靠账摆在外面，让每一方手里都有东西可对。

这一节留下两处。声誉不计量：一国的账在外面值多少，本篇不给数，也不证明它在每一件事上都起作用。读错了，账不改：读的人从一份记录里读出了什么，读错的那一笔记在读的人身上，不记在记录上，也不记在被记的一方身上。

XZ16　声誉是外部看得见的账。承认一个国家，认的是它的施为出自它的位置、账记在它的名下（国家论 N26）；体系的记录把这一地址上的账摆到外面（国家论原说一国承认另一国，这里推到体系与成员，自担）。一个构许不许人问它、被问时怎样答，从外部看得见（战争论 §16 原说战争方对追问的允许，这里推到平时的体系与成员，自担）；本篇把这份外部看得见的账称作声誉。没有上级去执行，账仍在地址上，平级者照账自行判断、调整配合（国家论 N36；收回配合见第四篇）。声誉是分布的观察，不设中心化的识别权威（道德律第九篇 §5.3 原说识别一个人的状态，这里推到国与国，自担）；体系的记录是这份账的一处，不替各方读它。声誉作数，靠看见账的各方据它作决定：信不信被记一方的约，给不给它配合（自担）；被记的一方在不在乎自己的声誉，不在本篇讨论之内。声誉不是作数的全部（权力论第一篇 §3）。声誉不独自作数，与约、问、牵制一起作数（XZ23）。声誉不计量；读错的账在读的人。

第二节　记录归到位置与人

报告里写"DRP 未交"。这里的"DRP"是谁？

一个国家不交钱，没有哪一个人从口袋里掏出过这笔钱又放了回去。不交是一项决定：决定由某个位置作出，依某份文书说出。改后的报告写的是"DRP 外务部第 17 年秋的函件所述理由附后（全文）"。不交被归到 DRP 外务部的那一份函件上，那是作出决定的位置与它所依的文书；记录没有拿"共和国"一个名义了事。同一份报告也记了 UCSE 的迟交。

国家论给一国的归账写过最低的内容：「归账的最低内容（自担）：决定与理由公开，记录归到具体的位置与人；受影响的人可以在一条独立的通道里就此发问并得到回答；有过失的位置，此后在同类决定上受更严的确认。」（国家论 N39）那里说的是一国对它施为所及的人。这里推到体系与它的成员：体系的记录记一个成员的施为，同样归到作出它的位置与人（自担）。

为什么要归到位置？因为国家只是地址。制度论说：「构是归责地址，不是施为的主体；归责地址与实际实施者不互相遮蔽。」（制度论 Z7a）同一篇还说：「构的名义不遮蔽有独立根据的个人责任，构的失责也不自动转记到某个人身上。」（制度论 Z6）记"DRP 未交"，记的是地址；记"DRP 外务部第 17 年秋的函件"，记的是地址上哪一个位置、凭什么作了这件事。两样都要。只记地址，问的人不知道该问谁；只记位置，地址的义务就像落在了一个人身上，那个人一换，义务也跟着走了。

体系自己的名义也是这样。以体系名义作出的东西，没有一样是"体系"自己作的。权力论讲权力的装置时说：「装置不施权, 个体调用装置施权。」（权力论第三篇 §V.5）那里说的是一国之内的权力装置；这里推到体系：以体系名义作出的决定与文书，还原到作出它的位置与票（自担）。宪约第 3 条把这一点写成了规则的一个例子：以体系名义发出的一切文书，载明作出它的机关、表决的票数与各成员的投票。所以体系名义下的账，在体系，也在投票、执行它的成员国与位置。政府论讲两级政府时说：「两个构的账各按来源核，不互相转记（自担）。」（政府论 G25）原说一国之内的两级政府，这里推到体系与成员国（自担）：两个地址并记，谁也不把账转到另一边去。

记录本身也是施为。分担报告的这一笔，有三个位置：起草的分析员；作删改的副秘书长，因为秘书长持 DRP 籍，回避了；以 3 比 0 通过记录的成员会议，DRP 不参加表决。删改依《记录规程》第 4 条作出，载入改动登记，各成员都可以查阅。谁改的，依什么改的，改前怎样写，改后怎样写，都在。宪约第 9 条与《记录规程》是规则层的例子，不是唯一的写法；要紧的是，记录自己的账也落在位置上，改过的地方查得到。一份可以悄悄改的记录，它记下的别人的账，也就不可信了（第四节）。

记录又是痕迹，不是判决。冲突论讲一段冲突从外面能看见什么时，把线索、判准与痕迹分开，说：「线索不是判准，痕迹也不是判决」（冲突论 §26）。原说第一人称之间的冲突从第三人称看到什么，这里推到体系对成员的记录（自担）：记录记下 DRP 欠交了多少，记下它的理由，不判它的理由成不成立。

DRP 的理由指向另一个成员：UCSE 仍冻结着 DRP 国家机构的资产。记录把函件原样附上，没有替 DRP 删，也没有替 UCSE 答。那项冻结是 UCSE 的施为；要问它，照它自己的对象另问，被问的是 UCSE（第二篇 XZ12）。一个成员不履约的理由指向另一个成员时，记录把两笔账各记在各自的地址上，不拿一笔去抵另一笔。那二千一百人计不计入抵扣，是成员会议的决定：第 18 年春按一半计入，决定与票照记。

照强力的判据看这份记录（前置篇第二节）：强力，是用自己的判断力替代对方就它自己的事的判断。这份记录替代了谁的哪一件判断？交不交、以什么理由不交，是 DRP 外务部就 DRP 的事作的判断；记录把这一判断与它的理由原样记下，没有替它改。理由成不成立、那二千一百人怎样计，是成员会议的判断；记录也没有替成员会议下。记录做的，是让两样判断都落在一个看得见的地址上。体系不强制执行，落地在各成员；落没落地，有了记录才查得到。

有人会说：归到位置与人，会把国与国之间的事说成个人的事，国家正好躲到人后面。DRP 可以说那是外务部的一封函件，换一位部长就换一种说法；体系也可以说删改是某位副秘书长的事，与各成员无关。这一说的分量在于，归到个人确实可能被拿来卸掉地址的账。

可是本节立的是两址并记，不是改记。DRP 的账在 DRP：五点二亿没有交，八千个名额没有接，这一笔记在 DRP 名下，不因外务部换了人而结。那一份函件的账在外务部，作出它的位置此后照它被问。体系的账在体系，删改的账在副秘书长与通过记录的成员会议。哪一边都不替另一边了账。制度论那两句，说的正是两个方向都不遮蔽：构的名义不挡住个人，个人也不替构担。

哪一样是规范，哪一样是例子，要分开。记施为、记作出它的位置与所依的文书，是规范层的要求。《记录规程》的条文、改动登记、宪约第 3 条与第 9 条，是规则层的例子，各方可以照约定另设。一件施为记到多细，交记录与报告的位置判断，改动照登，这一步在判断层。

这里留下一处，要照实指出。改后的文字对 DRP 的不交，记了数额、名额、作出决定的位置与所依的文书；对 UCSE 的迟交，只记了"迟交四个月"。UCSE 的迟交也出自一个位置、依一份文书：它的《接纳分担拨款法》到第 16 年冬才通过。照同一把尺，这里少记了一处；这把尺对哪一个成员都一样，不因记的是 UCSE 还是 DRP 而换。

那位分析员第 18 年春调往云河数据站，秘书处说是本人申请。调动与那一句有没有关系，没有记载，本篇不推。地址上的位置换了人时，账怎样走，第六节再写。

XZ17　记录归到位置与人（承国家论 N39、制度论 Z7a）。决定与理由公开，记录归到具体的位置与人（国家论 N39 原说一国，这里推到体系与成员，自担）。构是归责的地址，不是施为的主体；地址与实际作出决定的人不互相遮蔽（制度论 Z7a）。以体系名义作出的，还原到作出它的位置与票（权力论第三篇 §V.5，自担）；体系名义下的账在体系，也在投票、执行它的成员国与位置，两址并记，不互相转记（政府论 G25 原说两级政府，这里推到体系与成员国，自担）。记录本身也是施为：它的账落在起草、改动、通过它的位置上，改动照登。记录是痕迹，不是判决（冲突论 §26，自担）：它记施为与理由，不判理由成不成立；不履约的理由指向另一成员时，两笔账各记在各自的地址上，那一件照它的对象另问。地址上的位置换了人时，与 XZ21 合读。

第三节　记施为，不记国名

那一句为什么要删？

先把它与留下的几句放在一起。被删的一句（引言已照录）说了三件事：DRP 是哪一种国家；它不会履行对体系外之人的义务；它不交份额，是它国家性质的表现。留下的几句说的是：多少钱没有交，多少名额没有接，作出这一决定的是哪个位置、凭哪一份函件，函件说了什么理由，二千一百人得了暂住、计不计入抵扣待决，另一个成员迟交了多久。

被删的一句错在两处。

一处在它说的是什么。留下的几句说的是一国做了什么、落在谁身上、依什么文书；被删的一句说的是一国是什么。前者问得了：五点二亿是不是这个数，八千个名额是不是一人未接，函件是不是这样说的，二千一百人是不是得了暂住。DRP 可以答，可以反问，可以改。后者问不了：说一国"是"哪一种国家，它只能接受，或者拒绝。DRP 外务部的反应正是拒绝："秘书处的人在给共和国定性。"

另一处在它推出的东西。那一句由国家的性质推出"不会履行对体系外之人的义务"，再把不交读作这一性质的"表现"。可是同一份报告里，就有与它不合的施为：约二千一百名沙洲人，正是体系外的人，经海路到了 DRP，DRP 给了他们暂住。此后到第 20 年，它交了第 16 年份额的四成。由身份推下一项施为，推出来的与那一国自己的施为不合。

国家论讲偏向时立过一条纪律：「偏向是方向，不是身份。」又说：「不因一国更偏向 14DD 的方向，就预先认定它的每一项施为是扩张或压制，也不因一国更偏向 15DD 的方向，就预先认定它的每一项施为是防守。」（国家论前置篇第七节）那一句两样都犯了：把方向写成身份，又由身份预断下一项施为。

还有一层。第一节说过，体系不设中心化的识别权威。那一句若留在报告里，就是秘书处的一位分析员以体系的名义，替所有读报告的人认定了一国是什么。

所以照记施为的线，那一句要删。这是规范层的读法。在那件事里作出判断的，是分管记录与报告的副秘书长，依《记录规程》第 4 条删改；成员会议第 18 年春以 3 比 0 通过记录，DRP 不参加表决；石峡邦常驻代表在会上说那是粉饰，DRP 外务部抗议的是见报的草稿。

把那一句改写成方向的说法，也不进记录。比如改成"DRP 的方向更偏某一边"，仍然不进。方向是诊断，诊断照施为下（第八篇 XZ53）；记录只记施为，读记录的各方从施为里读出什么方向，是他们各自的读法（第一节）。这一篇对任何一国，也只写它做了什么。

石峡邦常驻代表的话要认真答：删去那一句，是在替不交钱的一方粉饰。这一说的分量在于，记录若怕得罪一国，就把它的不履约写轻，账就失真，以后谁也不必在乎记录。

对一对改前改后。改后少记的，只有那一句定性。欠交的五点二亿、未接收的八千个名额、DRP 理由的全文，一笔不少；还多记了两件：UCSE 的迟交，与二千一百人的待决。副秘书长的答话是："报告记的是没有交的钱与没有接的人，一笔不少。"

记施为不是少记，是把账记在问得到的地方。施为可以被问、被答、被改；定性只能被接受或被拒绝。记了定性，DRP 的回应就只剩下抗议，抗议的是定性，五点二亿反倒退到了后面。记了施为，问就落在钱与名额上：钱什么时候交，名额怎样接，理由指向的那一件怎样另问。到第 20 年交了的第 16 年份额的四成记在同一个地址上；那一年没交的六成，与第 17 年的全部份额，也记在同一个地址上。

同一个问题，在体系的记录里还出现过两次，问的是另一种施为的措辞。

第 17 年春，高原联邦的舰艇与部队进入石峡邦的领海与灯岛，在灯岛设"灯岛航道管理所"，要求经南航道的船只向该所登记，扣留了两艘未登记的货轮。第 4 日的临时会上，成员会议依宪约第 18 条第 1 款通过记录，写明"有一项殖民性的进入"；UCSE、NUT、石峡邦赞成，DRP 弃权。DRP 常驻代表说："记录的用语应当是'武装进入'。'殖民'一词是在给高原联邦定性。"他的话载入记录。

第 17 年秋，UCSE 的两艘舰艇与一个连在沙洲共和国的外沙大岛登岸，设"外沙临时管理处"，给岛上的居民登记、发居住证，收渔业费，换学校课本。沙洲共和国不在体系之内。第 18 年夏，成员会议依同一款作第二次记录，也写"有一项殖民性的进入"；NUT、石峡邦赞成，DRP 弃权。DRP 常驻代表说："共和国在灯岛一事上说过，'殖民'一词是在定性，记录的用语应当是'武装进入'；对外沙也一样。"石峡邦常驻代表说："灯岛上的叫管理所，外沙上的叫管理处。"UCSE 依第 7 条（乙）陈述，载入记录："那里没有人抵抗，也没有人死。"

这两次的措辞对不对，本篇不判。要判措辞，先要判施为：外沙那一次在第四篇第七节判，灯岛那一次在第五篇第一节判；那两处也各写明，那一句措辞在规则下由谁、怎样判的。这里只答一般的一半：措辞由什么看。

措辞跟着施为走，不跟着国名走。同一种施为，用同一种措辞，不论施为的是体系外的一国还是成员，是哪一国。判在施为：施为的一方是谁，不改变这一判。

DRP 的话也要认真答。它说，"殖民"一词本身就是定性。这一说的分量是真的：一个词若带着对一国的判决，写进记录，就与被删的那一句是同一种错。

可是"有一项殖民性的进入"记的是一项施为：以武力进入，在那里设管理，要那里的人与过往的船登记。它说的是那一回发生了什么，不说进入的一方是哪一种国家。分在哪里，可以用一个检验来看：换掉施为的一方，其余一字不改，这句话用到另一国的同一施为上，仍然成立（自担）。

拿被删的一句试。把"DRP"换成另一国，其余不动，这句话说的仍是那一国"是"什么、"不会"做什么。它里面没有一项施为可对；它对另一国成不成立，与那一国做了什么无关，只与说话的人怎样看那一国有关。检验过不了，因为它根本不是关于施为的句子。

再拿第 4 日的记录试，只试它的写法。记录先写高原联邦的舰艇与部队进入了哪里、设了什么、要求谁登记、扣了什么船，然后写"有一项殖民性的进入"。把"高原联邦"换成另一国，其余不动，用到另一国做的同样几件事上，这句话照样是一句关于那几件事的话。它由施为撑着，不由国名撑着。

这一试只说明那一句是关于施为的句子。过得了这一检验的写法不止一种："武装进入"也是关于施为的。哪一个词贴着那几件施为，要先判施为，再由成员会议在规则下判；检验不替它选。

这一检验是规范层的内容，不是照着打勾的判据。它说的是一句记录该是什么样子；它也不替谁判两项施为是不是同一种施为。灯岛与外沙是不是同一种，石峡邦说的"管理所"与"管理处"，与 UCSE 说的"没有人抵抗，也没有人死"，各指向一边；那是第四篇第七节与第五篇第一节要判的施为。

分三层说。只记施为，记录的措辞经得起上面的检验，是规范层。《记录规程》第 4 条、改动登记、宪约第 7 条（乙）的表决与"有权要求把它的陈述载入记录"，是规则层的例子。具体一句措辞写"武装进入"还是"殖民性的进入"，在规则下怎样判，是判断层：由成员会议里位置上的人判，理由与异议载入记录。灯岛那一次，DRP 的异议载入了；外沙那一次，DRP 的异议与 UCSE 的陈述都载入了。本系列不替成员会议判。

这里留下一处。记录记施为，读记录的人仍可能拿施为去推身份：读到五点二亿未交，有人照样会说"果然是那一种国家"。这一层记录管不到。记录能管的，是自己不这样写；读的人怎样读，读错的账在读的人（第一节）。不履约的记录，照实记份额、时点与理由，归到作出决定的位置（第二节）。

XZ18　记施为，不记国名（承国家论前置篇第七节）。体系的报告与记录只写施为、作出它的位置、所依的文书与它落在谁身上，不写一国是哪一种国家；不按偏向预断一国的下一项施为（国家论前置篇第七节：偏向是方向，不是身份）。同一种施为用同一种措辞，不论施为的是成员还是体系外一国、是哪一国。检验是：换掉施为的一方，其余一字不改，这句话用到另一国的同一施为上仍然成立（自担）。只记施为与这一检验是规范层；记录规则的条文、改动登记与异议载入记录是规则层的例子；具体一句措辞在规则下怎样判，交成员会议里位置上的人，理由与异议载入记录。不履约的记录照实记份额、时点与理由，归到作出决定的位置。

第四节　明说在时间里赢

分担报告还没有发出，草稿先上了 DRP 首都的报纸。这对记录是一次考验：被删的那一句此时人人看得见，删它的人会不会被说成在藏什么？

秘书处没有另作解释，也不必。删改载入了改动登记，各成员都查得到：改前怎样写，改后怎样写，依的是《记录规程》第 4 条。草稿与改后的文字都在外面，读的人可以自己对。DRP 外务部抗议的是草稿，石峡邦常驻代表说删改是粉饰；两种说法对着的，是同一组摆在外面的文字。

设若删改没有登记。草稿一出，人们看到的是：报告里本来有一句，发出时没有了，没有人说是谁、依什么删的。到那时，谁也说不清秘书处还改过别的什么。以后每一份报告，读的人都要先问一句：这是改过的吗？

战争论讲过这种打折。它讲的是战争方之间的阴谋：阴谋靠对方不知道自己的真实打算，短期可能奏效，可是「一旦对方识破就失败，而且对方会永久性地对所有姿态保持警惕」；「未来的一切合作、协议、信号都会被打折扣。」（战争论 §12）它又说：「真实的结构不需要维护（真实和它自己是自动一致的），虚假需要持续维护」（同上）。战争论说的是战争方之间。这里推到平时多边的约与信号（自担）：一个成员对体系的说明，出兵时说明的根据，该交的份额，不交的理由，一旦被识破是假的，它以后的说明都被打折；体系自己的记录也一样，一处改动被识破是暗改，以后的报告都被打折。推得过去，是因为打折扣的理由不在战争上，在识破以后对方怎样读你此后的每一句话；平时的约与信号，正是靠被人这样读才作数。

改动登记使秘书处不必维持"这份报告从来没有删改过"的假象：真的不需要维护，假的要一直维护。记录本身是否准确、删改是否合乎它的规则，仍须受问。

再看成员这一边。DRP 不交份额，它的理由写在函件里，明说了：UCSE 的那项冻结，分担公式没有计入北部各区的财政困难。记录附上函件的全文。理由好不好，由读的人与成员会议判；明说本身，让账有了地方可对。此后的事都记在同一个地址上：成员会议决定二千一百人按一半计入抵扣；DRP 到第 20 年交了第 16 年份额的四成。

战争论把明说推到极处，叫作阳谋：「把话摆在明面上，而且对方还不能不跟」；「阳谋的力量不来自信息差，来自结构位势差本身」（战争论 §12）。DRP 那封函件不是阳谋，它只是明说。可是明说与阳谋共有一样东西：不靠对方不知道。明说把理由与立场放到问得到的地方，不再靠对方不知道来维持；所说是否真实、以后是否做到，仍须核实。

声誉与牵制的合格形态由此可以说清：明说底线，背后有真实的能力（第四篇 XZ23）。一个成员不付，就明说不付、为什么不付；说要出兵，背后就要有出兵的能力，与本国已走完的认定。威慑也是这样。战争论说：「涵育深度是任何威慑在长时段里可信的底子。」（战争论 §10）原说战争方的威慑，这里推到成员与体系对外说的话（自担）：一句话在长时段里可不可信，落在说话的一方内部涵育的深度，不落在说得多响。

有人会说：明说在短期吃亏。DRP 明说不付，等于自认不履约；它若含糊其辞、一拖再拖，反倒少落下一笔把柄。这一说的分量在于，短期里确实如此：明说的不履约一字一句摆在外面，暗里拖着的，记录上只是"未交"。

可是账一样记。明说不付与暗里不付，五点二亿都记在 DRP 名下，未交就是未交。两样的分别不在这一笔，在以后：暗里拖着的一方，以后说的话被打折；明说的一方，以后说"交了四成"，读的人手里是一个可核的主张，不必先猜它认下的是几成；究竟交了没有，仍要对到付款与收款的记录。战争论说：「阳谋不在短期赢，阳谋在时间里赢」（战争论 §12）。明说赢在哪里，也要在时间里才看得出。

分寸要说清。明说是合格形态的方向，不另立一条对成员的义务：成员对体系说明什么、说明到哪里，照它已认的约（第二篇 XZ12）。一个成员的哪一句说明被识破为假，由读它的平级者与各国内部的通道判断；体系只记，不判谁说了谎。这一步在判断层。

这一节留下几处。本系列不判 DRP 的理由成不成立（第二节）。在时间里赢是结构的倾向，不是担保：说真话的一方未必在每一件事上都赢，这与国家论不担保和平是同一个分寸（国家论第五篇第七节）。

改动登记让删改经得起被看见。经得起被看见，还不等于对。康德在《永久和平论》附录二给过一个公开性的检验；国家论第五篇第四节已经拿它对过，连同康德自己说的这一原则「只是消极的」，以及经得起公开的准则不能反过来推为正当那一句。本篇不重做这一检验，指向那里。

XZ19　欺骗被打折，明说在时间里赢（承战争论 §12）。战争论原说战争方之间：阴谋一被识破，以后的一切合作、协议、信号都被打折扣；真实的结构不需要维护，虚假需要持续维护；阳谋不在短期赢，在时间里赢。这里推到平时多边的约与信号（自担）：一个成员对体系的说明（出兵时说明的根据、份额、理由）一旦被识破是假的，以后的说明都被打折；体系自己的记录也一样，改动不登，一处被识破，以后的报告都被打折。明说底线、背后有真实的能力，是声誉与牵制的合格形态（XZ23）；一句话在长时段里可信，落在涵育的深度，不落在说得多响（战争论 §10，自担）。明说是方向，不另立一条对成员的义务；哪一句说明被识破为假，由读的平级者与各国内部的通道判断，体系只记。在时间里赢是结构的倾向，不是担保。

第五节　平时的互认

一个成员两年不交份额，还拿另一个成员的事作理由。体系与它之间，除了一笔账，还剩下什么？

战争论讲平时，说不战也是备战，备的不是军力，是让涵育在平时深化。它列了几样，前两样是：「一，维持伐谋伐交通道的开放。」「二，维持主体性的互相承认。」又说：「涵育在平时没有持续深化，战时的底子就薄」（战争论 §10）。原说一个社会与它的对手在平时，这里推到协作体系与它的成员在平时（自担）：往来的通道开着，彼此仍把对方当作一个可以问、也可以答的一方，这是协作的底子。平时的互认薄了，到了要一同应对的时候，底子也薄。

互认是什么，先要说清，不然它会被读成好感，或者读成背书。冲突论讲两个人在不可通约处的互认，说互认所认的是「任何一方都无权替另一方撤销其承担」，接着说：「只认来源位置，不签发行动许可。」「承认不等于他的行动因此可被允许，不等于我须承受它的后果，不等于公共规则不得限制它，也不撤销伤害与权利那一账。」（冲突论 §25）国家论已经把这几句推到国与国（国家论 N26）；这里再推到体系与它的成员（自担）。体系与各成员认 DRP，认的是它的施为出自它的位置、账记在它的名下。这不许可它的不交，不使别的成员须承受不交的后果，不使宪约与分担决定不能约束它，也不撤销那五点二亿的账。

所以互认与问、记、收回配合并存，不互相排斥。体系就籍的法令向 DRP 发问，就分担记下它的不交；成员若照账调整对它的配合，也是一样。这些都不是不认它；反过来，认它，也不是放过这些。

还有一件要说清：体系不预设成员之间已经互信。《制度如何可能》（下称 F6）讲制度的前提时，把对人的默认与对具体操作的推定分开：可被证据击穿的，是关于某一项操作的推定，「据此可以限制权限、拒绝要求、中止合作、启动对第三方的保护」；所以它的但书「判操作，不判人」。同一处还写：「框架的不疑不替成员达成共构。」（F6 §2.1）那里的默认对的是人；国家不是目的，本系列不把那一层默认推到国家。推过来的是另外两样（自担）：证据击穿的，是对一国某一项施为的推定，不是让体系据此给一国下一个总的判定，这与第三节是同一条线；体系的框架不替成员达成互信，它能做的，是保住成员之间仍能问、能答、能拒绝的那些位置。

平时的互认落在事上，是什么样子？回到对 DRP 籍法令的那一次发问。DRP 被问时没有退场。它答了：内政，不溯及既往。它把对 UCSE 的反问写进答复：UCSE 第 9 年冬撤销过一位住在 NUT 的评论者的籍，UCSE 的《成员籍法》第 14 条至今要求无国籍儿童的父母合法居留满五年，"UCSE 自己也没有给这些孩子籍"。记录照全文载入，连那一段一起。它还就回避提了异议，说持 NUT 籍的副秘书长也应回避；异议也载入记录。第 15 年夏，UCSE 改了自己的法。被问的一方反问，问的一方也被问；通道两头都开着。平时的互认在做的，就是这件事。

分担那一次也一样。DRP 外务部抗议"秘书处的人在给共和国定性"。删去定性，是把 DRP 仍当作一个施为出自位置、可以被问也可以答的构（第三节）；DRP 的抗议，也是在同一条通道里说的话。此后它交了第 16 年份额的四成，成员会议把那二千一百人按一半计入抵扣。两边都还在这条通道里。

失灵是什么样子？战争论在同一节里写过一个联盟平时散开的样子：「联盟成员之间的互相承认逐渐弱化，内部的主体性互动减少。」那一句原是就一个虚构世界里的联盟说的；本篇不用那个例子，只取这一句作诊断：失灵不必是一次退出，可以是互认一点点变薄，问得少了，答得少了，成员一个一个疏远。

有人会说：对一个不交钱、还拿另一件事作理由的成员讲互认，是纵容。这一说的分量在于，若互认意味着放它一马，别的成员每多交一笔，就多吃一笔亏。

可是互认不是许可，也不撤账。账照记，五点二亿一笔不少；配合可以照账收回，那是牵制，是制约，不是强力（第四篇）。互认管的只是两件：通道不关，对方仍被当作能答的一方。一个被当作能答的一方的成员，才问得着；把它当作已经定了性的一方，问就没有对象了。

这一节留下两处。互认作方向写，深不深不计量；成员之间疏远到哪一步算失灵，本篇不给刻度，交位置上的判断。本篇也不担保互认不弱化：账看得见、通道开着，是底子，不是保证。

XZ20　平时的互认是协作的底子（承战争论 §10）。平时维持往来通道的开放、维持主体性的互相承认；涵育在平时没有持续深化，战时的底子就薄（战争论 §10 原说一个社会与它的对手在平时，这里推到体系与成员在平时，自担）。互认只认来源位置，不签发行动许可，不撤销伤害与权利的账（冲突论 §25；国家论 N26 已推到国与国，这里推到体系与成员，自担）；所以互认与问、记、收回配合并存。体系不预设成员之间已经互信：证据击穿的是对一国某一项施为的推定，不是对一国的总判定；体系的框架不替成员达成互信，只保住成员之间仍能问、能答、能拒绝的位置（F6 §2.1 原说制度与它的主体，这里推到体系与成员国，自担）。平时失灵的样子是互认弱化、成员逐个疏远（战争论 §10 的诊断）。互认作方向写，不计量；疏远到哪一步算失灵，交位置上的判断。

第六节　账跟着地址走

地址会变。一个成员可以退出，可以被暂停、被开除，可以再申请加入；体系本身也可以解散。地址的身份变了，地址上的账怎样？

本篇的回答是：这几样都不改写记录。退出以前的施为照记。再加入不洗账，退出期间的施为也照记、照问。开除不销账。解散时，记录按事项交相关的成员国保管，不随体系一起消失。

冲突论讲两个人的互认时，写过离开的一面：「若仍有共同资源，第三方责任或程序义务，离开也不结清它们，只能转入安排或仲裁。」（冲突论 §25）原说两个人各自离开以后，这里推到成员离开体系（自担）：成员退出了，它在体系里时作出的施为、所生的义务、已开始的程序，不因它走了而结清。同一节那一句「也不撤销伤害与权利那一账」，在这里照用。政府论说：「决定可以终局：具体事项可以答完、结案。位置不能终局：没有一个位置可以独占关门之权」（政府论 G36）。退出是一国的决定，它了结的是这一国此后在体系里的位置；它不能独自把此前的账一起关上。

宪约把这一点写成了几条，都是规则层的例子。第 25 条第 3 款："退出不影响退出以前的施为所生的义务，也不影响已开始的程序。"第 27 条第 4 款："暂停与开除不免除该成员此前施为所生的义务。"第 30 条：解散以前订后续安排，未结申诉有去处，档案按事项交相关成员保管，分担的账目清结。第 27 条与本系列的方向不全合：它的暂停只停表决权，比本系列的方向窄；它的开除条件不限于越线，比本系列的方向宽（第四篇、第七篇）。

一件事可以看出这些话落在哪里。第 17 年冬，石峡邦依第 25 条通知退出，第 18 年冬生效。通知期里，三界申诉委员会就东坪营地的外出规定那一件申诉，向石峡邦提过两次问，石峡邦都没有作答。这一件与另一件对石峡邦的申诉，委员会照第 25 条照常审结，第 19 年作出意见；石峡邦没有答复。第 20 年秋，石峡邦申请再加入。讨论中，UCSE 常驻代表把退出期间没有作答的两件申诉列为要处理的一项；石峡邦的代表说，石峡邦愿意对今后每一项共同措施逐项说明是否参加，"并答复那两件申诉"。

原则与这件事要分开写。原则是：没有答的问，不随退出生效而结（第七篇 XZ47）。这几件事实怎样判，石峡邦在通知期里不答、生效以后不答，各落在哪里，在第七篇第一、三、四节；再加入时拿这两件设什么条件，是第七篇 XZ48 的事。这里只看一件：到第 20 年秋，那两件申诉还记在石峡邦这个地址上。UCSE 能把它们列出来，是因为记录在。

地址上的位置也会换人。石峡邦这几年换过两次邦首席。第 17 年秋邦议院选举，新多数选出新的邦首席，她在就职时说："体系给了我们一份记录，灯岛还在高原联邦手里。"此后石峡邦通知退出。第 20 年春，邦议院通过不信任案后提前选举，新多数又选出新的邦首席，此后石峡邦申请再加入。通知期里没有作答，发生在第 17 年秋选出的那一位邦首席任内；不答出自哪一个位置的决定，没有记载。说愿意答复的，是第 20 年那一次申请时的常驻代表。

账怎样分？地址的义务照旧：那两件申诉要答，是石峡邦这个地址的事，不因邦首席换了人而结。人的账跟着人走：不答若出自某一个位置上的某一个人的决定，那一笔记在那个位置与人上（第二节）。暂停也是这样：暂停解除以后，恢复的是那个地址在共同程序里的权利；暂停期间位置上的人做过什么，那一笔不因此了结。国家论 N39 把账归到位置与人，说的是一国之内；这里推到体系的成员，并推到地址上的位置换了人的时候（自担）。地址不换，换的是位置上的人；所以地址的账与人的账要分开走。这几件事怎样判，在第七篇第三节。

分担那件也是这样。DRP 到第 20 年交了第 16 年份额的四成；其余照记在 DRP 名下。这几年里 DRP 外务部的位置上换没换人，没有记载；不论换没换，份额记在地址上，函件记在作出它的位置上。

有人会说：再加入是一项新约，新约另起一页，旧账不该带进来。这一说的分量在于，再加入确实是新约：照宪约第 26 条，再加入照第 7 条（甲）作新的接纳，须各方同意，各成员可以提出条件（第一篇 XZ1；第七篇 XZ48）。DRP 常驻代表在石峡邦那一次就说："不应另设条件；走是权利，回来也是。"

可是新约约的是此后。了结一件，不了结一切（第一篇 XZ3）：一项新约了结的是这一件，不改写此前任何一方做过什么。再加入可以不设任何条件，那两件申诉照样记在石峡邦名下：账在不在，与回来要不要条件，是两件事。

这里也分开说。账不随退出、开除、再加入、解散而销，是规范；宪约第 25、27、30 条是规则层的例子；再加入时拿旧账设什么条件，交各方的政治家照约定判断，账落在作判断的位置与人（第七篇 XZ48），这一步在判断层。

这一节留下两处。账照记，不等于账落了地：那四成交了，其余还没有；落没落地，照约或裁决认，不由一方独定（第四篇 XZ22）。解散时安排不到的，照实登为余项，不宣布已无亏欠（第七篇 XZ51）。退出怎样受审查、再加入的底线、开除的条件，这些程序的一面都在第七篇。

XZ21　账跟着地址走（承冲突论 §25、政府论 G36）。退出、开除、再加入、解散都不改写记录：退出以前的施为照记；再加入不洗账，退出期间的施为照记、照问；开除不销账；解散时记录按事项交相关成员国保管。承认不撤销伤害与权利那一账；离开也不结清共同资源、第三方责任与程序义务，只能转入安排或仲裁（冲突论 §25 原说两个人，这里推到成员与体系，自担）。决定可以终局，位置不能终局（政府论 G36）。程序的一面见第七篇。与 XZ17 合读：地址上的位置换了人，地址的义务照旧；人的账跟着人走，记在作出决定的位置与人上；暂停解除后，恢复的是该地址在共同程序里的权利，不了结人的账（自担）。

这一篇停在哪里

这一篇只证明了这些：没有上级去执行时，账仍在地址上。声誉是外部看得见的账，是分布的观察，体系不设中心化的识别权威。记录归到位置与人；体系名义下的账与成员国的账两址并记；记录本身也是施为，改动照登；记录是痕迹，不是判决。记录只记施为，不记国名；同一种施为用同一种措辞，检验是换掉施为的一方、其余一字不改，用到另一国的同一施为上仍然成立。欺骗被打折，明说在时间里赢。平时的互认是协作的底子；互认不是许可，体系也不替成员达成互信。账跟着地址走，不随退出、开除、再加入或解散消失；地址上的位置换了人，地址的账与人的账分开走。

它没有证明的：声誉怎样计量；公开性的检验本身，国家论第五篇第四节已拿康德对过，这里指向那里。灯岛、外沙两次记录的措辞对不对，本篇只答由什么看；两项施为在第五篇第一节、第四篇第七节判，措辞在规则下由谁、怎样判的，也写在那两处。退出、开除、再加入、解散的程序一面，在第七篇。账落没落地、平级者怎样照账收回配合，在第四篇。康德把联盟读作世界共和国的消极的替代物，这一排序本系列怎样读，在第八篇。

留下的余项：读记录的人怎样读，记录管不到，读错的账在读的人。互认弱化到哪一步算失灵，不计量，交位置上的判断。DRP 不交的理由成不成立，本篇不判。分担报告对 UCSE 的迟交只记了时长，少记了位置与文书，照实指出；那位分析员的调动与那一句有没有关系，没有记载。

本篇命题（合列）

XZ16　声誉是外部看得见的账。承认一个国家，认的是它的施为出自它的位置、账记在它的名下（国家论 N26）；体系的记录把这一地址上的账摆到外面（国家论原说一国承认另一国，这里推到体系与成员，自担）。一个构许不许人问它、被问时怎样答，从外部看得见（战争论 §16 原说战争方对追问的允许，这里推到平时的体系与成员，自担）；本篇把这份外部看得见的账称作声誉。没有上级去执行，账仍在地址上，平级者照账自行判断、调整配合（国家论 N36；收回配合见第四篇）。声誉是分布的观察，不设中心化的识别权威（道德律第九篇 §5.3 原说识别一个人的状态，这里推到国与国，自担）；体系的记录是这份账的一处，不替各方读它。声誉作数，靠看见账的各方据它作决定：信不信被记一方的约，给不给它配合（自担）；被记的一方在不在乎自己的声誉，不在本篇讨论之内。声誉不是作数的全部（权力论第一篇 §3）。声誉不独自作数，与约、问、牵制一起作数（XZ23）。声誉不计量；读错的账在读的人。

XZ17　记录归到位置与人（承国家论 N39、制度论 Z7a）。决定与理由公开，记录归到具体的位置与人（国家论 N39 原说一国，这里推到体系与成员，自担）。构是归责的地址，不是施为的主体；地址与实际作出决定的人不互相遮蔽（制度论 Z7a）。以体系名义作出的，还原到作出它的位置与票（权力论第三篇 §V.5，自担）；体系名义下的账在体系，也在投票、执行它的成员国与位置，两址并记，不互相转记（政府论 G25 原说两级政府，这里推到体系与成员国，自担）。记录本身也是施为：它的账落在起草、改动、通过它的位置上，改动照登。记录是痕迹，不是判决（冲突论 §26，自担）：它记施为与理由，不判理由成不成立；不履约的理由指向另一成员时，两笔账各记在各自的地址上，那一件照它的对象另问。地址上的位置换了人时，与 XZ21 合读。

XZ18　记施为，不记国名（承国家论前置篇第七节）。体系的报告与记录只写施为、作出它的位置、所依的文书与它落在谁身上，不写一国是哪一种国家；不按偏向预断一国的下一项施为（国家论前置篇第七节：偏向是方向，不是身份）。同一种施为用同一种措辞，不论施为的是成员还是体系外一国、是哪一国。检验是：换掉施为的一方，其余一字不改，这句话用到另一国的同一施为上仍然成立（自担）。只记施为与这一检验是规范层；记录规则的条文、改动登记与异议载入记录是规则层的例子；具体一句措辞在规则下怎样判，交成员会议里位置上的人，理由与异议载入记录。不履约的记录照实记份额、时点与理由，归到作出决定的位置。

XZ19　欺骗被打折，明说在时间里赢（承战争论 §12）。战争论原说战争方之间：阴谋一被识破，以后的一切合作、协议、信号都被打折扣；真实的结构不需要维护，虚假需要持续维护；阳谋不在短期赢，在时间里赢。这里推到平时多边的约与信号（自担）：一个成员对体系的说明（出兵时说明的根据、份额、理由）一旦被识破是假的，以后的说明都被打折；体系自己的记录也一样，改动不登，一处被识破，以后的报告都被打折。明说底线、背后有真实的能力，是声誉与牵制的合格形态（XZ23）；一句话在长时段里可信，落在涵育的深度，不落在说得多响（战争论 §10，自担）。明说是方向，不另立一条对成员的义务；哪一句说明被识破为假，由读的平级者与各国内部的通道判断，体系只记。在时间里赢是结构的倾向，不是担保。

XZ20　平时的互认是协作的底子（承战争论 §10）。平时维持往来通道的开放、维持主体性的互相承认；涵育在平时没有持续深化，战时的底子就薄（战争论 §10 原说一个社会与它的对手在平时，这里推到体系与成员在平时，自担）。互认只认来源位置，不签发行动许可，不撤销伤害与权利的账（冲突论 §25；国家论 N26 已推到国与国，这里推到体系与成员，自担）；所以互认与问、记、收回配合并存。体系不预设成员之间已经互信：证据击穿的是对一国某一项施为的推定，不是对一国的总判定；体系的框架不替成员达成互信，只保住成员之间仍能问、能答、能拒绝的位置（F6 §2.1 原说制度与它的主体，这里推到体系与成员国，自担）。平时失灵的样子是互认弱化、成员逐个疏远（战争论 §10 的诊断）。互认作方向写，不计量；疏远到哪一步算失灵，交位置上的判断。

XZ21　账跟着地址走（承冲突论 §25、政府论 G36）。退出、开除、再加入、解散都不改写记录：退出以前的施为照记；再加入不洗账，退出期间的施为照记、照问；开除不销账；解散时记录按事项交相关成员国保管。承认不撤销伤害与权利那一账；离开也不结清共同资源、第三方责任与程序义务，只能转入安排或仲裁（冲突论 §25 原说两个人，这里推到成员与体系，自担）。决定可以终局，位置不能终局（政府论 G36）。程序的一面见第七篇。与 XZ17 合读：地址上的位置换了人，地址的义务照旧；人的账跟着人走，记在作出决定的位置与人上；暂停解除后，恢复的是该地址在共同程序里的权利，不了结人的账（自担）。
