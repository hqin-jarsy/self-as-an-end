The Market: Securing
市场的保障

Abstract

This paper writes the first of the two things: the side of the Base Layer. The Prequel left a question: for an outcome with no decider, one can ask what the institution in which it lies has done with respect to it. This paper first looks at two supplies: Springhollow's water has one supplier, and Ridgefoot's housing has many. The Base Layer is a line; it is not on the market's scales: the acts of the constructs that make and enforce rules cross no line against any person in the interior, and are not discounted for price, scale, efficiency or form of ownership; an economic position does not convert accumulation into domination over people's Base Layer. That the line is not discounted for price does not mean that the supply must be free of charge; nor does the level of a price, on its own, complete a normative judgment. After Springhollow's threshold of five thousand households was lowered to five hundred, the line remained the same line; what changed was whether the standing review of the State Public Utilities Commission covered Springhollow Water Company; who takes up guarding the line and being questioned in the stretch below the threshold, and under what arrangement, the construct that sets the threshold must be able to answer (per Institution Theory Z34; SC1). Besides the line, the Base Layer also needs securing; the object of securing is the supply, not that construct. Southwindow Care Homes raised its monthly fee, and a relative took their mother home; asking only about Southwindow's business decisions cannot complete the examination of whether her care thereafter is provided for. Where there are many suppliers, whether they suffice to secure the Base Layer is examined separately, and this examination falls on the constructs that make and enforce rules. The closer to the life-substrate, the stronger the checks and supervision, and the more the question of how large the private share is must be something that can be asked about and answered for; it is not presupposed that the lower the private share, the better, and how large the public share is can be asked in the same way. Ownership lies at the rule layer; this paper only suggests, and writes one tendency: the closer to the life-substrate, the more the tendency is for supply not to be wholly privately owned (SC2). The line does not change with ownership: the Apple City Power Department is owned by the city, and reading it uses the same set of questions as reading a private supplier; state ownership is not automatically in the direction of the Kingdom of Ends, nor does it automatically cross the line (SC3). Last comes housing. The normative layer writes only a direction: the closer to the life-substrate, the less dispensable securing is, and having somewhere to live lies in this direction; whether housing in a given city or place falls on the life-substrate lies at the judgment layer, and the list and degree of securing lie at the rule layer. Ridgefoot is a negative example, and what it is a negative example of is that no securing for housing at all is embedded in the rules at any level; this paper does not judge from it that any landlord or any developer has crossed the line, nor does it infer line-crossing from the number of people seeking a bed. The paper has three propositions (SC1 to SC3), written out at the ends of their sections and listed together at the end.

Introduction

The Prequel left a question. No one set the rent in Ridgefoot; it is the result of many leases taken together; yet this result lands within an institution, and what can be asked is what the constructs that make and enforce rules have embedded with respect to it, what they have not, and what they have done. That paper said that this step is to be tested in the examples of the later papers. This paper carries on from here.

First, two supplies. Both are constructed examples of this series: Springhollow lies in the hills of the north of the Kingdom State, and Ridgefoot in the east of the Highland State; the Kingdom State and the Highland State are both states of the United City-States of Ends (UCSE). The places, companies and terms are not real.

Springhollow has two villages, Upper Hollow with about four hundred households and Lower Hollow with about six hundred, and its piped water comes from one company only, Springhollow Water Company. In the spring of Year 23 the company raised the water price by sixty percent; in winter it cut off water to twenty-three households, two of which had bedridden elderly members. At that time the Kingdom State's Public Utilities Act covered only water suppliers serving five thousand households or more, and the State Public Utilities Commission had no authority over Springhollow's water price. Four years later, in Year 27, the state amended the Act and lowered the threshold to five hundred households or more, and Springhollow Water Company thereby came under the Commission's regulation. That winter the Commission ruled: the water price returns to its level before the Year 23 increase, plus ten percent; households in arrears are not cut off in winter; households whose water was still cut off for arrears at the time of the ruling have their supply restored first, and the arrears are dealt with separately.

Ridgefoot's housing has many suppliers. From Year 25 to Year 28 the average rent for a one-bedroom apartment rose by about seventy percent; this example does not record any supplier cutting off anyone's supply, and no one can be found who set the rent. In the law of the Highland State and the ordinances of Ridgefoot, not a single provision writes housing into any securing: the city has no public housing; how rents rise is set by leases; a landlord who does not renew need only give thirty days' notice; one level up, the law of the UCSE leaves housing to each state to decide. The only shelter in the city is run by a charitable society and has forty beds; the people coming to seek a bed on winter nights averaged about twenty a night in Year 25 and about ninety in Year 28.

One place has one supplier, the other many. This paper asks in turn: where the line is, and to whom it is addressed (§1); what is needed beyond the line, and where it lands (§2); the closer to the life-substrate, how strong the checks must be, and how the question of who holds this supply is to be asked (§3); how a publicly run supplier is read (§4); in which direction housing lies (§5).

§1 The Base Layer Is a Line

"The line" is a word this series takes over from Institution Theory. In the market, the line is addressed to two places.

One is the constructs that make and enforce rules. Institution Theory has laid down that an institution's acts cross no line against any person in its interior; this ought "does not vary with relational thickness and is not discounted under any condition" (Institution Theory Z9). In the market, this sentence has to land on several conditions that might be used to discount it: price, scale, efficiency, ownership.

The other is the economic positions accumulated in the market. Political Economy has laid down the first gate: "accumulation is not converted into domination over people's Base Layer"; what people need to sustain their lives is not converted into control that compresses their life-substrate, "nor may money, housing or the manner of paying wages be used to place people's ability to leave, refuse or revoke a standing bond under another party's control" (Political Economy Paper 4, E21).

That the Base Layer is a line means that it is not on the market's scales. Everything in the market can be weighed: price and cost, scale and return, this supplier and that. The line is not a weight on the scales, and is not set off against anything else.

This sentence must guard against two readings.

One reads the line as a price. That the line is not discounted for price does not mean that the supply must be free of charge. Political Economy says that the sole position of supply at the life-substrate holds the accountability of supply, and "The supply need not be free"; "how those who cannot pay are kept from being cut off, who takes over on withdrawal, and how the price is questioned lie on the manager's side" (Political Economy Paper 2, E11a). That the line is not discounted for price means that a household's being unable to pay does not make it permissible to cut off the share that sustains its life; it does not say who pays for that share.

The other reads a price as a line. "The level of a price does not, by itself alone, complete a normative judgment" (Political Economy Paper 2, E10). Springhollow's water rose by sixty percent and Ridgefoot's rent by about seventy percent; neither figure by itself tells us where a line has been crossed. The line is not a price, and a price is not a line (own undertaking).

Scale and efficiency are the same. "These few households are too few to be worth setting up a body to oversee" is a reason of scale; "supplying these last few households does not pay" is a reason of efficiency. These two kinds of reason can decide who oversees, in what form, and who pays for this share; they do not decide whether this share is to be guarded (own undertaking). Ownership is taken up in §4.

Political Economy has already read Springhollow's threshold. Below the threshold, Springhollow Water Company still holds an accountability at the sole position of supply at the life-substrate; the Public Utilities Act "does not cover it along the route of water prices, and this does not cancel this layer either" (Political Economy Paper 2, §6). A threshold "decides which kind of formal review applies and which body has charge of it; it does not decide from what moment a construct must guard the line and can be questioned"; "If a threshold is set so as to leave the price of the only piped-water supply of two villages outside all questioning, it must be questioned by these two" (Political Economy Paper 3, §7); the two are: what is measured is the power-face, not only scale; below the threshold there is no exemption from guarding the line and being questioned. This paper does not redo these steps. By this sentence, before and after Year 27 Springhollow's water is the same water, and the line has not changed: cutting off the share of water that sustains life is what it is below a threshold of five thousand households, and it is the same below a threshold of five hundred. What changed is whether the standing review of the State Public Utilities Commission covers Springhollow Water Company: after the threshold was lowered, Springhollow Water Company came under the Commission's regulation, and the Commission issued three rulings, on the water price, on winter cut-offs and on restoring supply. Whether these three rulings are right, this paper does not judge.

What this paper additionally asks about is the stretch below the threshold (before Year 27, Springhollow was in it): who takes up guarding the line and being questioned there, and under what arrangement. One thing is recorded: the State Water Resources Agency's water-abstraction permit states that this water is for the public water supply of the two villages, and the manager has used it to embed this position (Political Economy Paper 2, §6); along the route of water prices, what positions open to questioning there were besides the Commission the example does not record, and this paper does not supply them. What can be settled is two accountabilities. The position of supply guards its own share, and does not use people's water at the life-substrate as leverage; the institution that manages it holds the accountability of creating conditions for this supply (Political Economy Paper 2, E11a). The threshold was set by this manager itself. Setting a threshold is an act of the rule-making construct itself, examined directly under that ought of Institution Theory, and not discounted for scale: that ought "governs the construct's own acts" (Institution Theory Z9). This is not to say that five thousand households was wrong: where a threshold is set lies at the rule layer (Political Economy Paper 3, E19). It is to say that, once a threshold is set, the construct that set it must be able to answer, for the stretch below it, where the line falls and what arrangement takes it up: Institution Theory says that an institution "bears the burden of explanation for what it has itself produced, and must answer the very matter it is questioned about" (Institution Theory Z34).

Which things count as the Base Layer, the normative layer does not list. Political Economy says: "The list lies at the rule layer, as examples; whether a particular supply falls on the life-substrate lies at the judgment layer" (Political Economy Paper 2, E10). So when this paper speaks of water, electricity, care and housing, these are all examples.

Judgment layer: whether a particular supply in a given city or place falls on the life-substrate is judged by the constructs that make and enforce rules for that place; once they judge, they must be able to state their grounds and bear the consequences of the judgment; those who receive this supply can question them.

SC1 The Base Layer is a line; it is not on the market's scales. The acts of the constructs that make and enforce rules cross no line against any person in the interior, and are not discounted for price, scale, efficiency or form of ownership (per Institution Theory Z9); an economic position does not convert accumulation into domination over people's Base Layer (per Political Economy E21). That the line is not discounted for price does not mean that the supply must be free of charge (E11a). As to what the Base Layer specifically includes, the list lies at the rule layer; whether a particular supply falls on the life-substrate lies at the judgment layer (per E10).

§2 The Object of Securing Is the Supply, Not That Construct

The line says: do not cross. The Base Layer needs one thing more: securing.

Political Economy Paper 3 has already laid down: where what a construct does falls in the Emergence Layer, checks are the main thing; where it falls in the Base Layer, beyond checks, the institution must also secure this supply (Political Economy Paper 3, E14a). What securing is, Institution Theory says: "Securing is a set of conditions given public form: making the line explicit and concrete, giving it institutional backing, and having real capacity of enforcement." It also says: "Protection on paper is not securing" (Institution Theory Paper 3, Z17). That paper used Springhollow and Southwindow Care Homes to read why checks alone are not enough: checks govern use, and presuppose that the supply is still there; when a bedridden old person presses the bell at night, what is needed is that someone comes, not a road of questioning afterward (Political Economy Paper 3, §2). It also held to three sentences: securing "is not limited to sole suppliers: even where there are several suppliers, whether they suffice to secure the Base Layer is examined separately"; "Meeting a minimum standard neither automatically proves that something is within the line nor of itself judges it to cross the line"; "The object of securing is the supply, not that construct" (Political Economy Paper 3, E14a).

This paper follows these sentences and asks about one more place: if the object of securing is the supply, then once a person walks away from a supplier, where does that person's share land?

Southwindow Care Homes has twelve homes in the Kingdom State, with eighteen hundred beds. In the autumn of Year 21 a buyout fund acquired it; after the acquisition, the monthly fee was raised by twenty-five percent. A relative said that their mother had lived at Southwindow for seven years, and that after the fee went up they took her back to their own home. Political Economy Paper 5 has read this: "This is exit; exit was possible, and it had a cost; it shows that someone left, not whether those who stayed have a road for asking" (Political Economy Paper 5, §2).

That paper asks about those who stayed. This paper asks about the one who left.

Asking Southwindow, one can ask why it raised the price, and what it explained before raising it; this is checks. But asking only about Southwindow's business decisions cannot complete another examination: whether that mother's care thereafter is provided for. She left Southwindow, and Southwindow's share of supply to her stopped with it. The price rise by itself does not complete a normative judgment (Political Economy Paper 2, E10); Southwindow may raise its price, and may have its reasons. What is to be asked is something else: when a person walks away from a place of care, does her share of the supply of care still have anywhere to land?

Taken home, the care lands in the home. Whether the family can provide that care, and whether this share has fallen on one relative alone, the example does not record, and this paper does not supply; being taken home can be a family's choice, and can also be the absence of any other choice; this paper does not read the relative's mind. But this question has an address. The overall examination does not lie with Southwindow: the object of securing is the supply, not that construct, and Southwindow does not guarantee all of that mother's future care. It lies on the side of the institution that manages care. This is not to say that Southwindow need not explain the effects of its own price rises, cessation of supply or handovers; that share is Southwindow's own, and is examined separately under its accountability.

"One can switch to another home": how is this to be read here? Whether the Kingdom State has other care homes, how many, whether they can take her, and what they charge, the example does not record; Political Economy Paper 3 also states explicitly that Southwindow is not used to prove that competition has already failed. Nor is it used so here. Yet whether there are others, whether one can switch, and whether one can afford to switch, are exactly what the sentence "examined separately" is to examine. That sentence says that the matter is examined separately; this paper asks one step further: by whom is it examined?

Each supplier supplying normally does not by itself prove that this kind of supply already reaches the people who need it. Since the institution bears the accountability of securing this supply (Political Economy Paper 3, E14a), it must carry out the examination of "whether it suffices"; in the market, those who carry it out are the constructs that make and enforce rules for that market. So where there are many suppliers, the examination of "whether they suffice to secure the Base Layer" falls on the constructs that make and enforce rules (extension: E14a originally says that the institution must secure this supply, and that where there are several suppliers the matter is still examined separately; here it is extended to the address of this examination; own undertaking). It is not derived from the words "there is competition": Political Economy has said that "competition can also drive all of them down to the minimum" (Political Economy Paper 3, §2); nor is it answered by the operations of any one home.

At Southwindow, what the example records on the side of the institution is a floor set by the State Health Agency, at least two night-shift care workers per thirty beds; inspections by the State Health Agency; and warnings to two homes. This is an explicit line and a capacity of enforcement, and it governs care within the homes. For the share outside the homes, such as access to care, what other arrangements the state has the example does not record. This paper does not judge whether there are any; it only points out that the overall examination lies on the side of the state, is not completed by Southwindow's business decisions, and does not fall on that relative either.

Nor is securing an endorsement. The Theory of Government says: "A guarantee is not an endorsement." The original says that where a government guarantees rights, its own power is not thereby legitimate: "the guarantee is a necessary condition, not a sufficient one"; Political Economy Paper 3, §2 extends this to not endorsing the construct that provides the supply (Theory of Government Paper 3, G22; Political Economy Paper 3, §2). By this extension, the institution's securing the supply of care does not endorse Southwindow's operations or that acquisition; securing Springhollow's water does not guarantee that Springhollow Water Company as a company will exist forever, and continuation can be arranged otherwise (Political Economy Paper 3, §2). That the supply must continue and whether that particular company is to be rescued are two different matters; Paper 5 returns here when it writes about public funding.

Judgment layer: whether care in a given place falls on the life-substrate, and how "whether it suffices" is examined there, is judged by the institution that manages care; the position that judges can state its grounds and bears the consequences; those cared for and their relatives can question it.

§3 The Closer to the Life-Substrate, the Stronger the Checks

The position Springhollow Water Company occupies is what Political Economy Paper 2 calls the sole position of supply at the life-substrate. That paper says that such a position "is a mixture of public utility and private operation": it carries out an executive accountability for a larger construct, and is not only operating what is its own; this supply is part of the accountability of the institution or government that manages it, and "this layer is there whether or not there is a permit" (Political Economy Paper 2, E11a).

"A mixture of public utility and private operation" speaks of the nature of the accountability, not of ownership. It says that this position does two things at once: it operates what is its own, and it carries a share of accountability for carrying out a public function; it does not say who should hold this position. Springhollow Water Company is a private company; in that position, it holds a mixed accountability.

What, then, of ownership? This paper's answer has two layers.

The normative layer gives only a direction. How wide the scope of "being responsible for the Base Layer" is, which supplies and which companies count within it, the normative layer does not draw. It gives only a direction: the closer something comes to touching the life-substrate, the stronger the checks and supervision, and the more the question of how large the private share is must be something that can be asked about and answered for.

This direction has the same form as a proposition of Political Economy. That proposition says: "the larger an economic position's power-face, the stronger the checks on it" (Political Economy Paper 3, E14). The original speaks of the power-face; here it is extended to how close a supply is to the life-substrate: the closer a supply is to the life-substrate, the stronger the checks and supervision (own undertaking). Neither gives a scale of degrees: "The gradations and the forms lie at the rule layer" (ibid.).

"Something that can be asked about and answered for" needs to be made clear. That it can be asked about means that those who receive this supply, and the positions that ask on their behalf, have a road by which to raise "who holds this supply, and why it is held by them," and that, once raised, there is somewhere to receive it. That it can be answered for means that the rule-making construct that made this arrangement, and the party that holds the supply, can state their grounds: why it is held this way here; and when the holding party withdraws, sells or changes direction, who takes over the line (own undertaking).

Why does this question matter more the closer one comes to the life-substrate? Because there, whether the supply is cut off is whether the line is guarded. Accountability binds the position, not the person: the party that holds a position of supply can exit; only how it withdraws is examined separately (Political Economy Paper 2, E11a). Whoever holds it holds the decisions to exit, to sell and to change direction. The closer to the life-substrate, the more directly these decisions press on people's lives; whoever holds them must all the more be one who can be asked and can answer (own undertaking).

There is one matter at Springhollow that can be looked at by this sentence. In Year 26, Farstream Water, a water group of the NUT, bought the shares in Springhollow Water Company held by the several Apple City investors, seventy percent in all; the Stone Bridge Franchise Company's thirty percent remained as before. In Year 27, Farstream paid to replace the equipment of the water treatment station, and both of the State Health Agency's spot checks that year were passed. Seventy percent of the two villages' only piped water changed owners. Which position this sale passed through, and whether anyone asked or anyone answered, the example does not record, and this paper does not supply; nor does it judge from this that the sale crossed the line, and it does not read the buyer's mind. What this paper points out is only that, by this direction, "whose hands the two villages' only water has passed into" is a question that must be one that can be asked about and answered for; where it is asked and answered at Springhollow is a matter for the rule layer and the judgment layer. Farstream is a company of another state, and this case later shows another face, which Paper 7 writes.

This direction has two places to guard.

One is not presupposing that the lower the private share, the better. The question asks how large the private share is; it does not ask that it be as small as possible; how large the public share is can be asked in the same way. Political Economy has laid down: "recognized by power-face, not by public or private" (Political Economy Paper 3, E15). A supplier wholly held by the public must also be able to answer why it is held this way, and who takes over the line when it withdraws or changes; §4 reads the Apple City Power Department.

The other is not reading the direction as a list. "The closer" is a direction, not a list; how close a supply is to the life-substrate lies at the judgment layer.

Ownership lies at the rule layer; this paper only suggests. The rule layer can have tendencies. This paper writes one: the closer to the life-substrate, the more the tendency is for supply not to be wholly privately owned (own undertaking). This is a tendency, not a line: a supplier wholly privately owned can guard the line all the same, and Springhollow's piped water was brought by a private company; a publicly run one can cross the line all the same. The reason for the tendency lies in continuation: the closer to the life-substrate, the less who takes over the line, when the holding party withdraws, sells or changes direction, can rest on the holding party alone.

The forms available at the rule layer are many. The following are only examples, not ranked, and not rules this paper lays down for any institution:

  • publicly run suppliers;
  • mixed public and private ownership;
  • a public option alongside private suppliers;
  • regulated private utilities with a universal service obligation, for instance connecting every household in an area;
  • someone to take over when supply is interrupted: preparations for takeover and continuation. Political Economy says that how this position withdraws must go through a procedure that does not seal off others' road of continuation, with the manager arranging the continuation (Political Economy Paper 2, E11a); preparations for takeover and continuation are one form of this procedure at the rule layer;
  • arrangements of the golden-share kind: the public side holds a vote only on the decisions to sell, to withdraw, and to change the use of the supply.

Each has its costs and its points of failure. In a publicly run one, the owner and the one who reviews it may be in the same place; in a regulated private one, the regulator may be captured; a golden share may be left in name only. Which to choose and how to combine them is set by each institution at the rule layer according to its own conditions; this paper does not say which is best.

The Kingdom State has one change at the rule layer that falls right in this direction: in Year 27 the state lowered the threshold of the Public Utilities Act from five thousand households to five hundred, and Springhollow Water Company thereby came under the Commission's regulation. A supply close to the life-substrate entered a standing position of supervision. As §1 said, this change does not change the line; what it changes is whether the Commission's standing review covers Springhollow Water Company. It also has another face: small water suppliers between five hundred and five thousand households all gained one more place where they must answer, which is a cost to them. How this cost is examined, Paper 2 says. In Year 28 a member of the same State Legislature proposed raising the threshold back to five thousand households, and by the end of the year it had not been put to a vote; Papers 6 and 7 return to that matter.

Apple City's electricity takes another form: about forty percent of the city's electricity is generated by the city-owned Power Department's own power plant, and the rest is bought from two private generating companies in the state. A publicly run supplier alongside private generators is one form; this does not prove that it is the best; §4 reads it.

Judgment layer: how close a supply is to the life-substrate, and how its holding is arranged, are set by the rule-making construct that manages that place; once it sets them, it must be able to answer the two questions above; those who receive this supply can question it.

SC2 The closer to the life-substrate, the stronger the checks and supervision, and the more the share of private ownership must be something that can be asked about and answered for. The accountability of the sole position of supply at the life-substrate is a mixture of public utility and private operation (per E11a); for a construct that does the work of the Base Layer, the institution secures its supply, and this is not limited to sole suppliers (per E14a). The normative layer does not draw the scope of "being responsible for the Base Layer"; it gives only a direction: the closer something comes to touching the life-substrate, the stronger the checks and supervision, and the more the question of how large the private share is must be something that can be asked about and answered for; it does not presuppose that the lower the share, the better, and how large the public share is can be asked in the same way (the same form as E14; extension; own undertaking; the public and private sides per E15). Ownership lies at the rule layer; this series only suggests.

§4 The Line Does Not Change with Ownership

The previous section said that how large the public share is can be asked in the same way. This section reads a publicly run supplier.

The Apple City Power Department is a city-owned body that supplies the whole city with electricity; its director is nominated by the mayor and appointed by the City Council. Article 3 of the Kingdom State's Electricity Supply Regulations provides that city-owned electricity suppliers are supervised by the city council they belong to, and that the State Public Utilities Commission does not review their electricity prices. The Power Department's prices are deliberated by the City Council's utilities panel and voted on by the City Council.

The Power Department connects every household in the city, including the old buildings of the old quarter; households with incomes below a set level get a thirty percent discount on electricity; from December to February each year, households in arrears are not cut off. Each year it transfers twelve percent of its electricity sales revenue to the city treasury, written into the city budget from Year 26 on. From Year 26 to Year 28 the electricity price was raised three times, by about twenty percent in all. In Year 28 a report of the Kingdom State Audit Office said that the portion transferred to the city treasury "is not shown separately in the composition of the electricity price"; the Power Department answered: "The electricity price is voted on publicly by the City Council, and the money transferred to the city treasury is also spent on the citizens."

It is read with the same set of questions as a private supplier. Political Economy has laid down: "An accumulated position with a comparable power-face is not exempted from checks for being private; nor does the public side gain a separate exemption on that account" (Political Economy Paper 3, E15). That paper read this proposition with the pension fund, the Stone Bridge Franchise Company and two positions of settlement; this paper asks about one more place: for a publicly run position of supply very close to the life-substrate, how does the line fall on it? In four steps.

The line is there all the same. The Power Department is a construct; its acts fall under that ought of Institution Theory, and are not discounted for ownership. Whether it cuts off electricity in winter is read the same way as whether Springhollow cuts off water: its being publicly run does not make a cut-off that presses on the life-substrate permissible, nor does it make it graver. The Power Department's not cutting off electricity in winter, its thirty percent discount for low-income households and its connecting every household are forms of support recorded in the example; they are recorded as they are, and do not thereby certify that it is in the direction of the Kingdom of Ends.

The accumulation side passes the two gates. The Power Department's power plant and its electricity sales revenue are accumulated. The first gate asks whether it has used this accumulation to press on people's Base Layer or to place people's ability to leave under its control; the example records nothing of the kind. The second gate asks whether it has used this accumulation to obtain a standing in which the party questioned "can on its own close off questioning of itself, correction, or necessary delivery" (Political Economy Paper 4, E22). Users who dispute their bills go first to the Power Department's customer service office, then to the City Council's utilities panel; the Audit Office said that in the handling of user complaints "no independent review was found." Whether these roads of questioning are independent, Paper 6 reads closely; it is not unfolded here.

The granted side is subject to checks according to its source. The Power Department's position of supplying the whole city is given by the city: the director is nominated by the mayor and appointed by the City Council, and the electricity price is reviewed by the City Council. This is a public grant, read under the Theory of Government: "A public grant must retain the function of revocability" (Theory of Government Paper 6, G37); who granted it, what was granted, and who changes it under what procedure, must be statable. Transferring twelve percent to the city treasury is a decision the owner wrote into its own budget. With a private supplier, the owner also takes a share out of the revenue, called profit or dividends; with a public one, it is called a transfer to the treasury. The names differ; what is asked is the same few things: whether those who pay can see the share taken out; once they see it, whether they can ask about it; whether it presses on anyone's Base Layer. The Audit Office says that share is not shown separately; the Power Department says the money is also spent on the citizens. Both sentences are recorded as they are. What relation the three price rises bear to this twelve percent the example does not record, and this paper does not attribute the price rises to it.

A position that has both sources is examined under each separately. The Power Department holds a public grant, and also holds a power plant and revenue that were accumulated; it buys electricity from two private generating companies, which are positions of accumulation. Each is examined under its own source, and one source does not wash the accounts of the other (Political Economy Paper 3, E15).

So "the line does not change with ownership" says two things. State ownership is not automatically in the direction of the Kingdom of Ends: no winter cut-offs, the thirty percent discount and connecting every household do not offset the three price rises and the portion not shown separately; nor do the three price rises offset those; each is examined on its own. Nor does state ownership automatically cross the line: transferring twelve percent to the city treasury, and the City Council reviewing its own Power Department, do not in themselves prove that anyone has crossed the line. What reads them is structure: on whom the cost falls, whether the roads of questioning are there, who decides, who is responsible.

Companies held by the state are not only in public utilities. A company in which the state holds shares and which competes, borrows, and merges and acquires in the market alongside others is read by the same set of readings: judge structures, not names. If it obtains through the state's backing conditions others do not have, that share is examined according to the source of the grant; if it becomes a place others cannot get around, under Paper 3; its competition with others, under Paper 4; the public funding it obtains, under Paper 5.

Judgment layer: the Apple City Power Department's electricity price and the share transferred to the city treasury are set by the City Council, which bears that share of responsibility for making the decision; those who bear the effect are, first of all, the households that pay for electricity; they can ask. Whether the position that reviews it is independent of the Power Department and of its owner, Paper 6 writes.

SC3 The line does not change with ownership. Positions are recognized by power-face, not by public or private: for a publicly run position of supply, its accumulation side passes the two gates, and its granted side is subject to checks according to its source and retains the function of revocability; a position that has both sources is examined under each separately (per Political Economy E15, E21, E22). State ownership is not automatically in the direction of the Kingdom of Ends, nor does it automatically cross the line.

§5 Housing

This section returns to Ridgefoot.

The normative layer: a direction sentence. The closer to the life-substrate, the less dispensable securing is; having somewhere to live lies in this direction (own undertaking).

There is one corroboration. When Political Economy Paper 4 wrote the first gate, it said: "Food, water, electricity, housing: whatever people need to sustain their lives can, once it is hoarded in one place, be converted into control of this kind" (Political Economy Paper 4, §1). There it is giving examples, and what it says is that these things, hoarded in one place, can be converted into control; this paper takes only the one item it gives, "housing," as corroboration for the direction sentence. The list remains at the rule layer.

The direction sentence must guard against three readings. What it speaks of is having somewhere to live, not owning a home of one's own, nor living in a particular place or a particular kind of house. It speaks of a direction, not a number: whether housing in a given city or place falls on the life-substrate lies at the judgment layer; the list and degree of securing lie at the rule layer (Political Economy Paper 2, E10). Nor does it say that housing cannot be left to the market: housing supplied by many, and rent set by leases, do not in themselves cross the line; above the line, the market is left open as usual.

So an institution in which a market oriented toward the Kingdom of Ends lies will give housing some degree of securing; to what degree, and in what form, lies at the rule layer (own undertaking). The forms can be many: public housing, rent or housing subsidies, procedures for renewal and relocation, a bed on a winter night. These are only examples, not ranked.

A negative example: Ridgefoot. Ridgefoot lies in the east of the Highland State, with a population of about three hundred thousand. Crossways Group's warehouse in the Highland State is on the outskirts of Ridgefoot; it was expanded in Year 25, and its employees went from about three thousand to about seven thousand. From Year 25 to Year 28 the average rent for a one-bedroom apartment rose by about seventy percent; in Year 28 a warehouse sorter's monthly rent was about half a month of his wages. A longtime landlord did not raise the rent for his existing tenants in three years; a developer built about two thousand apartments in the north of the city, opening at prices twenty percent above older housing. In Year 27 a member of the city council proposed that landlords raising the rent by more than thirty percent in a year must file their reasons with the city; it was voted on in Year 28 and did not pass.

In the law of the Highland State and the ordinances of Ridgefoot, not a single provision writes housing into any securing: the city has no public housing; how rents rise is set by leases; a landlord who does not renew need only give thirty days' notice. The law at the level of the UCSE leaves housing to each state to decide, and has no direct provision.

Ridgefoot has handed housing over to leases entirely. What has been handed over?

Read it by that sentence of Institution Theory: securing is making the line explicit and concrete, giving it institutional backing, and having real capacity of enforcement (Institution Theory Paper 3, Z17). Read by this sentence, what Ridgefoot has handed over is these three (own undertaking). There is no explicit line: at what point a person counts as having nowhere to live, the recorded rules do not say. There is no institutional backing: in the recorded rules at every level, there is no housing supply backed by the institution, such as public housing. With neither the line nor backing embedded, enforcement has nothing to act on.

What the negative example is a negative example of is this one thing: no securing at all is embedded at the rule layer (own undertaking). It is not against the rent's rising: the rise and fall of rents is many leases taken together. It is not against securing being given sparingly: how much is given lies at the rule layer, and each institution has its latitude. It is against not giving a single one. Giving not a single one puts something that lies in the direction "the closer to the life-substrate, the less dispensable securing is" entirely above the line, treating it only as a transaction. The negative lies in direction, not in degree.

§2 said that securing is not limited to sole suppliers: where there are several suppliers, whether they suffice to secure the Base Layer is still examined separately, and this examination falls on the constructs that make and enforce rules. Ridgefoot's housing has many suppliers. The recorded rules do not embed securing for housing; which particular position has taken up the examination of "whether it suffices," and how it is activated, the example does not record.

The Theory of Government has said: "where a government does not guarantee it, its power is likewise illegitimate and must be questioned" (Theory of Government Paper 3, G18). The original speaks of the backing of basic rights. The Theory of Government itself keeps two things apart: "'Open to questioning' holds for every government and at every time; 'must be questioned' means that a verified lapse owes an explanation and a correction" (Theory of Government Paper 3, G17). Extending these two sentences to housing: if housing in a place is examined at the judgment layer and found to fall on the life-substrate, and no securing at all is embedded at the rule layer, then by G18 it must be questioned; before that examination, by G17, it is open to questioning (own undertaking). Whether housing in Ridgefoot already falls on the life-substrate, and which lapse has been verified, must be examined at the judgment layer; this paper does not examine it in that layer's place. What this paper reads is structure: that no securing at all is embedded at the rule layer is, in direction, the negative; this structure can be questioned of the rule-makers who produced it.

Whom to ask? The law of the Highland State and the ordinances of Ridgefoot each stand at their own level. The UCSE leaves housing to each state, so this matter falls to each state. The city council's rejection of the filing proposal in Year 28 is a decision Ridgefoot's rule-makers made on housing, and can be questioned; what that proposal required was that landlords explain their reasons for raising rents, which is a duty of explanation, not yet securing. That it did not pass is recorded only as a fact, and the city council is not judged from it to have crossed the line.

Several things kept apart. The Prequel said that being able to ask the rule-making construct does not mean that every rise in the rent is booked to its name; that no securing has been embedded does not mean, either, that anyone has already crossed the line. Here these are kept apart.

Landlords and developers are not judged. Landlords and developers are suppliers in the market; for them, the line is the first gate: housing is not used to place people's ability to leave, refuse or revoke a standing bond under another party's control (Political Economy Paper 4, E21). Political Economy has read one such matter: in Bluekiln, dormitories were tied to the job, and one had to move out within thirty days of leaving (Political Economy Paper 4, §1). The Ridgefoot example records nothing of the kind. That a longtime landlord has not raised the rent in three years and a developer has built about two thousand apartments are recorded as they are: someone is supplying; opening prices twenty percent above older housing do not on their own constitute an effect of closing a road.

Crossways is not judged. The warehouse expansion and the increase of employees to about seven thousand stand side by side with the rise in rent as facts, not as an established cause; rent, warehouse expansion and the number of people seeking a bed placed together do not prove that any accumulation has eroded securing; that securing is not embedded does not prove, either, that it was squeezed out by any party. From Year 26 on, Crossways has given warehouse workers a monthly housing allowance worth about ten percent of the rent, which stops in the month they leave; this is examined separately by the three points by which Political Economy reads conditional payments (Political Economy Paper 2, E12a), and Paper 5 returns to it.

Line-crossing is not inferred from numbers. The only shelter in the city is run by a charitable society and has forty beds; the society's records say that the people coming to seek a bed on winter nights averaged about twenty a night in Year 25 and about ninety in Year 28, and that people were often left outside the door, unable to get in. This figure is read on the records' own terms: it counts people who came to seek a bed, not people who stayed, nor everyone in the city who spent the night outdoors. What it can say is: on those nights more people came than there were beds, and some who came had no bed. What it cannot say is how many people in the city have nowhere to live, why, and which construct has crossed the line. It is an entrance for asking: where those who could not get in ended up that night, and whether Ridgefoot has a position that must answer. The recorded institutions at every level record no such position; the forty beds are a charitable society's supply, and whether the institution gives it any backing the example does not record either.

Under the same UCSE, Apple City in the Kingdom State has public housing (as recorded in Theory of Government Paper 3). This is recorded here only as a fact, and the two places are not compared; a comparison could not show where Ridgefoot crosses the line either; it only shows that under one and the same level above, the rule layers of the states can differ.

Judgment layer: whether housing in Ridgefoot falls on the life-substrate, and to what degree securing is given, are judged and set by the rule-makers of the Highland State and of Ridgefoot; once they judge, they must be able to state their grounds, and once they set, they bear the consequences; the people who live there can question them. This paper only writes out the questions to be answered and the positions that answer them, without answering for them.

Where This Paper Stops

This paper has laid down three propositions: the Base Layer is a line, not on the market's scales and not discounted for price, scale, efficiency or form of ownership, and this does not mean that the supply must be free of charge (SC1); the closer to the life-substrate, the stronger the checks and supervision, and the more the question of how large the private share is must be something that can be asked about and answered for, with ownership at the rule layer (SC2); the line does not change with ownership, and state ownership is not automatically in the direction, nor does it automatically cross the line (SC3). The section on housing writes only a direction sentence, and lays down no proposition.

What it has not proved: which form of ownership is best; the list of the Base Layer; which supply falls on the life-substrate; how particular public-utility regulation is to be designed; line-crossing inferred from the numbers of people sleeping outdoors or seeking a bed; which lapse in Ridgefoot has been verified.

Handed to later papers: how a securing measure is examined when it restricts others' Emergence Layer, such as the cost the lowered threshold adds for small water suppliers, in Paper 2; that the supply must continue and whether that particular company is to be rescued, in Paper 5; whether the position that reviews a publicly run supply is independent, and the legislator's proposal to raise the threshold back, in Paper 6; the side of the Springhollow case that crosses borders, in Paper 7.

Remainders left: what arrangement takes up guarding the line and being questioned in the stretch below the threshold; how the constructs that make and enforce rules examine "whether it suffices" where there are several suppliers, and by what method; the degree and form of securing for housing. These lie at the rule layer; this paper has given only a direction and an address.

引言

前置篇留下一个问题。岭下的房租没有人定，它是许多份租约合起来的结果；可这个结果落在一套制度里，可以问的，是立规、执规的构就它载入了什么、没有载入什么，作了什么。那一篇说，这一步要在后面各篇的事例里受检验。这一篇从这里接着走。

先看两处供给。两处都是这个系列的设例：泉坳在王国州北部的丘陵里，岭下在高岭邦东部；王国州与高岭邦同是目的联合城邦（UCSE）的邦。地方、公司与条款都不是真实的。

泉坳有两个村，上坳约四百户，下坳约六百户，自来水只有泉坳水业公司一家。第 23 年春，公司把水价上调六成；冬天，它对二十三户停了水，其中两户家里有卧床的老人。那时王国州的《公用事业法》只管服务五千户以上的供水者，州公用事业委员会对泉坳的水价没有权限。四年以后，第 27 年，州修了这部法，把门槛降到五百户以上，泉坳水业由此归委员会监管。委员会当年冬天裁定：水价回到第 23 年调价前的水平再加一成；冬季欠费的不停水；裁定时仍因欠费停着水的，先恢复供水，欠费另议。

岭下的房子有许多家供。第 25 年至第 28 年，一居室的平均房租涨了约七成；本例没有记哪一家停了谁的供给，也找不到一个定房租的人。高岭邦的法与岭下市的条例里，没有一条把住房写进任何保障：城里没有公租房，房租怎样涨由租约定，房东不续约提前三十日通知即可；再上一级，UCSE 的法把住房留给各邦自定。城里唯一一处收容所是一家慈善会办的，四十张床；冬天夜里前来求宿的人，第 25 年平均每晚约二十人，第 28 年约九十人。

一处只有一家供，一处有许多家供。这一篇依次问：线在哪里，说给谁（第一节）；线以外还要什么，它落在哪里（第二节）；离生命基底越近，制衡要多强，谁持有这一份供给，这一问怎样问（第三节）；公营的供给者怎样读（第四节）；住房在哪一个方向上（第五节）。

第一节　基础层是线

"线"是这个系列从制度论接过来的词。在市场里，线说给两处。

一处是立规、执规的构。制度论定过：制度的施为，对每一个内部的人都不越线；这一条「不随关系厚度调节，也不随任何条件打折」（制度论 Z9）。到了市场里，这一句要落到几样可能被拿来打折的条件上：价格，规模，效率，所有制。

另一处是市场里积累起来的经济位置。政治经济学定过第一道闸：「积累不兑换为对人的基础层的支配」，不把人维持生活所必需的东西换成压缩其生命基底的控制，「也不拿钱、住处或工资的发放方式去锁住人的离开、拒绝与撤回」（政治经济学第四篇 E21）。

基础层是线，说的是它不在市场的天平上。市场里的东西都可以称：价格与成本，规模与回报，这一家与那一家。线不是天平上的一个砝码，不拿去与别的东西相抵。

这一句要防两种读法。

一种是把线读成一个价。线不随价格打折，不是说供给须无偿。政治经济学说，生命基底处唯一的供给位置持供给的职责，「供给不必无偿」；「付不起的人怎样不断供、撤出时谁接续、价格怎样受问，在管理者那一侧」（政治经济学第二篇 E11a）。线不随价格打折，说的是一户交不起，不使断掉维持他生命的那一份变得可以；它不说那一份由谁来付。

另一种是把价读成一条线。「一个价格的高低，单凭它不完成规范的判断」（政治经济学第二篇 E10）。泉坳的水涨了六成，岭下的房租涨了约七成，两个数字本身都不告诉我们哪里越了线。线不是一个价，价也不是一条线（自担）。

规模与效率也是这样。"这几户太少，不值得设一个机关去管"，是一个规模上的理由；"供最后这几户不划算"，是一个效率上的理由。这两种理由可以决定由谁来管、用什么形式管、这一份由谁来付，不决定这一份要不要守（自担）。所有制，第四节说。

泉坳那一道门槛，政治经济学已经读过。泉坳水业在门槛以下，在生命基底处唯一的供给位置上仍持职责；《公用事业法》在水价这一路「没有覆盖它，也不取消这一层」（政治经济学第二篇第六节）。门槛「决定的是哪一种正式的审查、哪一个机关管它，不决定它从哪一刻起才须守线、才可被问」；「一道门槛若定得把两个村唯一的水的价钱留在一切受问之外，它就要按这两样被问」（政治经济学第三篇第七节）；两样是：量的是权力面，不只是规模；门槛以下不豁免守线与受问。这一篇不重做这几步。照这一句，第 27 年以前与以后，泉坳的水还是那一份水，线没有变：断掉维持生命的那一份水，在五千户的门槛以下是那样，在五百户的门槛以下也是那样。变的是州公用事业委员会这一常设审查覆不覆盖泉坳水业：门槛降了以后，泉坳水业归委员会监管，它就水价、冬季停水与复水作了三项裁定。这三项裁定对不对，本篇不判。

这一篇多问的是门槛以下的那一段（第 27 年以前，泉坳就在里面）：它由谁、以什么安排承接守线与受问。已记的有一样：州水利厅的取水许可写明这份水用于两村的公共供水，管理者已用它把这一位置载入（政治经济学第二篇第六节）；水价那一路，委员会以外还有哪些受问的位置，事例里没有记，本篇不补。可以确定的是两处职责。供给的位置守它自己的那一份，不在生命基底上拿人家的水作杠杆；管理它的制度，持为这一供给创造条件的职责（政治经济学第二篇 E11a）。门槛是这个管理者自己定的。定一道门槛，是立规的构自己的施为，照制度论那一条直接核，不随规模打折：那一条「管构自己的施为」（制度论 Z9）。这不是说五千户定错了：门槛定在哪里，在规则层（政治经济学第三篇 E19）。这是说，一道门槛定下来，门槛以下的那一段，线落在谁那里、由什么安排承接，定门槛的构要答得出：制度论说，制度「对它自己造出的东西负说明之责，并须答到被问的那件事上」（制度论 Z34）。

哪些东西算基础层，规范层不列清单。政治经济学说：「清单在规则层，作例子；一项具体供给是否落在生命基底上，在判断层」（政治经济学第二篇 E10）。所以这一篇说水、电、照护、住处，都是例子。

判断层：一城一地的一项供给落不落在生命基底上，判的是管理那一处的立规、执规的构；判了，它要说得出根据，担判的后果；受这项供给的人，可以问它。

SC1　基础层是线，不在市场的天平上。立规、执规的构的施为，对每一个内部的人不越线，不随价格、规模、效率或所有制打折（承制度论 Z9）；经济位置不以积累兑换对人的基础层的支配（承政治经济学 E21）。线不随价格打折，不是说供给须无偿（E11a）。基础层具体包括什么，清单在规则层；一项供给是否落在生命基底上，在判断层（承 E10）。

第二节　保障的对象是供给，不是那个构

线说的是不越。基础层还要多一样：保障。

政治经济学第三篇已经立下：构所做的事落在涌现层的，以制衡为主；落在基础层的，制衡之外，制度还要保障这一供给（政治经济学第三篇 E14a）。保障是什么，制度论说：「保障是一组公共化的条件：把线显明、具体化，给它制度的背靠，并有真实的执行能力。」又说：「纸面上的保护不算保障」（制度论第三篇 Z17）。那一篇用泉坳与南窗养老院读过为什么光有制衡不够：制衡管的是用法，它预设供给还在；一个卧床的老人夜里按了铃，要的是有人来，不是事后有一条问的路（政治经济学第三篇第二节）。它还守了三句：保障「不限唯一的供给者：即使有多家供给，是否足以保障基础层，仍须另核」；「达到最低标准，不自动证明在线内，也不自身判为越线」；「保障的对象是供给，不是那个构」（政治经济学第三篇 E14a）。

这一篇照这几句走，多问一处：保障的对象是供给，那么一个人从一家供给者那里走开以后，他的那一份落在哪里。

南窗养老院在王国州有十二家院、一千八百张床。第 21 年秋，一家并购基金收购了它；收购以后，月费上调两成五。一位家属说，母亲在南窗住了七年，月费涨了以后，接回了自己家。政治经济学第五篇读过这一件：「这是退出，退得出，也有代价；它说明有人走了，不说明留下来的人有没有问的路」（政治经济学第五篇第二节）。

那一篇问的是留下来的人。这一篇问走了的那一位。

问到南窗，问得出它为什么涨价，涨价以前说明了什么；这是制衡。可只问南窗的经营决定，完成不了另一核：那位母亲此后的照护有没有着落。她离开了南窗，南窗对她的那一份供给也就停了。涨价本身不完成规范的判断（政治经济学第二篇 E10），南窗可以涨价，也可以有它的理由。要问的是另一件：一个人从一处照护里走开，她那一份照护的供给，还有没有着落。

接回家，照护落在家里。家里照护得了照护不了，这一份是不是落到了一位家属一个人的身上，这一例里没有记，本篇不补；接回家可以是一家人的选择，也可以是没有别的选择，本篇不读那位家属的心。可这一问有一个地址。总体的那一核不在南窗：保障的对象是供给，不是那个构，南窗不担保那位母亲往后的全部照护。它在管理照护的制度那一侧。这不是说南窗对自己涨价、停供或交接的作用不必说明；那一份是南窗自己的，照它的职责另核。

"可以换一家"在这里怎样读？王国州还有没有别的养老院，有几家，收不收得下，收多少钱，这一例里都没有记；政治经济学第三篇也写明，不拿南窗去证明竞争已经失灵。这一篇也不。可是有没有别家、换不换得到、换不换得起，正是"仍须另核"那一句要核的东西。那一句说的是要另核；这一篇往下问一步：另核由谁来核。

一家一家正常供给，不独自证明这一类供给已经够得着需要它的人。制度既承担保障这一供给的职责（政治经济学第三篇 E14a），就须落实"够不够"的核查；在市场里，落实它的是给这一市场立规、执规的构。所以，许多家供给的时候，"够不够保障基础层"这一核，落在立规、执规的构那里（推及：E14a 原说制度要保障这一供给，多家供给时仍须另核；这里推到这一核的地址，自担）。它不由"有竞争"三个字推出来，政治经济学说过，「竞争也可以让几家都排到最低」（政治经济学第三篇第二节）；也不由某一家院的经营去答。

南窗这一处，事例里记下的制度一侧，有州卫生厅定的一条底：每三十张床的夜班护理员最低二人；有州卫生厅的检查，有对两家院的警告。这是一条显明的线、一种执行的能力，管的是院里的照护。院外的那一份，比如照护的可及，州另有什么安排，这一例里没有记。这一篇不判它有没有；它只指出，总体的那一核在州那一侧，不由南窗的经营决定完成，也不落到那位家属身上。

保障也不是背书。政府论说：「保证不是背书。」原说政府保证了权利，它自己的权力也不因此正当，「保证是必要条件，不是充分条件」；政治经济学第三篇第二节把它推到不替提供供给的构背书（政府论第三篇 G22；政治经济学第三篇第二节）。照这一推，制度保障照护这一份供给，不替南窗的经营或那一笔收购背书；保障泉坳的水，不保证泉坳水业这家公司永远存续，接续可以另行安排（政治经济学第三篇第二节）。供给要接续，与那一家要不要救，是两件事；第五篇写公共资金时回到这里。

判断层：照护在一处地方是否落在生命基底上，那一处的"够不够"怎样核，由管理照护的制度判；判的位置说得出根据，担后果；被照护的人与家属可以问它。

第三节　越接近生命基底，制衡越强

泉坳水业所在的位置，政治经济学第二篇叫它生命基底处唯一的供给位置。那一篇说，这样的位置「是公用与私营的混合」：它在为更大的构承担执行之责，不只是在经营自己的东西；这一供给是管理它的制度或政府职责的一部分，「有无许可，这一层都在」（政治经济学第二篇 E11a）。

"公用与私营的混合"说的是职责的性质，不是所有制。它说这个位置同时在做两件事：经营自己的东西，又承担一份公共的执行；它不说这个位置该由谁来持有。泉坳水业是一家私营公司，它在那个位置上，持的是混合的职责。

那么所有制呢？这一篇的回答分两层。

规范层只给方向。 "负责基础层"的范围有多宽，哪些供给、哪些公司算在里面，规范层不划。它只给一个方向：越接近触及生命基底，制衡、监督越强，私有占多少这一问越要问得出、答得出。

这一方向与政治经济学的一条同形。那一条说：「经济位置的权力面越大，对它的制衡越强」（政治经济学第三篇 E14）。原说的是权力面；这里推到供给离生命基底有多近：供给越接近生命基底，制衡与监督越强（自担）。两条都不给刻度，「刻度与形态在规则层」（同上）。

"问得出、答得出"要说清楚。问得出，是受这项供给的人，以及替他们问的位置，有一条路能把"这一份供给由谁持有，为什么由它持有"提出来，提出来有地方接。答得出，是作出这一安排的立规的构，与持有它的一方，说得出根据：为什么这一处这样持有；持有的一方撤出、卖出、改了方向的时候，线由谁接着（自担）。

为什么越接近生命基底，这一问越要紧？因为在那里，供给断不断，就是线守不守。职责束缚位置，不束缚人：持有一个供给位置的一方可以退出，只是怎样撤出要另核（政治经济学第二篇 E11a）。谁持有，谁就握着退出、卖出、改方向这几样决定。离生命基底越近，这几样决定越直接压到人的生命上；谁握着它们，就越要问得出、答得出（自担）。

泉坳有一件事可以照这一句看。第 26 年，NUT 的一家水务集团远川水务，买下苹果城那几位投资人所持的泉坳水业股份，合计七成；石桥特许公司的三成照旧。第 27 年，远川出资换了净水站的设备，州卫生厅当年两次抽检都合格。两个村唯一的自来水，七成换了东家。这一次买卖经过了哪一个位置，有没有谁问过、谁答过，这一例里没有记，本篇不补；也不由此判这一次买卖越了线，不读买方的心。这一篇要指出的只是：照这个方向，"两个村唯一的水换了谁的手"是要问得出、答得出的一问；它在泉坳问在哪里、答在哪里，是规则层与判断层的事。远川是另一国的公司，这一案后来还有一面，第七篇写。

这个方向有两处要防。

一处是不预设私有的比例越低越好。这一问问的是私有占多少，不是要它越少越好；公有占多少，同样可问。政治经济学定过：「按权力面认，不按公私认」（政治经济学第三篇 E15）。一家全由公家持有的供给者，也要答得出它为什么这样持有，撤改时线由谁接着；第四节读苹果城的电力局。

另一处是不把方向读成名单。"越接近"是方向，不是一张名单；一项供给离生命基底有多近，在判断层。

所有制在规则层，这一篇只作建议。 规则层可以有倾向。这一篇写一条：越接近生命基底，越倾向于不全由私人所有（自担）。这是倾向，不是线：一家全由私人所有的供给者，照样可以守线，泉坳的自来水正是一家私营公司带来的；一家公营的，照样可以越线。倾向的理由在接续：离生命基底越近，持有的一方退出、卖出或改方向时，线由谁接着，越不能只靠持有的那一方自己。

规则层可用的样子很多。下面几样只是例子，不排次序，也不是这一篇替哪一个制度定的规：

  • 公营的供给者；
  • 公私混合所有；
  • 公共的选项与私营的供给者并存；
  • 私营的公用事业受监管，带普遍服务的义务，比如一片地方里每一户都接；
  • 供给中断时有人接手：接管与接续的预备。政治经济学说，这一位置怎样撤出，要经一条不封他人接续之路、由管理者安排接续的程序（政治经济学第二篇 E11a）；接管与接续的预备，是这一程序在规则层的一种样子；
  • 特别股一类的安排：公共的一方只就出售、撤出、改变供给用途这几样决定持一票。

每一样都有它的代价与失灵处。公营的，主人与审它的人可能是同一处；受监管的私营，监管者可能被捕获；特别股可能只剩名义。选哪一样，怎样搭，由各个制度按自己的条件在规则层定；这一篇不说哪一种最好。

王国州有一件规则层的改动，正落在这个方向上：第 27 年，州把《公用事业法》的门槛由五千户降到五百户，泉坳水业由此归委员会监管。离生命基底近的一份供给，进了一个常设的监督位置。第一节说过，这一改动不改线，改的是委员会这一常设审查覆不覆盖泉坳水业。它也有另一面：五百户到五千户之间的小供水者，都多了一处要答的地方，这对它们是一份代价。这一份代价怎样核，第二篇说。同一个州议会，第 28 年有议员提出把门槛改回五千户，到年底没有表决；那一件，第六篇与第七篇回到它。

苹果城的电是另一种样子：全城的电约四成由市属的电力局自己的电厂发，其余向州内两家私营发电厂买。公营的供给者与私营的发电厂并存，这是一种样子，不证明它最好；第四节读它。

判断层：一项供给离生命基底有多近，持有怎样安排，由管理那一处的立规的构定；定了，它要答得出上面两问；受这项供给的人可以问它。

SC2　越接近生命基底，制衡与监督越强，私有的比例越要问得出、答得出。生命基底处唯一的供给位置，职责是公用与私营的混合（承 E11a）；做基础层之事的构，制度保障它的供给，不限唯一的供给者（承 E14a）。"负责基础层"的范围，规范层不划，只给方向：越接近触及生命基底，制衡、监督越强，私有占多少这一问越要问得出、答得出；不预设比例越低越好，公有占多少同样可问（与 E14 同形，推及自担；公私两侧照 E15）。所有制在规则层，本系列只作建议。

第四节　线不随所有制变

上一节说，公有占多少同样可问。这一节读一个公营的供给者。

苹果城电力局是市属的机构，供全城的电；局长由市长提名、市议会任命。王国州《电力供应条例》第 3 条规定：市属的供电者，由所属的市议会监督，州公用事业委员会不审它的电价。电力局的电价，由市议会公用事业小组审议、市议会表决。

电力局给全城每一户接电，老城区的旧楼也接；收入在一条线以下的家庭，电价打七折；每年十二月至二月，欠费的不断电。它每年把售电收入的一成二转入市财政，第 26 年起写进市预算。第 26 年至第 28 年，电价三次上调，合计约两成。第 28 年，州审计署的报告说，转入市财政的部分"在电价构成中未单列"；电力局答："电价是市议会公开表决的，转给市财政的钱也用在市民身上。"

读它，用的是读一家私营供给者的同一套问。政治经济学定过：「权力面相当的积累位置，不因私有而豁免制衡；公的一侧也不因此另得豁免」（政治经济学第三篇 E15）。那一篇用养老基金、石桥特许公司与两样结算的位置读过这一条；这一篇多问一处：一个公营的供给位置，离生命基底很近，线怎样落在它身上。分四步。

线照样在。 电力局是一个构，它的施为照制度论那一条，不随所有制打折。冬天断不断电，读法与泉坳停不停水一样：它是公营的，不使一次压到生命基底上的断电变得可以，也不使它更重。电力局冬季不断电，给低收入家庭打七折，每一户都接，这几样是事例里记下的支持；照实记下，不因此认证它在目的王国方向上。

积累的一侧过两道闸。 电力局的电厂、它的售电收入，是积累来的。第一道闸问它有没有拿这份积累去压人的基础层、锁人的离开；这一例里没有记这样的事。第二道闸问它有没有借这份积累换成这样一种地位：被问的一方「能够独自关闭对它自身的追问、纠错或必要的兑现」（政治经济学第四篇 E22）。用户对电费有异议，先找电力局的客服处，再找市议会公用事业小组；审计署说，用户投诉的处理"未见独立复核"。这几处问的路独不独立，第六篇细读，这里不展开。

授予的一侧照它的来源受制衡。 电力局供全城电的这个位置，是市里给的：局长由市长提名、市议会任命，电价由市议会审。这是一项公共的授予，照政府论读：「公共授予须保留可撤的功能」（政府论第六篇 G37）；谁授的，授了什么，谁依什么程序改它，要说得出。一成二转入市财政，是主人写进自己预算的一项决定。私营的供给者，主人也从收入里拿走一份，叫利润，叫分红；公营的，叫转入财政。名称不同，问的是同几件：拿走的那一份，付钱的人看不看得见；看见了，问不问得到；它有没有压到谁的基础层上。审计署说那一份未单列；电力局说钱也用在市民身上。两句都照实记下。三次调价与这一成二是什么关系，这一例里没有记，本篇不把涨价归到它上面。

一个位置两种来源都有的，分别核。 电力局既持一项公共的授予，又持有积累来的电厂与收入；它向两家私营发电厂买电，那两家是积累的位置。各照各的来源核，一种来源不替另一种洗账（政治经济学第三篇 E15）。

所以，线不随所有制变，说的是两句。国有不自动在目的王国方向上：冬季不断电、七折、每一户都接，不抵销三次调价与未单列的那一份；三次调价也不抵销那几样；各自核。国有也不自动越线：一成二转入市财政，市议会审自己的电力局，这几样本身不证明谁越了线。读它们的是结构：代价落在谁身上，问的路在不在，谁定，谁负责。

国家持有的公司，不只在公用事业里。一家国家持股的公司在市场里与别家竞争、借钱、并购，照的也是同一套读法：判结构，不判名称。它若靠国家的背靠取得别家没有的条件，那一份照授予的来源核；它若成了别人绕不过去的地方，照第三篇；它与别家的竞争，照第四篇；它取得的公共资金，照第五篇。

判断层：苹果城电力局的电价与转入市财政的比例，由市议会定，市议会担作出这一决定的那一份责任；承受作用的，首先是付电费的人家；他们可以问。审它的位置是不是独立于电力局与它的主人，第六篇写。

SC3　线不随所有制变。按权力面认，不按公私认：公营的供给位置，积累一侧过两道闸，授予一侧照它的来源受制衡，保留可撤的功能；一个位置两种来源都有的，分别核（承政治经济学 E15、E21、E22）。国有不自动在目的王国方向上，也不自动越线。

第五节　住房

这一节回到岭下。

规范层：方向句。 越接近生命基底，保障越不可缺；有地方住，在这一方向上（自担）。

有一处旁据。政治经济学第四篇写第一道闸时说：「粮食、饮水、电力、住处，凡人维持生活所必需的东西，一旦囤在一处，都可以被换成这样的控制」（政治经济学第四篇第一节）。那里是在举例，说的是这些东西囤在一处可以被换成控制；这一篇只取它所举的"住处"一样，作方向句的旁据。清单仍在规则层。

方向句要防三种读法。它说的是有地方住，不是有一套自己的房，也不是住在某一处、某一种房里。它说的是方向，不是一个数：住处在一城一地是否落在生命基底上，在判断层；保障的清单与程度，在规则层（政治经济学第二篇 E10）。它也不是说住房不能交给市场：房子由许多家供，房租由租约定，本身不越线；线以上，市场照常放开。

所以，一个目的王国方向的市场所在的制度，会在某种程度上给住房一份保障；给到什么程度，取什么形式，在规则层（自担）。形式可以很多：公租房，租金或住房的补助，续租与搬迁的程序，冬夜里有一张床。这些只是例子，不排次序。

反面例子：岭下。 岭下市在高岭邦东部，人口约三十万。通衢集团在高岭邦的仓库设在岭下市郊，第 25 年扩建，雇员由约三千人增至约七千人。第 25 年至第 28 年，一居室的平均房租涨了约七成；第 28 年，一位仓库分拣工的月房租约是他半个月的工资。一位老房东三年没有给原来的住户涨租；一家开发商在城北盖了约两千套公寓，开盘价比旧房高两成。第 27 年，市议会有人提案：房租一年涨幅超过三成的，房东须向市里申报理由；第 28 年表决，未通过。

高岭邦的法与岭下市的条例里，没有一条把住房写进任何保障：城里没有公租房；房租怎样涨，由租约定；房东不续约，提前三十日通知即可。UCSE 一级的法把住房留给各邦自定，没有直接的规定。

岭下把住房整个交给了租约。交出去的是什么？

照制度论那一句读：保障是把线显明、具体化，给它制度的背靠，并有真实的执行能力（制度论第三篇 Z17）。照这一句看，岭下交出去的，是这三样（自担）。没有一条显明的线：一个人到什么地步算是没有地方住了，已记的规则里没有说。没有制度的背靠：已记的各级规则里，没有一处由制度给背靠的住房供给，比如公租房。线与背靠都没有载入，执行也就无从谈起。

反面例子反的是这一样：规则层一项保障也不载入（自担）。不是反房租涨了：房租涨落是许多份租约合起来的。不是反保障给得少：给多少在规则层，各个制度有它的余地。反的是一项也不给。一项也不给，是把一样在"越接近生命基底，保障越不可缺"那一方向上的东西，整个放到线以上，只当作一笔交易。这一反，反在方向上，不在程度上。

第二节说，保障不限唯一的供给者：多家供给，是否足以保障基础层，仍须另核，这一核落在立规、执规的构那里。岭下的房子有许多家供。已记的规则没有把住房保障载入；"够不够"的核查已由哪一个具体位置承接、怎样发动，事例里没有记。

政府论说过：「政府不保证它，权力同样不正当，须被追问」（政府论第三篇 G18）。原说的是基础权利的背靠。政府论自己又分开两样：「"可被追问"对一切政府、一切时候；"须被追问"指一处已核的失守欠下一份说明与修正」（政府论第三篇 G17）。照这两句推到住处：一处住处若在判断层核为落在生命基底上，规则层一项保障也不载入，照 G18 是须被追问的；核以前，照 G17，是可被追问的（自担）。住处在岭下是否已经落在生命基底上，哪一处的失守已经核实，要由判断层去核；本篇不替它核。本篇读到的是结构：规则层一项也不载入，在方向上是反面的；这一结构，可以对着作出它的立规者问。

问谁？高岭邦的法，岭下市的条例，各在自己那一层。UCSE 一级把住房留给各邦，这一件就落到了各邦。市议会第 28 年否决了申报一案，是岭下市的立规者就住房作出的一项决定，可以问它；那一案要的是房东说明涨租的理由，是一项说明的义务，还不是一项保障。它未通过，只记作事实，不由此判市议会越线。

分开几样。 前置篇说过，可以问立规的构，不等于房租的每一次上涨都记在它名下；没有载入一项保障，也不等于已经有谁越了线。这里把这几样分开。

不判房东与开发商。房东与开发商是市场里的供给者；对它们，线是第一道闸：不拿住处去锁住人的离开、拒绝与撤回（政治经济学第四篇 E21）。政治经济学读过一处这样的事：青窑镇的宿舍与工作拴在一起，离职三十日内搬出（政治经济学第四篇第一节）。岭下的事例里没有记这样的事。一位老房东三年没涨租，一家开发商盖了约两千套，这两样照实记下，是有人在供；开盘价比旧房高两成，不独自构成关路的作用。

不判通衢。仓库扩建、雇员加到约七千人，与房租上涨是并存的事实，不是已经查明的原因；租金、扩仓与求宿的人数放在一起，不证明哪一项积累已经侵蚀了保障；保障没有载入，也不证明它曾被哪一方挤掉。通衢从第 26 年起给仓库工人每月一份住房津贴，约合一成房租，离职当月停发；这一件照政治经济学读附条件的给付的三件另核（政治经济学第二篇 E12a），第五篇回到它。

不由人数推出越线。城里唯一一处收容所是一家慈善会办的，四十张床；慈善会的记录说，冬天夜里前来求宿的人，第 25 年平均每晚约二十人，第 28 年约九十人，常有人在门外排不上。这个数字照记录的口径读：它是前来求宿的人，不是入住的人，也不是全城在外面过夜的人。它能说的是：那些夜里，来的人比床多，有人来了没有床。它不能说的是：全城有多少人没有地方住，为什么，哪一个构越了线。它是一个问的入口：排不上的那些人，那一夜落在哪里，岭下有没有一个位置要答。已记的各级制度里，没有记这样的位置；那四十张床是一家慈善会的供给，制度有没有给它背靠，事例里也没有记。

同在 UCSE 之下，王国州的苹果城有公租房（政府论第三篇所载）。这里只记作事实，不拿两地相比；比也比不出岭下哪一处越了线，只说明同一个上级之下，各邦的规则层可以不同。

判断层：岭下的住处是否落在生命基底上，保障给到什么程度，由高岭邦与岭下市的立规者判、定；判了要说得出根据，定了担后果；住在那里的人可以问它们。这一篇只把要答的问题与答它的位置写出来，不替它们答。

这一篇停在哪里

这一篇立了三条：基础层是线，不在市场的天平上，不随价格、规模、效率或所有制打折，也不是说供给须无偿（SC1）；越接近生命基底，制衡与监督越强，私有占多少这一问越要问得出、答得出，所有制在规则层（SC2）；线不随所有制变，国有不自动在方向上，也不自动越线（SC3）。住房一节只写方向句，不立命题。

它没有证明的：哪一种所有制最好；基础层的清单；哪一项供给落在生命基底上；具体的公用事业规制怎样设计；由露宿、求宿的人数推出越线；岭下哪一处的失守已经核实。

交给后面各篇的：保障措施限制别人涌现层时怎样核，比如门槛降低给小供水者添的那一份代价，在第二篇；供给要接续与那一家要不要救，在第五篇；审公营供给的位置独不独立，议员提出把门槛改回去的那一件，在第六篇；泉坳那一案跨了国界的一面，在第七篇。

留下的余项：门槛以下那一段由什么安排承接守线与受问；多家供给时"够不够"由立规、执规的构怎样核，用什么方法；住房保障的程度与形式。这几样在规则层，这一篇只给了方向与地址。

本篇命题（合列）

SC1　基础层是线，不在市场的天平上。立规、执规的构的施为，对每一个内部的人不越线，不随价格、规模、效率或所有制打折（承制度论 Z9）；经济位置不以积累兑换对人的基础层的支配（承政治经济学 E21）。线不随价格打折，不是说供给须无偿（E11a）。基础层具体包括什么，清单在规则层；一项供给是否落在生命基底上，在判断层（承 E10）。

SC2　越接近生命基底，制衡与监督越强，私有的比例越要问得出、答得出。生命基底处唯一的供给位置，职责是公用与私营的混合（承 E11a）；做基础层之事的构，制度保障它的供给，不限唯一的供给者（承 E14a）。"负责基础层"的范围，规范层不划，只给方向：越接近触及生命基底，制衡、监督越强，私有占多少这一问越要问得出、答得出；不预设比例越低越好，公有占多少同样可问（与 E14 同形，推及自担；公私两侧照 E15）。所有制在规则层，本系列只作建议。

SC3　线不随所有制变。按权力面认，不按公私认：公营的供给位置，积累一侧过两道闸，授予一侧照它的来源受制衡，保留可撤的功能；一个位置两种来源都有的，分别核（承政治经济学 E15、E21、E22）。国有不自动在目的王国方向上，也不自动越线。
