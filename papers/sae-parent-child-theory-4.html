Arrangements in the Parent-Child Relation

Han Qin (秦汉) · Independent Researcher · 2026

Abstract

This paper reads the arrangements parents set for a child, and the household's rules. Only the side of them that lies between person and person, the part the institution does not provide for or that goes beyond its provisions, is read here; provisions concerning guardianship, role authority and constructs are acknowledged but not judged. These arrangements are the parents' today's arrangements for the child's tomorrow: what the layer of relation reads is one and the same bearer's today toward his tomorrow, and carrying this to the parents' today toward the child's tomorrow is this series' own undertaking. An arrangement is read by three questions: whether there is an arrangement transferring the authority to interpret as the first-person position matures; whether the refusals, deviations and restructurings the child gradually produces can really alter that arrangement; whether the present configuration is marked as a present arrangement open to revision, or as a final content irrefutable in future. The authority to interpret is handed over domain by domain as the child matures in each matter, not all on one day; a line that is externally fixed, not defeasible by the facts of a case, and applied uniformly across matters carries a risk of systematic misclassification.

When 13DD emerges, the child's "no" is where negation first targets the other and the "I" is chiseled out; one "no" does not by itself certify that 13DD has emerged. Reading it uniformly as not yet understanding is exactly what the second question blocks. At the end of the father or the mother, what is read here is: keeping one's own direction, while not maintaining it by pressing down the child's negation. The ex ante structural condition of a cultivative intervention, carried to the child, is checked at the parent's end, without first judging which layer the child has reached; where there is something he cannot yet do, it is checked separately for pre-occupation: whether his position for later first-person running and revision is sealed in advance, before he can do it.

Marking a present arrangement as a final content the child will be unable to refute in future is a signal of pre-occupation; to read it as pre-occupation further requires that the not-yet-running position be sealed shut before the gate runs of itself: the difference between maintaining and fixing is what this whole judgment turns on. An arrangement does not become pre-occupation merely by constraining the child; and a relation's long absence of conflict is not taken as evidence of cultivation. This paper interfaces with Rousseau and the Analects, and reads an account by Lu Xun. It does not prove that parents ought not to set rules for a child, nor that the child's "no" is always right, nor that the fewer arrangements the better.

Introduction

One winter morning, Lin Wan handed He Zhou a thick coat. He Zhou stood at the door and said: "Not wearing it." Lin Wan said it was cold out, the temperature had dropped today. He Zhou said he wasn't cold, he didn't want to wear that one, it was too puffy, the other kids would laugh. Lin Wan said it again; He Zhou took a step back and put his hands behind his back: "I'm not wearing it."

Lin Wan looked at the clock, folded the coat and stuffed it into his schoolbag: "Take it with you, then, and put it on yourself if you get cold." He Zhou shouldered his bag and left. That afternoon, when school let out, he walked out of the school gate wearing the coat.

The household had another rule: no screens before bed. This one had been set when He Zhou was very small, back when he couldn't fall asleep after watching cartoons. Later, when He Zhou was bigger, he said one evening: "I've finished all my homework. Why can't I watch for ten minutes?" The first thing He Chuan said was: "A rule is a rule. You'll understand when you're older." Lin Wan said: "When we set this, it was because you couldn't sleep after watching. What about now?" He Zhou said he could fall asleep after watching now. The family talked it over for a long time, and changed it to: on weekends, screens until nine; on weekdays, as before.

Several things in these two have to be read apart. Whether the matter of that coat was settled by the parents, by He Zhou, or by neither; what He Zhou's "no" changed, and what it did not; whose today set the bedtime rule, and whose tomorrow it governs; whether "you'll understand when you're older" and "when we set this, it was because of what; what about now" are the same kind of response; and what the rule that was finally changed shows.

What this paper reads. The parent-child relation this series reads has as its premise that the end of the father or the mother is headed in the 15DD direction, and it is read relation by relation.1 The two previous papers read the securing and support parents give a child; this paper reads the arrangements parents set for a child.

This paper reads six things. First, how the household's rules are read: only the side between person and person, read as the parents' today's arrangements for the child's tomorrow. Second, the three questions by which an arrangement is read. Third, the domain-by-domain handing over of the authority to interpret. Fourth, the child's "no." Fifth, the ex ante structural condition of a cultivative intervention, carried to the child. Sixth, pre-occupation.

Shared words. "Arrangement" in this paper means something parents set for a child that governs him for some time afterward: when he goes to bed, what he wears, whether he can go out on his own. "Rules" are the kind of arrangement a household states out loud and follows again and again.

"The authority to interpret" means who says how a matter is to be read, what it counts as, how it is to be. While a child is small, the authority to interpret many matters lies in the parents' hands; this paper reads how it is handed over.

"Pre-occupation" follows the usage at the value layer: a not-yet-running position filled before it begins to run.2 Where the reading reaches this layer, colonization takes the value layer's narrow sense, marked on the spot.3

Method. As in the previous three papers. What this paper reads is those effects at the parents' end by which arrangements are set and the child's "no" is answered. Which layer He Zhou has reached is read only as a condition of that end's acting.

§1 Today Toward Tomorrow

PC21 The arrangements parents set for a child, and the household's rules, are read here only on their side between person and person, the part the institution does not provide for or that goes beyond its provisions; provisions concerning guardianship, role authority and constructs are acknowledged but not judged. What is read is how they become today's arrangements for the child's tomorrow: the layer of relation reads one and the same bearer's today toward his tomorrow; here it is the parents' today toward the child's tomorrow

Only the side between person and person

Part of a household's rules falls on the side of the institution: who is the child's guardian, who signs for him, who has the authority to decide for him which school he attends. This side belongs to the institutional side, and is acknowledged but not judged.4 This series reads the other side: between He Chuan and He Zhou, between Lin Wan and He Zhou, how a rule is set, how it is followed, how it is changed. A rule can have both sides at once; reading one side does not declare that the other does not exist.

Arrangements of today for tomorrow

An arrangement is set today and governs what comes after. The rule of no screens before bed was set on one evening, and governs every evening after.

The layer of relation has read today's arrangements for tomorrow; what it spoke of there is one and the same person: the relation among his yesterday, today and tomorrow; a position in time is not another subject, and what actually acts is always today alone.5 There, one sentence runs: today cannot sign for tomorrow; tomorrow may revise, and revising keeps the books on the change.6

The arrangements parents set for a child differ from that place: the one who sets them is the parent, the one governed is the child. Today is the parents' today; tomorrow is the child's tomorrow. This step, from one bearer to two bearers, is this series' own undertaking. Once carried over, that sentence bears more weight: the parents' today can sign for the child's tomorrow even less. The bedtime rule was set on the basis of the situation that he could not sleep after watching a screen; on the day He Zhou can say "I can fall asleep after watching now," that day's tomorrow is his, and he can speak up.

§2 Three Questions

PC22 An arrangement is read by three questions: whether there is an arrangement transferring the authority to interpret as the first-person position matures; whether the refusals, deviations and restructurings the child gradually produces can really alter that arrangement, or are uniformly read as not yet understanding; whether the present configuration is marked as a present arrangement open to revision, or as a final content irrefutable in future

Three signals

The value layer, reading the configuration in which a still-growing subject stands, lists three signals that can be read.7 Borrowed for the household's rules:

The first question: in this rule, is there an arrangement that, as he grows, hands back to him how this matter is to be read? That evening, the family changed the weekend time limit; on the strength of this change alone, it cannot yet be said that the authority to interpret has been handed over. Whether the authority to interpret has been handed over as he matures in this matter is to be seen, separately, in how this matter is settled afterward and who says so; the example does not reach this step, and nothing is filled in here.

The second question: can the refusals, deviations and restructurings He Zhou gradually produces really alter this rule, or are they uniformly read as not yet understanding? What He Chuan said first, "you'll understand when you're older," falls on the latter side if it is the whole of the answer to He Zhou's question; that evening it was not the whole: Lin Wan went on to ask whether the facts had changed, the family talked it over for a long time, and the rule was changed. By the second question, what can be read from this occasion is that the objection He Zhou raised actually changed the original arrangement.

The third question: is this rule now marked as a present arrangement that can be changed, or as something that can never be refuted later? The sentence "a rule is a rule," on its own, is not yet enough to mark this rule as something that can never be refuted; one has to see whether it is used to shut out later objection and renegotiation along with it. That evening, after this sentence, the rule was changed.

What is read is the arrangement, not the person

The three questions each ask one thing; they are signals that can be read, and what they read is an arrangement, not a father; changing an arrangement is not handing over the authority to interpret, and a common saying does not automatically become a signal. That He Chuan said "you'll understand when you're older" does not certify which layer he is at; that evening the rule was changed, and he was sitting there too. A signal says only where an arrangement falls; reading out pre-occupation requires one thing more (§6).

The layer of relation has borrowed the shape of these three signals to read commitment; there it says that their main use is between parents and child.8

§3 Domain by Domain

PC23 The authority to interpret is handed over domain by domain as the child matures in each matter, not all on one day. Using a line that is externally fixed, not defeasible by the facts of a case, and applied uniformly across matters, to determine once that he has arrived in every matter or in none, carries a risk of systematic misclassification; a judgment that can be overturned by the facts, made matter by matter, and kept open to appeal and review is not in this class

The judgment is by domain

A person still growing does not mature in one piece. The judgment is by domain, not over the whole person.9 What He Zhou wears has long been his own business; how to spend his pocket money, he still asks his parents' advice about; taking the bus on his own to his grandmother's, his parents said one summer, was now fine. The arrangements in the three matters differ, and cannot be merged into one judgment of maturity. Whether the authority to interpret has been handed over is seen by who, in each matter, interprets, avows and decides, not by whether he asks his parents.

An externally fixed line

Merging these matters into one line, so that on some day every matter is handed over together, or none: a line of this kind, externally fixed, not defeasible by the facts of a case, and applied uniformly across matters, carries a risk of systematic misclassification.10 Some things he could do long ago may be kept out by the line; some things he cannot yet do may be pushed out along with the rest.

Not every rule stated out loud is a line of this kind. "Taking the bus on your own, start with this route to Grandma's; any day you feel you can't, call": this can be overturned by the facts, is set matter by matter, and leaves room to change and to ask, and is not in this class.10

What is handed over

What is handed over domain by domain is the position from which that matter is read and settled. In some matters, the position of raising and refusing has long been his own, and does not wait for the parents to hand it over (Paper 7); what is read here is the parents' end: in one matter after another, how the interpretations they used to hold for him are given back to him, one by one.

§4 That "No"

PC24 When 13DD emerges, the child's "no" is where negation first targets the other and the "I" is chiseled out; one "no" does not by itself certify that 13DD has emerged. Reading it uniformly as not yet understanding is exactly what the second question blocks. At the end of the father or the mother, what is read here is: keeping one's own direction, while not maintaining it by pressing down the child's negation; how one answers on one occasion is read by words and deeds, and does not on that basis certify which layer the parent as a whole is at

Negation targeting the other for the first time

Upstream has read a stretch in which the child's negation first targets another's will, and the "I" is thereby chiseled out.11 The "no" of this stretch is not refusing a thing; it is refusing what another person wants him to do.

One "no" does not by itself certify that this stretch has occurred. He Zhou pushing away the spoon can be not being hungry; his saying "not wearing it" can be that the coat really is too puffy. Upstream itself keeps several kinds of negation apart, and does not reduce this stretch to a behavioral marker.12 The series does not read from one "no" which layer the child has reached; it reads how the parents' end answers it.

The matter of that coat is not the first "no": by then He Zhou could already say why he did not want something. The "no" spoken of in the first half of this section comes earlier, like the pushing away of the spoon in Paper 2; what the coat is borrowed here to read is how the parents' end answers a "no."

Read uniformly as not yet understanding

Reading the child's "no" uniformly as not yet understanding is the kind the second question blocks.13 "You're little, you don't know what cold is": if this sentence is the whole of the answer to "not wearing it," that "no" has changed nothing.

The parents' end

That morning, Lin Wan had her direction: the temperature had dropped, the child should be kept warm; this is a matter of the Base Layer, and she did not set it down. Nor did she keep to this direction by pressing down He Zhou's "no": the arrangement changed from having to put it on before going out to taking it along, with whether and when to wear it left to him to decide at the time. He Zhou's "no" changed this arrangement; the condition that there is something to wear if he is cold still holds. That he was wearing the coat when school let out is another thing that happened later, and does not cancel the fact that the morning's "no" had already changed the arrangement.

What is read here is: keeping one's own direction, while not maintaining it by pressing down the child's negation. Upstream, writing the cultivation of this stretch, writes its condition as the cultivator standing at 14DD: a person with a cannot-not of his own will not be threatened by the child's negation.14 What this series takes from that sentence is only the side that can be read: how one answers on one occasion is read by words and deeds. Lin Wan's answer that morning does not on that basis certify which layer she as a whole stands at; on another morning, pressed for time, she could say "Put it on, or you're not going out," and that would be another occasion.

Room for "no" within securing

Paper 2 said that once 13DD has emerged, securing gains one more thing: room for the child's "no" not to be punished.15 That morning He Zhou said "no" three times, and was not scolded for it, nor kept from going out for it. This falls on the face of securing: his saying "no" does not put his Base Layer at risk.

§5 The Ex Ante Structural Condition

PC25 The ex ante structural condition of a cultivative intervention, carried to the child, is checked at the parent's end, without first judging which layer the child has reached: whether this end's arrangements compress his authority, channels and practical possibility of refusing, interrupting, revising, exiting and restating himself. Where there is something he cannot yet do, it is checked separately for pre-occupation: whether his position for later first-person running and revision is sealed in advance, before he can do it. That the child cannot do it for now neither falsely claims that he already has the real capacity, nor on that account hands that position to the parents to pre-occupy. Rules of guardianship, of exercise on his behalf and of particular disposition belong to the institutional side

Checked at this end

A cultivative intervention has an ex ante condition: the intervention may not compress the other's authority, channels or practical possibility of refusing, interrupting, revising, exiting and restating himself; this condition is checkable in the first person, and needs no judgment of the other's layer or inner state.16 Carried to what lies between parents and child, what is checked is still the parents' end: does this arrangement of mine compress his authority, channels and practical possibility of saying no, stopping, changing, getting out, saying it another way? One does not first judge which layer he has reached, and only then decide whether to check.

For the bedtime rule, what is checked is: can He Zhou open his mouth and ask; if he asks, is there somewhere that listens; if it listens and the facts have changed, can the rule be changed? That evening, all three were there.

Where he cannot yet do it

There are things a child cannot yet do. A child who cannot yet talk cannot say why he does not want something; a child who cannot yet give reasons does not want to go to that class, and cannot say why. Where he cannot yet do it, this cannot be treated as leaving nothing to check for compression, and let pass. That place is checked separately for pre-occupation: whether his position for later first-person running and revision has been sealed in advance, before he can do it.17 That a child cannot give reasons for now does not mean the parents may first close off his road to giving reasons later.

Neither a false claim nor pre-occupation

Both ends are blocked. One end is the false claim: the child cannot do it, yet is treated as if he already could, and a matter he cannot yet carry is handed to him whole, in the name of respecting his "no." The other end is pre-occupation: the child cannot do it, so that position is treated as the parents', filled for him, and not handed back later. That the child cannot do it for now neither falsely claims that he already has the real capacity, nor on that account hands that position to the parents to pre-occupy.

This proposition does not require a small child to decide everything himself, nor does it cancel necessary care. Who signs for him, who exercises things on his behalf, and how a mishap is disposed of belong to the institutional side.18

§6 Pre-occupation

PC26 Marking a present arrangement as a final content the child will be unable to refute in future is a signal of pre-occupation; to read it as pre-occupation further requires that the not-yet-running position be sealed shut before the gate runs of itself: the difference between maintaining and fixing is what this whole judgment turns on. An arrangement does not become pre-occupation merely by constraining the child. Colonization here takes the value layer's narrow sense

A signal, not a judgment

Sayings such as "you'll understand when you're older" and "a rule is a rule," if used to shut out along with them the road to raising objections and renegotiating later, marking an arrangement as something that can never be refuted later, only then become, in this effect, signals of pre-occupation.19 A single sentence is not enough to complete this reading. Nor is a signal a judgment. To read pre-occupation requires one thing more: that the not-yet-running position has been sealed shut before it can run of itself, already filled at the moment it begins to run.20 That evening, after He Zhou spoke up, the rule was changed; this objection entered the arrangement, and the position was not sealed shut.

Maintaining and fixing

For a person still growing, that not-yet-running position is now his own.20 Parents guarding this position for him, keeping others from occupying it while he cannot yet run it, is maintaining; fixing a candidate configuration as a value content he has already completed, filling the position for him, is fixing. The difference between maintaining and fixing is what this whole judgment turns on.20

The bedtime rule was at first aimed at his being unable to sleep after a screen, and is first read as care at the Base Layer. Whether it also maintained He Zhou's position for later first-person running and revision is still to be seen in whether, once he could raise objections, the arrangement kept open the channels of renegotiation and change. Only if it were marked "you'll have to do this even when you're grown up," and this were used to block renegotiation later, would it be a signal heading toward fixing.

An arrangement in itself is not pre-occupation

An arrangement does not become pre-occupation merely by constraining the child. No screens before bed constrained He Zhou; bedtime constrained him; taking a coat in winter constrained him. Constraint is part of arrangement; reading pre-occupation looks not at how tight the constraint is, but at whether that position has been sealed shut and filled.

The colonization read in this section is the value layer's narrow sense: the source and occurrence of value being occupied.3

No conflict is not evidence of cultivation

A household that has not quarrelled over its rules for years is not, on that account, evidence of cultivation. The direction of colonization can proceed without passing through conflict: whether the path to saying no is made hard to travel or is locked, conflict can fail to appear either way.21 He Zhou's three "no"s that morning, and his asking that evening "why can't I watch for ten minutes," these spoken "no"s, together with the arrangements that were changed, are what this paper reads.

§7 Interfacing with the Tradition

Rousseau: dependence on things alone. In Book II of Émile, Rousseau says the child should be made to depend on things alone, not on people: the limits he meets should come from the necessity of things, not from the will of grown-ups.22 The common question: how a child can meet constraints without being caught in personal relations of command and submission. Rousseau turns constraint over to dependence on things; this paper asks whether a child's refusal can actually alter an arrangement made by another person. Where they part: Rousseau has the tutor design a whole "necessity of things," so that the constraints the child meets seem not to come from people, while in fact they come from one person's arrangements; what this paper reads is exactly who sets an arrangement and whether the child can change it. By the third question, a constraint arranged as "the necessity of things," if the child is not allowed to know that it can be changed, comes close to a signal of pre-occupation.

The Analects: remonstrating gently. The Analects, "Li Ren": "In serving your parents, remonstrate gently. If you see that they are not inclined to follow, remain respectful and do not disobey; work on, and do not resent." SAE's reading of the Analects has already annotated this chapter, and it is pointed back to here.23 The series sets it against itself at one point only: the child's "no" in this chapter falls as gentle remonstrance; when the parents are not inclined to follow, the chapter's words to the child are "remain respectful and do not disobey." What this paper reads is the other end: whether the child's "no" can really alter the parents' arrangements. The two readings run in opposite directions; here only structure is read, and the chapter is not evaluated.

A historical source: Lu Xun, "The Fair of the Five Fierce Gods." In "The Fair of the Five Fierce Gods," one of the pieces in Dawn Blossoms Plucked at Dusk, Lu Xun writes that as a child he was eagerly looking forward to going to see the procession for the gods; just as they were setting out, his father required him first to recite a passage of a book from memory, and only once he had recited it could he go; he recited it, and went, but the fair that day had lost its savor for him. At the end, the adult Lu Xun writes that to this day, thinking of it, he still wonders why his father made him recite at that moment.24 Here only how the adult Lu Xun writes is read: what he writes down is an arrangement hung on something a child was looking forward to; what the adult author writes down is still his incomprehension of that arrangement. This paper does not on this basis read the mind of the child of that time for him, nor certify which direction his father was headed in. By the second question, the existing account lets one read that the recitation and the going out were tied together; whether at the time the child could ask, and whether asking could change it, this passage does not give enough words and deeds to say, and nothing is filled in here.

§8 Drawing Together, and Downstream

This paper has established six propositions. Taken together, they say: the arrangements parents set for a child and the household's rules are read only on their side between person and person, as the parents' today's arrangements for the child's tomorrow; the parents' today cannot sign for the child's tomorrow. An arrangement is read by three questions: whether the authority to interpret is handed over as he matures; whether his refusals, deviations and restructurings can really alter it; whether it is marked as a present arrangement open to revision, or as a final content irrefutable in future. The three questions each ask one thing; they are signals, and they read the arrangement, not the person; changing an arrangement is not handing over the authority to interpret, and a common saying does not automatically become a signal. The authority to interpret is handed over domain by domain; a line that is externally fixed, not defeasible by the facts of a case, and applied uniformly across matters carries a risk of systematic misclassification, while a judgment that can be overturned, is made matter by matter, and keeps review open is not in this class. That "no" is where negation first targets the other, and one "no" does not by itself certify; reading it uniformly as not yet understanding is what the second question blocks; the parents' end keeps its own direction, without maintaining it by pressing down the child's negation, and how one answers on one occasion is read by words and deeds. The ex ante structural condition is checked at the parents' end, without first judging the layer; where he cannot yet do it, it is checked separately for pre-occupation; neither a false claim nor pre-occupation. A signal of pre-occupation is not pre-occupation, and reading pre-occupation further requires that the position be sealed shut; care for his sleep is first read as care at the Base Layer, and does not directly vouch for maintaining; the difference between maintaining and fixing is what this whole judgment turns on; constraint in itself is not pre-occupation; no conflict is not evidence of cultivation.

This paper does not prove that parents ought not to set rules for a child, nor that the child's "no" is always right, nor that the fewer arrangements the better.

What the next paper reads. He Zhou said later that he wanted to learn something neither of his parents knew much about, different from what He Chuan had planned for him. This time it is not refusing to wear a coat: what he is speaking of is a road of his own to take. If the parents' arrangements meet a cannot-not that has grown in him, two laws are meeting.

引言

有一年冬天的早上，林晚把一件厚外套递给何舟。何舟站在门口，说：「不穿。」林晚说外面冷，今天降温。何舟说不冷，他不要穿那件，那件太鼓了，同学会笑。林晚又说了一遍，何舟往后退了一步，把手背到身后：「我不穿。」

林晚看了看表，把外套叠起来塞进他的书包：「那你带着，冷了自己拿出来穿。」何舟背上书包走了。那天下午放学，他是穿着那件外套出的校门。

家里还有一条规矩：睡前不看屏幕。这一条是何舟很小的时候定的，那时他看完动画片总睡不着。后来何舟大了，有一天晚上说：「我作业都写完了，为什么不能看十分钟？」何川第一句说的是：「规矩就是规矩，等你长大就懂了。」林晚说：「定这条的时候，是因为你看完睡不着。现在呢？」何舟说他现在看完就睡得着。一家人说了半天，改成：周末可以看到九点，平时照旧。

这两件里，有几样要分开读。那件外套的事，是父母定的，还是何舟定的，还是都不是；何舟那一声「不」，改动了什么，没有改动什么；睡前那一条，是谁的今天定的，管的是谁的明天；「等你长大就懂了」，与「定这条的时候是因为什么，现在呢」，是不是同一种回应；最后改了的那一条，又说明什么。

第一节 今天对明天

PC21 父母为孩子定的安排、家里的规矩，这里只读其中人与人之间、制度没有规定或超出规定的那一面；涉及监护、角色权限与构的规定，认而不判。读的是它们怎样成为今天对孩子明天的安排：关系一层读的是同一个承担者的今天对明天，这里是父母的今天对孩子的明天

只读人与人之间那一面

家里的规矩，有的一部分落在制度那一侧：谁是孩子的监护人，谁替他签字，谁有权替他决定上哪所学校。这一面归制度侧，认而不判。4 这个系列读的是另一面：何川与何舟之间、林晚与何舟之间，一条规矩怎样定下、怎样照着做、怎样改。一条规矩可以同时有两面；读一面，不宣布另一面不存在。

今天对明天的安排

一条安排是今天定下的，管的是以后。睡前不看屏幕这一条，定的那天是一个晚上，管的是以后的每一个晚上。

关系一层读过今天对明天的安排，那一处说的是同一个人：他的昨天、今天、明天之间的关系；时间上的位置不是另一个主体，真正在行动的始终只有今天。5 那一处有一句：今天签不了明天的字，明天可以改，改要认账。6

父母为孩子定的安排，与那一处不同：定的人是父母，被管的是孩子。今天是父母的今天，明天是孩子的明天。这一步，从一个承担者推到两个承担者，由这个系列自担。推过来以后，那一句更吃重：父母的今天，更签不了孩子明天的字。睡前那一条，定下时依据的是他看完屏幕后睡不着这一情形；到了何舟能说出「我现在看完就睡得着」的那一天，那一天的明天是他的，他可以开口。

第二节 三问

三个信号

价值一层读一个还在长的主体所处的配置，列了三个可以读的信号。7 借到家里的规矩上：

第一问，这条规矩里，有没有一项随他长大、把怎样读这件事交还给他的安排。那一晚，家里改了周末的时限；仅凭这一改，还不能说解释权已经移交。解释权有没有随他在这一件事上的成熟而移交，要另看此后这件事怎样定、由谁来说；例子没有写到这一步，这里不补。

第二问，何舟逐步出现的拒绝、偏离与重构，能不能真实地改变这条规矩，还是一律被读作还没懂事。何川第一句说的「等你长大就懂了」，若是对何舟那一问的全部回应，就落在后一种；那一晚它不是全部：林晚接着问的是事实变了没有，一家人说了半天，规矩改了。照第二问，这一次读得出的是：何舟提出的异议实际改动了原来的安排。

第三问，现在这条规矩，被标作一个可以改的当下安排，还是被标作以后都不能反驳的东西。「规矩就是规矩」这一句，单凭它还不足以把这条规矩标成以后不能反驳的东西；要看它是不是被用来把此后的异议与重议一并挡在外面。那一晚，这一句之后，规矩改了。

读的是安排，不是人

三问各问一件，是可以读的信号，读的是一项安排，不是一个父亲；改了一项安排，不等于交了解释权，一句常见的话，也不自动成为信号。何川说了「等你长大就懂了」，不因此认证他在哪一层；那一晚，规矩改了，他也坐在那里。信号只说一项安排落在哪里；读出预占，另要一件（第六节）。

关系一层借过这三个信号的形状，读承诺；那一处说，这一形状主用在亲子之间。8

第三节 一域一域

PC23 解释权随孩子在各件事上的成熟，一域一域移交，不是某一天一起交出。用一道外部固定、不可由个案事实反驳、跨事项统一使用的线，一次判定他在所有事上都已到了或都未到，有系统性错分的风险；可被事实推翻、按事项分别判、留着申诉与复核的判断，不在此列

判分域

一个还在长的人，成熟不是整块到来的。判分域，不判整个人。9 何舟穿什么，早就是他自己的事；零花钱怎样花，他还会向父母请教；一个人坐公交车去外婆家，有一年夏天起，父母说可以了。三件事的安排不同，不能合成一个成熟判断。解释权有没有移交，要看各件事上谁在解释、认下与决定，不凭他问不问父母来判。

一道外部固定的线

把这些事并成一道线，到了某一天，所有的事一起交出去，或者一件也不交：这样一道外部固定、不可由个案事实反驳、跨事项统一使用的线，有系统性错分的风险。10 有的事他早就能了，可能被线挡在外面；有的事他还不能，可能被线一起推了出去。

不是所有明说的规则都是这样一道线。「一个人坐公交车，先从外婆家这一条线路开始；哪一天你觉得不行，打电话」：这一条可以被事实推翻，按事项分别定，留着改与问的余地，不在此列。10

移交的是什么

一域一域移交的，是那件事怎样读、怎样定的位置。有的事，提出与回绝的位置在他那里早就是他的，不等父母交（第七篇）；这里读的是父母那一端：在一件件事上，原先替他拿着的解释，怎样一件一件还给他。

第四节 那一声「不」

否定第一次指向他人

上游读过一段：孩子的否定第一次指向他人的意志，「我」由此被凿出来。11 这一段里的那一声「不」，不是在拒绝一样东西，是在拒绝另一个人要他怎样。

一次「不」，不独自认证这一段已经发生。何舟推开勺子，可以是不饿；他说「不穿」，可以是那件外套真的太鼓。上游自己也分开了几种否定，不把这一段约化成一个行为指标。12 这个系列不从一声「不」读出孩子到了哪一层；它读父母那一端怎样回应这一声。

那件外套的事，不是第一次的那一声「不」：何舟那时已经会说为什么不要。这一节前半说的那一声在更早的时候，像第二篇里推开勺子的那一下；这里借外套一例读的，是父母那一端怎样回应一声「不」。

一律读成尚未懂事

把孩子的「不」一律读成还没懂事，是第二问所挡的那一种。13 「你还小，你不懂冷」：这一句若是回应「不穿」的全部，那一声「不」就没有改动任何东西。

父母那一端

那一早，林晚有她的方向：降温了，孩子要穿暖，这是基础层的事，她没有放下。她也没有靠压掉何舟的「不」来守住这一方向：安排从出门前必须穿上，改成了带着，穿不穿、什么时候穿，留给他当下定。何舟的「不」改动了这一项安排；冷了有衣服可穿这一条件仍在。放学时他穿着外套，是后来发生的另一件事，不撤销早上那声「不」已经改动安排。

这里读的是：保有自己的方向，同时不靠压掉孩子的否定来维持它。上游写这一段的涵育时，把它的条件写作涵育者站在 14DD，自己有不得不的人，才不会被孩子的否定威胁到。14 这个系列从这一句取的，只是可读的那一面：一次怎样回应，照言行读。林晚那一早的回应，不据此认证她整个人站在哪一层；换一个早上，她赶时间，可以说「穿上，不然别出门」，那是另一次。

保障里给「不」留出的余地

第二篇说过，13DD 涌现以后，保障里多了一样：给孩子的「不」留出不被罚的余地。15 那一早，何舟说了三次「不」，没有因此挨骂，没有因此不让出门。这一样落在保障那一面：他说「不」，不使他的基础层出事。

第五节 事前的结构条件

PC25 涵育性介入的事前结构条件推到孩子，照父或母这一端核，不先判孩子到了哪一层：这一端的安排有没有压缩他拒绝、中断、修订、退出与重新表述的权限、通道及现实可行性。他还做不到的那一处，另照预占核：他此后第一人称运行与修订的位置，是不是在他能做之前就被预先封住。孩子暂时做不到，不冒称他已有现实能力，也不因此把那个位置交给父母预占。监护、代行与具体处置的规则，归制度侧

照这一端核

一次涵育性的介入，有一个事前的条件：干预不能压缩对方拒绝、中断、修订、退出与重新表述自己的权限、通道及现实可行性；这一条第一人称可核，不需要判对方的层级或内心。16 推到父母与孩子之间，核的仍是父母这一端：我的这项安排，压没压缩他说不、停下、改、退出、换一种说法的权限、通道与现实可行性。不先判他到了哪一层，再决定要不要核。

睡前那一条，核的是：何舟能不能开口问；问了，有没有一个地方会听；听了，事实变了，规矩能不能改。那一晚，三样都在。

做不到的那一处

有些事，孩子还做不到。一个还不会说话的孩子，说不出他为什么不要；一个还说不清理由的孩子，不想去那个班，也讲不出为什么。做不到的那一处，不能当作没有压缩可核，就此放过。那一处另照预占核：他此后第一人称运行与修订的位置，是不是在他能做之前，就被预先封住了。17 一个孩子暂时说不清理由，不意味着父母可以先把他以后提出理由的路关掉。

不冒称，也不预占

两头都挡。一头是冒称：孩子做不到，却当他已经能做，把一件他还担不起的事整个交给他，名义上是尊重他的「不」。另一头是预占：孩子做不到，就把那个位置当作父母的，替他填满，以后也不交还。孩子暂时做不到，不冒称他已有现实能力，也不因此把那个位置交给父母预占。

这一条不要求一个还小的孩子自己决定一切，也不取消必要的照料。谁来替他签字、谁来代他行使、出了事怎样处置，归制度侧。18

第六节 预占

PC26 把当前的安排标为孩子将来不可反驳的最终内容，是预占的信号；读成预占，另要那个尚未运行的位置在门自行之前就被封死：维护与定格之别，是这一判的全部所系。安排本身，不因约束了孩子而成为预占。这里的殖民取价值层的窄义

信号，不是判定

「等你长大就懂了」「规矩就是规矩」这样的话，若被用来把此后提出异议与重议的路一并排除，把一项安排标成以后不能反驳的东西，才在这一作用上成为预占的信号。19 单独一句话，不足以完成这一读法。信号也不等于判定。读成预占，另要一件：那个尚未运行的位置，在它能自己运行之前就被封死了，开始运行的那一刻已经被填满。20 那一晚，何舟开口以后，规矩改了；这一次的异议进入了安排，位置没有被封死。

维护与定格

一个还在长的人，那个尚未运行的位置现在就是他的。20 父母替他守着这个位置，在他还不能运行的时候不让别人占了它，这是维护；把一个候选的配置定格成他已经完成的价值内容，替他把位置填满，这是定格。维护与定格之别，是这一判的全部所系。20

睡前那一条，当初针对的是看完屏幕后睡不着，先照基础层的照料读。它是否也维护了何舟此后第一人称运行与修订的位置，还要看他能提出异议以后，安排是否留着重议与改变的通道。若把它标成「你长大了也得这样」，并以此挡住此后的重议，才是往定格那一边去的信号。

安排本身不是预占

安排本身，不因约束了孩子而成为预占。睡前不看屏幕约束了何舟，几点睡约束了他，冬天带外套也约束了他。约束是安排的一部分；读预占，看的不是约束有多紧，是那个位置是不是被封死、被填满。

这一节读到的殖民，是价值层的窄义：价值的来源与发生被占住。3

没有冲突，不作涵育的证据

一个家里多年没有为规矩吵过架，这一件不作涵育的证据。殖民的方向可以不经过冲突：说不的路被弄得难走或被锁住，冲突都可以不出现。21 何舟那一早说了三次「不」，那一晚问了「为什么不能看十分钟」：这些说出来的「不」，与改了的安排一起，才是这一篇读的东西。

第七节 与传统对接

卢梭：只依赖于物。 卢梭在《爱弥儿》第二卷里说，要让孩子只依赖于物，不依赖于人：他碰到的限制，来自事物的必然，不来自大人的意志。22 共同的问题，是孩子怎样面对约束而不陷入对人的服从与支配。卢梭把约束转到对物的依赖；这一篇则追问，人的安排能不能因孩子的拒绝而真实改变。分开的一处：卢梭让导师设计出整套「物的必然」，孩子碰到的约束看起来不来自人，实际上来自一个人的安排；这一篇读的正是安排由谁定、能不能被孩子改。照第三问，一项被安排成「物的必然」的约束，若不让孩子知道它可以改，就靠近预占的信号。

《论语》：几谏。 《论语·里仁》：「事父母几谏。见志不从，又敬不违，劳而不怨。」这一章，SAE 的《论语》解读已经注过，这里回指。23 这个系列只在一处对照：孩子那一端的「不」，在这一章里落作「几谏」，委婉地劝；父母不从，孩子「又敬不违」。这一篇读的是另一端：孩子的「不」，能不能真实地改变父母的安排。两处读的方向相反；这里只读结构，不评这一章。

史料：鲁迅《五猖会》。 鲁迅在《朝花夕拾》的《五猖会》一篇里，写他小时候盼着去看迎神赛会，临出门，父亲要他先把一段书背出来，背出来才准去；他背出来了，去了，那一天的会却已经没有了兴味。文末，成年的鲁迅写，他至今想起，还诧异父亲为什么要在那时候叫他背书。24 这里只读成年的鲁迅怎样写：他写下的，是一项安排挂在了一件孩子盼着的事上；成年作者写下的，仍是对这一安排的不解。这一篇不据此替当年的孩子读心，也不据此认证他父亲在哪个方向上。照第二问，现有叙述能读出背书与出门被绑在一起；至于当时孩子能不能问、问了能不能改，这一段没有给出足够言行，不在这里补写。

第八节 收束与下游

这一篇立了六条。合起来说的是：父母为孩子定的安排与家里的规矩，只读人与人之间那一面，读作父母的今天对孩子明天的安排；父母的今天签不了孩子明天的字。读一项安排照三问：解释权有没有随他成熟移交；他的拒绝、偏离与重构能不能真实地改变它；它被标作可修订的当下安排，还是未来不可反驳的最终内容。三问各问一件，是信号，读的是安排，不是人；改了安排不等于交了解释权，一句常见话不自动成为信号。解释权一域一域移交；外部固定、不可由个案事实反驳、跨事项统一使用的线有系统性错分的风险，可推翻、分事项、留复核的判断不在此列。那一声「不」，是否定首次指向他人的地方，一次「不」不独自认证；把它一律读成尚未懂事，是第二问所挡的；父母那一端保有自己的方向，不靠压掉孩子的否定来维持它，一次回应照言行读。事前的结构条件照父母这一端核，不先判层；做不到的那一处另照预占核；不冒称，也不预占。预占的信号不等于预占，读成预占另要位置被封死；保护睡眠先照基础层的照料读，不直接代证维护；维护与定格之别是这一判的全部所系；约束本身不是预占；没有冲突，不作涵育的证据。

这一篇不证明父母不该给孩子定规矩，不证明孩子的「不」都对，也不证明安排越少越好。

下一篇读的。 何舟后来说，他要去学一样父母都不太懂的东西，跟何川原先替他打算的不一样。这一回不是不穿一件外套：他说的是他自己要走的一条路。父母的安排若遇上的是他自己长出来的不得不，就是两条法相遇。

下游。 孩子自己的不得不长出来以后与父母的法相遇，14DD 上殖民的三种形态，冲突论的门槛，互凿，第五篇读。几个照料者之间定下的安排怎样逐人读，第六篇读。提出与回绝的位置随门自行归他本人，以及交出，第七篇读。
