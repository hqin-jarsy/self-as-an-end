How Is the Inter-Ontological Possible: The Subjective Conditions and Emergent Structure of Freedom
本体之间如何可能：自由的主体条件与涌现结构

Han Qin (秦汉) · Independent Researcher · 2026
DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.19297847 · Full PDF on Zenodo · CC BY 4.0
Abstract

How is the inter-ontological possible? The standard answer comes from institutional philosophy: design the right laws and rules so that different subjects can coexist peacefully within the same space. This paper offers a different answer: the inter-ontological is possible not because institutions maintain order from without, but because each subject is already a coexistence structure of multiple cannot-nots from within. The "between" does not first happen externally. It first happens internally.

Working from the Self-as-an-End (SAE) framework, this paper argues that freedom is not the space of choice granted by institutions, but the capacity to grow cannot-not — inner necessity — from within. A two-dimensional model is proposed: the base layer asks whether the subject's own cannot-not is established; the emergent layer asks whether the subject can empathize with and acknowledge the cannot-not of others. The paper introduces a three-criterion test for genuine versus pseudo cannot-not, names the mechanism of introjected colonization (where a pseudo cannot-not occupies the growth space of genuine ones), analyzes the transmission pathways of colonization and nurturing through institutional and relational layers, and engages in dialogue with Kant's autonomy ethics, Spinoza's freedom-as-necessity, and Deci & Ryan's Self-Determination Theory. Four non-trivial predictions are derived. Greta Thunberg and Alysa Liu serve as running case studies illustrating colonization and nurturing transmission modes.

1. The Problem: Why Freedom Is a Subjective Condition

1.1 Three Layers of Freedom Through Progressive Negation

Freedom is not doing what you want.

What looks like absolute freedom is, in substance, enslavement to biological impulse, dopamine cycles, and external stimulation. A person who "follows their heart" without ever examining what drives that heart is not exercising freedom — they are executing a set of instructions they never chose. In Self-Determination Theory (SDT), this corresponds to external regulation: behavior driven by sources outside the subject — rewards, punishments, social approval, market incentives. Remove these external contingencies and the behavior stops immediately. This is not freedom. This is the pseudo-freedom of libertinism.

Freedom is not refusing what you don't want.

This is the existentialist answer: existence precedes essence, and human freedom manifests in the refusal of externally imposed labels. It is one level above pseudo-freedom — it at least contains a negating subjective stance. But this freedom floats. It can dismantle every given meaning yet cannot construct a single one of its own. A subject who can only say "no" hovers in a vacuum of its own making. In SDT, this maps to the borderland between introjected regulation and amotivation — not doing something because doing it produces anxiety and guilt, or simply having lost all reasons for anything. This is not freedom. This is the suspended freedom of existentialism.

Freedom is doing what you cannot-not do — knowing what must be done and doing it with everything you have.

The "cannot-not" here is emphatically not coercion at gunpoint, nor the psychological pressure of guilt. It is necessity grounded in objective law or inner absolute imperative. Spinoza said freedom is the recognition of necessity. Kant said genuine freedom is the autonomy of the will — reason legislating for itself. The SAE framework goes further: when a subject establishes the self as an end in itself at the axiological level — not as an instrument for wealth, status, pleasure, or any other worldly derivative — then that sense of "cannot-not" ceases to be passive acceptance of external necessity and becomes the inner call of pure reason. In SDT, the closest correspondence is not the commonly cited identified regulation or intrinsic motivation, but integrated regulation — behavior fully integrated with the subject's core values, becoming part of "who the person is" (Ryan & Deci, 2017). But SAE's positioning is deeper than SDT's: SDT describes the conditions and effects of autonomous motivation; SAE asks about the ontological ground of that motivation's content — why this cannot-not and not another.

These three layers form a sequence of progressive negation: from being driven by desire (pseudo-freedom), to confirming existence through negation (suspended freedom), to acting from inner necessity (cannot-not). Each layer negates the one before it. Each is closer to the essence of freedom.

Terminological clarification: three levels of cannot-not. Cannot-not operates at three levels in this paper, clarified here once. The phenomenal level: the first-person experience of "I must do this" — a subjective report that proves nothing about its source. The structural level: a genuine cannot-not that has passed the three-criterion test (§3.1) and plurality verification (§3.2) — it coexists with other ends, persists when external contingencies are stripped away, and does not produce introjected collapse when interrupted. The ontological level: the action-necessity that emerges from the subject's self-establishment as an end — the ultimate ground of cannot-not and the point where SAE contributes beyond SDT (SDT describes the structural level's conditions and effects; SAE asks about the ontological level's source). The phenomenal level is available to everyone, including those under introjected colonization. The structural level requires verification. The ontological level is the philosophical foundation that makes the structural level possible.

1.2 Why Subjective Conditions, Not Institutional Conditions

Political philosophy's discussion of freedom typically unfolds along two dimensions. Negative liberty — following Berlin — concerns minimizing external interference: less government intrusion, fewer market restrictions, maximum unimpeded space. Positive liberty — rooted in Kant — concerns the capacity for self-legislation: whether a subject can be the genuine author of their own actions.

Negative liberty is an institutional question. Positive liberty is a subjective question. The core argument of this paper is: without cannot-not growing from within the subject, even the largest institutional freedom-space produces only libertinism or suspension, never genuine freedom. A person under zero institutional interference who has never developed inner action-necessity will either be paralyzed before infinite options or drift with the nearest dopamine signal.

But this does not mean institutions are irrelevant. Institutional freedom is necessary but not sufficient. Krieger & Sheldon's (2004, 2007) longitudinal study of law students showed that institutional culture can systematically suppress intrinsic motivation and well-being, shifting students' value orientation toward extrinsic markers — those who entered law school pursuing public service often graduated pursuing salary and ranking. Institutions are not neutral containers; they continuously shape the subject's motivational structure through the relational layer (mentors, peers, evaluation systems). Therefore, freedom analysis cannot stop at the institutional layer or the subjective layer — it must trace the complete transmission chain: institution → relationship → subject. The true transformation bottleneck is in the subject — but the subject is continuously being shaped by institutions through relationships.

1.3 Two Contemporary Cases

This paper uses two contemporary public figures as running case studies — not to judge their moral character, but to demonstrate the structural consequences of cannot-not's construction and absence in real life.

Greta Thunberg is a subject of enormous energy who entered the public sphere at fifteen through climate activism, rapidly becoming a symbolic figure for a global movement. Her energy and sincerity are beyond question; she is still young and still developing. This paper focuses not on her personal rightness or wrongness, but on a structural question: when a subject's direction of action is primarily supplied by external narratives (climate movement, political machinery, media demand), and the subject's own internal cannot-not is still under construction, what structural challenges arise? Her trajectory — from climate strikes to UN speeches to Gaza solidarity — presents a subject searching for an anchor across multiple external constructs, a process still underway. We diagnose, and we prescribe.

Alysa Liu presents a similar starting point but an opposite trajectory. Her father, Arthur Liu, was a fan of Michelle Kwan and enrolled her in skating from age five, investing heavily to mold her into the next world champion. At thirteen she became the youngest women's singles champion in U.S. Championship history. But after the 2022 Beijing Winter Olympics, at sixteen, she announced her retirement — exhausted from political pressure, isolation, and her father's demanding expectations. During two years of retirement, she worked at Starbucks, studied psychology at UCLA, went hiking and skiing. Then during a skiing trip she discovered she still loved movement, music, and dance, and decided to return to the ice. 2025 World Championship gold. 2026 Milan Olympic women's singles gold — becoming the first American woman to achieve a complete figure skating grand slam. Her father also completed a transformation: he committed to no longer intervening in her coaching or training decisions. This is not an unconditional success narrative — survivorship bias must be acknowledged — but a structural demonstration of the chisel-construct cycle from "forced stop" to "active reconstruction."

These two cases run throughout the paper.

2. The Two-Dimensional Structure: Base Layer and Emergent Layer

2.1 Establishing the Model

If the core of freedom is cannot-not, then inter-subjective freedom — "how is the inter-ontological possible" — requires an analytical structure that can simultaneously accommodate "my cannot-not" and "the other's cannot-not." This paper proposes a two-dimensional model:

Horizontal axis (base layer): whether the subject's own cannot-not is established. This is the "I am an end" dimension — whether the subject has grown at least one genuine action-necessity from within, and possesses the internal plurality needed to verify its authenticity.

Vertical axis (emergent layer): the capacity to empathize with and acknowledge others' cannot-not. This is the "the other is an end" dimension — whether the subject can acknowledge that the other's cannot-not is incommensurable with their own and not attempt to absorb or dissolve it. More precisely, the vertical axis measures not the abstract judgment "others matter" but the concrete capacity to "sustain the other's irreducibility."

The key structural claim: the vertical axis is not independent of the horizontal axis; it emerges from it. A subject whose own cannot-not is not yet established cannot genuinely acknowledge another's cannot-not. They will either instrumentalize the other for their own goals (Q2) or dissolve themselves into the other's goals (Q4) — neither constitutes genuine acknowledgment. Only a subject who is already-an-end can see another end without attempting to absorb it.

This is the paper's main theorem: the inter-ontological is not primitively given but emergently produced. It becomes possible only when a self that has become an end acquires enough internal plurality to recognize another irreducible end.

The theorem's jurisdiction. What this theorem explains is how acknowledgment is possible — how one subject can come to take another as an irreducible end — not coexistence in general. Two subjects can coexist for a long time with no acknowledgment whatsoever: instrumentalizing each other, attaching to each other, or avoiding each other while intersections accumulate all the same. Those structures persist too, sometimes quite stably. The theorem does not say they will not occur; it says they are not acknowledgment. The stretch in which acknowledgment has not occurred and coexistence must proceed regardless lies outside this paper's range, and this paper asserts nothing there.

2.2 Four-Quadrant Analysis

This two-dimensional structure generates four quadrants, but they are not symmetrical geometric regions. Because the vertical axis emerges from the horizontal, only one quadrant is genuinely high on both axes; the other three are different forms of deficit.

Q1 (genuinely high-high): Cooperation between two self-sufficient subjects. Both have their own cannot-not; both can acknowledge that the other's cannot-not is incommensurable with theirs. This is the only relational structure that requires neither suppression, surrender, nor dissolution to sustain itself. When two Q1 subjects' cannot-nots conflict, they engage in mutual chiseling — each re-examining their own construct in light of the other's frame of reference — until they find consensus or mutually acknowledge incommensurability. If neither works, both have the capacity to walk away.

Q2 (pseudo-high horizontal): Subjectivity inflation. Appears to be "I am an end," but is actually not genuine self-as-end — rather, it is the universalization of a single cannot-not, imposing an individualized action-necessity as a law everyone should obey. Colonizers, enlightenment elites, and missionaries of "I'm here to save you" often occupy this position. They have energy, sincerity, and a powerful experience of cannot-not — but because they lack internal plural reference frames, they erroneously elevate their personal ethics into universal moral commands. This is not a moral character defect but a structural one.

Q4 (pseudo-high vertical): Subjectivity surrender. Appears to be "the other is an end," but is actually not genuine acknowledgment of the other's cannot-not — rather, it is post-self-dissolution proxy attachment (borrowed telos). The self has not stood up; the apparent "other-high" is merely role-based surrender — treating some external entity (nation, leader, movement, company) as the reason for one's existence. They cannot distinguish between "the other's cannot-not" and "the other's co-optation of me."

Q3 (double-low): Bilateral subjectivity collapse. Nothing is worth doing; no one is worth caring about. The floor of nihilism. Existentialism that has not found its way forward — stuck at suspended freedom — eventually slides here.

Quadrant asymmetry is the key to understanding this model. The four quadrants are not a symmetrical taxonomy but an asymmetric topology organized by the emergence relation. Q1 is the only genuinely high-high quadrant. Q2 and Q4 are two pseudo-high states (one faking horizontal height, one faking vertical height). Q3 is the collapse floor. Genuine vertical height is possible only after the horizontal axis has genuinely stood up.

2.3 The Emergence Mechanism: Why the Vertical Emerges from the Horizontal

The claim that the vertical axis emerges from the horizontal requires a mechanistic explanation, not mere assertion. The key lies in a commonly overlooked distinction: empathy and acceptance are two entirely different operations, and the horizontal axis determines which one a subject performs when facing another's cannot-not.

Empathy: I have my own cannot-not. I see that you have a different one. I know from internal experience what "must" feels like, so I can understand yours, even if your direction is entirely different from mine. This requires a precondition: I must first possess the experience of cannot-not in order to recognize the same structure in someone else.

Acceptance: I have no cannot-not of my own. You tell me what your must is. I have no reference frame to judge whether it is genuine or implanted, whether it is yours or was installed in you. I can only take it at face value. This is not empathy — it is the precondition for being colonized, because a person who has never experienced "must" themselves cannot even formulate the question "is this really your must?"

Therefore, the horizontal axis is not merely the vertical axis's "prerequisite" — it is the watershed between the vertical axis's two directions: with a horizontal axis, the vertical emerges as empathy (Q1 direction); without one, the vertical tends to collapse into acceptance (Q4 direction). A person who has not yet experienced what "must" feels like will, when facing another's cannot-not, struggle to spontaneously generate acknowledgment and respect — their more likely response is compliance or following. Q4's core problem is not "too much empathy" but that the capacity for empathy has not yet obtained the structural conditions for growth.

This explains why emergence flows from horizontal to vertical, not the reverse. A person who already has their own cannot-not — especially one with the internal tension and coordination experience of multiple cannot-nots (philosophy competing with entrepreneurship for time, mathematics competing with literary research for attention) — has already practiced internally what it feels like when "multiple things are all real, all important, but not fully commensurable." When they encounter another person's cannot-not, they have a ready-made processing framework because they handle the same kind of problem every day inside themselves.

Conversely, a subject who has not recognized that they have multiple cannot-nots — or who has suppressed their other cannot-nots — has no internal tension, no coordination experience. Their world is monophonic. When they encounter a different cannot-not, they have no processing framework, only two responses: "you should be like me" (Q2 slide) or "okay, I'll follow you" (Q4 slide). The former is subjectivity inflation; the latter is subjectivity surrender. Neither is empathy.

Therefore, emergence is not the product of moral education ("you should respect others"), nor a conclusion of rational deduction ("universalizability requires you to acknowledge the other's finality"). It is a structural byproduct of internal plurality. The precondition for empathy is not kindness but complexity.

2.4 The Symbiotic Structure of Q2 and Q4

Q2 and Q4 are not two independent personality types but two ends of a symbiotic structure. Colonizers need the colonized; leaders need followers; "I'm here to save you" needs someone "waiting to be saved." Without Q4's surrender, Q2's inflation has nowhere to land; without Q2's conscription, Q4's surrender has nothing to attach to. They need each other, produce each other, sustain each other.

Stated more precisely: the Q2–Q4 symbiosis is a self-sustaining structure without acknowledgment. It is not a transitional state on the way to Q1, nor a failed Q1; it is a solution in its own right, each end drawing from the other the half it lacks, and it therefore cannot be expected to dissolve from internal discomfort. It is worth naming separately because it is the specimen case of coexistence without acknowledgment: the main theorem explains how acknowledgment becomes possible, while this quadrant shows the structure running on perfectly well when acknowledgment does not occur.

Q1 is the existential threat to both. Q1's premise — that every subject possesses an irreducible finality that cannot be absorbed — simultaneously cancels Q2's justification for conquest ("you cannot universalize your cannot-not") and Q4's justification for surrender ("you cannot treat someone else's cannot-not as your reason for existing"). This is why Q2 and Q4 often politically unite against Q1: not because they think Q1 is wrong, but because Q1's very existence means their entire game is invalid.

3. Domain-Specific Distinctions: The Authenticity Problem of Cannot-Not

3.1 Introjected Colonization: Being Colonized by Cannot-Not Itself

The moment cannot-not is proposed as a concept, it faces its sharpest challenge: how do you know your "must" is genuine?

This is not a rhetorical question. A teenager burning everything for climate activism, a father pouring all resources into his child's championship dream, an entrepreneur working seven days a week for a company mission — they all have a powerful "must" experience, enormous energy, and deep sincerity. But the intensity of an experience cannot prove the source of an experience. An externally installed "must" and an internally grown "must" can feel exactly the same from the inside.

This paper names this condition introjected colonization: an externally installed "must" that has turned around to define the subject. Cannot-not has become the master; the subject has become its executor.

Introjected colonization is far more dangerous than external control. External control is at least transparent — someone points a gun at your head, you know you are not free, you retain the awareness of resistance. Introjected colonization is invisible — the gun has been internalized as your own voice. You think that "must" is you. You may even take pride in it, because it looks exactly like a product of free will.

SDT's empirical research provides an evidence base for this distinction. Howard et al.'s (2021) meta-analysis found that introjected regulation can indeed predict persistence and performance-goal pursuit — meaning a person under introjected colonization does keep going, and may even perform well. But simultaneously, introjected regulation also rises alongside anxiety, psychological depletion, and other ill-being indicators. Persistence is not a sufficient condition for freedom.

In SDT's framework, the construct closest to cannot-not is not the commonly cited identified regulation or intrinsic motivation but integrated regulation — behavior fully integrated with the subject's core values, becoming part of "who the person is," and not conflicting with the subject's other values and needs (Ryan & Deci, 2017). This qualifier — "not conflicting with other values" — points directly to the key for distinguishing genuine from pseudo cannot-not.

Three criteria for genuine cannot-not:

First, does it persist after external contingencies are removed? Strip away rewards, punishments, social approval, and identity labels. Does the "must" still exist? Alysa Liu, after two years of retirement with no competitions, no audiences, and no paternal expectations, discovered during a skiing trip that she still loved movement and music — that is the test with all external contingencies removed. If Greta were to leave climate activism, leave the media spotlight, leave the "voice of a generation" label — would she still have a clear cannot-not? Her trajectory across multiple causes suggests the answer may be uncertain.

Second, can it coexist with other important ends without consuming them all? Genuine cannot-not allows other cannot-nots to exist alongside it; pseudo cannot-not tends to devour everything. A person who genuinely loves mathematics will not deny the value of music because of mathematics; but a person colonized by the identity "math genius" may treat all non-math time as waste. The test: does this cannot-not enrich your life, or narrow it?

Third, does temporary interruption produce a purely introjected shame/anxiety collapse? When a genuine cannot-not is interrupted, it produces regret and a drive to return — "I want to go back to doing this." When a pseudo cannot-not is interrupted, it produces identity collapse and existential anxiety — "without this I don't know who I am." Alysa Liu after retirement went to work at Starbucks, attended university, traveled — no collapse. She found her return motivation in calm. If retirement had immediately triggered identity crisis and existential anxiety, the "must" would need examination.

These three criteria transform introjected colonization from an intuitive label into an operational diagnostic.

3.2 Plurality: Verification Mechanism, Empathy Prerequisite, Decidability Condition

The three-criterion test provides case-by-case tools. But a more fundamental question remains: if a person has only one cannot-not, how do they conduct this test?

The answer: they cannot.

A single cannot-not is in principle undecidable as to its authenticity. Not that it is necessarily false — but that the question of true-or-false has no answer under single conditions, not even for the subject themselves. Because their entire self is bound up in this one cannot-not, chiseling it away equals chiseling away the self, so they dare not chisel, will not chisel, and may not even realize they need to chisel. They have no reference frame.

Plurality solves this reference-frame problem. When a person has multiple independently sourced cannot-nots — philosopher and mathematician and entrepreneur, for instance — these cannot-nots automatically generate friction and tension among themselves. An inconsistency between a pseudo cannot-not and other genuine ones is itself the signal. Plurality needs no external judge; it is an automatically running internal verification system.

But a critical distinction: pluralization is not projection-pluralization. Greta's multi-issue engagement — climate, Palestine, Armenia, LGBT — appears to span multiple domains, but these are not multiple independent cannot-nots. They are projections of a single external construct ("I am the voice of justice") onto different issues. Genuine pluralization is not one cannot-not projected onto multiple surfaces but multiple independently sourced, non-mutually-consumable cannot-nots coexisting. The test: if you remove one, do the others still stand independently?

Plurality serves three functions in this paper. First, it is the verification mechanism for cannot-not's authenticity. Second, it is the structural prerequisite for inter-subjective empathy — §2.3 has argued that internal plurality experience is the necessary condition for empathizing with another's cannot-not. Third, and most strongly: plurality is the necessary condition for cannot-not's decidability. Without plurality, the subject cannot even formulate the question "is my must genuine?"

Sport psychology's empirical data supports this judgment. Lochbaum et al.'s (2022) systematic review showed that athletic identity is a double-edged sword — correlated with both positive engagement and vulnerability to negative affect upon role loss. The critical variable is not identity strength but identity breadth. Haslam et al. (2021) further found that multiple social identities and identity-based resources predicted better retirement adjustment, suggesting that identity breadth can buffer the impact of losing a single role.

The classic "single cannot-not" cases — Van Gogh, Ramanujan — do not survive close inspection. Van Gogh was not only about painting: his relationship with Gauguin was a cannot-not collision disaster site (the cannot-not for deep interpersonal connection), and his early mission to the mining districts was a religious-salvational cannot-not. His problem was not too few cannot-nots but insufficient coordination capacity among multiple ones — every collision was destructive. Ramanujan was not only about mathematics: his Brahmin vegetarianism and devotion to the goddess Namagiri were a cannot-not independent of mathematics. His physical collapse at Cambridge was precisely the battlefield of two cannot-nots in conflict — mathematics brought him to an environment that could not satisfy his faith-based cannot-not.

Truly single-cannot-not individuals may not exist. Apparent singularity is usually the result of other cannot-nots being suppressed or unacknowledged. The question was never "do you have multiple cannot-nots" but "are you aware of having multiple, and capable of coordinating them?"

3.3 The Hardest Cut: Rupture as Bootstrap Condition

The plurality verification mechanism faces a bootstrap problem: if a subject is currently occupied by a single pseudo cannot-not that tends to consume all time and attention, how does a second independent cannot-not emerge to initiate verification?

Alysa Liu's case provides the answer: the second cannot-not did not grow while the first construct was still fully operating. It grew after the first construct was interrupted by external events — retirement created a vacuum — and only then was there space for new growth. This suggests an important dynamic mechanism: the transition from singularity to plurality typically requires a rupture. Rupture can be externally triggered (injury, retirement, job loss, relationship breakdown, institutional change) or internally accumulated to threshold (burnout, breakdown, existential crisis). Rupture itself is not chiseling — it carries no direction; it merely opens a space. Chiseling is the active examination and selection that happens within that space.

This means one key function of nurturing is: to preserve sufficient blank space before rupture occurs, so that seeds of other cannot-nots have an opportunity to sprout. The value of Alysa Liu's post-retirement activities — working at Starbucks, studying at UCLA, hiking and skiing — lay not in the activities themselves but in the fact that they created time and attention not filled by a single construct. A child completely consumed by exam preparation has no such space; an athlete completely filled by training has no such space. One institutional condition for nurturing is not handing over the subject's entire time and attention to a single institutional goal.

The hardest object to chisel is not what was externally imposed — those can at least be identified as "foreign." The hardest is what you believe to be your own but was actually installed. Alysa Liu's two years of retirement were precisely this cut. What she chiseled away was not a bad thing but something that looked like herself — "become the next Michelle Kwan." This construct was father-supplied, installed from age five, fully internalized after eleven years of training and competition. Chiseling it required not courage (though courage was also needed) but first having somewhere else to stand.

Her father Arthur Liu also completed a parallel chisel — from the "Kwan fan raising the next Kwan" position to committing to non-intervention in his daughter's coaching and training. This was not indifference but a return of control to the subject — transforming from a colonization transmission node to a nurturing transmission node.

Similar "withdrawal-exploration-return" trajectories appear in other elite athletes. Simone Biles withdrew from the Tokyo 2020 all-around final and later returned to elite competition after a period of reorientation. Naomi Osaka stepped away from major press obligations and competition in 2021, later returning with a different stance. These cases are viewed in sport psychology as positive signals related to identity broadening and motivational restructuring, though survivorship bias must be acknowledged: high-profile successful returns are over-reported while more numerous withdrawal-without-return cases remain invisible.

Note that projection-pluralization testing (§3.2) should not be conducted during the acute period following rupture. Short-term reactions to removing a core construct may be traumatic collapse regardless of whether the construct was genuine or pseudo — even a genuinely grown cannot-not produces strong grief when suddenly interrupted. The third criterion (interruption response pattern) achieves diagnostic validity only after the subject has passed the acute phase and entered a stable period.

4. Colonization and Nurturing: Negative and Positive Transmission

4.1 The Colonization Transmission Pathway

Institutions never directly tell an individual: "hand over your subjectivity." Their operation is more precise: colonization is accomplished through the relational layer's trust interfaces.

Relational transmission nodes — parents, mentors, romantic partners, closest friends — influence individuals without passing through rational review, entering the subject's construct directly. A fifteen-year-old child does not run the three-criterion cannot-not test on her father. She trusts him; his expectations naturally become her "must." If this transmission node has itself been captured by institutional logic — whether intentionally (father deliberately cultivating a Kwan successor) or unintentionally (father genuinely believing this is the best arrangement for his daughter) — then the individual's cannot-not is replaced at the source, and the individual has no awareness of this whatsoever.

Greta Thunberg's case illustrates how this transmission pathway operates. The initial transmission node was her father — public reporting indicates he played an important supporting role in her entry into public visibility. But the transmission chain extends further: the media machine needs a young, high-impact face for climate activism; the NGO network needs a symbolic figure to mobilize the public; political figures need moral high ground to adorn their agendas. Each institutional layer exerts influence through relational-layer contact points (movement organizers, politicians, journalists). From the SAE perspective, what requires attention here is not whether her personal choices are correct, but a structural risk: when multiple institutional layers simultaneously transmit demands through the relational layer toward a young person still constructing her subjectivity, the growth space for her cannot-not may be compressed.

The cruelest feature of this transmission pathway: the relational layer's trust structure is precisely the institutional layer's most efficient channel for co-opting individuals. The institution does not need direct contact with the individual — it only needs to capture a key node in the individual's trust network. Constructs installed through that node will not only not be resisted; they will be worn with pride.

SDT's empirical research extensively documents this transmission mechanism. Nicaise et al. (2025) found that elite adolescent athletes perceiving higher parental pressure and directive behaviors reported higher burnout and more controlled motivation. Aunola et al. (2018) explicitly flagged parental psychological control as a risk factor for adolescent athletes' burnout symptoms.

The same transmission pattern appears in non-sport domains. Krieger & Sheldon's (2004, 2007) longitudinal tracking of American law students showed that law school's institutional culture (competitive ranking, hierarchy, extrinsic achievement orientation) transmitted through student-faculty relationships and peer evaluation systems to individual students, causing their intrinsic motivation, public-service value orientation, and subjective well-being to decline significantly after enrollment, while extrinsic value orientation (salary, prestige) rose significantly. The institution commanded no one to abandon their ideals, but through every micro-interaction in the relational layer, it reshaped students' motivational structure.

4.2 The Nurturing Transmission Pathway

Nurturing is not the opposite of colonization — it is not installing a "better" cannot-not to replace a "worse" one. Nurturing is making space for cannot-not to grow from within.

A critical distinction: support is not permissiveness. SDT literature clearly shows that autonomy support comprises three elements: choice provision, meaningful rationale provision, and structure with boundaries. A completely permissive environment — "do whatever you want" — is not autonomy support; it is throwing the individual into the first-layer space of pseudo-freedom. Genuine nurturing provides structure while not predetermining structure's content.

Alysa Liu's father demonstrated this transition in his later approach. He shifted from "tiger dad" mode (selecting coaches, setting training plans, defining "Kwan successor" goals) to committing to non-intervention in his daughter's coaching and training. This was not indifference — he was still supportive, still present — but control was returned to the subject. He did not say "you should return to skating" or "you should not return to skating." He said "this is your decision."

Su & Reeve's (2011) meta-analysis quantified the overall effect of autonomy-support intervention programs, reporting a weighted effect size of approximately 0.63 — indicating that autonomy-supportive behaviors can be reliably increased through training. Steingut et al.'s (2017) meta-analysis showed that providing meaningful rationales tends to enhance autonomous motivation and engagement, with effects depending on whether the rationale is autonomy-supportive or controlling — confirming once more that "how you give" matters more than "what you give."

Nurturing transmission can run from the institutional layer downward. The 2025 Nordic five-country teacher study found that the more teachers perceived their principals as autonomy-supportive, the more they adopted autonomy-supportive classroom practices. The transmission belt is not exclusively a colonization conduit — it can also run positively.

But a trap that must be confronted: not all "autonomy support" constitutes nurturing. Google's "20% time" policy is a classic case. But this "autonomy" carries an implicit condition: you should ideally use this free time to produce something the company can commercialize. Gmail and AdSense were indeed born from 20% time — exactly the output the institutional layer desired. This is not nurturing. This is instrumental autonomy support — Q2 wearing Q1's clothing.

The distinguishing criterion: instrumental autonomy support carries an implicit output expectation ("I give you freedom, but you'd better use that freedom to produce what I want"); ontological autonomy support carries no such condition ("I give you space; whatever grows is yours"). Alysa Liu's father in his later period did the latter: committing to non-intervention without presupposing whether she would return to skating or do something else entirely.

An important limitation: ontological autonomy support, strictly speaking, exists only at the relational layer. Every institution has built-in maintenance and production purposes — schools exist for educational metrics, companies for profit, sports teams for results. Institutional autonomy support necessarily carries an instrumental undertone: it hopes that whatever grows in the freedom-space will not ultimately violate the institution's survival baseline. If a teacher, under the principal's "support," develops a cannot-not to "dismantle the existing school system," the institution will inevitably activate boundary mechanisms. Therefore, the institutional layer's autonomy support is more accurately positioned as: minimizing interference with the subject's cannot-not growth within the bounds of institutional survival. It can manage to not colonize, but it cannot achieve pure nurturing. Pure ontological autonomy support — "whatever grows is yours" — is possible only in the relational layer: intimate relationships, genuine mentorships.

4.3 The Asymmetry Between Colonization and Nurturing

Colonization is easy; nurturing is hard. This is not accidental but structural.

Installing a construct is far faster than waiting for one to grow from within. A controlling father can set a "world champion" goal for his child at age five and immediately launch an action plan. A nurturing father must wait — wait for the child to discover interests on their own, encounter setbacks on their own, grow new directions from setbacks on their own. The former can report progress monthly; the latter cannot report anything, because "currently waiting" is not a KPI any institutional layer will accept.

Institutions naturally favor colonization because external regulation is observable, quantifiable, and standardizable. You can measure test scores, GDP growth, athletes' competition results and world rankings. Autonomous motivation is internal — you cannot externally measure whether a person's cannot-not is genuine or pseudo, or even whether it exists. A person with strong autonomous motivation and a person deeply under introjected colonization can look exactly the same from outside: both work hard, both persist, both produce results. The institutional layer cannot distinguish them, and does not need to — it wants output.

This explains a paradox: SDT has decades of empirical foundation, with cross-cultural, cross-domain data consistently showing autonomous motivation outperforms controlled motivation, yet SDT has achieved almost no penetration in educational policy or organizational management implementation. Not because no one knows — but because the institutional layer's operating system is built on external regulation, and SDT's conclusions amount to asking institutions to operate on themselves.

5. Theoretical Positioning: Dialogue with Existing Frameworks

5.1 Dialogue with Kant

The SAE framework shares a core equation with Kantian ethics: freedom = autonomy. Cannot-not corresponds to the unconditionality of the categorical imperative — acting from duty (aus Pflicht), not merely in accordance with duty (pflichtmäßig). A person acting from duty does so not because the action brings good results, but because reason itself demands it. This is the Kantian formulation of cannot-not.

But SAE and Kant diverge in three fundamental ways.

Divergence one: at the point where duties conflict, Kant leaves the remainder no place. His system demands that moral laws hold universally without exception. When two equally valid moral imperatives collide head-on — the classic dilemma of "cannot lie" versus "protect the innocent" — Kant's framework either falls silent or produces counterintuitive conclusions (such as his famous judgment that one cannot lie to a murderer). SAE acknowledges that conflict cannot be fully dissolved and uses the remainder argument (§5.4) to handle this irreducibility.

This divergence is narrowed to the point where duties conflict; it is not the claim that Kant's system contains no shortfall anywhere. At the postulates of practical reason (immortality of the soul, the existence of God) he marked out, by his own hand, a debt that theoretical reason cannot discharge — that is a shortfall he declares himself, and it is not what this divergence is about. What this divergence asserts is one thing only: when two equally valid duties collide head-on, his framework has no place in which to register the collision itself.

Divergence two: Kant's "humanity as end" formula points toward others; SAE reverses it toward the self. Kant's second formulation — "act so that you treat humanity, whether in your own person or in that of another, always as an end and never merely as a means" — is mainly used in practice to constrain how you treat others. SAE's reversal: before you can treat others as ends, you must first establish yourself as an end. Self-as-end is not selfishness but the structural prerequisite for empathizing with the other (the emergence argument of §2.3).

Divergence three: universality versus individuality. This is the deepest divergence. The categorical imperative demands universalizability — "your maxim must be capable of becoming a universal law of nature." This means that if your cannot-not cannot be simultaneously adopted by all rational beings without self-contradiction, it is not a genuine moral imperative. But SAE's cannot-not is inherently individualized. Alysa Liu's cannot-not (skating) absolutely does not apply to everyone; a philosopher's cannot-not (investigating the conditions of existence) does not apply to everyone either. This is not SAE's weakness but its greater honesty.

Kant attempted to use universal reason to eliminate conflict — if everyone's moral maxims could be universalized, they would not logically conflict. But reality is: two entirely legitimate, non-universalizable individual cannot-nots do collide. SAE acknowledges the incommensurability of individual cannot-nots. Precisely because they are incommensurable, mutual chiseling, walk-away, and ultimately external arbitration are needed to handle conflict; and the remainder argument is needed to acknowledge what cannot be dissolved. Universality is Kant's strategy for avoiding the remainder; individuality is SAE's premise for confronting it.

5.2 Dialogue with Spinoza

Spinoza said "freedom is the recognition of necessity." A person who understands the natural causal chain no longer blindly struggles against it, and thereby gains a special kind of freedom — not freedom from chains, but freedom through understanding chains.

SAE and Spinoza agree: freedom is not the absence of external constraint but the correct relationship with necessity. But SAE continues where Spinoza stopped. Spinoza solved the epistemological problem: understanding necessity, thereby no longer blindly resisting. SAE asks the axiological problem: after understanding necessity, why do you act?

Recognizing necessity does not equal freedom of action. A person who perfectly understands the laws of physics does not thereby acquire a direction for action. They know "this is how the world works" but not "what should I do." Between recognition and action lies a leap — the subject's value-affirmation. SAE's answer: when the subject establishes the self as an end in itself, rather than an instrument for any external goal, the direction of action emerges from inner necessity. Spinoza tells you what freedom is (recognition of necessity); SAE tells you where freedom comes from (the subject's self-finalization) and how freedom guides action (cannot-not).

5.3 Dialogue with Self-Determination Theory

SAE and SDT are not opposed but hierarchically complementary.

SDT's motivation continuum — from external regulation through introjected regulation through identified regulation through integrated regulation to intrinsic motivation — maps well onto SAE's three layers of freedom. Pseudo-freedom corresponds to external regulation; suspended freedom to the borderland of introjected regulation and amotivation; cannot-not to integrated regulation and intrinsic motivation. SDT's "self-concordant goals" concept — goals aligned with developing interests and values that predict sustained effort, attainment, and longitudinal well-being (Sheldon & Elliot, 1999) — is the closest empirical operationalization of cannot-not.

SAE's incremental contribution to SDT: SDT describes the conditions of autonomous motivation (three basic needs: autonomy, competence, relatedness) and its effects (higher well-being, performance quality, persistence), but does not answer the question of motivational content's ontological source. Why is this person's cannot-not philosophy while that person's is skating? SDT cannot answer this because its analytical framework concerns the form of motivation (autonomous versus controlled), not its content (what to do). SAE fills this gap: motivational content emerges from the subject's self-establishment as an end — when you stop treating yourself as an instrument for any external goal, your direction of action emerges from your internal structure as this unique being.

But this mapping requires three qualifications.

First, SAE's "pseudo-freedom" (doing what you want) cannot be simply equated with SDT's "external regulation." SDT's external regulation means "doing something because of external rewards/punishments," while SAE's pseudo-freedom critique includes a more covert condition: a person's preferences themselves may already be structured by external contingencies — they subjectively experience "this is what I want," but the content of that "wanting" was shaped by markets, media, and social expectations. SAE's analysis goes one layer deeper than SDT's external regulation concept because it questions not only the source of motivation but the content of desire itself.

Second, "refusal" is not uniformly introjected regulation or amotivation. SDT acknowledges that some refusals are highly autonomous — a person who has identified with their values refusing an immoral demand is an identified refusal, not suspended freedom. SAE's "suspended freedom" critique targets purely negating, non-constructive refusals, not all forms of refusal.

Third, intense identity commitment, if too narrow, becomes a source of vulnerability upon role loss. SDT and sport psychology literature both point to this. SAE's response: cannot-not must coexist with identity breadth — this is the function of plurality verification.

5.4 The Remainder Argument

When two cannot-nots meet, what happens?

Routing goes by the current conditions of the relation, not by the person. The question asked here is not whether the other is an SAE subject, with the route following from that answer. Two reasons. First, this paper offers — and intends to offer — no third-person test for determining which layer another person operates at; routing on a test that does not exist amounts to sorting people by a criterion nobody has. Second, what actually determines the route is not what the other is but which handling moves are available in this relation right now. Relational conditions change, and the reading is revised with them, whereas "is he an SAE subject" pins the person down once it is pronounced.

Routing therefore runs through three cells by relational condition, plus one further cell that is not a route but a handover.

One · The mutual process is available: mutual chiseling. When both parties can enter the same mutual process, what they do is not to suppress each other but to chisel mutually — not chiseling the other's cannot-not, but re-examining one's own construct in the other's frame of reference. This looks like debate; in substance it is each party conducting self-examination before the other's cannot-not. Two outcomes: either consensus (both chisel away some pseudo-constructs and find underlying compatibility) or mutual acknowledgment of incommensurability (each retains their own, in peaceful coexistence). A genuine SAE subject's self does not depend on the outcome of this particular conflict.

Two · The mutual process is unavailable but exit is available: walking away. Mutual chiseling presupposes that both parties enter the same process. Where that presupposition fails, staying to entangle is not bravery but a lack of purchase — I can go on chiseling my own construct, but the "mutual" half has no other side. Here exit is available, and walking away is itself an expression of freedom: my cannot-not does not need your validation to stand.

What matters is that this cell judges the availability of the process, not the other party's qualification. If mutual conditions are restored later, the route turns back to the first cell; if they lapse later, it turns to this one. Switching routes requires no re-determination of what the other is, and leaves behind no conclusion about them.

Three · Exit does not dissolve a persisting intersection: handed to institutions. Exit cannot always dissolve the shared problem. In entangled cases — shared children, shared institutions, scarce resources, shared responsibility toward third parties — exit itself may mean abandoning responsibility to third parties, or the exit channel may be institutionally blocked. This cell exceeds what an inter-subjective ethics can handle, and this paper hands it on: how a joint framework that respects subjecthood is to be derived from ontological first principles is the next paper's question.

A further cell that is not a route · handover when the way out is forcibly compressed. When one party's subject position or exit is forcibly compressed — not "we cannot talk this through," but actual suppression of physical safety, basic livelihood, or the exit channel — this is not a fourth route alongside the three above but a handover: protect first, then transfer to a procedure with the corresponding authority. Writing it as a parallel handling route would queue it behind mutual chiseling and walking away, and it is precisely the cell that cannot wait. Protection may precede any mutual handling.

External arbitration: three possible grounds, not three sufficient conditions. Within the third cell, the SAE subject's recourse is not violence but external arbitration — law, institutions, third-party adjudication. Ceding adjudicative authority to an external body is not a failure of subjectivity but an awareness of subjectivity's boundaries: I acknowledge that this conflict exceeds what inter-subjective mutual chiseling can resolve and requires an external framework to handle it.

But "external" does not mean any external body whatever. The claim holds only for a certain class: procedures both parties have already acknowledged; mechanisms with lawful authority; or institutions with legitimate standing to protect basic rights. These three are possible grounds, not an any-one-of-three that automatically confers full authority — a mechanism meeting one of them must still be examined for whether it holds applicable authority in this particular matter. The converse must be written just as precisely: that a third party was nominated by one side does not by itself prove it has been captured by that side; what must be examined is whether it still acts solely on that side's will, and whether it holds independent procedure and applicable authority. A third party unilaterally selected by the stronger side and acting solely on that side's will is an extension of that side's power; handing the dispute to it is not a transfer out but the same thing continuing under another name.

What is ceded must also be written precisely. What is ceded is procedural adjudicative authority over disputes about action, not constitutive authority over one's own law. Arbitration may determine who uses which resource and when, which action may be carried out, and how procedures are arranged; it cannot determine which law is truer in the first person, nor whose standing as a subject is higher.

The remainder structure. This produces the paper's remainder structure: a framework whose purpose is subjective autonomy — SAE's entire ethical stance rests on "each subject establishing the self as an end" — has as its logical remainder the ceding of adjudicative authority to external bodies. This is not a framework failure but the cost of completeness. A fully coherent inter-subjective ethics, when encountering entangled situations, must acknowledge that its jurisdiction has boundaries and must interface with the institutional layer's arbitration function.

The remainder is ineliminable, and this paper assigns it no magnitude. The remainder is theoretically ineliminable: entangled situations necessarily exist in reality. The range this paper argues for carries an explicit antecedent — when both parties are genuine SAE subjects — under which the mutual process is available and mutual chiseling and walking away each have their place; for the stretch that does not satisfy that antecedent, neither handling applies, and it falls to the third cell.

This paper does not adjudicate how much any one cell accounts for. Frequency judgments are not what the argument here requires: so long as there exists a class of situations in which exit does not dissolve the shared problem, the institutional question already stands, and whether that cell is rare has no bearing on whether it stands. Nor is the remainder said to be "practically minimized" — that applies the language of measure to the remainder itself, and the remainder has no measure, hence no total, hence nothing that could be made small.

There is one structural isomorphism here with SAE's mathematical parallel project ZFCρ, and the isomorphism stops there: a formal system can prove that some residual term necessarily exists, and proving its existence does not abolish it. The claim in ZFCρ that "ρ's actual value can be pushed arbitrarily small" is a conclusion internal to a formal system about a quantity that has a definition; this paper's remainder has no such quantity, and that latter half does not extrapolate to the inter-subjective remainder.

6. Non-Trivial Predictions

This paper's two-dimensional model is not merely a descriptive taxonomy; it generates four testable non-trivial predictions. "Non-trivial" means these predictions are not direct restatements of the framework but conclusions derived from it that would not be expected without the framework.

6.1 Base → Emergent (Positive): Internal Plurality Predicts Empathy Capacity

Prediction: Individuals possessing multiple independent, non-mutually-consumable cannot-nots will more readily empathize with others' cannot-nots than those with a single cannot-not.

Non-triviality: empathy capacity is typically attributed to personality traits (kindness, sympathy) or moral education ("you should respect others"). This paper predicts that empathy is not a product of moral education but a structural byproduct of internal plurality. Adjacent literature in social identity complexity research finds that low-complexity individuals (multiple identities highly overlapping) show less tolerance toward outgroups, consistent with this prediction's direction.

Testable corollary: individuals with multiple independent identity domains (versus single-domain identity) display higher perspective-taking in interpersonal conflict scenarios.

6.2 Base → Emergent (Negative): Singular Cannot-Not Predicts Moral Absolutization

Prediction: Individuals whose cannot-not is highly concentrated in a single domain tend to universalize their personal ethical imperative into a universal moral command.

Non-triviality: the most sincere, most energetic subjects are precisely the most likely to universalize their own imperative — not from malice but from lacking a plural internal reference frame. They have never experienced what it feels like for "two things to both be real but mutually incompatible," so they assume their cannot-not applies to everyone. This is a structural characteristic, not a character defect.

Testable corollary: individuals with identity highly concentrated in a single domain display lower value-pluralism tolerance in moral judgment tasks. Legault et al. (2011) provide adjacent evidence: controlling-framed antiprejudice messages can backfire and increase prejudice — pointing toward "externally moralizing interventions trigger defensiveness."

6.3 Emergent → Base (Positive): Empathy as Self-Chiseling Tool

Prediction: Individuals who have been genuinely moved by another's cannot-not more readily identify pseudo cannot-nots within themselves.

Non-triviality: empathy is typically viewed as an outward relational capacity. This prediction identifies an inward feedback effect. When you see another person's cannot-not — entirely different from yours but equally genuine — your reference frame opens. You gain the ability to examine which of your own cannot-nots were installed, because you now have an external reference for "what genuine cannot-not looks like."

Testable corollary: individuals who have experienced deep cross-cultural or cross-domain relationships more easily distinguish introjected motivation from identified motivation in self-reflection tasks. This is the most original of the four predictions and the one with the thinnest empirical bridge — flagged as the highest-risk prediction.

The positive feedback loop between 6.1 and 6.3. Predictions 6.1 (internal plurality → empathy capacity) and 6.3 (empathy experience → pseudo cannot-not identification) are not two independent linear relationships. They constitute a positive feedback loop: internal plurality opens the possibility of empathy, and deep empathy experience — being genuinely moved by another person's genuine cannot-not — serves as an external reference frame that accelerates the subject's internal chiseling process, helping identify and strip away pseudo-constructs. After stripping pseudo-constructs, the internal genuine cannot-nots become clearer, plurality quality improves, and empathy capacity further strengthens. This positive feedback loop means: Q1 is not a static terminal state but a self-reinforcing dynamic process — the freer you are, the more you can empathize; the more you empathize, the more you can chisel away pseudo-freedom.

6.4 Emergent → Base (Negative): Unanchored Empathy Leads to Subjectivity Surrender

Prediction: Individuals high in empathy but low in self-differentiation, when persistently facing others' cannot-nots, are more likely to lose their own cannot-not.

Non-triviality: empathy is universally considered a positive trait. This prediction identifies that empathy, when it loses base-layer anchoring, becomes another form of Q4. A person who over-empathizes continually internalizes others' cannot-nots as their own responsibility, eventually losing their own cannot-not and becoming a server for others' finality.

But a precision is needed: not all "empathy" carries this risk. SDT-related research (Weinstein & Ryan, 2010) shows that autonomously motivated helping benefits both helper and recipient. What truly constitutes risk is not perspective-taking (cognitively understanding others' situations) but self-oriented empathic distress — experiencing others' pain in a self-involved manner without self-differentiation. Therefore, 6.4's more precise formulation: empathy that is self-involved and lacking self-differentiation erodes the base layer.

Testable corollary: individuals with high empathic traits but low self-differentiation display higher burnout and identity confusion in sustained helping contexts. The "over-involved parent" in sport psychology is a variant: parents who internalize their child's cannot-not as their own, ultimately depleting both.

7. Conclusion

7.1 Recovery

Freedom is not doing what you want — that is pseudo-freedom, enslaved by desire. Freedom is not refusing what you don't want — that is suspended freedom, hovering in negation. Freedom is doing what you cannot-not do — knowing what must be done and doing it with everything you have.

But a person's cannot-not is a genuinely free expression only when it was not installed, is not singular, and does not consume everything. Introjected colonization — being defined by a "must" that looks like your own — is the most covert and most pervasive form of unfreedom.

The inter-ontological is possible not because institutions maintain order from without, but because a self that has become an end, having acquired sufficient internal plurality, gains the capacity to recognize another irreducible end. The "between" does not first happen externally. It first happens internally. The other as end becomes possible only in a subject that no longer universalizes itself.

7.2 Contributions

This paper makes four incremental contributions to existing discussions.

First, an ontological supplement to SDT. SDT describes the conditions and effects of autonomous motivation but does not answer the question of motivational content's ontological source. SAE answers: motivational content emerges from the subject's self-establishment as an end.

Second, a mechanism for determining cannot-not's authenticity. The three-criterion test (removing external contingencies, coexistence with other ends, interruption response pattern) provides case-by-case tools; plurality verification provides an automatic structural mechanism; plurality as the necessary condition for decidability closes the question of "can a single cannot-not prove itself" at the epistemological level.

Third, the naming and pathway analysis of introjected colonization. The mechanism by which a pseudo cannot-not occupies the growth space of genuine ones, and the pathway by which institutions colonize through the relational layer's trust interfaces, provide a structural explanation for "why a person with energy and sincerity may still not be free."

Fourth, the remainder argument. An ethical framework aimed at subjective autonomy has as its logical remainder the ceding of adjudicative authority to external arbitration when encountering entangled situations; that ceding holds only for a certain class of external body, and what is ceded is only procedural adjudicative authority over disputes about action. This paper does not adjudicate how much that cell accounts for: the remainder has no measure, and frequency is not what the argument requires. The cost of completeness, not the failure of the framework.

7.3 Open Questions

Three questions are left for subsequent work.

First, cannot-not in the age of AI. When AI assumes all tasks that external regulation can drive, the market value of external regulation approaches zero. Cannot-not transforms from a philosophical ideal into a survival necessity — a person without cannot-not, in the age of AI, has no irreplaceable value. Does this fundamentally change the conditions for cannot-not's nurturing?

Second, can cannot-not be taught, or can it only be nurtured? SDT's autonomy support provides a direction for nurturing (creating conditions for intrinsic motivation to grow), but between autonomy support and the growth of cannot-not there remains an ontological leap: SDT can help satisfy the needs for autonomy, competence, and relatedness, but it cannot answer for you "who am I" and "what is my must." Can this leap be institutionally supported, or is it something only the individual can complete alone?

Third, institutional cannot-not. This paper focuses on the individual and relational layers. But can an organization, a nation, have its own cannot-not? If so, what is its relationship to the cannot-nots of the individuals composing it? When an institution's cannot-not conflicts with an individual's, does the remainder argument still apply? This is the bridge from individual ethics to political philosophy, and the next paper takes it up. One thing should be flagged in advance: in posing the question, this paper does not presuppose that the answer is yes — "an organization has a cannot-not of its own" may be a real question, or it may be the mis-assignment of a first-person source to a construct that has no interior source.

摘要

本体之间如何可能？这个问题的标准回答来自制度哲学：设计正确的法律和规则，让不同主体在同一空间内和平共存。本文给出一个不同的回答：本体之间之所以可能，不是因为制度在外部维持了秩序，而是因为每个主体内部已经是多个cannot-not（不得不）的共存结构。"之间"不是外部发生的事，它先在内部发生。

本文从Self-as-an-End (SAE)框架出发，论证自由不是制度赋予的选择空间，而是主体内部生长出cannot-not的能力。在此基础上建立二维分析结构：基础层为自我cannot-not是否确立，涌现层为对他人cannot-not的共情承认能力。本文提出cannot-not的三重判定标准和多元性校验机制，命名"内摄殖民"（伪cannot-not占据真cannot-not生长位置）并分析其传导路径，与康德自律伦理学、斯宾诺莎必然性自由、Deci & Ryan自我决定理论展开对话，给出四个非平凡预测。以Greta Thunberg与刘美贤(Alysa Liu)为贯穿案例，展示殖民与涵育两种传导模式的对比。

1. 问题的提出：自由为什么是主体条件问题

1.1 三层自由的递进否定

自由不是想干什么就干什么。

这看似是绝对的自由，本质上却是被生物本能、多巴胺和外界诱惑所奴役。在这种状态下，人只是欲望的执行器。一个"追随内心"的人，如果从未审视过那颗心被什么东西驱动，就不是在行使自由，而是在执行一组他从未选择过的指令。在自我决定理论(Self-Determination Theory, SDT)的框架中，这对应着外部调节(external regulation)——行为的动力来源在主体之外，来自奖惩、社会认可或市场激励。去掉这些外部contingent，行为立刻停止。这不是自由，这是放纵主义的伪自由(pseudo-freedom)。

自由也不是不想干什么就不干什么。

这是存在主义给出的答案：存在先于本质，人的自由体现在对外界强加标签的拒绝上。这比伪自由高了一个层级——它至少包含了一个否定性的主体姿态。但这种自由是悬空的：它能拆解一切被给定的意义，却不能建构哪怕一个自己的意义。一个只会说"不"的主体，悬浮在自己制造的真空里。在SDT框架中，这对应着内摄调节(introjected regulation)与无动机(amotivation)的交界地带——不做某件事是因为做了就会焦虑、内疚，或者干脆对一切都丧失了理由。这不是自由，这是存在主义的悬空自由(suspended freedom)。

自由是做cannot-not的事——明知不得不，便全力以赴。

这里的"不得不"(cannot-not)，绝不是外界拿枪指着头的强迫，也不是"不做就内疚"的心理压力，而是基于客观规律或内在绝对律令的必然性。斯宾诺莎说"自由是对必然的认识"；康德说真正的自由是意志的自律——理性为自身立法。SAE框架在两者之上更进一步：当主体在价值论层面将自我确立为一种最终目的(self-as-an-end)，而非实现其他世俗欲望的手段时，那种"不得不"就不再是外在必然的被动接受，而是纯粹理性的内在召唤。在SDT框架中，这最接近的对应不是通常被引用的认同调节(identified regulation)或内在动机(intrinsic motivation)，而是整合调节(integrated regulation)——行为与主体的核心价值完全整合，成为"who the person is"的一部分(Ryan & Deci, 2017)。但SAE的定位比SDT更深：SDT描述了自主动机的条件和效果，SAE追问的是自主动机内容的本体论根据——为什么是这个cannot-not而不是那个？

这三层构成一个递进否定的序列：从被欲望驱动（伪自由），到通过否定来确认存在（悬空自由），到从内部必然性出发行动（cannot-not）。每一层都否定了前一层，每一层都更接近自由的本质。

术语澄清：cannot-not的三个层次。 Cannot-not在本文中同时运作于三个层次，需要在此一次性澄清。现象层：主体体验到的"我不得不做这件事"的内在驱力感——这是一个第一人称报告，本身不证明真伪。结构层：经过三重判定标准（3.1节）和多元性校验（3.2节）检验后的genuine cannot-not——它与其他ends共存、去掉外部contingent后仍然成立、中断时不产生内摄式崩塌。本体论层：主体将自我确立为目的（self-as-an-end）之后，从这个本体论位置涌现出的行动必然性——这是cannot-not的最终根据，也是SAE框架对SDT的增量贡献所在（SDT描述结构层的条件和效果，SAE追问本体论层的来源）。三个层次之间的关系是：现象层的cannot-not人人都可以体验到（包括内摄殖民者），结构层的cannot-not需要通过判定和校验才能确认，本体论层的cannot-not是结构层得以成立的哲学基础。全文在不同语境中使用cannot-not时，所指的层次应从上下文中辨识。

1.2 为什么是主体条件而非制度条件

政治哲学中关于自由的讨论，通常在两个维度上展开。消极自由(negative liberty)——以Berlin为代表——关心的是外部干涉的最小化：政府不要管太多，市场不要限制太多，个人的不受干预空间越大越好。积极自由(positive liberty)——以康德为源头——关心的是自我立法的能力：主体能否成为自己行动的真正作者。

消极自由是制度层面的问题；积极自由是主体层面的问题。本文的核心论证是：没有主体内部的cannot-not，再大的制度自由空间也只产生放纵或悬空，不产生真正的自由。一个在制度上完全不受干预的人，如果从未发展出内在的行动必然性，他的"自由"就是在无限选择面前的瘫痪，或者随波逐流地被最近的多巴胺信号带走。

但这不意味着制度不重要。制度自由是必要但不充分条件。Krieger & Sheldon (2004, 2007)对法学院的纵向研究显示，制度文化可以系统性地压低学生的内在动机和幸福感，并将他们的价值重心推向更外在的方向——进入法学院时追求公共服务的学生，毕业时追求的往往是薪酬和排名。制度不是中性的容器；它通过关系层（导师、同学、评价体系）持续塑造主体的动机形态。因此，自由的分析不能停在制度层也不能停在主体层，而是必须追踪制度→关系→主体的完整传导链。真正的转化瓶颈在主体——但主体又持续通过关系层被制度塑形。

1.3 两个当代案例

本文以两个当代公共人物作为贯穿案例，不是为了评判他们的道德品质，而是为了展示cannot-not的建构与缺失在现实中的结构性后果。

Greta Thunberg是一个有巨大能量的主体，十五岁因气候议题进入公共视野，迅速成为全球运动的符号性人物。她的能量与真诚毋庸置疑，她还很年轻，仍在发展。本文聚焦的不是她作为个人的对错，而是一个结构性问题：当一个主体的行动方向主要由外部叙事（气候运动、政治机器、媒体需求）提供，而主体自身的内部cannot-not尚在建构过程中时，会面临什么样的结构性挑战？她的轨迹——从气候罢课到联合国演讲到加沙声援——展现的是一个主体在多个外部构之间寻找锚点的过程，这个过程仍在进行中。我们做诊断，也给药方。

刘美贤(Alysa Liu)是一个起点相似但轨迹相反的案例。她的父亲刘俊是关颖珊的粉丝，从小带她滑冰，投入大量资源培养她成为下一个世界冠军。十三岁夺得全美锦标赛史上最年轻的女单冠军。但在2022年北京冬奥会后，十六岁的她宣布退役——经历了政治压力、孤独感和父亲的严苛期望，她感到身心俱疲。退役两年间，她去星巴克打工，在UCLA读心理学，去远足和滑雪。然后在一次滑雪中，她发现自己仍然热爱动感、音乐与舞蹈，决定重返冰场。2025年世锦赛金牌，2026年米兰冬奥会女子单人滑金牌——成为美国史上首位金牌大满贯女单选手。她的父亲也完成了转变：承诺不再介入女儿的教练和训练事务。这不是一个无条件的成功叙事——需要警惕幸存者偏差——而是一个凿构循环（从"被迫停止"到"主动重建"）的结构性示范。

这两个案例将贯穿全文。

2. 二维结构：基础层与涌现层

2.1 模型的建立

如果自由的核心是cannot-not，那么主体间自由——"本体之间如何可能"——就需要一个能同时容纳"我的cannot-not"和"他人的cannot-not"的分析结构。本文提出一个二维模型：

横轴（基础层）：自我的cannot-not是否确立。这是"我是目的"的维度——主体是否已经从内部生长出至少一个genuine的行动必然性，并拥有判定其真伪的内部多元性。

纵轴（涌现层）：对他人cannot-not的共情承认能力。这是"他人是目的"的维度——主体是否能够承认他者的cannot-not与自己的不可通约，且不试图吞并或消解它。更精确地说，纵轴衡量的不是"他人是否重要"这一抽象判断，而是"能否sustain他者的不可吸收性"这一具体能力。

关键的结构性主张是：纵轴不独立于横轴，而是从横轴涌现出来。一个自身cannot-not尚未确立的主体，不可能genuine地承认他者的cannot-not。他要么把他者当作自己目标的工具（Q2），要么把自己消解在他者的目标中（Q4），但这两种状态都不是对他者cannot-not的真正承认。只有一个自身already-an-end的主体，才有能力看到另一个end而不试图将其吸收。

这就是本文的主定理：本体之间(inter-ontological)不是原始给定，而是涌现结果。它只有在一个已经成为目的的自我获得了足够的内部复数性，以至于能够识别另一个不可化约的目的时，才成为可能。

主定理的辖域。 这条定理解释的是承认之可能——一个主体何以可能把另一个主体认作不可化约的目的——不是共存之一般。两个主体完全可以在没有任何承认的情况下长期共处：互相工具化、互相依附，或者彼此回避而交集照旧累积。那些结构也会维持下去，有时维持得相当稳。本定理不说它们不会发生，只说它们不是承认。承认尚未发生而共存仍须进行的那一片，超出本文的射程，本文不在那里下断言。

2.2 四象限分析

这个二维结构产生四个象限，但它们不是对称的几何区域。由于纵轴从横轴涌现，只有一个象限是genuinely双高的，其余三个是不同形态的缺损。

Q1（genuinely双高）：两个自足主体的合作。 双方都有自己的cannot-not，双方都能承认对方的cannot-not与自己的不可通约。这是唯一一个不需要通过压制、让渡或消解来维持的关系结构。当两个Q1主体的cannot-not发生冲突时，他们会互相凿——在对方的参照系面前重新审视自己的构——直到找到共识或互相承认不可通约性。实在谈不拢，双方都有能力walk away。

Q2（伪高横轴）：主体性膨胀。 看似"我是目的"，实际上不是真正的self-as-end，而是把自己的单一cannot-not普遍化了——将一个个体化的行动必然性强加为所有人都应该服从的法则。殖民者、启蒙精英、"我来拯救你"的传教士，往往在这个位置。他们有能量，有真诚，有cannot-not的强烈体验——但因为缺乏内部的多元参照系，他们把自己的伦理律令(ethics)错误地升格为普遍的道德命令(morality)。这不是一个道德品质问题，而是一个结构性缺陷。

Q4（伪高纵轴）：主体性让渡。 看似"他人是目的"，实际上不是对他者cannot-not的真正承认，而是自我消解后的代理性依附(borrowed telos)。自我没有站住，所谓的"他人高"只是角色性让渡——把某个外部实体（国家、领袖、运动、公司）当作自己存在的理由。民族主义的狂热信徒、邪教成员、无条件服从组织的人，都在这个位置。他们的"奉献"看起来像是把他人当目的，但因为self-as-end从未建立，他们实际上无法分辨"他人的cannot-not"和"他人对我的征用"。

Q3（双低）：主体性双向坍缩。 没有什么值得做，没有什么人值得在乎。虚无主义的底部。存在主义如果没有走通——停留在悬空自由那一层而未能向前推进——最终滑到这里。

象限的非对称性是理解这个模型的关键。四象限不是对称的分类学，而是一个由涌现关系组织的非对称拓扑。Q1是唯一genuinely双高的象限；Q2和Q4是两种伪高态（一种伪装了横轴的高度，一种伪装了纵轴的高度）；Q3是坍缩底部。纵轴的genuine高度只有在横轴真正站住之后才可能出现。

2.3 涌现机制：为什么纵轴从横轴涌现

纵轴从横轴涌现，这个主张需要一个机制性的解释，不能只是断言。这里的关键在于一个常被忽略的区分：共情(empathy)和接受(acceptance)是两个完全不同的动作，而横轴决定了一个主体面对他人的cannot-not时走向哪一个。

共情是：我有自己的cannot-not，我看到你也有一个不同的cannot-not，我从内部经验里知道那种"不得不"的感觉是什么，所以我能理解你的，即使你的方向与我的完全不同。这需要一个前提：我自己得先拥有cannot-not的体验，才能在他人身上识别出同样的结构。

接受是：我没有自己的cannot-not，你告诉我你的不得不是什么，我没有任何参照系来判断它是真是假、是你的还是被灌入的，我只能照单全收。这不是共情，这是被殖民的前置条件——因为一个自己都没有"不得不"体验的人，连"这真的是你的不得不吗"这个问题都提不出来。

因此，横轴不只是纵轴的"前提"，它是纵轴两个方向的分水岭：有横轴，纵轴涌现为共情（Q1方向）；没有横轴，纵轴倾向于坍缩为接受（Q4方向）。一个自己尚未体验过"不得不"是什么感觉的人，在面对他人的cannot-not时，很难自发涌现出承认和尊重——他更可能的反应是服从或跟随。Q4的核心问题不是"共情太多"，而是共情的能力尚未获得生长的结构条件。

这就解释了为什么涌现是从横轴到纵轴，而不是反过来。一个人先有了自己的cannot-not，尤其是有了多个cannot-not之间的内部张力与协调经验——哲学研究和创业之间争夺时间，数学和红楼梦之间争夺注意力——他在自己内部已经练习过"多个都是真的、都是重要的、但互相之间不可完全通约"是什么感觉。当他遇到另一个人的cannot-not时，他有现成的处理框架，因为他天天在自己内部处理同类问题。

反过来，一个没有意识到自己有多个cannot-not、或者压抑了其他cannot-not的主体，他内部没有张力，没有协调的经验。他的世界是单声道的。当他遇到一个跟他不同的cannot-not时，他没有处理框架，只有两种反应：要么"你也应该跟我一样"（Q2滑落），要么"好的，我跟你走"（Q4滑落）。前者是主体性膨胀，后者是主体性让渡。两者都不是共情。

因此，涌现不是道德教育的产物（"你应该尊重他人"），也不是理性推导的结论（"普遍化要求你承认他者的目的性"），而是内部多元性的结构性副产品。共情能力的前提不是善良，而是复杂度。

2.4 Q2与Q4的共生结构

Q2和Q4不是两种独立的人格类型，而是一个共生结构的两端。殖民者需要被殖民者，领袖需要追随者，"我来拯救你"需要一个"等待被拯救"的对象。没有Q4的让渡，Q2的膨胀无处安放；没有Q2的征召，Q4的让渡无所依附。它们互相需要，互相生产，互相维持。

说得更准一点：Q2 与 Q4 的共生是一种会自我维持的无承认结构。 它不是通向 Q1 的过渡态，也不是一个失败了的 Q1；它自己就是一个解，两端各从对方那里取得自己缺的那一半，因此不能指望它因为内部不适而自行解体。这一格之所以要单独点名，是因为它恰好是"有共存而无承认"的标本：主定理解释承认如何可能，这里展示的是承认不发生时结构照样能运转。

Q1是两者的共同存在性威胁。Q1的存在前提——每个主体都有不可被吸收的目的性——同时取消了Q2的征服正当性（"你不能普遍化你的cannot-not"）和Q4的让渡正当性（"你不能把别人的cannot-not当作你的存在理由"）。这就是为什么Q2和Q4在政治上往往联合起来反对Q1：不是因为他们觉得Q1是错的，而是因为Q1的存在本身就意味着他们的整个游戏不成立。

3. 领域特有区分：cannot-not的真伪问题

3.1 内摄殖民：被cannot-not反过来殖民

cannot-not的概念一旦被提出，立刻面临一个最尖锐的挑战：你怎么知道你的"不得不"是真的？

这不是修辞性的追问。一个为气候运动燃烧一切的少女、一个为孩子的冠军梦倾注全部资源的父亲、一个为公司使命七天无休的创业者——他们都有强烈的"不得不"体验，都有巨大的能量和真诚。但体验的强度不能证明体验的来源。一个被外部灌入的"不得不"，和一个从内部生长出来的"不得不"，在主观感受上可以完全一样。

本文将这种状态命名为内摄殖民(introjected colonization)：外部灌入的"不得不"反过来定义了主体。cannot-not成了主人，主体成了它的执行器。

内摄殖民比外部控制危险得多。外部控制至少是透明的——有人拿枪指着你的头，你知道自己不自由，你保留着反抗的意识。内摄殖民是隐形的——枪已经内化成了你自己的声音，你以为那个"不得不"就是你。你甚至会为这个"不得不"感到骄傲，因为它看起来完全像是自由意志的产物。

SDT的实证研究为这个区分提供了经验基础。Howard等人(2021)的元分析发现，内摄调节(introjected regulation)确实可以预测坚持和表现目标的追求——也就是说，一个被内摄殖民的人确实会持续行动，甚至表现不差。但同时，内摄调节也与焦虑、心理消耗等ill-being指标一起上升。坚持不是自由的充分条件。一个人可以非常努力、非常执着、非常真诚，但如果他的驱动力来源是"不做就会崩溃"而不是"做了就是我"，那他就是在内摄殖民的状态下运转。

SDT体系中，真正最接近cannot-not的不是通常被引用的认同调节或内在动机，而是整合调节(integrated regulation)——行为与主体的核心价值完全整合，成为"who the person is"的一部分，且与主体的其他价值和需求不冲突(Ryan & Deci, 2017)。这个"与其他价值不冲突"的限定条件，恰好指向了区分真伪cannot-not的关键。

真cannot-not的三重判定标准：

第一，去外部contingent后是否还成立。剥离奖惩、社会认可、身份标签之后，这个"不得不"是否依然存在？刘美贤在退役两年后、在没有比赛、没有观众、没有父亲期望的情况下，在一次滑雪活动中发现自己仍然热爱动感和音乐——这就是去掉了全部外部contingent之后的检验。Greta如果离开气候运动、离开媒体聚光灯、离开"一代人的声音"这个标签，她是否仍然有一个清晰的cannot-not？她的轨迹——从气候到巴勒斯坦到亚美尼亚——暗示答案可能是否定的。

第二，能否与其他重要ends共存而不把它们全吃掉。真正的cannot-not允许其他cannot-not存在；伪cannot-not倾向于吞噬一切。一个真正热爱数学的人，不会因为数学而否定音乐的价值；但一个被"数学天才"这个身份殖民的人，可能会把所有非数学的时间都视为浪费。检验标准是：这个cannot-not是丰富了你的生命，还是缩窄了它？

第三，暂时中断时是否出现纯内摄式的羞耻/焦虑崩塌。真cannot-not被中断时产生的是遗憾和重新趋近的动力——"我想回去做这件事"。伪cannot-not被中断时产生的是身份崩溃和存在性焦虑——"没有这个我就不知道自己是谁了"。刘美贤退役后去打工、上学、旅行，没有崩溃；她是在平静中发现了回归的动力。如果退役立刻导致了身份危机和存在焦虑，那就需要审视那个"不得不"是不是真正从内部生长出来的。

这三条标准将内摄殖民从一个直觉性的命名转化为可操作的判据。

3.2 多元性：校验机制、共情前提、可判定性条件

三重判定标准提供了逐案检验的工具，但还有一个更根本的问题：如果一个人只有一个cannot-not，他怎么做这个检验？

答案是：他做不了。

单一cannot-not在原则上不可判定真伪。不是说它一定是假的，而是说真假这个问题在单一条件下没有答案——连主体自己都没有答案。因为他的全部自我绑定在这一个cannot-not上面，凿掉它等于凿掉自己，所以他不敢凿，也不会凿，甚至不会意识到需要凿。他没有参照系。

多元性解决的就是这个参照系问题。当一个人有多个独立来源的cannot-not时——比如既是哲学家又是数学家又是创业者——这些cannot-not之间会自动产生摩擦和张力。某一个伪的cannot-not与其他真的cannot-not之间的不一致，本身就是信号。多元性不需要外部的审判官，它是一个自动运行的内部校验系统。

但这里有一个关键区分：多元化不等于投影多元化。Greta的多议题参与——气候、巴勒斯坦、亚美尼亚、LGBT——看起来涉及多个领域，但它们不是多个独立的cannot-not。它们是同一个外部构（"我是正义的代言人"）在不同议题上的投影。真正的多元化不是一个cannot-not的多个投射面，而是多个独立来源、不可互相吞并的cannot-not共存。检验标准是：如果去掉其中一个，其他的是否还独立成立？刘美贤去掉滑冰之后，心理学学业和日常生活仍然独立运转——这是真多元。Greta去掉气候议题之后，其他议题参与是否还有独立的动力来源？

多元性在本文中承担三重功能。第一，它是cannot-not真伪的校验机制（如上所述）。第二，它是主体间共情的结构前提——2.3节已经论证，一个有多个cannot-not的人在内部已经练习过"不可通约的东西如何共存"，这个内部经验是共情他人cannot-not的必要条件。第三，也是最强的主张：多元性是cannot-not可判定性的必要条件。没有多元性，主体连"我的不得不是真的吗"这个问题都没有资格提出。

运动心理学的实证数据支持这个判断。Lochbaum等人(2022)的系统综述显示，运动身份认同(athletic identity)是一把双刃剑——它既与积极投入相关，也与角色丧失时的负面情绪和心理脆弱性相关，关键变量不是身份的强度，而是它的宽度。Haslam等人(2021)的研究进一步发现，多重社会身份和身份资源与更好的退役调适相关，暗示身份的广度可以缓冲单一角色丧失带来的冲击。

所谓"单一cannot-not"的经典案例——梵高、拉马努金——经不起细查。梵高不只有画画：他与高更的关系是一个cannot-not碰撞的灾难现场（对深度人际连接的cannot-not），早年去矿区传教则是宗教性救赎冲动的cannot-not。他的问题不是cannot-not太少，而是多个cannot-not之间缺乏协调能力，每次碰撞都是毁灭性的。拉马努金也不只有数学：他的婆罗门素食主义和对Namagiri女神的虔诚信仰是独立于数学的cannot-not，他在剑桥的身体崩溃恰恰是两个cannot-not（数学把他带到了一个无法满足信仰cannot-not的环境）的冲突战场。

真正单一cannot-not的人可能并不存在。看似单一，往往是其他cannot-not被压抑或不被承认的结果。问题从来不是"有没有多个cannot-not"，而是"有没有意识到自己有多个，并有能力协调它们"。

3.3 凿的最难一刀：断裂作为启动条件

多元性校验机制面临一个启动悖论(bootstrap problem)：如果一个主体目前被单一伪cannot-not完全占据，而该伪cannot-not倾向于吞噬一切时间和注意力，他如何生发出第二个独立的cannot-not来启动校验？

刘美贤的案例给出了答案：第二个cannot-not不是在第一个构仍然完整运转时生长出来的，而是在第一个构被外部事件中断后——退役创造了一个"真空期"——才有了生长空间。这暗示了一个重要的动力学机制：从单一状态向多元状态的过渡，通常需要一个断裂(rupture)。 断裂可以是外部触发的（受伤、退役、失业、关系破裂、制度变迁），也可以是内部积累到阈值的（倦怠、崩溃、存在危机）。断裂本身不是凿——它不带方向性，它只是打开了一个空间。凿是在这个空间里发生的主动审视和选择。

这意味着涵育的一个关键功能是：在断裂尚未发生时，为主体预留足够的空白空间，使得其他cannot-not的种子有机会萌发。刘美贤退役后去打工、上学、旅行——这些活动的价值不在于它们本身，而在于它们创造了不被单一构填满的时间和注意力。一个被应试教育完全占据的孩子没有这种空间；一个被运动训练完全填满的运动员没有这种空间。涵育的制度条件之一，就是不把主体的全部时间和注意力都交给单一的制度目标。

凿(chisel)是SAE框架的核心动作之一：去除不属于自己的构，让真正的构显现。但凿的最难对象不是外部强加的东西——那些至少还能被识别为"外来的"。最难凿的，是你以为是自己的、但实际上是被植入的东西。

刘美贤退役两年做的事就是这一刀。她凿掉的不是一个坏东西，而是一个看起来像她自己的东西——"成为下一个关颖珊"。这个构是父亲提供的，从五岁开始灌注，经过十一年的训练和比赛已经完全内化。凿掉它需要的不是勇气（虽然勇气也需要），而是先有一个可以站立的其他地方。去星巴克打工、去UCLA读心理学、去远足和滑雪——这些看起来不起眼的事情，在SAE框架中的功能是为凿提供支点：你需要先有一个"不是滑冰的我"存在，才能审视"滑冰的我"到底是谁的构。

她的父亲刘俊也完成了一个平行的凿。他从"关颖珊粉丝培养下一个关颖珊"的位置，转向承诺不再介入女儿的教练选择和训练安排。这不是不关心，而是把控制权交还给主体——从殖民传导的节点转变为涵育传导的节点。

类似的"退出-探索-回归"轨迹在其他高水平运动员身上也有体现。Simone Biles在2021年东京奥运会中退出女子全能决赛，此后经历了一段重新定位的时期，再回到精英赛场。Naomi Osaka在2021年退出大满贯赛事的媒体义务和部分比赛，后来以不同的姿态回归。这些案例在运动心理学中被视为与身份拓宽和动机重构相关的积极信号，但需要警惕幸存者偏差：高知名度的成功回归案例被过度报道，而更多的退出-未回归案例则不可见。

需要注意的是，投影多元化（3.2节）的检验不应在断裂发生后的急性期进行。抽掉核心构后的短期反应可能是创伤性的崩塌，这与该构是真是伪无关——即使是真正的cannot-not被突然中断，也会产生强烈的失落感。三重判定中的第三条标准（中断时的反应模式）应在主体度过急性期、进入稳定期后才具有诊断意义。

4. 殖民与涵育：传导的负向与正向

4.1 殖民的传导路径

制度层不会直接对一个个体说："把你的主体性交出来。"它的操作方式更精密：通过关系层的信任接口完成殖民。

关系层的传导节点——父母、导师、恋人、最亲密的朋友——对个体的影响不经过理性审查，直接进入主体的构。一个十五岁的孩子不会对自己的父亲做cannot-not真伪的三重检验。她信任他，他的期望自然而然地成为她的"不得不"。如果这个传导节点本身已经被制度层的逻辑俘获——无论是有意（父亲刻意培养关颖珊接班人）还是无意（父亲自己也相信这是对女儿最好的安排）——那么个体的cannot-not在源头就被替换了，而个体自己完全意识不到。

Greta Thunberg的案例展示了这个传导路径的运作方式。最初的传导节点是她的父亲——据公开报道，她的父亲在她进入公共视野的过程中扮演了重要的支持角色。但传导链不止于此：媒体机器需要一个年轻的、有冲击力的气候运动面孔；NGO网络需要一个符号性人物来动员公众；政治人物需要一个道德高地来装饰自己的议程。每一层制度都通过关系层的具体接触点（运动组织者、政客、记者）对她施加影响。从SAE的视角看，这里需要关注的不是她作为个人的选择是否正确，而是一个结构性的风险：当多重制度层同时通过关系层向一个尚在建构主体性的年轻人传导需求时，她的cannot-not的生长空间可能被挤压。

这个传导路径最残酷的特征是：关系层的信任结构恰恰是制度层征用个体的最高效通道。 制度层不需要直接接触个体，它只需要俘获个体信任网络中的一个关键节点。通过这个节点植入的构，个体不仅不会抗拒，还会为之骄傲。

SDT的实证研究大量记录了这个传导机制的运作。Nicaise等人(2025)发现，感知到更高父母压力和指导行为的精英青少年运动员，报告了更高的倦怠水平和更多的受控动机。Aunola等人(2018)的研究将父母的心理控制明确标记为青少年运动员倦怠症状的风险因素。

非运动领域也有同样的传导模式。Krieger & Sheldon (2004, 2007)对美国法学院学生的纵向追踪研究显示，法学院的制度文化（竞争排名、等级制度、外在成就导向）通过师生关系和同伴评价系统传导到学生个体，导致他们的内在动机、公共服务价值取向和主观幸福感在入学后显著下降，而外在价值取向（薪酬、声望）显著上升。制度没有命令任何人放弃理想，但它通过关系层的每一个微观互动重塑了学生的动机结构。

4.2 涵育的传导路径

涵育(nurturing)不是殖民的反面——它不是灌输一个"更好的"cannot-not来替换一个"更差的"。涵育是为cannot-not的内部生长腾出空间。

这里有一个关键区分：支持(support)不等于放任(permissiveness)。SDT文献清楚地表明，自主性支持包含三个要素：提供选择空间(choice provision)、提供有意义的理由(rationale provision)、以及提供结构和边界(structure)。一个完全放任的环境——"你想干什么就干什么"——不是自主性支持，那是把个体扔进第一层"伪自由"的空间。真正的涵育是在提供结构的同时不预设结构的内容。

刘美贤的父亲在后期展示了这个转变。他从"虎爸"模式（选教练、定训练计划、设定"关颖珊接班人"目标）转向承诺不再介入女儿的教练和训练事务。这不是不关心——他仍然支持、仍然在场——但控制权被交还给了主体。他没有说"你应该回去滑冰"，也没有说"你不应该回去滑冰"。他说的是"这是你的事"。

Su & Reeve (2011)的元分析对自主性支持干预项目的整体效果进行了量化，报告了约0.63的加权效应量——表明通过训练可以可靠地增加教师、父母、管理者等的自主性支持行为。Steingut等人(2017)的元分析则显示，提供有意义的理由倾向于增强自主动机和投入，效果取决于理由是自主性支持的还是控制性的——再次确认了"怎么给"比"给什么"更重要。

涵育传导可以从制度层向下运行。2025年的北欧五国教师研究发现，教师感知到校长的自主性支持越高，他们越倾向于在课堂上采用自主性支持的教学实践。也就是说，传导带不只是殖民的通道，它也可以正向运行——校长支持教师的自主性，教师支持学生的自主性，学生发展出自己的cannot-not。

但这里有一个必须正视的陷阱：并非所有"自主性支持"都是涵育。硅谷公司给员工20%的自由时间，谷歌的"20% time"政策是经典案例。但这种"自主性"有一个隐含条件：你最好用这个自由时间产出一些公司可以商业化的东西。Gmail和AdSense确实诞生于20% time——这正是制度层想要的产出。这不是涵育，这是工具性自主支持(instrumental autonomy support)——Q2穿着Q1的衣服。

区分标准在于：工具性自主支持有隐含的产出期待（"我给你自由，但你最好用这个自由产出我想要的东西"）；本体论自主支持(ontological autonomy support) 没有这个条件（"我给你空间，你长出什么是你的事"）。刘美贤的父亲后期做的是后者：承诺不再介入，不预设她回来滑冰还是去做别的。她可能去做心理咨询师，可能去滑雪，可能回来滑冰——这些在他的支持框架里都是等价的。这才是涵育。

一个重要的限定：本体论自主支持严格说来只在关系层成立。 任何制度(institution)的存在都有其内置的维持和生产目的——学校为了教育指标，公司为了利润，运动队为了成绩。制度提供的自主性支持，在底层逻辑上必然带有工具性底色：它期望在这个自由空间里生长出的东西，最终不违背制度的生存底线。如果一个教师在校长的"支持"下，长出的cannot-not是"摧毁现有的学校教育系统"，制度必然会动用边界机制。因此，制度层的自主性支持更准确的定位是：在不违反制度生存底线的范围内，尽量减少对主体cannot-not生长的干预。它可以做到不殖民，但做不到纯粹的涵育。纯粹的本体论自主支持——"你长出什么是你的事"——只有在亲密关系、真正的师徒关系等关系层才可能实现。

4.3 殖民与涵育的不对称性

殖民容易，涵育难。这不是偶然的，而是结构性的。

灌入一个构比等待一个构从内部生长出来快得多。一个控制型的父亲可以在孩子五岁时就给她设定"世界冠军"的目标，立刻启动行动方案。一个涵育型的父亲需要等——等孩子自己发现兴趣，自己遇到挫折，自己从挫折中长出新的方向。前者可以在月报里写进展，后者无法汇报，因为"正在等待"不是任何制度层愿意接受的KPI。

制度层天然偏好殖民，因为外部调节可观测、可量化、可标准化。你可以测考试分数，可以测GDP增速，可以测运动员的比赛成绩和世界排名。自主动机是内在的——你无法从外部测量一个人的cannot-not是真的还是假的，你甚至无法测量它是否存在。一个自主动机很强的人和一个内摄殖民很深的人，在外部行为上可以完全一样：都很努力，都很坚持，都有成果。制度层分不清，也不需要分清——它只要产出。

这就解释了一个悖论：SDT有几十年的实证基础，跨文化、跨领域的数据一致显示自主动机优于受控动机，但SDT在教育政策和组织管理的实际实施中几乎没有穿透力。不是因为没人知道，而是因为制度层的操作系统建立在外部调节之上，SDT的结论等于要求制度层对自己做手术。

5. 理论定位：与既有讨论的对话

5.1 与康德的对话

SAE框架与康德伦理学共享一个核心等式：自由=自律。cannot-not对应康德绝对律令的无条件性——行动出于义务(aus Pflicht)，而不仅仅是合乎义务(pflichtmäßig)。一个出于义务行动的人，不是因为行动会带来好结果，而是因为理性本身要求如此。这就是cannot-not的康德式表述。

但SAE与康德有三个根本分歧。

分歧一：在义务冲突这一处，康德不给余项留位置。 他的体系要求道德法则无例外地普遍有效。当两个同样有效的道德律令正面碰撞时——经典困境如"不能撒谎"vs"保护无辜者"——康德的框架要么陷入沉默，要么给出反直觉的结论（如他著名的"不能对杀人犯撒谎"的判断）。SAE承认冲突不可完全消解，并用余项论证（5.4节）来处理这个不可消解性。

这一条收窄到义务冲突这一处，不是说康德整个体系里没有任何余差。他在实践理性的悬设处（灵魂不朽、上帝存在）自己标出过一笔不能由理论理性兑付的欠账——那是他自陈的余差，不在本条所指之内。本条所指只有一件：两条同样有效的义务正面相撞时，他的框架里没有一个位置用来登记这个冲突本身。

分歧二：康德的"人是目的"公式指向他者，SAE将其翻转指向自身。 康德的第二公式——"以如此方式行动，使你永远同时把人性（无论在你自己身上还是在他人身上）当作目的，而绝不仅仅当作手段"——在实际使用中主要用于约束你对待他人的方式。SAE的翻转是：在你能够把他人当作目的之前，你必须先把自己确立为目的。自我作为目的不是自私，而是共情他者的结构性前提（2.3节的涌现论证）。

分歧三：普遍性vs个体性。 这是最深的分歧。康德的绝对律令要求普遍化(Universalizability)——"你的行动准则必须能成为普遍的自然法则"。这意味着，如果你的cannot-not不能被所有理性存在者同时采纳而不自相矛盾，它就不是真正的道德律令。但SAE的cannot-not天然是个体化的(Individualized)。刘美贤的cannot-not（滑冰）绝对不适用于所有人；一个哲学家的cannot-not（追问存在的条件）也不适用于所有人。这不是SAE的弱点，而是它比康德更诚实的地方。

康德试图用普遍理性消除冲突——如果所有人的道德准则都能普遍化，那么它们在逻辑上就不会冲突。但现实是：两个完全正当的、不可普遍化的个体cannot-not确实会碰撞。SAE承认个体cannot-not的不可通约性，正因为不可通约，才需要互凿、walk away、乃至外部仲裁来处理冲突，才需要余项论证来承认不可消解的东西。普遍性是康德回避余项的策略；个体性是SAE正面面对余项的前提。

5.2 与斯宾诺莎的对话

斯宾诺莎说"自由是对必然的认识"。一个理解了自然因果链的人，不再盲目地与之抗争，因此获得了一种特殊的自由——不是挣脱锁链的自由，而是理解锁链的自由。

SAE与斯宾诺莎的重合在于：两者都认为自由不是外部约束的缺失，而是与必然性的正确关系。但SAE在斯宾诺莎停下的地方继续前进。斯宾诺莎解决的是认识论层面的问题：理解必然性，从而不再盲目抗拒。SAE追问的是价值论层面的问题：理解了必然性之后，你为什么行动？

认识必然不等于行动自由。一个完全理解了物理定律的人，并不因此获得了行动的方向。他知道"世界是这样运作的"，但他不知道"我应该做什么"。从认识到行动之间，有一个跳跃——主体的价值确认。SAE的回答是：当主体将自我确立为目的本身，而非任何外部目标的手段时，行动的方向就从内部必然性中涌现出来。斯宾诺莎告诉你自由是什么（对必然的认识）；SAE告诉你自由从哪里来（主体的自我目的化）以及自由如何指导行动（cannot-not）。

5.3 与自我决定理论的对话

SAE与SDT的关系不是对立，而是层级互补。

SDT的动机连续谱——从外部调节到内摄调节到认同调节到整合调节到内在动机——与SAE的三层自由高度对应。伪自由对应外部调节，悬空自由对应内摄调节与无动机的交界，cannot-not对应整合调节与内在动机。SDT的"自我和谐目标"(self-concordant goals)概念——与个人发展中的兴趣和价值吻合的目标能预测持续努力、目标达成和长期幸福感(Sheldon & Elliot, 1999)——是cannot-not在经验层面最接近的操作化。

SAE对SDT的增量贡献在于：SDT描述了自主动机的条件（三个基本需求：自主性、胜任感、关系性）和效果（更高的幸福感、表现质量、持续性），但不回答动机内容的本体论来源。为什么这个人的cannot-not是哲学而那个人的是滑冰？SDT无法回答这个问题，因为它的分析框架关注的是动机的形式（自主vs受控）而非动机的内容（做什么）。SAE补上了这个缺口：动机内容来源于主体将自我确立为目的这个本体论动作——当你不再把自己当作实现任何外部目标的手段时，你的行动方向就从你作为这个独特存在者的内部结构中涌现出来。

但这个映射需要三个限定条件。

第一，SAE说的"伪自由"（做想做的）不能简单等同于SDT的"外部调节"。SDT的外部调节是"做一件事因为有外部奖惩"，而SAE批判的伪自由包含了一个更隐蔽的状态：一个人的偏好本身就已经被外部contingency结构化了，他主观体验为"这是我想要的"，但这个"想要"的内容是市场、媒体、社会期待塑造的。SAE的分析比SDT的外部调节概念更深一层，因为它质疑的不只是动力来源，还有欲望内容本身。

第二，"拒绝"不全是内摄调节或无动机。SDT会承认，有些拒绝是高度自主的——一个认同了自己价值观的人拒绝一个不道德的要求，这是认同性拒绝，不是悬空自由。SAE的"悬空自由"批判的是那些纯粹否定性的、不指向任何建构的拒绝，而不是所有形式的拒绝。

第三，强烈的身份认同如果过于狭窄，在角色丧失时反而是脆弱性的来源。SDT和运动心理学的文献都指向这一点。SAE的回应是：cannot-not需要与身份宽度共存，这就是多元性校验的功能所在。

5.4 余项论证

当两个 cannot-not 相遇，会发生什么？

分路按当前的关系条件分，不按人分。 这里不先问对方是不是 SAE 主体、再据此分路。理由有两条。其一，本文没有给出、也不打算给出任何第三人称的检验，去认定另一个人运行在哪一层；没有检验而据以分路，等于用一个不存在的判据给人分类。其二，分路的实际依据本来就不是对方是什么，而是此刻这段关系里哪些处理动作还可用；关系条件会变，读数随之修订，而"他是不是 SAE 主体"一旦判下，就把人钉住了。

因此按关系条件分三格，另加一格不是路的交接。

其一 · 互向过程可用：互凿。 双方都能进入同一个互向过程时，做的事不是压制对方，而是互相凿——不是凿对方的 cannot-not，而是在对方的参照系面前重新审视自己的构。这个过程看起来像辩论，本质上是双方各自在对方的 cannot-not 面前进行自我审查。结果有两种：要么找到共识（双方都凿掉了一些伪构，发现底层其实兼容），要么发现不可通约但互相承认（各自保留，和平共处）。一个 genuine 的 SAE 主体，他的自我不依赖于这场具体冲突的胜负。

其二 · 互向不可用而退出可用：走开。 互凿的前提是双方进入同一个过程。这个前提不成立时，留下来纠缠不是勇敢，是没有着力点——我凿我自己的构可以继续，但"互"这一半没有对侧。此时退出是可用的，而走开本身就是自由的表达：我的 cannot-not 不需要你的认可来成立。

要紧的是这一格判的是过程的可用性，不是对方的资格。互向条件后来恢复，就转回其一；后来失效，就转过来。换路不需要重新认定对方是什么，也不留下任何关于对方的结论。

其三 · 退出不解除持续交集：交给制度。 退出并不总能解除共同的问题。在共同孩子、共同制度、稀缺资源、对第三方的共同责任等纠缠性情境(entangled cases)中，退出本身可能意味着放弃对第三方的责任，或者退出的通道已经被制度性地封堵。这一格超出主体间伦理所能处理的范围，本文把它交出去：如何从本体论第一原理推导出一个尊重主体性的共同框架，是下一篇的问题。

另一格不是路 · 退路被强力压缩时的交接。 当一方的主体位置或退路被强力压缩——不是"谈不下去"，而是人身安全、基本生计或退出通道受到实际压制——这不是上面三格之外的第四条路，而是一次交接：先保护，再移交给有相应权限的程序。把它写成一条平行的处理路线，会让它排在互凿与走开之后等候，而它恰恰是不能等的那一格。保护可以先于任何互向处理。

外部仲裁：三项可能根据，不是三个充分条件。 其三那一格里，SAE 主体的选择不是诉诸暴力，而是诉诸外部权力仲裁——法律、制度、第三方裁决。把裁决权让渡给外部，不是主体性的失败，而是主体性的边界意识：我承认这个冲突超出了主体间互凿所能解决的范围，需要一个外部框架来处理。

但"外部"不是随便哪一个外部。这一句只对某一类外部成立：双方已经承认的程序；依法有权的机制；或者为保护基础权利而有正当权限的制度。这三项是可能根据，不是任选其一即自动取得全部权限——一个机制符合其中一项，仍要看它在这件事上有没有适用权限。反过来一面也要写准：第三方由一方提名，这个事实本身不证明它已被那一方俘获；要查的是它是否仍只按那一方的意志行事，是否拥有独立的程序与适用权限。由强势一方单独选定、且只按那一方意志行事的第三方，是那一方权力的延伸；把分歧交给它不叫移出，是同一件事换了个名字继续。

让渡出去的是什么，也要写准。 让渡的是行动争议的程序裁决权，不是自己之法的构成权。仲裁可以决定谁在什么时候使用哪一份资源、哪一项行动可以实施、程序怎么安排；它不能决定哪一条法在第一人称上更真，也不能决定谁的主体资格更高。

余项结构。 这就产生了本文的余项结构：一个以主体自治为目的的框架——SAE 的全部伦理立场都建立在"每个主体将自我确立为目的"之上——其逻辑余项恰恰是让渡裁决权给外部。这不是框架的失败，而是完备性的代价。一个完全自洽的主体间伦理，在遇到纠缠性情境时，必须承认自己的管辖范围有边界，并接入制度层的仲裁功能。

余项不可消除，而本文不给它量。 余项在理论上不可消除：纠缠性情境在现实中必然存在。本文所论证的处理范围带一个明确前件——当双方都是 genuine 的 SAE 主体时，互向过程可用，互凿与走开各有其位；不满足这个前件的那一片，这两种处理都不适用，落在其三。

本文不判哪一格占多少。频率判断不是这里的论证所需要的：只要存在一类退出不解除共同问题的情形，制度的问题就已经成立；这一格是不是少数，与它成不成立无关。也不说余项"在实践中被最小化"——那是把测度语言用在了余项本身，而余项没有测度，谈不上总量，也就谈不上被压小。

这与 SAE 的数学平行项目 ZFCρ 有一处结构同构，而同构止于此：一个形式系统可以证明某个剩余项必然存在，而证明它存在并不取消它。ZFCρ 里"ρ 的实际数值可以被压到任意小"是形式系统内部关于一个有定义的量的结论；本文的余项没有这样的量，这后半句不外推到主体间余项。

6. 非平凡预测

本文的二维模型不仅是描述性的分类工具，它产生四个可检验的非平凡预测。"非平凡"意味着这些预测不是框架的直接复述，而是框架推出的、在没有框架的情况下不会被预期的结论。

6.1 基础层→涌现层（正面）：内部多元性预测共情能力

预测：拥有多个独立且不可互相吞并的cannot-not的个体，比单一cannot-not的个体更容易共情他人的cannot-not。

非平凡性在于：共情能力通常被归因于人格特质（善良、同理心）或道德教育（"你应该尊重他人"）。本文的预测是，共情不是道德教育的产物，而是内部多元性的结构性副产品。一个人有多个cannot-not时，他已经在内部练习过"多个不可通约的东西如何共存"的协调，面对他人的cannot-not时有现成的处理框架。

可检验推论：拥有多个独立身份领域（而非单一身份认同）的个体，在人际冲突情境中表现出更高的perspective-taking能力。邻近文献中，社会身份复杂度(social identity complexity)的研究发现，低复杂度（多个身份高度重叠）的个体对外群体更不宽容，方向与本预测一致。

6.2 基础层→涌现层（负面）：单一cannot-not预测道德绝对化

预测：cannot-not高度集中于单一领域的个体，倾向于将个人伦理律令绝对化为普遍道德命令。

非平凡性在于：最真诚、最有能量的主体恰恰是最容易将自己的律令普遍化的——不是因为恶意，而是因为内部缺乏多元cannot-not的参照系。他没有体验过"两个都是真的但互相不兼容"是什么感觉，所以他假设自己的cannot-not对所有人都成立。这是一个结构性特征，不是品德问题。

可检验推论：身份认同高度集中于单一领域的个体，在道德判断任务中表现出更低的价值多元主义容忍度。Legault等人(2011)的研究提供了邻近证据：控制性框架下的反偏见信息可能适得其反，反而增加偏见——指向"外部道德命令式的干预会触发防御"这一方向。

6.3 涌现层→基础层（正面）：共情作为自我凿的工具

预测：被他人的cannot-not真正触动过的个体，更容易识别自己内部的伪cannot-not。

非平凡性在于：共情通常被视为向外的关系能力，本预测指出它有一个向内的回馈效应。当你看到另一个人的cannot-not跟你的完全不同但同样真诚时，你的参照系被打开了——你开始有能力审视自己的cannot-not中有哪些是被植入的，因为你现在有了一个"真的cannot-not长什么样"的外部参照物。

可检验推论：经历过深度跨文化或跨领域关系的个体，在自我反思任务中更容易区分内摄动机和认同动机。这是四个预测中最具原创性的，也是实证桥梁最薄弱的——标记为最高风险预测(highest-risk prediction)。

6.1与6.3的正反馈循环。 预测6.1（内部多元性→共情能力）和预测6.3（共情经历→识别伪cannot-not）不是两个独立的线性关系，它们构成一个正反馈循环：内部多元性开启了共情的可能，而深度的共情经历——被另一个人的genuine cannot-not真正触动——又作为外部参照系，反过来加速了主体内部的凿的过程，帮助他识别和剥离伪构。剥离伪构后，内部的真cannot-not更加清晰，多元性的质量提升，共情能力进一步增强。这个正反馈循环意味着：Q1不是一个静态的终点状态，而是一个自我强化的动态过程——越自由，越能共情；越共情，越能凿掉伪自由。

6.4 涌现层→基础层（负面）：失锚共情导致主体性让渡

预测：高共情但低自我分化的个体，在持续面对他人cannot-not的情境中更容易丧失自己的cannot-not。

非平凡性在于：共情被普遍视为积极特质，本预测指出它在失去基础层锚定时会变成另一种形式的Q4。一个过度共情的人不断将他人的cannot-not内化为自己的责任，最终失去自己的cannot-not，变成他人目的性的服务器。

但这里需要一个精确化：并非所有"共情"都有这个风险。SDT相关研究(Weinstein & Ryan, 2010)显示，出于自主动机的助人行为对帮助者和被帮助者都有积极效果。真正构成风险的不是perspective-taking（认知层面理解他人处境），而是self-oriented empathic distress（以自我为中心的共情痛苦）——即在缺乏自我分化的情况下，以卷入式的方式体验他者的痛苦。因此，6.4的更精确表述是：缺乏自我分化的、以自我卷入式方式体验他者痛苦的共情，会侵蚀基础层。

可检验推论：高共情特质但低自我分化(self-differentiation)的个体，在持续助人情境中表现出更高的倦怠和身份混乱。运动心理学中的"过度卷入型父母"是一个变体：父母将孩子的cannot-not内化为自己的cannot-not，最终两者都被消耗。

7. 结论

7.1 回收

自由不是做想做的，那是被欲望奴役的伪自由。自由不是拒绝不想做的，那是悬浮在否定中的悬空自由。自由是做cannot-not的事——明知不得不，便全力以赴。

但一个人的cannot-not，只有在它不是被植入的、不是唯一的、不是吞噬一切的时候，才是真正自由的表达。内摄殖民——被一个看起来像是自己的"不得不"反过来定义——是最隐蔽也最普遍的不自由形式。

本体之间之所以可能，不是因为制度在外部维持了秩序，而是因为一个已经成为目的的自我，在拥有了足够的内部复数性之后，获得了识别另一个不可化约的目的的能力。"之间"不是外部发生的事，它先在内部发生。他者之为目的，只在一个不再把自身普遍化的主体那里才成为可能。

7.2 贡献

本文对既有讨论做出四个增量贡献。

第一，对SDT的本体论补充。SDT描述了自主动机的条件（三个基本需求）和效果（幸福感、表现、持续性），但不回答动机内容的本体论来源。SAE回答了这个问题：动机内容涌现于主体将自我确立为目的这个本体论动作。

第二，cannot-not真伪的判定机制。三重判定标准（去外部contingent、与其他ends共存、中断时的反应模式）提供了逐案检验工具；多元性校验提供了结构性的自动校验机制；多元性作为可判定性的必要条件，则在认识论层面封闭了"单一cannot-not能否自证"的问题。

第三，内摄殖民的命名与传导路径分析。伪cannot-not占据真cannot-not生长位置的机制，以及制度层通过关系层的信任接口进行殖民传导的路径，为理解"为什么有能量、有真诚的人仍然可能不自由"提供了结构性解释。

第四，余项论证。一个以主体自治为目的的伦理框架，在遇到纠缠性情境时，其逻辑余项是让渡裁决权给外部仲裁，而这一让渡只对某一类外部成立，让渡出去的也只是行动争议的程序裁决权。本文不判这一格占多少：余项没有测度，频率也不是本文论证所需要的。完备性的代价，非框架的失败。

7.3 开放问题

三个问题留待后续工作。

第一，AI时代的cannot-not。当AI接管了所有外部调节可以驱动的任务之后，外部调节的市场价值趋近于零。cannot-not从一个哲学理想变成了一个生存必需品——一个没有cannot-not的人，在AI时代将没有任何不可替代的价值。这是否根本改变了cannot-not的涵育条件？

第二，cannot-not可教还是只能涵育？SDT的自主性支持提供了涵育的方向（创造条件让内在动机生长），但从自主性支持到cannot-not的生长之间，还有一个本体论跳跃：SDT可以帮你满足自主性、胜任感、关系性这三个需求，但它不能替你回答"我是谁"以及"我的不得不是什么"。这个跳跃是否可以被制度化地支持，还是它本质上只能由个体独自完成？

第三，制度层的cannot-not。本文聚焦个体层与关系层。但一个组织、一个国家是否可以有自己的cannot-not？如果可以，它与组成它的个体的cannot-not之间是什么关系？当制度的cannot-not与个体的cannot-not冲突时，四层递进是否仍然适用？这是从个体伦理走向政治哲学的桥梁，由下一篇接手。须预先标明一件：本文提出这个问题时没有预设答案是肯定的——"一个组织有它自己的 cannot-not"可能是一个真问题，也可能是把第一人称的来源错挂到了一个没有内部之源的构上。

六件改动

其一 · 主定理的辖域写明。 原文给出主定理后直接进入四象限。本版在主定理之后加一段，写明它解释的是承认之可能，不是共存之一般：两个主体完全可以在没有任何承认的情况下长期共处，那些结构也会维持下去。本定理不说它们不会发生，只说它们不是承认。§2.4 相应加一句，把 Q2 与 Q4 的共生点名为一种会自我维持的无承认结构——它不是通向 Q1 的过渡态，也不是失败的 Q1，而是「有共存而无承认」的标本。主定理本身（含「纵轴从横轴涌现」）未改，其强度另案审。

其二 · §5.4 的分路改按关系条件，不按人分。 原文以一个前置判断开篇——「对方是不是SAE主体」——再据此分三层（双SAE互凿／面对非SAE走开／不可行则仲裁）。本版撤去这个问法，改按当前关系条件分三格：互向过程可用（互凿）；互向不可用而退出可用（走开）；退出不解除持续交集（交给制度）。撤的理由写在正文里：本文没有给出、也不打算给出任何第三人称的检验去认定对方运行在哪一层，没有检验而据以分路，等于请读者用一个不存在的判据给人分类。并写明路线是可修订的读数，换路不留下关于对方的结论。

另立一格不是路：退路被强力压缩时的交接——先保护，再移交给有相应权限的程序。原文把这种情形并入「walk away 不可行」一类，使它排在互凿与走开之后等候，而它恰恰不能等。

来源：本系列后出的《SAE 冲突论》先写出「分路不按人分，按当前的关系条件分」这一裁定，以及两路、停止门与移出的结构，并立「保护可以先于任何互向处理」。按本系列体例，基础论文正文不引下游；该裁定收回为本文自己的内容，来源记于此。

其三 · 外部仲裁补三项可能根据与两条防误用。 原文只写「诉诸外部权力仲裁——法律、制度、第三方裁决」，未限定哪一类外部。本版补：这一句只对某一类外部成立——双方已经承认的程序；依法有权的机制；或者为保护基础权利而有正当权限的制度。并写明三项是可能根据，不是任选其一即自动取得全部权限；反过来，第三方由一方提名这个事实本身也不证明它已被俘获，要查的是它是否仍只按那一方意志行事、是否拥有独立的程序与适用权限。另补一句写准让渡的对象：让渡的是行动争议的程序裁决权，不是自己之法的构成权。
