Abstract

The aesthetics series, the Laughter Series and Judgment and Weeping treat judgment in its expressions: beauty is seeing and cannot-not acknowledging; laughter is judgment felt directly; weeping is the jolt felt by way of emotion. This series is the last series on the side of judgment. It no longer treats judgment itself, but how judgment is practiced. There is more than one path to practice; this series takes up only one, play. This paper is the Overview, and its position is this: play is human nature; the focal point is the game itself; as regards the play-directed share, the practice of judgment is a result, not a purpose, and whether participants themselves take practice as their purpose is another matter; what is practiced is judgment, not the same judgment. The main thesis is: the same game can be played again, and each time judgment can differ.

A game takes rules as its construct, and the rules do not exhaust the grasping and responding that participants still have to do. Judgment, in this paper's sense, is seeing that "this game still has such a remainder"; which move to make is action around that judgment. Play is defined by four conditions: game, person, focal point, voluntariness. The focal point is read from structure alone, not from the inner life: if this share is tied to what lies inside the game, it is play-directed; if it is tied to something outside the game, it is non-play-directed. The verdict falls on a given participant's share in this game; so a match can be play and work at once, when a player bets on the match it is only his own share that is not play, and when all the participants cheat together, it is no longer play. The three papers are divided by the structure of the game: the solo game, the competitive game and the cooperative game newly open, respectively, 13DD, 14DD and 15DD as layers at which the subject can stand; the layer lies with the subject and is often mixed, and the game itself is not at any layer. 15DD is worth practicing, yet it cannot be forced, much like cultivation.

The main contrast is Schiller. Both accounts say that play comes from human nature; he says that the aesthetic decides nothing for cognition or will, which is close to this paper's "practice is a result". They part at two points: he sets up the aesthetic as the only path, while this paper says play is one path; his play is directed at beauty, while in this paper the remainder in a game need not be beauty. Groos is the nearest neighbour: he answers why play exists in nature, this paper answers where judgment falls in a game; they are not asking the same question. Aristotle, Kant, Huizinga and Wittgenstein serve as further contrasts. This paper is about judgment, not a theory of play; the boundaries of play serve only to hold the scope and are not load-bearing: gambling is handled by a scope convention, games of pure chance as a boundary condition, and war and conflict are not play; all of these are registered only, not developed.

0. Introduction: The Question and the Position

0.1 The Question

A child asks an adult to tell, once more, a story he has heard many times. He has long known that the wolf will come, and still he wants to hear it. Just before the familiar line, the adult pauses; the child does not wait, and supplies the line himself.

What does he get from this telling? The story has not changed, and he knows the ending. Merely foreseeing that the next line is "Here comes the wolf!" is prediction; it is material, and not yet judgment in this paper's sense (2.3). What this paper points to is something else: he sees that this time the same line can be picked up by him, here. "At this point it can still be picked up like this" is that judgment; whether to speak up, and how to shout it, is action around that judgment. The game of the story has been restarted, and the remainder he sees this time can differ from the one he saw last time. The same game can be played again, and each time judgment can differ. This is the main thesis of this paper; the example is worked through in 5.4.

The child meant here is already at 13DD; on the side of younger children there is the prediction of 12DD, which is registered only (Section 8).

SAE Judgment and Aesthetics with its thirteen rays, the five papers of the Laughter Series and the Laughter Series' Special Study, Judgment and Weeping, treat judgment as expression. This series is the last series on the side of judgment, and it asks a different question: how judgment is practiced. There is more than one path to practice; this series takes up only one, play.

0.2 The Position

Play is human nature. Play has no purpose outside the game; without using the word "purpose", one can say that the focal point is the game itself. This paper says "the focal point is the game itself", and not "play is an end in itself", because in SAE the word 目的 (purpose, end) is the word for one's own purpose at 14DD, and also the word in "a person is an end".

The practice of judgment is a result, not a purpose. What is practiced is judgment, not the same judgment. This concerns how this paper accounts for practice in play: as regards the play-directed share, practice is a result that may come about, and this paper does not take "play is for the sake of practice" as a general explanation. That someone may himself take practice as his purpose, this paper does not deny (1.2); this does not change the focal point (3.1).

Play is one path, not the only path. This paper does not say that judgment can be practiced only through play; it says only that the practice of judgment can go by way of play.

About judgment, not a theory of play. The boundaries of play serve only to hold the scope and are not load-bearing. Any derivation that serves only to draw the line of "play" more precisely, without changing a single sentence on the side of judgment, this paper does not undertake; marginal cases are left to Section 13.

Scope. This paper treats only play in which a subject takes part. Animals play too; that lies outside this series (Section 8).

For why the practice of judgment is approached through play, see 3.5; that is a reason, not a demand that anyone play.

0.3 Three Sentences

  • The game: the rules are a construct, and do not exhaust the grasping and responding that participants still have to do.
  • Play: the focal point is the game itself.
  • Practice: what is practiced is judgment; when the same game is played again, judgment can differ.

0.4 What This Paper Does Not Claim

This paper does not claim that play makes people better, does not claim that practice necessarily leaves a stable change, and does not claim that judgment practiced in a game carries over to outside the game; all of these need separate testing and are left to Section 13. It does not claim that everyone ought to play, and it does not rank persons: someone who does not care for a certain kind of game is not thereby judged to lack anything. Nor does it claim that all play guarantees practice: condition 2 of the four-part definition (6.1) requires only that a subject take part in this game; whether there is in fact judgment in this game, and whether there is practice, depends on the instance. Finally, this paper does not cover everything that everyday language calls play or a game. It takes the path of definition: claims such as those about human nature and practice are made only of the class so defined (Section 6, 11.3).

The order of the paper is as follows: first the question is separated from that of the nearest neighbour, Groos (Section 1); then come the game, the remainder and judgment (Section 2), the focal point (Section 3), the unit of the verdict (Section 4), the main thesis (Section 5) and the definition (Section 6); then the three kinds of game and the division of labour among the three papers (Section 7), the scope (Section 8), and practice and cultivation (Section 9); finally the contrasts with Schiller and the others (Sections 10 and 11).

1. Groos: Not the Same Question

1.1 The Preparation Theory

In the preface to The Play of Animals (1896), Karl Groos says that play is the "Vorübung und Einübung" of the relevant instincts, as opposed to "der späteren ernsten Ausübung" (pp. V–VI). The often-quoted sentence carries a qualification in the original: "vielleicht ist die Einrichtung der Jugendzeit selbst zum Theil um der Spiele willen getroffen; die Thiere spielen nicht, weil sie jung sind, sondern sie haben eine Jugend, weil sie spielen müssen." The preface to the English translation of The Play of Man (1899) describes itself as keeping to the same "practice theory". The book's introduction sets two criteria side by side: "The biological criterion of play is that it shall deal not with the serious exercise of the special instinct, but with practice preparatory to it"; "The psychological criterion corresponds with it; thus, when an act is performed solely because of the pleasure it affords, there is play" (English translation, p. 5).

1.2 Not the Same Question

Groos answers why play exists in nature: youth is perhaps set up in part for the sake of play, and play rehearses the serious exercise to come. This paper answers where judgment falls in a game. The two ask different questions and speak of different relations: he speaks of the relation between play and the serious exercise to come, this paper of the relation between judgment and the game. These are not opposite answers to one and the same proposition; this follows the method by which the last paragraph of Judgment and Weeping 1.4 compares that paper with Kant.

So when this paper does not adopt the functional theory, this means that it does not take it as its own answer, not that it denies it. A functional explanation can coexist with the player's not having practice as his focal point at this moment: a child running after a ball may at the same time be building up his body for the future; but what he is chasing at this moment is the ball, and this share is tied to what lies inside the game. Nor does this paper say that "practice is the player's own purpose"; this too only means that it does not take that as its answer. That someone is at this moment playing precisely with practice as his purpose, this paper does not deny, and this does not change his focal point either (3.1).

One point at which the two can be compared is their attitude to the future. The preparation theory stresses the serious exercise to come; this paper says only that judgment is put to use in the game, and makes no claim about whether it carries over to outside the game (0.4).

1.3 The Name, and müssen

In Chinese, Groos's account is usually called the "practice theory" (练习说), and also the "preparation-for-life theory" (生活准备说). This paper also uses the word "practice" (练习), so the two must be separated from the outset: his practice is the instinct's rehearsal for the exercise to come; this paper's practice is judgment actually put to use in the game, owned by the person himself (Section 5).

The "spielen müssen" in the sentence about youth is a functional necessity of instinct. When this paper says that the subject will never be forever without the intention to take part in play (3.5), it speaks of intention, and does not adopt the functional theory. The two are set out side by side here so that they are not read as one thing.

The entry "Spiel" in Eisler's Wörterbuch der philosophischen Begriffe (1904) reports that Groos lists "Freude am »Ursachesein«" (the joy of being a cause) as one moment of play; according to secondhand material, the phrase appears on S. 190 of the original, written "Ursache-sein". If this holds, it speaks of the player's joy, a matter of the inner life; it can be read side by side with "the focal point is the game itself", but does not serve as its ground: the focal point is read by structure, and is not set by where the joy falls (3.1, 3.3).

1.4 Aristotle: The Opposite End

Nicomachean Ethics X.6 cites Anacharsis: "παίζειν δʼ ὅπως σπουδάζῃ", play in order to be in earnest; amusement is like rest, and rest is for the sake of activity ("ἕνεκα τῆς ἐνεργείας", 1176b27–1177a1). The English translations have exert oneself (Ross) or work (Rackham); this work is not "work" in the technical SAE sense.

For him, one plays in order to be in earnest, and amusement is for the sake of activity. This is an answer to "why play?", on the same side as the functional theory (1.2). By 3.1, such an "in order to" is not a structural marker and does not change the focal point. This paper stands at the opposite end, and the opposition lies in the answer: this paper does not answer play with "for the sake of what"; the focal point is the game itself; whether, having played, people are better able to be in earnest, this paper does not claim (0.4).

2. Game and Remainder; Judgment in This Paper's Sense

2.1 The Game

Following Laughter Overview, Section 0 and 2.1, a construct is any way of grasping that is currently in operation; a remainder is the point that the construct has not fully taken in, and that is nonetheless right there before one's eyes.

A game takes rules as its construct. The rules do not exhaust the grasping and responding that participants still have to do: the rules of chess lay down how each piece moves, but not which move to make in this game; the steps of a dance are fixed, but not which way one's dance partner turns at this moment; the story has been told up to here, and the next line has not yet been spoken.

2.2 The Same Shape as Laughter I, 4.1, but a Different Remainder

Laughter I, 4.1 gave an example in which construct and remainder are kept at the same time: in rough-and-tumble play, the shape of attack is there, and the remainder "not really an attack" is there too. This paper puts forward a separate proposition: in each of the kinds of game discussed, the rules do not exhaust the grasping and responding that participants still have to do. This proposition is shown by this series kind by kind; it does not rely on that example from the Laughter Series for its warrant.

In this way, a story whose ending is known and a well-rehearsed performance can also be accommodated: the remainder need not be only "what happens next"; it can also be how the teller tells it this time, or how one's dance partner responds this time.

2.3 Judgment in This Paper's Sense

Judgment in this paper's sense takes the shape it has in the Laughter Series: seeing that "this game still has such a remainder". In an endgame, it is "here one can still move like this"; in a story, it is "it can still go like this".

This judgment has to be kept apart from two things. One is action: which move to make is action around this judgment, by the same method as Laughter III, 1.3 uses to separate "laughter as judgment" from "doing things with laughter". A person can see a move and not make it, or can make a move and only see afterwards what was good about it. The other is prediction: predicting the next move is already there at 12DD (Laughter I). A listener to a story will predict what happens later; that is material. From 13DD up, that judgment has an owner, "this judgment is mine" (Laughter II): he does not merely foresee what the next step will be; he sees that the story can still go like this.

2.4 Games of Pure Chance: A Boundary Condition

Games of pure chance, such as throwing dice for the higher number with nothing staked, are registered by this paper as a boundary condition; it does not decide whether they are play. Pure chance does little to help the practice of judgment; this is the author's observation, not a proof. This paper keeps it only as a boundary condition, and does not go on to assert that there is no remainder to be seen in such games. Caillois says that in games of chance the player is passive, which can serve as collateral evidence for this observation (Paper II).

3. The Focal Point: Structure Only

3.1 Why Participants Take Part

This paper looks only at structure, not at the inner life, which it could not see in any case. Whether a person takes part out of vanity or out of interest, as long as the focal point lies inside the game, this share is structurally play-directed; if the focal point lies outside the game, it is non-play-directed. "Play-directed" speaks only to the focal-point condition; whether a share counts as play also depends on the other conditions of Section 6.

Why do participants take part? This paper brings the question down to structure: it looks at what this share is structurally tied to. Most often this is what it is tied to at the moment of entering the game; stakes placed only after the match has begun are treated the same way (4.2). There are four checkable structural markers: stakes, contracts, prize money and the demands of others. In gambling, winning and losing are tied to the stakes; in a match with prize money, the result is tied to the prize money; when professional players take the field, a contract is attached; writing to satisfy an editor is tied to the demands of others, and is work.

Playing for the sake of one's own practice is not among these four, and it keeps the focal point on play (this share is still tied to what lies inside the game, and is play-directed): someone who wants to practice, once in the game, is all the same playing this game of chess. Whether "wanting to practice" is, for a given person, a passing intention or a purpose on the line of "what I should become" depends on what he actually holds at this moment, which this paper, following the Laughter Series and Judgment and Weeping, calls "what is held" (Section 7); whichever it is, it does not change the focal point.

3.2 Each Kind of Material Has Its Own Office

Three things come up in discussing the focal point, and each has a different office. The checkable material is rules, agreements and settlement relations; who stakes and who pays is a checkable item within the settlement relations, used to show how participation is taken up, not to derive the boundary. The auxiliary supposition is the deletion test: remove the thing outside the game and see whether it is still that game; this needs grounds and cannot bear weight on its own. The fallible hint is the subsequent drift of talk: what it looks at is attention, and attention is recorded by subject; it does not enter the determination of the focal point, nor can it be inferred backwards from whether someone plays or not: a person who stops playing may simply be tired.

3.3 Focal Point and Focus on Separate Axes; the Focal Point Can Be Split into Shares

The "focus" of the Laughter Series and Judgment and Weeping is whether attention falls on the remainder or on the construct; this paper's "focal point" is structural and says whether this share is tied to what lies inside the game or outside it. The two do not lie on one axis and can cross. This paper does not use the word "focus" to speak of the focal point.

Winning and losing in a game can be tied at once to the game and to something outside it, so the focal point can be split into shares. When a match has prize money, the match is both play and work, and the two shares are looked at separately; professional players lean more toward play during the match, while preparation and discussion outside the match lean more toward work. "Leaning toward play" speaks of what is primary and what is secondary in the structure; it does not claim already to know the proportions of attention in the player's mind: during the match, every move is taken up by the construct of the game, and prize money and contract are tied only to the result; preparation outside the match, which is not itself a game, leans toward work. What is primary and what is secondary is settled by which construct is taking up the moves at this moment.

Sportsmanship is a direction: toward treating the match as play. The world of sport may require it of competitors; that is the field's own convention. This paper writes no "ought" for players.

Rankings can lie inside the game. Rankings and results decided by the rules lie inside the game; how they are tied to income, qualification or contracts is another matter. League points cross games relative to a single match, and yet may be part of the constitutive rules relative to the league as a whole.

This point concerns only the focal point, not the layer. The layer, by the method of the Laughter Series and Judgment and Weeping, depends on what the subject actually holds (Section 7).

3.4 Doing Things with Play

Laughter III, 1.3 separates laughter as judgment from doing things with laughter, and Judgment and Weeping separates weeping from doing things with weeping; following them, this paper separates doing things with play from play. Playing to win something else, arranging games for others, institutions organizing games (military exercises or leagues, for instance): all of these are doing things with play. Doing things with play can be cultivation, and it can be colonization (Section 9); doing it to satisfy the demands of others is work. In general: as regards a given share, where play itself is not the focal point, it is not called play; the other shares in the game are each looked at on their own (Section 4). Playing for the sake of one's own practice does not belong in this list (3.1).

3.5 The Subject Will Never Be Forever Without the Intention to Take Part in Play

This paper's position on play as human nature is this: the subject has judgment and so will want to improve it; play is a natural way in; the subject will never be forever without the intention to take part in play.

This sentence speaks of play as a whole class, not of any particular game. A particular game must be entered of one's own accord and must be possible to leave (6.1, condition 4); one is free not to play, and free to stop. The sentence does not contradict any of this, nor does it reach so far as whether a particular game can be stopped.

"Intention" here is taken in its everyday sense, not as the intention of Laughter III, 2.2 cited in 7.4. For a given person, whether it is a passing intention or a purpose on the line of "what I should become" depends on what he actually holds; whichever it is, it does not change the focal point. So it does not contradict "play has no purpose outside the game": the focal point is read by structure, the layer by what is held (7.1).

Nor does it follow from this sentence that anyone must be playing some game at this moment, or that someone who cannot be seen to be playing lacks anything. This follows the same method as Judgment and Weeping 1.4, which says that the overlap of cannot-nots "cannot be turned into an external demand, nor into an external verdict".

Of the word "cannot-not", this paper keeps only the upstream sense tied to judgment. If that judgment in a game is felt as the acknowledgment of beauty, as laughter or as weeping, its cannot-not is as the upstream papers state it (Laughter Overview, 5.2; Judgment and Weeping 1.4); whether the judgment in the game is itself a cannot-not, this paper does not say.

4. On Whose Share the Verdict Falls

4.1 The Unit

The verdict "play or not" falls on a given participant's share in this game; of the activity as a whole, one speaks only of its structural orientation. This is not the same unit as the one used in reading the layer: whether something is play is read by structure and falls on a share; the layer and the judgment are read by what is held and fall on a judgment (7.1).

4.2 Some Examples

When a player bets on the match, it is no longer play for that player himself, while the match, proceeding normally, is still play. His share is tied to the stakes placed on that game, and it makes no difference whether they were placed before or after the match began (this paper's reading).

Military exercises, like sports matches, are structurally both play and work. Those taking part are looked at one by one under 6.1, conditions 3 and 4; how to view the share of someone who takes part under orders and cannot leave is left to Section 13. Holding exercises to prepare for war is an institution doing things with play.

In education, the share of the one who arranges the game is doing things with play; the child's share in the game is looked at under the four conditions. A conflict between parents in front of a child, if it is a real conflict, is not play; if it is a sham conflict staged to educate the child, the focal point again lies outside the game, and it does not count as play either. If the child does not know it is staged, he is not a player who has entered the game, and that scene does not form a game for the child.

For someone who has been dragged in and cannot leave, it is only for him that that game is not play.

Cheating is breaking, or covertly altering, the rules both sides acknowledge, so that the game the opponent entered diverges from the game actually being played. When one side cheats, the cheater's share is non-play-directed and no longer counts as play; the opponent is playing according to the game he entered, and his share is still play; whether this amounts to colonization of the opponent is a separate question. When all the participants cheat together, it is no longer play. Cheating does not necessarily close off the remainder; it may only change the odds (Paper II).

4.3 Gambling and Matches with Prize Money

Gambling is not play; a game without stakes is play, and its focal point is the game itself (games of pure chance are treated separately as a boundary condition; see 2.4). This paper states the exclusion of gambling explicitly as a scope convention of this series, on the ground that the focal point lies outside the game. When a player bets on the match, what is excluded is his share, not the whole match.

Taken share by share: in gambling, the share of each person in the game is tied to the stakes, and the focal point lies outside the game; in a match with prize money, each participant has two shares side by side, play and work. The ground is whether the focal point lies outside the game; the items below only help to see this clearly and are not used to derive a universal criterion for drawing the line. Both have rules as their construct; whether 6.1, condition 1 is met does not affect the scope convention here. Settlement relations show how participation is taken up: stakes pass, with the outcome, from the losing side to the winning side, and the other party may be a player or the house; prize money, by contrast, is paid by a party that has staked nothing on the outcome of this game; chips that are counted only within the game and cannot be taken out of it are a way of keeping score, not stakes; trophies and certificates only mark a ranking, and lie inside the game. Removing the thing outside the game and looking again can also serve as an aid, but it is not load-bearing: the same card game without stakes has its focal point back inside the game; remove the prize money, and the outside tie formed by that prize money is removed with it, while the other ties, such as contracts, preparation for the match and the demands of others, still have to be looked at one by one.

As for judgment, a share whose focal point lies outside the game can have judgment in the game all the same: someone playing cards for money can just as well see that "this hand can still be played like this". Whether there was practice, and how far it went, depends on what actually happened in this game (Section 5). In this series the example serves only to hold the scope.

Whether entry fees pooled into a prize pot that the winner takes count as stakes, this paper does not conclude; it is left to Section 13.

5. The Same Game Can Be Played Again

5.1 The Main Thesis

The same game can be played again, and each time judgment can differ. This does not mean taking back a move within one game, but that the same game can be started again, time after time.

This is the main thesis of this paper, and it does not enter the definition: an improvised game, played once and never again, is still play.

5.2 Restarting

Restarting is returning to the starting point the rules lay down; how the results, points and records of the previous game are carried into the new one is up to the rules. Judgments, injuries, fatigue and trust that have already occurred are not revoked by a restart. For the item of judgment, this paper borrows the second half of Judgment and Weeping 4.1, condition 6: reflexive negation can afterwards withdraw the validity of what was judged; it cannot withdraw the fact that the jolt happened. Nor does a restart guarantee that judgment changes, still less that it improves.

5.3 What Is Practiced Is Judgment

What is practiced is judgment, not the same judgment. So whether practice has occurred cannot be settled merely by whether what was judged before and after is the same or different: judging the same way twice does not show that there was no practice; judging differently twice does not show that there was. Practice falls on judgment as a power, not on any single judgment.

The minimal commitment is this: judgment is actually put to use, and put to use anew, time after time in the same game, owned by the person himself. The ownership connects with Laughter II's "this judgment is mine": that judgment is his, and what is put to use is his judgment. In a game played only once, the one time judgment is put to use is the first of the "time after time".

As for practice in play as this series treats it, practice has to come down to the subject actually seeing the remainder of the game, owned by the person himself. This states how such practice is recorded; it does not determine where the focal point lies, nor does it exclude the practice of judgment outside play.

Each time, judgment can differ. Whether it does differ, where the difference lies, whether it can be retained, and whether it carries over to outside the game all need separate testing and are left to Section 13.

5.4 Tell It Again

A small child wanting to hear a story he knows well yet again is precisely the same game played again. Let us return to the child at the beginning (0.1) and work the example through.

The familiar course of the story is still there, and the convention of telling and listening already allows the child to chime in: this is the construct. The teller stops just before the familiar line; the course of the story does not lay down who is to say this line, and this point, which the construct has not fully taken in and which is nonetheless right there before one's eyes, is the remainder. The child does not merely foresee that the next line is "Here comes the wolf!"; he sees that the same line can, as it turns out, still be picked up by him, here. "At this point it can still be picked up like this" is judgment in this paper's sense; foreseeing the next line is material (2.3). Whether to speak up, and how to shout it, is action around that judgment; if he merely shouts out the familiar words by habit, the shouting alone does not show that he has practiced.

If this time it really was he who saw it and he who picked it up, then, in terms of the minimal commitment, judgment has once again been put to use in the same game, owned by him (5.3). This does not prove that his judgment is better than last time; neither judging the same way nor judging differently from before can prove such an improvement; nor does it mean that next time he will certainly pick it up like this again.

Laughter Overview, 4.3 also lists children asking again and again to hear the same joke as a difficulty of the same kind. One of its candidate explanations is that one stands once more in the place of the self that had not yet seen, re-enacting the original gap; from this it infers (again as a candidate) that the order and the pauses of the telling still matter. This paper's reading is different: what is seen this time can be a new remainder. The two can be read side by side; neither is evidence for the other.

6. Definition and Boundaries

6.1 Four Conditions

  1. Game. There are rules as a construct, and the rules do not exhaust the grasping and responding that participants still have to do (Section 2). The rules can be tacit, but one must be able to point out what constraints this stretch of interaction actually keeps to: which moves or signals are taken up, and in what way, and what would change or end this game. Bateson's "This is play" is a hint of such a convention; the naming alone cannot prove that something is play. This is a weak condition; the weight falls on conditions 3 and 4. Casual conversation counts as play in the broad sense, just as a race two people decide on, on the spur of the moment, also counts.
  2. Person. At least one subject takes part in this game. Whether there is in fact judgment in this game, and whether there is practice, depends on the instance; where no material can point to judgment or practice, this paper does not say that there is any in this game.
  3. Focal point. Structurally, a focal point inside the game is play-directed, and one outside the game is non-play-directed (Section 3). A non-play-directed share is doing things with play, or work; the game is still a game.
  4. Voluntariness. Everyone in the game entered it of his own accord and can also leave; in a solo game, this condition concerns only the one who plays. Toward a party not yet counted as a subject, the way for it to turn away and not respond is not sealed off either; chimpanzees and the like are discussed separately in Section 8. For someone who has been dragged in and cannot leave, it is only for him that that game is not play; dragging him in is substituting my purpose for his law, that is, colonization (in the broad sense of Laughter Overview, 7.3). Bullying is colonization. Once someone is in the game, if the way out is completely blocked, the same holds: for him that game is not play; he is colonized. A game designed so that one cannot stop may still be play if the way out remains open, but it plainly infringes the right of exit (Paper I).

6.3 War and Conflict; "Outside Ordinary Life"

War and conflict are both not play; in neither is the focal point play itself. War is conflict between states: the attacking side acts to colonize, the defending side to resist colonization. "Attack" and "defence" refer to the structure discussed in §5 of the SAE War Treatise, in which a 14DD side expands and a 15DD side defends, not to who in fact strikes first; the pairing "attack as colonization, defence as resistance to colonization" applies only to war. In a conflict there may be no colonizing side; it is also possible that both sides are resisting colonization; how to read this is handed over to the SAE Conflict Theory, and this paper does not develop it. Military exercises and boxing matches count as play.

Huizinga puts "outside ordinary life" into the definition; this paper does not adopt that item: it cannot, without interpretation, be equated with "harm does not carry over to outside the game". The boxing example serves only to illustrate this point and does not on its own constitute a counterexample to him (11.2).

7. Three Kinds of Game; the Layer Lies with the Subject

7.1 Two Ledgers

This series keeps two ledgers.

The game is recorded by structure. How many positions there are in the game, who takes up each, and how what each seeks stands toward what the others seek. The reading side may as yet have no real reader, as in writing: the reader imagined by the writer does not count as someone already in the game (7.8).

The layer is recorded by subject. At which layer a given person stands at a given moment depends on what he actually holds, and is often mixed. The layer sometimes cannot be read from outside; where it cannot, one says the material is insufficient, and does not fix the layer for him.

Judgment and practice also each have their unit. Judgment is analyzed by that judgment of a given person at a given moment (Laughter Overview, 6.1); practice speaks of this subject's judgment actually being put to use, and put to use anew, in the game, owned by the person himself (Section 5).

This method of keeping separate ledgers is borrowed from Laughter Overview, 6.1: "The layer of a laugh is set by the layer at which the laugher stands at that moment; the layer of the construct laughed at is recorded separately." What this series borrows is the method; this does not mean that the Laughter Series has already proved this series' threefold division; the threefold division is shown by this series, paper by paper.

It is hard to say that a game is at some layer. A layer is something the subject can himself stand at in the game, and very often it is mixed. 7.3 The Solo Game and the Yardstick

Why does the solo game newly open 13DD? The game needs a subject; opposite there are only rules and material, so what the game sets out is only "this move is mine", and nothing sought by another person. So the solo game too has a layer it newly opens.

In a solo game, if the rules set up a yardstick one can lose to, such as a score, a time limit, a record or one's previous self, the subject can stand at 14DD. Clearing a level, finishing a puzzle or solving a problem is an end state, not a yardstick. The rules are counted as the constitutive rules actually adopted this time.

7.4 The Competitive Game and Intention

The competitive game sets up winning and losing as what is contested, and opposite stands another subject, who seeks the same as I do, the two excluding each other. In establishing the possibility of losing, it is like the solo game with a yardstick; another subject standing opposite is what the competitive game adds. The game thereby invites "I want to win" to become the purpose of this moment, but does not guarantee it.

Following Laughter III, 2.2, an intention is not a purpose. A casual wish to win in a competitive game is a passing intention and does not yet reach 14DD; as regards this item of what is held, he often stands at 13DD, but where he stands still has to be looked at on its own; the other things he holds at the same moment are also looked at separately. Only when wanting to win falls on the line of "what I should become" (Laughter III, 1.1) does it reach 14DD.

7.5 The Cooperative Game and 15DD

Why does the cooperative game newly open 15DD? In both kinds of two-person game, the move of the one opposite is his. The difference lies in what is sought: in a competitive game, his move is what I want to beat; in a cooperative game, his move is part of what we want together, and this game comes off only when his move is still his and also joins up with mine. The writing side of the telling–listening game is treated separately; see 7.8 and Section 13.

So what the cooperative game sets out is a partner who makes his own judgments, and what he seeks lies in one place with what I seek. This is what "acknowledging him, not occupying his place in advance", in what is held at 15DD, looks like in behaviour; acknowledgment itself the game can only invite. The division of labour between the two, agreed moves and well-practiced choreography do not by themselves cancel his position as a subject either: such a structure leaves conditions for not occupying his place in advance, but does not guarantee that I have acknowledged him. Whether instrumental treatment has crushed his participation in the game is a separate question; my stance alone cannot change the structure of the game.

15DD can not only be practiced; it is very much worth practicing; worth it, but it cannot be forced, much like cultivation. "Cannot be forced" holds at two ends: others cannot force it, for forcing someone into a game is colonization (6.1, condition 4); nor can one force it on oneself: gritting one's teeth to make oneself acknowledge him at this moment is willpower, not a stance.

So the item of what is held that is aimed, at this moment, at "practicing 15DD" cannot reach 15DD; two doors stand in the way. One is toward him: to treat the partner as material for my practice is to require him to serve my purpose; Judgment and Weeping 4.1, condition 4 says "not requiring his loss to serve my good heart or my purpose", and carrying this into the game here is this series' extension. The other is toward oneself: non dubito is "not through self-coercion" (Psychoanalysis IV, §2.2).

On the same occasion, the player can also hold other things, such as wanting to dance this dance well; these are looked at separately, and having aims of one's own does not mean there is no 15DD at this moment.

"Worth practicing" comes down to going and playing, and to giving play the conditions in which it can happen. These are developed in Paper III.

7.6 Reading the Layer

The layer is read by the four rules following the table in Judgment and Weeping, Section 3:

  • if a demand is withdrawn, re-read by the changed operation;
  • if self-report conflicts with checkable words and deeds, that is a conflict of material;
  • if different acts or shares of attention run in parallel, look at them separately; extending this rule to several things held in parallel is this series' extension;
  • if there is only self-report, the material is insufficient.

The layer sometimes cannot be read from outside; where it cannot, it is not fixed for him.

7.7 The Timed Jigsaw

Someone is doing a jigsaw puzzle and wants to finish it in ten minutes. Opposite are the rules and the pieces: this is a solo game.

Whether the ten minutes have entered the constitutive rules of this game is the first question. If, before entering the game, he lays down that "it counts as done only if finished within ten minutes", the ten minutes become this game's condition of success, and are a yardstick; if it is only a wish and has not entered the condition of success, it is not a yardstick. Whether it has entered is read from checkable material: a timer set, a condition of success announced, a time recorded; if there is only self-report, the material is insufficient. With a yardstick, he can stand at 14DD; without one, 13DD opens as usual.

But the ten minutes having entered the constitutive rules shows only that this game has a yardstick set. Whether he has at this moment an ordinary intention or a purpose on the line of "what I should become" still depends on what he actually holds; whether or not he looks at the clock cannot, on its own, fix the layer. Where this cannot be seen clearly, one says the material is insufficient. In both cases the focal point lies inside the game, and both are play-directed: the yardstick is a rule inside the game, not something outside it.

Racing. Anthropologically, racing as play goes back very early (the author's observation; the anthropological material to be verified). In the historical sources, the funeral games held for Patroclus in Book XXIII of the Iliad include a footrace; Koroibos, the first recorded victor of the ancient Olympics, won the stadion sprint in 776 BC by the traditional dating (secondhand).

Those who watch are in the game too. Watching a ball game, a play or a film is play, and the watcher is in the game too. The telling–listening and performing–watching games have more of cooperation in them: in a live performance, performers and audience interact and create together. Watchers are not set up as a separate kind; the spectators at a sports match are also part of that match as a game, so someone watching a ball game is in the competitive game of Paper II or the mixtures of Paper III, not in the performing–watching game.

Writing. Writing, too, is not solitary: the writer has to imagine how the reader will read, as when planting a clue. While writing, the writer imagines for himself a virtual reader, that is, how he himself would think if he were the reader. So writing does not go into Paper I; it belongs to the writing side of the telling–listening game in Paper III; planting a clue is the "hiding" of hiding and guessing. At the moment of writing, a structure facing the reader is already set up: construct and remainder are organized toward another person's reading, without waiting for a reader actually to come. The use of writing in this series is that the writer changes places between the two positions of writing and reading (Paper III). Writing to satisfy an editor is work.

Casual conversation. Both people's focal point is on this conversation, and the partner's next remark is his, so it belongs to Paper III (this paper's reading).

8. Scope and Exclusions

The scope of this series is play in which a subject takes part; the subject is counted at 13DD (the terminology lock of Laughter Overview, 6.4).

Downward, several things are registered only, not developed. The tendency of 12DD, such as peek-a-boo on the infant's side; on the adult's side of the same game there is a subject, and that side is treated by this series. The play of animals; chimpanzees are read, following Laughter I, 4.2, as having entered the early stage of 13DD, and how their play is to be viewed is left to Section 13. The perceptual layer: by the method Laughter Overview, 8.2 uses for tickling, it is split into two stages; the jolt of dizziness is at 10DD; how high to swing, whether one dares let go, when to stop and how to coordinate with others already make a game with a subject, and the line between the two stages is left to Section 13.

Upward, 16DD, in which each takes the other as an end, is registered only.

What is not play: work, gambling (scope convention), bullying, war and conflict, and conflict between parents in front of a child (Section 6). Games of pure chance are a boundary condition (2.4). Doing things with play is kept apart from play (3.4). The estimate that, in hearing stories, one laughs first and weeps later belongs to the Laughter Series (Section 13).

9. Practice, Training, Work, Cultivation

As regards the play-directed share, the practice of judgment is a result. Arranging games for someone else's practice is doing things with play; playing for the sake of one's own practice keeps the focal point on the game (3.1). For the minimal commitment of practice, see Section 5; what it leaves behind needs separate testing. Work has its focal point outside the game; writing to satisfy an editor is work. Of training, this paper defines only one kind: repetition that is not itself a game and whose focal point is the next game or the next assessment leans toward work; this is not a common definition of all training. Practice matches and military exercises that are games in themselves are play; for someone who plays them for the sake of his own practice, the focal point is still on the game, and the work share comes from his post, a contract or an assessment.

Arranging games for others is doing things with play; it can be cultivation, and here this paper connects with Not Legislating for Others (SAE Action Theory, Paper 6). D34 says that cultivation needs three things, that the other be willing to receive it, that he be suited to receive it, and that the time be ripe, and that none of the three can be answered on the other's behalf; and that cultivation therefore necessarily leaves blank space: what is left is not words but the other's own part: whether he accepts, how he judges, whether he changes, whether he leaves. D35 says that what still cannot be cancelled includes not legislating for the other, and not sealing off his channels of refusal, revision, exit and change of direction; this connects with the "can also leave" of 6.1, condition 4, and also with the right of exit in the example of games one cannot stop (Paper I). The one who designs a game one cannot stop is not necessarily cultivating; applying D35 there is this series' extension. D36 says that once something is claimed by his own law, it is his: in a game someone else arranges, the result that is practice is owned by the player; this item states only to whom it belongs constitutively, and does not imply that the one who arranged it contributed nothing. D37 says that the one who cultivates can also be cultivated, which connects with the "create together" of live performance in Paper III.

Doing things with play can also be colonization: forcing someone into a game and sealing off the way out (6.1, condition 4); using consequences to force someone to claim a win or a loss, or a judgment (Laughter Overview, 7.3).

There are two places upstream to connect with. The sentence in Education as Subject-Condition, §3.2, says that peek-a-boo is not merely a game for amusing small children but a training device by which 12DD is built. It speaks of the role this activity plays in building prediction at 12DD, on the side of developmental function; it does not follow that the players must take training as their purpose; this paper governs 13DD and above, on the players' side, and does not rewrite the upstream paper. Education as Subject-Condition, §7.3, places one meaning of prenatal education on the mother's side: for the mother, it is practice at 14DD–15DD. This paper borrows this as a contrast (this paper's reading): if on a given occasion the mother is directed toward the child, practice can occur on the mother's side as a result. This does not bring prenatal education into this series' play, nor does it use one upstream sentence to determine the actual layer of any occasion of prenatal education.

10. Contrast: Schiller (the Main Contrast)

10.1 The Passages Used

The passages in Schiller's Letters on the Aesthetic Education of Man (1795) that bear on this paper:

  • Letter 15: "der Mensch spielt nur, wo er in voller Bedeutung des Worts Mensch ist, und er ist nur da ganz Mensch, wo er spielt."
  • Letters 14 and 15: the play drive is the one in which the sensuous drive and the form drive act in combination; its object "wird also lebende Gestalt heißen können", that is, beauty in the widest sense. For the sentence "der Mensch soll mit der Schönheit nur spielen, und er soll nur mit der Schönheit spielen", this paper works directly from the German.
  • Letter 21: "denn die Schönheit gibt schlechterdings kein einzelnes Resultat, weder für den Verstand noch für den Willen"; by this, man "von Natur wegen möglich gemacht ist, aus sich selbst zu machen, was er will".
  • Letter 23: the aesthetic state "an sich selbst weder für unsere Einsichten noch Gesinnungen etwas entscheidet", and yet it is "die nothwendige Bedingung"; "es gibt keinen andern Weg, den sinnlichen Menschen vernünftig zu machen, als daß man denselben zuvor ästhetisch macht".
  • Letter 2: "weil es die Schönheit ist, durch welche man zu der Freiheit wandert".

10.2 Two Points of Proximity

One is that play comes from human nature. The sentence in Letter 15 places play where the human being is human; this paper says that play is human nature (0.2).

The other is deciding nothing on a person's behalf. The aesthetic in itself decides nothing for cognition or will; it only makes a person able "aus sich selbst zu machen, was er will". This is very close to this paper's "practice is a result", and close too to the blank space that cultivation leaves (Section 9): play supplies conditions, and putting judgment to use is the player's own affair.

10.3 Two Points of Divergence

One is whether it is the only path or one path. He sets up the aesthetic state as the necessary condition for making the sensuous human being rational, "keinen andern Weg", no other way; this paper says that play is one path for the practice of judgment, not the only path.

The other is the object of play. His play drive is directed at beauty, and man "soll nur mit der Schönheit spielen", should play only with beauty; in this paper, beauty is one expression of judgment, play is one path for the practice of judgment, and the remainder in a game need not be beauty.

10.4 "Ganz Mensch"

The sentence about being "ganz Mensch", fully human, has to be read back in the context of the two drives and of the whole of one's capacities; it is not to be reduced to rating how complete a person is by how much everyday play he does. The limit of 0.4 still holds here: someone who does not play is not thereby lacking anything; nor does this paper, on the ideal of human wholeness, set up universal demands on people.

The ranges of what the two call "play" must first be aligned; this is a comparison of theoretical positions and is not used as counterevidence against one and the same empirical proposition. Schiller's educational side is developed in Paper III.

11. Further Contrasts

11.1 Kant

§9 of the Critique of Judgment says that in the aesthetic judgment the cognitive powers are "in einem freien Spiele" (AA 5:217). For him, free play is the state of mind in the aesthetic judgment; in this paper, play is one path for the practice of judgment; the "play" of the two places is not the same thing.

The Remark to §54 says: "Alles wechselnde freie Spiel der Empfindungen (die keine Absicht zum Grunde haben) vergnügt, weil es das Gefühl der Gesundheit befördert" (5:331); and he divides it into "Glücksspiel, Tonspiel und Gedankenspiel". This paper goes in the same direction as this passage only as far as the clause "die keine Absicht zum Grunde haben"; and "keine Absicht" takes gambling in as well. The "weil es das Gefühl der Gesundheit befördert" that follows is a functional explanation, on the same side as the functional theory that this paper does not take as its answer (1.2).

On gambling, he says that the gain in it "bei weitem nicht so groß ist, als das Interesse an der Art, wie wir es uns zu verschaffen suchen". This compares the strength of two interests and is a matter of the inner life; this paper looks only at structure and does not judge this piece of psychology on his behalf (3.1). He at once sets gambling aside with "da das Glücksspiel kein schönes Spiel ist"; what gambling is excluded from is the "schönes Spiel" discussed in that place, not play as such.

11.2 Huizinga

The definition in chapter 1 of Homo ludens (1938) contains the following items: a free action; consciously standing outside ordinary life; able to absorb the player completely; "geen direct materieel belang"; within deliberately bounded time and space; according to fixed rules; giving rise to communities.

"Geen direct materieel belang", no direct material interest, is itself a structural formulation, close to 6.1, condition 3. In the definition of chapter 1 it is a restriction on the activity of play as a whole; this paper, by contrast, looks at ties inside and outside the game separately, share by share. What is compared here is only the unit in which this definition is formulated: in chapter 3 he also says that the stakes of play can have material value, "Prijs kan een lauwerkrans zijn of een geldsom, of elke andere materieele waarde" (p. 79).

This paper does not adopt "outside ordinary life" (6.3).

Discussing war, he says that the contest element is at work when the warring parties regard each other as antagonists fighting for something they have a right to contend for; with the theory of "total war", the last trace of war's cultural function, together with its play function, is given up (chapter 5, p. 118). This paper says that war is not play. He looks at the contest element in war; this paper looks at whether the focal point is the game itself.

11.3 Wittgenstein

Philosophical Investigations §66: "Wie gesagt: denk nicht, sondern schau!"; §67: "die ›Spiele‹ bilden eine Familie"; §69: "Wie gesagt, wir können – für einen besondern Zweck – eine Grenze ziehen."

This paper does not present him as forbidding definition, nor does it use a definition to refute him. It draws this class for its own purpose, under two conditions: claims such as those about human nature and practice are made only of the class so defined; and the procedure is the same as in Judgment and Weeping 4.1, both drawing a class for the paper's own purpose, though this class is not as narrow as the one there. Whether the four conditions are circular, and whether they have explanatory power, is to be shown by the analyses in the papers of this series.

12. Terms

  • Construct, remainder (构、余项): following Laughter Overview, Section 0 and 2.1. A construct is any way of grasping that is currently in operation; a remainder is the point that the construct has not fully taken in, and that is nonetheless right there before one's eyes.
  • Game (局): a stretch of interaction that takes rules as its construct; the rules can be tacit, but the constraints it actually keeps to must be possible to point out (6.1, condition 1).
  • Judgment, seeing (判断、判见): judgment in this paper's sense is seeing, that is, seeing that "this game still has such a remainder". Which move to make is action; prediction is material (2.3).
  • Focal point (焦点): structural; it says whether this share is tied to what lies inside the game or outside it; tied inside, it is play-directed; tied outside, non-play-directed (Section 3). It does not lie on one axis with the "focus" (聚焦) of the Laughter Series and Judgment and Weeping: focus says whether attention falls on the remainder or on the construct. This paper does not use the word "focus" to speak of the focal point.
  • Share (份): reserved for the unit of the verdict, which belongs to the focal-point axis, that is, "a given participant's share in this game" (Section 4). The "several things held in parallel" spoken of for the layer, and the "shares of attention" spoken of for attention, are different from this "share".
  • Doing things with play (用游戏做事): action around play, kept apart from play (3.4).
  • The layer newly opened (新打开的层): the layer a kind of game newly opens, at which the subject can stand; not the layer of the game (7.2). Opening is not a guarantee.
  • Yardstick (尺): in a solo game, something set up by the rules that one can lose to: a score, a time limit, a record, one's previous self. Clearing a level, finishing a puzzle or solving a problem is not a yardstick (7.3).
  • Practice (练习): judgment actually put to use, and put to use anew, in a game, time after time, owned by the person himself; as regards the play-directed share, it is a result, not a purpose (0.2, Section 5).
  • Training (训练): this paper defines only one kind: repetition that is not itself a game and whose focal point is the next game or the next assessment; it leans toward work (Section 9).
  • Cultivation, colonization (涵育、殖民): following Not Legislating for Others (SAE Action Theory, Paper 6); colonization is taken in the broad sense of Laughter Overview, 7.3: substituting one's own purpose for the other's own law (Section 9).
  • Insufficient material (材料不足): when there is only self-report and no checkable words or deeds, the layer is not fixed for him (7.6).
  • Cannot-not (不得不): not set up as a term of this series; only its upstream sense, tied to judgment, is kept. For play as a whole class, the paper says "the subject will never be forever without the intention to take part in play" (3.5).
  • English: first apply this series' definitions, then fix the rendering context by context; it is not presupposed that 游戏 and 局 correspond word for word to play and game. Everything else follows the Laughter Series glossary and the Judgment and Weeping addendum.

13. Questions Left Open

On practice. What practice leaves behind: whether judgment differs, whether it can be retained, whether it carries over to outside the game; this paper answers none of these (Section 5). Whether "much like cultivation" can be said of oneself toward oneself is also a question: SAE Relation Theory Paper 2: Yesterday, Today, Tomorrow says that the past self can cultivate or colonize the present self; this reaches the cross-temporal side, but not the side of "gritting one's teeth at this moment" to make oneself acknowledge him, that is, the side "toward oneself" of the second door in 7.5.

On boundaries. Games of pure chance are only a boundary condition; no conclusion is drawn (2.4). Whether entry fees pooled into a prize pot, the money coming from the competitors and the winner taking it, count as stakes: no conclusion is drawn (4.3). Cheating together can be each participant breaking, or covertly altering, the acknowledged rules, or it can be collusion directed at a third party outside the game; everyone openly agreeing to change a rule is starting a new game; how the two are told apart, this paper does not develop (4.2). Research out of curiosity, or a meal cooked for fun, can be let in by the weak condition of 6.1, condition 1; this paper does not go through them case by case (6.1).

On military exercises and work. For the share of someone taking part in an exercise under orders, this paper's reading is: if he cannot leave because of the work he has taken on (a post, a contract), it counts as work; if it is because someone legislates for him and drags him in, it falls under 6.1, condition 4. How he entered that work itself (conscription, for example), this paper does not judge. The same holds for a match in which a professional player must take the field under contract (4.2). Institutions organizing games, that is, doing things with play at the level of institutions, also awaits separate treatment (3.4).

On conflict and exit. How is a conflict in which both sides are resisting colonization to be read? One reading is that what is contested is not assigned to either side in advance; another is that each side holds its position of not being legislated for, and one side is not judged a colonizer in advance just because the two laws are incompatible. Both are handed over to the SAE Conflict Theory, and this paper does not develop them (6.3). How the right of exit corresponds to the SAE Rights Theory, and its concrete mechanisms and application, await separate treatment (6.1, condition 4; Paper I).

On writing. When, and whether, writing forms a game as a cooperative game, this paper does not settle; the use of writing in this series is changing places, which need not wait for this question to be answered (7.5, 7.8).

On scope. How the play of chimpanzees and other animals that have entered the early stage of 13DD is to be viewed (Laughter I, 4.2; Section 8), and where the line falls between the perceptual layer and games with a subject (Section 8): both await separate treatment. Education as Subject-Condition, §3.2, says that 15DD has its own peek-a-boo: the parents, in front of the child, going through conflict, acknowledgment, conflict, acknowledgment; and it says that "the 15DD peek-a-boo" is not a designed game but the quality of the relationship between the parents. The Education paper borrows the word peek-a-boo as a structural analogy; by Section 4, conflict between parents is not play; whether it is anyone's practice of judgment, this paper does not judge.

Conclusion

This series is about how judgment is practiced, and it takes up only one path, play. Play is human nature; the focal point is the game itself; as regards the play-directed share, the practice of judgment is a result, not a purpose, and whether participants themselves take practice as their purpose is another matter; what is practiced is judgment, not the same judgment. The same game can be played again, and each time judgment can differ. A game takes rules as its construct, and the rules do not exhaust the grasping and responding that participants still have to do; judgment in this paper's sense is seeing that this game still has such a remainder, and which move to make is another matter.

Whether something is play is read from structure alone: if this share is tied to what lies inside the game, it is play-directed; if it is tied to something outside the game, it is non-play-directed. The verdict falls on a given participant's share in this game, so a match can be play and work at once; when a player bets on the match, it is only for him that it is not play; for someone dragged in who cannot leave, it is only for him that it is not play; and when all the participants cheat together, it is no longer play. Playing for the sake of one's own practice keeps the focal point on play. The layer lies with the subject and is often mixed: the solo game, the competitive game and the cooperative game newly open, respectively, 13DD, 14DD and 15DD as layers at which the subject can stand, and opening is not a guarantee. 15DD is worth practicing; it cannot be forced, much like cultivation.

The subject has judgment and so will want to improve it; play is a natural way in. This paper is about judgment, not a theory of play: gambling is handled by a scope convention, games of pure chance as a boundary condition, and war and conflict are not play; all of these are registered only and are not load-bearing. Schiller is the main contrast: both say that play comes from human nature; he says that the aesthetic decides nothing for cognition or will, which is close to this paper's "practice is a result"; he sets up the aesthetic as the only path, while this paper says play is one path. Groos is the nearest neighbour: he asks why play exists in nature, this paper asks where judgment falls in a game.

美学、笑论与哭论写的是判断力的表现：美是看见了不得不认可，笑是判断直接被感到，哭是经由情被感到的那一下。本系列是判断力这一侧的最后一个系列，不再写判断力本身，而写它怎样练习。练习的路不只一条，本系列只写游戏这一条。本文是总论，立场是：游戏是人的天性，焦点在局本身；就游戏向的那一份而言，判断力的练习是结果，不是目的，参与者自己是否以练为目的，是另一回事；练的是判断力，不是同一个判断。主命题是：同样的局可以重来，每次判断力可以不同。

局以规则作构，规则不穷尽参与者仍需作出的把握与回应。本文所说的判断，是看出"这一局还有这么一个余项"；走哪一步，是围绕这一判的动作。游戏以四条来界定：局、人、焦点、自愿。焦点只看结构，不看内心：这一份接在局内，是游戏向；接在局外，是非游戏向。判词落在某一位在这一局里的一份上，所以一场比赛可以同时是游戏与工作，球员赌球只对他自己那一份不是游戏，所有参与者一起作弊，就不再是游戏了。三篇按局的结构分：一个人的局、竞争之局、合作之局，分别新打开主体可以站到的 13DD、14DD、15DD；层在主体身上，常是混合的，局本身不在哪一层。15DD 值得练，却无法强制，跟涵育很像。

〇 引言：问题与立场

0.1 问题

一个孩子要大人把听熟了的故事再讲一遍。他早知道狼会来，还是要听。讲到熟悉的那一句之前，大人停了一下；孩子没有等，自己把那一句接了出来。

他从这一遍里得到了什么？故事没有变，结局他也知道。只预计下一句是"狼来了"，是预测，是材料，还不是本文所说的判断（2.3）。本文要指出的是另一样：他看见，同一句话这一回可以由他在这里接上。"这一处还可以这样接"，是那一判；开不开口、怎样喊，是围绕这一判的动作。故事这一局重开了，他这一回看见的余项，可以和上一回不一样。同样的局可以重来，每次判断力可以不同。这是本文的主命题，这个例子在 5.4 走完。

这里说的孩子，已经在 13DD；更小的孩子那一边，是 12DD 的预测，只登记（第八节）。

美学总纲与十三射线、笑论五篇、笑论专论《判断力与哭》，写的是判断力作为表现。本系列是判断力这一侧的最后一个系列，问的是另一件事：判断力怎样练习。练习的路不只一条，本系列只写游戏这一条。

0.2 立场

游戏是人的天性。游戏没有局外的目的；不用"目的"一词，可以说焦点在局本身。本文说"焦点在局本身"，不说"游戏本身就是目的"，因为"目的"在 SAE 里是 14DD 的自目的，也是"人是目的"的那个词。

判断力的练习是结果，不是目的。练的是判断力，不是同一个判断。这说的是本文怎样说明游戏中的练习：就游戏向的这一份而言，练习是可能发生的结果，本文不拿"游戏是为了练"作总的解释。某人自己以练为目的，本文不否认（1.2）；这不改变焦点（3.1）。

游戏是一条路，不是唯一的路。本文不说只有游戏才能练判断力，只说判断力的练习可以经由游戏。

写的是判断力，不是游戏论。游戏的边界只用来守住范围，不承重。凡是只为把"游戏"划得更准、却不改判断力一侧任何一句话的推导，本文不作；边缘的例子留到第十三节。

辖域。本文只论有主体参与的游戏。动物也有游戏，不在本系列之内（第八节）。

为什么从游戏来写判断力的练习，见 3.5；那是一个理由，不是要求谁去玩。

0.3 三句

  • 局：规则是构，不穷尽参与者仍需作出的把握与回应。
  • 游戏：焦点在局本身。
  • 练习：练的是判断力；同样的局重来，判断力可以不同。

0.4 本文不主张的

本文不主张游戏让人变得更好，不主张练习一定留下稳定的改变，也不主张局里练成的判断力会移到局外；这些都要另受检验，留到第十三节。本文不主张人人应当游戏，也不评人：不爱玩某一类游戏的人，不因此被判为少了什么。本文也不主张凡游戏都保证练习：四条界定的第 2 条（6.1）只要求有主体参与这一局，这一局里实际有没有判断、有没有练，要看实例。最后，本文不统摄日常语言里一切叫"游戏"的东西。本文走界定的路：天性、练习这些主张，只对界定出的这一类说（第六节、11.3）。

本文的顺序是：先与最近的邻居 Groos 分开问题（第一节）；再立局、余项与判断（第二节），焦点（第三节），判词的单位（第四节），主命题（第五节），界定（第六节）；然后是三类局与三篇的分工（第七节），辖域（第八节），练习与涵育（第九节）；最后对照席勒与其余各家（第十、十一节）。

一 Groos：问的不是一件事

1.1 准备说

Karl Groos 在《动物的游戏》（1896）序言里说，游戏是相关本能的"Vorübung und Einübung"，与"der späteren ernsten Ausübung"相对（pp. V–VI）。常被引的那一句，原文带着限定："vielleicht ist die Einrichtung der Jugendzeit selbst zum Theil um der Spiele willen getroffen; die Thiere spielen nicht, weil sie jung sind, sondern sie haben eine Jugend, weil sie spielen müssen." 《人的游戏》（1899）英译本序言自称沿用同一套"practice theory"。该书导论把两条判据并列："The biological criterion of play is that it shall deal not with the serious exercise of the special instinct, but with practice preparatory to it"；"The psychological criterion corresponds with it; thus, when an act is performed solely because of the pleasure it affords, there is play"（英译 p. 5）。

1.2 问的不是一件事

Groos 答的是游戏为什么在自然里有：幼年也许部分是为游戏而设，游戏为将来认真的施行作预演。本文答的是局里判断力落在哪里。两边问的问题不同，说的关系也不同：他说的是游戏与将来认真施行的关系，本文说的是判断力与局的关系。这不是对同一个命题的相反回答；这与哭论 1.4 末段比较康德的办法相同。

所以本文不取功能说，是不拿它作本文的回答，并不否认它。功能的说明，可以与玩的人此刻不以练习为焦点并存：一个孩子追着球跑，可以同时在为将来练身体；可他此刻追的是球，这一份接在局里。本文也不说"练是玩的人自己的目的"，这同样只是不拿它作回答；有人此刻正是以练为目的在玩，本文不否认，这也不改他的焦点（3.1）。

两边可以比较的一处，是对将来的态度。准备说着重于将来的认真施行；本文只说判断力在局里用起来，至于它会不会移到局外，本文不主张（0.4）。

1.3 名称与 müssen

中文里，Groos 的说法常叫"练习说"，又称"生活准备说"。本文也用"练习"二字，所以一开头就要分开：他的练习，是本能为将来的施行作预演；本文的练习，是判断力在局里实际用起来，由本人承接（第五节）。

幼年那一句里的"spielen müssen"，是本能功能上的必须。本文说主体不会永远没有参与游戏的意图（3.5），说的是意图，不取功能说。两处并排写明，免得读成一件事。

Eisler《哲学概念词典》（1904）"Spiel"条转述说，Groos 把"Freude am »Ursachesein«"（作原因的快乐）列为游戏的一个环节；据二手材料，原书 S. 190 有此语，写作"Ursache-sein"。若此说属实，它说的是玩的人的快乐，是心里的事，可以与"焦点在局本身"对读，却不作它的根据：焦点按结构看，不由快乐落在哪里来定（3.1、3.3）。

1.4 亚里士多德：反向的一端

《尼各马可伦理学》X.6 引阿那卡尔西斯的话："παίζειν δʼ ὅπως σπουδάζῃ"，为了认真去做而玩；消遣像休息，休息是为了活动（"ἕνεκα τῆς ἐνεργείας"，1176b27–1177a1）。英译作 exert oneself（Ross）或 work（Rackham）；这一处的 work，不等于 SAE 技术意义上的"工作"。

在他那里，玩为了认真去做，消遣为了活动。这是对"为什么玩"的回答，与功能说同侧（1.2）。照 3.1，这样的"为了"不是结构上的标志，不改焦点。本文在反向的一端，反向处在回答：本文不以"为了什么"来回答游戏，焦点在局本身；玩过之后人是否更能认真去做，本文不主张（0.4）。

二 局与余项；本文所说的判断

2.1 局

照笑论总论第〇节与 2.1，构是任何正在运作的把握方式；余项是构没有收尽、却又就在眼前的那一点。

局以规则作构。规则并不穷尽参与者仍需作出的把握与回应：棋规定了每个子怎样走，没有定这一盘走哪一步；舞步定了，舞伴此刻往哪边转，没有定；故事讲到这里，下一句还没有讲出来。

2.2 与笑论一 4.1 同形，余项不同

笑论一 4.1 给过一个构与余项同时被保留的例子：打闹游戏里，攻击的形状在，"不是真的攻击"这一余项也在。本文另提出一个命题：在所论的各类局中，规则并不穷尽参与者仍需作出的把握与回应。这个命题由本系列逐类说明，不靠笑论那一例来担保。

这样，结局已知的故事、熟练的演出也容得下：余项不必只是"接下来会发生什么"，也可以是讲的人这一回怎样讲、舞伴这一回怎样接。

2.3 本文所说的判断

本文所说的判断，取笑论的形状：看出"这一局还有这么一个余项"。残局里，是"这里还可以这样走"；故事里，是"它还可以这样走"。

这一判要和两样东西分开。一样是动作：走哪一步，是围绕这一判的动作，这与笑论三 1.3 分开"笑作为判断"与"用笑做事"同法。一个人可以看出了这一步却不走它，也可以走了一步，事后才看出它好在哪里。另一样是预测：预测下一步，12DD 已经有了（笑论一）。听故事的人要预测后面会发生什么，这是材料。到 13DD 以上，那一判有了主人，"这一判是我的"（笑论二）：他看见的不只是下一步会是什么，而是故事还可以这样走。

2.4 纯运气的局：边界条件

纯运气的局，比如没有赌注的掷骰子比大小，本文当作边界条件登记，不定它是不是游戏。纯运气对判断力没有什么练习的帮助；这是作者的观察，不作证明。本文只把它留作边界条件，不进一步断言这样的局里没有可以看出的余项。Caillois 说运气游戏里玩的人是被动的，可以给这一观察作旁证（第二篇）。

三 焦点：只看结构

3.1 参与者为什么参与

本文只看结构，不看内心，也看不了。不管一个人是为了虚荣还是出于兴趣，只要焦点在局内，这一份在结构上就是游戏向；焦点在局外，是非游戏向。"游戏向"只说焦点这一条；一份算不算游戏，还要看第六节的其余几条。

参与者为什么参与？本文把这一问落到结构上：看这一份在结构上接着什么。最常见的是入局那一刻接着什么；开赛之后才押上的赌注，本文看作一样（4.2）。可查的结构标志有四样：赌注、合同、奖金、别人的要求。赌博里，输赢接到赌注上；有奖金的比赛里，结果接到奖金上；职业选手出场，接着一份合同；为了应付编辑而写，接着别人的要求，是工作。

自己为了练而玩，不在这四样里，焦点依然是游戏（这一份仍接在局内，是游戏向）：想练的人入了局，照样是在下这一盘棋。"想练"在某一位那里是一时的意图，还是落在"我应当成为什么"一线上的目的，要看他此刻实际持着什么，本文照笑论、哭论称作"所持"（第七节）；不论哪一种，都不改焦点。

3.2 各种材料各有职务

谈焦点时会碰到三样东西，职务各不相同。可查的材料是规则、契约、结算关系；谁押、谁付，是结算关系里可查的一条，用来说明参与怎样被接住，不用来推出边界。辅助的设想是删除测试：拿掉局外那一样，看还是不是那一局；这要有依据，不能单独承重。可错的提示是此后的话头：它看的是注意，注意按主体记，不进焦点的判定，也不能由玩不玩倒推回去：人不玩了，可以只是累了。

3.3 焦点与聚焦分轴；焦点可以分份

笑论、哭论说的"聚焦"，是注意落在余项上还是构上；本文的"焦点"按结构，说的是这一份接在局内还是局外。两者不在一条轴上，可以交叉。本文不用"聚焦"一词来说焦点。

一局的输赢，可以同时接到局上与局外，所以焦点可以分份。比赛有奖金，所以比赛既是游戏，也是工作，两份分开看；职业选手在比赛中更偏于游戏，比赛之外的准备、讨论更偏于工作。"偏游戏"说的是结构上的主次，不是说已经知道选手心里注意力的比例：比赛中，每一个动作都由局的构接住，奖金与合同只接在结果上；比赛之外、本身不成局的准备，偏于工作。主次按此刻接住动作的是哪一样构来定。

体育精神是一个方向，向着把比赛当游戏。体育这一行可以拿它要求参赛者，那是这一行自己的约定；本文不对玩的人写"应当"。

名次可以在局内。按规则判出的名次与胜负在局内；它们怎样接到收入、资格、合同上，是另一回事。积分对单场是跨局的，对整个联赛又可能是构成规则的一部分。

这一条只管焦点，不管层。层照笑论、哭论的办法，看主体实际持着什么（第七节）。

3.4 用游戏做事

笑论三 1.3 把笑作为判断与用笑做事分开，哭论把哭与用哭做事分开；本文照此，把用游戏做事与游戏分开。为了赢别的东西而玩，给别人安排游戏，机构办游戏（比如演习、联赛），都是用游戏做事。用游戏做事可以是涵育，也可以是殖民（第九节）；为了应付别人的要求，是工作。总的说：就那一份而言，游戏本身不是焦点的，不叫作游戏；局里其余各份，各看各的（第四节）。自己为了练而玩，不在这一列（3.1）。

3.5 主体不会永远没有参与游戏的意图

本文对游戏天性的立场是：主体有判断力，就会想怎么提高判断力，游戏是一个自然的入口；主体不会永远没有参与游戏的意图。

这句话说的是游戏这一整类，不是某一局。某一局要自己入、也走得了（6.1 第 4 条），可以不玩，可以停；这句话与这些并不矛盾，也管不到某一局停不停得下来。

这里的"意图"取日常的意思，不是 7.4 所援引的笑论三 2.2 的那个意图。在某一位那里，它是一时的意图，还是落在"我应当成为什么"一线上的目的，要看他实际持着什么；无论哪一种，都不改焦点。所以它与"游戏没有局外的目的"并不矛盾：焦点按结构看，层按所持看（7.1）。

这句话也推不出某人此刻一定在玩某种游戏，推不出看不出在玩的人少了什么。这与哭论 1.4 说不得不的交叠"不能拿来作外部的要求，也不能拿来作外部的判定"同法。

"不得不"一词，本文只留上游的判断义。局里那一判，若被感到为美的认可、为笑、为哭，它的不得不照上游说（笑论总论 5.2、哭论 1.4）；局里的判断本身是不是不得不，本文不说。

四 判词落在谁的哪一份上

4.1 单位

"是不是游戏"的判词，落在某一位在这一局里的一份上；对整场活动，只说它结构上的取向。这与看层不是同一个单位：是不是游戏按结构，落在一份上；层与判断按所持，落在一判上（7.1）。

4.2 几个例子

球员赌球，对这位球员本人就不再是游戏，正常进行的比赛仍是游戏。他那一份接到押在那一局上的赌注上，押在开赛前或开赛后都一样（本文读法）。

演习和体育比赛一样，结构上是游戏，也是工作。参加的人一个个照 6.1 第 3、4 条看；奉命参加、走不了的那一份怎样看，留到第十三节。为备战而办演习，是机构用游戏做事。

教育里，安排游戏的人那一份，是用游戏做事；孩子在局里的那一份，照四条看。父母在孩子面前的冲突，若是真冲突，就不是游戏；若是为了教育孩子而演的假冲突，焦点也在局外，也不算游戏。孩子不知道是演的，就不是入局的玩家，那一场不成孩子的局。

被拉进来、走不了的那一位，那一局只对他一个人不是游戏。

作弊，是违反或隐蔽改变双方所承认的规则，使对手所进的那一局与实际在进行的那一局出现偏离。一方作弊，作弊的人那一份是非游戏向，不再算游戏；对手照着他所进的那一局在玩，他那一份仍是游戏；这对对手算不算殖民，是另一个问题。所有参与者一起作弊，就不再是游戏了。作弊未必关上余项，它可能只改了胜算（第二篇）。

4.3 赌博与有奖金的比赛

赌博不是游戏；没有赌注的局是游戏，焦点在局本身（纯运气的局另作边界条件，见 2.4）。本文把赌博的排除写明为本系列的范围约定，依据是焦点在局外。球员赌球，排除的是他那一份，不是整场比赛。

落到每一份上：赌博里，局中人的那一份接在赌注上，焦点在局外；有奖金的比赛里，每一位都是游戏与工作两份并存。依据是焦点在不在局外；下面几样只帮助看清这一点，不拿来推出一条普遍的划界标准。两边都有规则作构，6.1 第 1 条满不满足，不影响这里的范围约定。结算关系说明参与怎样被接住：赌注随输赢从输的一方转到赢的一方，对方可以是玩家，也可以是庄家；奖金却由不押在这一局输赢上的一方来付；只在局内计数、带不出局的筹码是计分，不是赌注；奖杯、奖状只表名次，在局内。拿掉局外那一样再看，也可以作辅助，但不承重：没有赌注的同一局牌，焦点回到局内；拿掉奖金，由这笔奖金形成的局外承接随之取消，合同、备战、别人的要求等其余承接，还要一样样看。

对判断力而言，焦点在局外的那一份，局里照样可以有判断：打牌赌钱的人，一样可以看出"这一手还可以这样打"。练没练、练到哪里，要看这一局里实际发生了什么（第五节）。这一例在本系列里只用来守住范围。

报名费汇成奖池、赢的人拿走，算不算赌注，本文不下结论，留到第十三节。

五 同样的局可以重来

5.1 主命题

同样的局可以重来，每次判断力可以不同。这说的不是一场之内悔一步，而是同样的局可以一回回再开。

这是本文的主命题，不进界定：一场即兴的游戏，玩过一回再也不玩，仍然是游戏。

5.2 重开

重开，是回到规则规定的起点；前一局的输赢、积分、纪录怎样计入新局，照规则。已经发生的判断、伤、疲劳、信任，不因重开而撤销。判断那一项，借哭论 4.1 第 6 条的后一半：反身否定能事后撤回所判的效力，撤不回那一下发生过。重开也不保证判断改变，更不保证改善。

5.3 练的是判断力

练的是判断力，不是同一个判断。所以不能单凭前后所判的同异，认定练习有没有发生：两回判得一样，不说明没有练；两回判得不一样，也不说明练了。练习落在判断力上，不落在某一判上。

最低的承诺是：判断力在同样的局里一回回实际用起来、重新用起来，由本人承接。承接一层接笑论二"这一判是我的"：那一判是他的，用起来的是他的判断力。只玩一回的局，判断力用起来的那一回，就是"一回回"的头一回。

就本系列所论游戏中的练习而言，练要落实到主体实际判见局的余项，并由本人承接。这说明怎样记这种练习，不由此推出焦点在哪里，也不排除游戏以外的判断力练习。

每次判断力可以不同。有没有不同、不同在哪里、能不能留住、会不会带到局外，都要另受检验，留到第十三节。

5.4 再讲一遍

小孩子听熟了的故事还要再听，正是同样的局重来。回到开头那个孩子（0.1），把这个例子走完。

熟悉的故事走向仍在，讲—听的约定也本来容许孩子接话，这是构。讲的人在熟悉的一句之前停了下来；故事的走向没有规定这一句由谁说出来，这一点构没有收尽，又就在眼前，这是余项。孩子不只是预计下一句是"狼来了"，而是看见：同一句话，原来还可以在这里由他接上。"这一处还可以这样接"，是本文所说的判断；预计下一句，是材料（2.3）。开不开口、怎样喊，是围绕这一判的动作；他若只是照惯例喊出熟悉的话，不能单凭喊了就说他练了。

若这一回确是他自己看见、自己接上，就照最低的承诺说：判断力在同样的局里又一次用了起来，由他承接（5.3）。这并不证明他的判断力比上一回好，前后判得同或不同都证明不了；也不是说下一回他一定还这样接。

笑论总论 4.3 也把孩子反复要听同一个笑话列为同类的难处。它的一个候选解释，是重新站回那个还没看见的自己，重演当初的落差；由此推出（也是候选）：讲法的次序与停顿仍然起作用。本文的读法不同：这一回看见的，可以是新的余项。两处可以对读，不互为证据。

六 界定与边界

6.1 四条

  1. 局。有规则作构，规则不穷尽参与者仍需作出的把握与回应（第二节）。规则可以是默会的，但要能指出这段互动实际守着什么约束：哪些动作或表示按什么方式被接住，什么会改变或结束这一局。Bateson 所说的"这是玩"（"This is play"）是对这种约定的提示，不能单凭这一命名证明是游戏。这是一条弱条件，分量落在第 3、4 条上。闲聊在广义上算游戏，就像两个人临时起意的赛跑也算。
  2. 人。至少有一位主体参与这一局。这一局里实际有没有判断、有没有练，要看实例；没有材料能指出判断或练习，本文就不说这一局里有。
  3. 焦点。结构上，焦点在局内是游戏向，在局外是非游戏向（第三节）。非游戏向的那一份，是用游戏做事或工作；局仍然是局。
  4. 自愿。局中每一位都是自己进的局，也走得了；一个人的局，这一条只管玩的那一位自己。对还不按主体计的一方，也不封住它转开、不接的路；黑猩猩等，照第八节另说。被拉进来、走不了的那一位，那一局只对他不是游戏；把他拉进来，是以我的目的替代他的法，即殖民（笑论总论 7.3 的宽义）。欺凌是殖民。入了局，退出的路完全被封堵，也是这样：那一局对他不是游戏，是被殖民。被设计成让人停不下来的局，退出的路若还开着，可能还是游戏，但明显侵犯了退出权（第一篇）。

6.3 战争与冲突；"在日常生活之外"

战争与冲突都不是游戏，焦点都不在游戏本身。战争是国家之间的冲突：进攻方为了殖民，防守方为了反殖民。"进攻、防守"指战争论 §5 所论 14DD 一方扩张、15DD 一方防守的结构，不看现实里谁先动手；"进攻为殖民、防守为反殖民"这一对只用于战争。冲突里也许没有殖民的一方，双方都在反殖民也是可能的；怎样读，交给冲突论，本文不展开。军事演习、拳击比赛算游戏。

七 三类局，层在主体身上

7.1 两本账

本系列记两本账。

局按结构记。局里有几个位置，各由谁承接，各自的所求怎样相对。读的一侧可以还没有真的读者，比如写作：写的人设想中的读者，不算已经在局中的一位（7.8）。

层按主体记。某一位在某一刻站在哪一层，要看他实际持着什么，常是混合的。层从外面有时候判不了；判不了的，就说材料不足，不替他定层。

判断与练习也各有单位。判断按某一位在某一刻的那一判来分析（笑论总论 6.1）；练习说的是这位主体的判断力在局中实际用起来、重新用起来，并由本人承接（第五节）。

这套分账的办法借自笑论总论 6.1："一笑的层，按笑者此刻所站之层定；所笑之构的层另记。"本系列借的是这个办法，不是说笑论已经证明了本系列的三分；三分由本系列逐篇说明。

7.2 三类局

局 对面是什么 余项 新打开的、主体可以站到的那一层
一个人的局 规则与材料 规则没定的那一步；材料怎样回应 13DD：这一步是我的。设了尺，可以站到 14DD

表里列的是余项可以从哪里显出来；实际的那一判，仍要指出主体看见了哪一个构没有收尽的哪一点。预测他会怎样做，或随后决定我怎样做，不因此就算本系列所说的判断。

局很难说在某一层。层是主体在局里自己可以站到的，而且很多时候是混合的。篇题里的 DD，读作这一类局新打开的、主体可以站到的那一层，不是局的层。打开不等于担保。各类局里，主体常是几层混合：一个人的局设了尺，主体可以站到 14DD；竞争之局里，也可以以对手为目的，站到 15DD。

7.3 一个人的局与尺

一个人的局为什么新打开 13DD？局要有一位主体；对面只有规则与材料，局摆出来的只有"这一步是我的"，没有另一个人的所求。所以一个人的局也有它新打开的层。

一个人的局，若规则立了一把可以输给它的尺，比如分数、时限、纪录、上一回的自己，主体就可以站到 14DD。通关、拼完、解出是终局的状态，不算尺。规则按这一回实际采纳的构成规则算。

7.4 竞争之局与意图

竞争之局把输赢立为要争的东西，对面是另一位主体，所求与我的相同而相斥。立了可以输的东西，这一点它与设了尺的一个人的局相同；对面站着另一位主体，是竞争之局多出来的。局由此邀请"我要赢"成为此刻的目的，但不担保。

照笑论三 2.2，意图不是目的。竞争之局里随手的想赢，是一时的意图，还不到 14DD；就这一份所持而言，他常站在 13DD，但站在哪里仍要单独看；同一刻他持着的别的东西，也分开看。想赢落在"我应当成为什么"一线上（笑论三 1.1），才到 14DD。

7.5 合作之局与 15DD

合作之局为什么新打开 15DD？两个人的局里，对面的下一步都是他的。区别在所求：竞争之局里，他那一步是我要胜过的；合作之局里，他那一步是我们一起要的东西的一部分，这一局只在他那一步仍是他的、又与我的接上时才成。讲—听之局里写的一侧，另论，见 7.8 与第十三节。

所以合作之局摆出的，是一位自己作判断、又和我所求在一处的伙伴。这是 15DD 所持里"承认他、不预占"在行为上的样子；承认本身，局只能邀请。双方的分工、约好的动作、熟练的编排，也不会单凭自己取消他的主体位置：这样的结构给不预占留下了条件，却不担保我已经承认他。工具性的对待是否压掉了他在局里的参与，是另一个问题，我的姿态单独改变不了局的结构。

15DD 不仅可以练，而且非常值得练；值得，但无法强制，跟涵育很像。无法强制有两头：别人强制不了，逼人入局是殖民（6.1 第 4 条）；自己也强制不了，咬着牙要自己此刻承认他，是意志力，不是姿态。

所以此刻对准"练 15DD"的那一份所持，到不了 15DD，有两道门挡着。一道对着他：把伙伴当作我练习的材料，就是要他为我的目的服务；哭论 4.1 第 4 条说"不要求他的失去为我的善心或我的目的服务"，这里把它推到了局里，这是本系列的推及。一道对着自己：不疑"不通过自我强制"（精神分析（四）§2.2）。

同一场里，玩的人还可以持着别的东西，比如想把这支舞跳好；这些分开看，不能因为有自己的目标，就说这一刻没有 15DD。

"值得练"落在去玩上，落在给游戏以发生的条件上。这些在第三篇展开。

7.6 层的判读

看层，照哭论第三节表后的四条：

  • 撤回了要求，就按改变后的运作重读；
  • 自述与可查的言行相抵，是材料冲突；
  • 不同的动作或注意份并行，分开看；把这一条推到并行的几份所持上，是本系列的推及；
  • 只有自述，就是材料不足。

层从外面有时候判不了，判不了就不替他定。

7.7 限时拼图

一个人拼一幅拼图，想十分钟拼完。对面是规则与拼块，这是一个人的局。

十分钟进没进这一局的构成规则，是第一个问题。若他入局前定下"十分钟内拼完才算成"，十分钟就成了这一局的成功条件，是一把尺；若只是一个愿望，没进入成功条件，就不是尺。进没进，看可查的材料：定了计时、宣布了成功条件、记了成绩；只有自述，就是材料不足。有尺，他可以站到 14DD；没有尺，13DD 照常打开。

可是十分钟进了构成规则，只说明这一局设了尺。他此刻是一般的意图，还是落在"我应当成为什么"一线上的目的，仍要看他实际持着什么；看不看时钟，单凭这一点定不了层。看不清的，就说材料不足。两种情形，焦点都在局内，都是游戏向：尺是局内的规则，不是局外的东西。

闲聊。两个人的焦点都在这一场谈话上，伙伴的下一句是他的，所以归第三篇（本文读法）。

八 辖域与排除

本系列的辖域是有主体参与的游戏；主体按 13DD 计（笑论总论 6.4 的术语锁）。

往下，有几样只登记，不展开。12DD 的趋向，比如婴儿那一边的躲猫猫；同一局里大人那一边有主体，照本系列论。动物的游戏；黑猩猩按笑论一 4.2 读作已入 13DD 的初级阶段，它们的游戏怎样看，留到第十三节。感知层：照笑论总论 8.2 挠痒的办法分两段，眩晕那一下在 10DD；荡多高、敢不敢松手、什么时候停、和别人怎样配合，已是有主体的局，两段的分界留到第十三节。

往上，16DD，双方互以对方为目的，只登记。

不是游戏的，有工作、赌博（范围约定）、欺凌、战争与冲突、父母在孩子面前的冲突（第六节）。纯运气的局是边界条件（2.4）。用游戏做事与游戏分开（3.4）。听故事先会笑、后会哭这一估计，归笑论（第十三节）。

九 练习、训练、工作、涵育

就游戏向的这一份而言，判断力的练习是结果。为别人的练习安排游戏，是用游戏做事；自己为了练而玩，焦点仍在局上（3.1）。练习的最低承诺见第五节；它留下了什么，要另受检验。工作的焦点在局外，为了应付编辑而写是工作。训练，本文只界定一类：本身不成局、以下一局或下一场考核为焦点的反复，偏于工作；这不是一切训练的共同定义。本身成局的练习赛、演习是游戏；自己为了练而打的，焦点仍在局上，工作那一份来自职务、合同或考核。

给别人安排游戏，是用游戏做事；它可以是涵育，这接行动论第六篇。D34 说："涵育要对方愿意接受、适合接受、时机成熟，这三样不能替对方答"；"所以涵育必然留白：留的不是话，是对方那一份，认不认、怎么判、改不改、走不走"。D35 说，取消不了的仍有"不替对方立法；不封对方拒绝、修订、退出与改向的通道"；这接 6.1 第 4 条的"走得了"，也接停不下来的局一例里的退出权（第一篇）。设计停不下来的局的人不一定在涵育，把 D35 用到那里，是本系列的推及。D36 说："一经他自己的法认领，就是他的"：别人安排的游戏里，练习这个结果归玩的人承接；这一条只说明构成上归谁，不推出安排的人没有贡献。D37 说"涵育者也可以被涵育"，接第三篇现场表演的"一起创作"。

用游戏做事也可以是殖民：逼人入局，封死退出的路（6.1 第 4 条）；以后果逼人认领输赢或判断（笑论总论 7.3）。

上游有两处可以对接。教育篇 §3.2 的原句是"Peek-a-boo（遮脸游戏）不只是逗小孩的游戏，它是12DD建立的训练装置"。它说的是这一活动在 12DD 对预测建立的作用，在发育功能一边，推不出玩的人必须以训练为目的；本文管的是 13DD 以上、玩的人那一侧，不改写上游。教育篇 §7.3 把胎教的一项意义放在母亲一侧："在母亲（14DD-15DD的练习）"。本文借此作对照（本文读法）：若母亲这一次所向的是孩子，练习可以在母亲一侧作为结果发生。这不把胎教纳入本系列的游戏，也不由上游一句话认定哪一次胎教的实际层位。

十 对照：席勒（主要对照）

10.1 所据

席勒《审美教育书简》（1795）里与本文相关的几处：

  • 第十五封信："der Mensch spielt nur, wo er in voller Bedeutung des Worts Mensch ist, und er ist nur da ganz Mensch, wo er spielt."
  • 第十四、十五封信：游戏冲动，是感性冲动与形式冲动结合起作用的那一种；它的对象"wird also lebende Gestalt heißen können"，即最宽意义上的美。"der Mensch soll mit der Schönheit nur spielen, und er soll nur mit der Schönheit spielen"一句，本文直接据德文。
  • 第二十一封信："denn die Schönheit gibt schlechterdings kein einzelnes Resultat, weder für den Verstand noch für den Willen"；人由此"von Natur wegen möglich gemacht ist, aus sich selbst zu machen, was er will"。
  • 第二十三封信：审美状态"an sich selbst weder für unsere Einsichten noch Gesinnungen etwas entscheidet"，却是"die nothwendige Bedingung"；"es gibt keinen andern Weg, den sinnlichen Menschen vernünftig zu machen, als daß man denselben zuvor ästhetisch macht"。
  • 第二封信："weil es die Schönheit ist, durch welche man zu der Freiheit wandert"。

10.2 相近的两处

一处是游戏出于人的天性。第十五封信那一句，把游戏放在人之为人的地方；本文说游戏是人的天性（0.2）。

另一处是不替人决定什么。审美自身不替认识与意志决定什么，只使人能"aus sich selbst zu machen, was er will"。这很近本文的"练习是结果"，也近涵育的留白（第九节）：游戏给出条件，判断力用起来是玩的人自己的事。

10.3 分开的两处

一处是唯一的路还是一条路。他把审美状态立为使感性的人成为理性的人的必要条件，"没有别的路"；本文说游戏是判断力练习的一条路，不是唯一的路。

另一处是游戏的对象。他的游戏冲动对着美，人"只应当同美游戏"；本文里，美是判断力的一种表现，游戏是判断力练习的一条路，局里的余项不必是美。

10.4 "完全是人"

"完全是人"一句，要放回两种冲动与整全能力的语境里读，不简化为按日常游戏玩得多少，来评一个人完整不完整。这里仍守 0.4 的限度：不玩的人不因此少了什么；本文也不以整全人性的理想，对人立普遍的要求。

两家所说的"游戏"，范围先要对齐；这是理论位置的比较，不拿来作同一经验命题的反证。席勒的教育一面，在第三篇展开。

十一 其余对照

11.1 康德

《判断力批判》§9 说，认识能力在审美判断里处于"in einem freien Spiele"（AA 5:217）。在他那里，自由游戏是审美判断里的心意状态；本文的游戏是判断力练习的一条路，两处的"游戏"不是一件事。

§54 附释说："Alles wechselnde freie Spiel der Empfindungen (die keine Absicht zum Grunde haben) vergnügt, weil es das Gefühl der Gesundheit befördert"（5:331）；他又把它分为"Glücksspiel, Tonspiel und Gedankenspiel"。本文与它同向，只到"不以意图为根据"一句为止；而"keine Absicht"也把赌博包在了里面。其后的"weil es das Gefühl der Gesundheit befördert"，是功能的解释，与本文不拿来作回答的功能说同侧（1.2）。

说到赌博，他说赌博里的利益"bei weitem nicht so groß ist, als das Interesse an der Art, wie wir es uns zu verschaffen suchen"。这是两种兴趣的强弱比较，是心里的事；本文只看结构，不替他判这一段心理（3.1）。他随即以"da das Glücksspiel kein schönes Spiel ist"把它放在一边，排出去的是该处所论的"美的游戏"，不是一切游戏。

11.2 Huizinga

《游戏的人》（1938）第一章的定义含这样几项：自由的行动；有意识地立在日常生活之外；能叫玩的人全神投入；"geen direct materieel belang"；在有意划定的时空里；照一定的规则；会生出团体。

"没有直接的物质利益"本身就是结构上的说法，近于 6.1 第 3 条。它在第一章的定义里，是对游戏活动整体的限定；本文则把局内与局外的承接按份分开看。这里比较的只是这一定义的表述单位：他在第三章又说，游戏的赌注可以有物质价值，"Prijs kan een lauwerkrans zijn of een geldsom, of elke andere materieele waarde"（p. 79）。

"在日常生活之外"，本文不取（6.3）。

他论战争时说，交战双方把对方当作为一件有权争的事而战的对手时，竞赛的一面在起作用；到了"总体战"的理论，战争最后一点文化的功能，连同游戏的功能，也被放弃了（第五章，p. 118）。本文说战争不是游戏。他看的是战争里竞赛的一面，本文看的是焦点在不在局本身。

11.3 维特根斯坦

《哲学研究》§66："Wie gesagt: denk nicht, sondern schau!"；§67："die ›Spiele‹ bilden eine Familie"；§69："Wie gesagt, wir können – für einen besondern Zweck – eine Grenze ziehen."

本文不把他写成禁止定义，也不拿定义去反驳他。本文为本文的目的划出这一类，有两个条件：天性、练习这些主张，只对界定出的这一类说；做法与哭论 4.1 相同，都是为本文的目的划出一类，但这一类不像哭论那样窄。四条是不是循环、有没有解释力，要由本系列各篇的分析来表明。

十二 术语

  • 构、余项：照笑论总论第〇节与 2.1。构是任何正在运作的把握方式；余项是构没有收尽、却又就在眼前的那一点。
  • 局：以规则作构的一段互动；规则可以是默会的，须指得出它实际守着的约束（6.1 第 1 条）。
  • 判断、判见：本文所说的判断，是判见、即看出"这一局还有这么一个余项"。走哪一步是动作；预测是材料（2.3）。
  • 焦点：按结构，说这一份接在局内还是局外；接在局内是游戏向，接在局外是非游戏向（第三节）。与笑论、哭论的"聚焦"不在一条轴上：聚焦说注意落在余项上还是构上。本文不用"聚焦"一词说焦点。
  • 份：专指判词的单位，属于焦点一轴，即"某一位在这一局里的一份"（第四节）。说层时的"并行的几份所持"、说注意时的"注意份"，与这一"份"不同。
  • 用游戏做事：围绕游戏的行动，与游戏分开（3.4）。
  • 新打开的层：一类局新打开的、主体可以站到的那一层，不是局的层（7.2）。打开不等于担保。
  • 尺：一个人的局里，规则所立、可以输给它的东西：分数、时限、纪录、上一回的自己。通关、拼完、解出不算尺（7.3）。
  • 练习：判断力在局里一回回实际用起来、重新用起来，由本人承接；就游戏向的这一份而言，是结果，不是目的（0.2、第五节）。
  • 训练：本文只界定一类：本身不成局、以下一局或下一场考核为焦点的反复，偏于工作（第九节）。
  • 涵育、殖民：照行动论第六篇；殖民取笑论总论 7.3 的宽义：以自身目的替代他者自己的法（第九节）。
  • 材料不足：只有自述、没有可查的言行时，不替他定层（7.6）。
  • 不得不：不立为本系列的术语，只留上游的判断义。说游戏这一整类时，用"主体不会永远没有参与游戏的意图"（3.5）。
  • 英文：先用本系列的定义，再逐个语境定译；不预设"游戏""局"逐词对应 play 与 game。其余照笑论术语表与哭论补编。

十三 留下的问题

关于练习。练习留下了什么：判断力有没有不同、能不能留住、会不会带到局外，本文都没有回答（第五节）。"跟涵育很像"能不能说到自己对自己身上，也是问题：关系论第二篇说，过去的自己可以涵育或殖民现在的自己；这接得到跨时的一面，却接不到"此刻咬着牙"要自己承认他的那一面，即 7.5 第二道门"对着自己"的那一面。

关于边界。纯运气的局只作边界条件，不下结论（2.4）。报名费汇成奖池，钱来自参赛者，赢的人拿走，算不算赌注，不下结论（4.3）。一起作弊，可以是各自违反或隐蔽改变所承认的规则，也可以是合谋对着局外的第三方；大家明着商定改一条规则，则是开了新局；两者怎样分，本文不展开（4.2）。出于好奇的研究、做着玩的一顿饭，6.1 第 1 条的弱条件放得进来，本文不逐例看（6.1）。

关于演习与工作。演习里奉命参加的那一份，本文的读法是：走不了，若出于他所担的工作（职务、合同），算工作；若出于有人替他立法、把他拉进来，落到 6.1 第 4 条。那项工作本身怎样进的（比如征召），本文不判。职业选手按合同必须出场的那一场，同此（4.2）。机构办游戏，即用游戏做事在机构一层的样子，也待另论（3.4）。

关于冲突与退出。冲突里双方都在反殖民，怎样读？一种读法是所争的那一块不先定归谁；另一种是双方各自守住不被代立法的位置，不因两条法不相容，就先把一方判成殖民者。都交冲突论，本文不展开（6.3）。退出权与 SAE 权利论怎样对位，具体机制与适用，待另论（6.1 第 4 条；第一篇）。

关于写作。写作在什么时候、是否作为合作之局成局，本文不定；写作在本系列的用处是换位，不必等这个问题有了答案（7.5、7.8）。

关于辖域。黑猩猩等已入 13DD 初级阶段的动物，它们的游戏怎样看（笑论一 4.2；第八节）；感知层与有主体的局在哪里分界（第八节）；都待另论。教育篇 §3.2 说"15DD有自己的peek-a-boo"：父母在孩子面前"冲突-承认-冲突-承认"；又说"15DD的peek-a-boo不是设计出来的游戏，是父母之间关系的质量"。教育篇借 peek-a-boo 一词作结构上的类比；照第四节，父母之间的冲突不是游戏；它是不是谁的判断力练习，本文不判。

移交笑论的。听故事，先会笑，后会哭。哭论第十一节把这一估计移交本系列：小朋友听故事，笑得出来在先，哭得出来在后。这一项归笑论系列，本系列不展开；同层比较、理解负荷、量词与自发话头等设计，随之归笑论。

术语。英文术语的定译（第十二节）。

结论

本系列写判断力怎样练习，只写游戏这一条路。游戏是人的天性，焦点在局本身；就游戏向的那一份而言，判断力的练习是结果，不是目的，参与者自己是否以练为目的，是另一回事；练的是判断力，不是同一个判断。同样的局可以重来，每次判断力可以不同。局以规则作构，规则不穷尽参与者仍需作出的把握与回应；本文所说的判断，是看出这一局还有这么一个余项，走哪一步是另一回事。

是不是游戏，只看结构：这一份接在局内，是游戏向；接在局外，是非游戏向。判词落在某一位在这一局里的一份上，所以一场比赛可以同时是游戏与工作，球员赌球只对他自己不是游戏，被拉进来、走不了的那一位只对他自己不是游戏，所有参与者一起作弊，就不再是游戏了。自己为了练而玩，焦点依然是游戏。层在主体身上，常是混合的：一个人的局、竞争之局、合作之局，分别新打开主体可以站到的 13DD、14DD、15DD，打开不等于担保。15DD 值得练，无法强制，跟涵育很像。

主体有判断力，就会想怎么提高判断力，游戏是一个自然的入口。本文写的是判断力，不是游戏论：赌博作范围约定，纯运气的局作边界条件，战争与冲突不是游戏，都只登记，不承重。席勒是主要对照：两家都说游戏出于天性；他说审美不替认识与意志决定什么，近于本文的"练习是结果"；他立审美为唯一的路，本文说游戏是一条路。Groos 是最近的邻居：他问游戏为什么在自然里有，本文问判断力在局里落在哪里。
